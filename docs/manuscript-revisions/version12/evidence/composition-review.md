## Mathematical checks

1. **Mixed precision.** The order-12 and order-40 truncations use the same integral formal group and logarithm as the retained proof. At the permitted unramified large primes their denominators are units to the required truncation order; the original factorial estimate also bounds the tail after those orders. The primary and opposite-place comparisons first use integral unit factors and the two consecutive integers `h,h-1`; they do not divide by an unknown unit. The cross tests transfer positivity before the high-order ratios are used. The primary depth is at least 20, hence its order-four leading error has order at least 80. Only the input comparison uses order 12 at the alternate place; the Lucas comparisons retain order 40. The reduced denominator of `n-beta` is a unit at every place where its valuation is positive, so passing to its integer numerator is valid.

2. **Normalized detector poles.** The retained detector guards force the rational parameter `b` to be nonzero: otherwise the auxiliary sum-of-squares/Pell guard gives its prohibited zero. Scaling the seven old witnesses by the specified powers of `b` converts the old equations to the six new equations and gives a rational inverse. No zero witness is excluded by these scalings. The scalar pole is not one of these three normalized detector poles; it retains the full realization predicate.

3. **Half-trace guards.** Requiring the rational coefficient of each of five differences to be nonzero is a strengthening, not an equivalence for arbitrary old witnesses. Soundness follows because a nonzero rational coefficient makes the field difference nonzero, recovering the old inverse. Completeness fixes the root points and excludes their finitely many denominator primes before selecting contact primes. At a selected split prime, one conjugate difference has negative valuation and the other is integral; their sum cannot vanish. This supplies all five guards with the same prime selection. No scalar `S-P` difference is included in this replacement.

4. **Anchor.** The computed leading residue of `f([s]P)` for nonzero even `s` has coefficient valuations `v_3(f_1)=2+2v_3(s)` and `v_3(f_0)>=3+2v_3(s)`. The retained 2-integrality proof applies to both coefficients. Consequently the one-coefficient semilocal predicate selects exactly the old allowed nonzero even subgroup for `C=3^(2^25)`. The ternary predicate includes zero and is justified by the quaternion ramification test at 2 and 3, with its real place split. Translating its private witness yields the displayed residual with term `-4Cw`; it does not alter its solvability. The largest coefficient is `16C^2` at `w^2`, with no extra constant contribution at that monomial.

5. **Short scalar norm tree.** Odd valuation of the contact parameter prevents cancellation in each successive rational norm. The final pole obstruction forces positivity of the contact valuation and bounds the two coordinate valuations of `z(R)` by at least 32. Positivity of `Norm(f(H))` gives at least one formal place for `H`; it is not necessary, or valid in general, to infer that `R` itself is formal. If `R` is near the other zero of `z`, the duplication formula puts `[2]R` in the formal group and gives the same positive valuation for its formal parameter. Apply the logarithm to `[h]([2]R)=[2s]H` there. Since the prime is odd and `h` is integral, the resulting bound is the required `v_p(s)>=32`. Thus the previous height bound with exponent `19/32` survives unchanged. For completeness the old stronger six-leaf preparation implies the new tree's integrality and unit conditions, after which fresh full scalar pole witnesses are chosen.

6. **Reciprocal scalar chart and cancellation.** The denominator `d=c_*x+D` is positive in both real embeddings for the odd chart `x=h^2`, so it never vanishes. The identities `dg=N` and `d^2k=M` recover the ordinary subtraction chart when `x!=c_*`. The retained inverse of `f_0=g_0^2+2g_1^2` forces `g!=0`; the identity `k^2+Dg^4=1` excludes `k=0` by the field norm. At `x=c_*`, the four chart signs give only the actual cases `S=P` and `S=-P`. The first is excluded by `g!=0`. The second is the genuine point `R=-2P`, not a spurious rational point, but its `f_1` has 3-adic valuation 2 and fails the retained physical anchor. This exclusion uses the explicit small point, not the later large-index conclusion, so the argument is not circular. The squared-denominator cancellation uses `dL=a_*K-2Db_*G` with `d,a_*` nonzero. It restores the deleted squared denominator uniquely and changes no outside coordinate. The construction does not apply the reciprocal replacement to detector `V_0`.

7. **Mixed toric circuit.** The retained equations force `e,q!=0`. Solving the seven equations successively gives the stated Laurent monomials and the outputs `q_12=q e^22` and `q_40=q e^78`. The stored square of `q_12` is used only after `q_12` has been determined. Thus there is no circular constraint or hidden zero component. The six old internal powers are recovered, and all external consumers are retained.

8. **Norm reuse.** The existing coordinate is `u=h_0 h_1`, not the full irrational coefficient `2h_0 h_1`. Accordingly the correct identity is `Norm(f(H))^2=t^2-8u^2`. Deleting the norm coordinate is a polynomial substitution with a unique inverse value. It needs neither a division nor a nonzero assumption, and the scalar soundness proof applies after this reinsertion.

## Global soundness and simultaneous completeness

The final rational solution recovers every old point domain needed by the retained odd-label and height arguments. It does not recover every old private witness for the strengthened arithmetic predicates, and the proof correctly does not claim that.

The anchor and scalar argument give `log_2|a-1|>25165823.5` and `h(beta)<=H|a-1|^(19/32)`. With `H<2^2093966`, the displayed margins are valid. If the Lucas polynomial is nonzero, its bound by `5B^4` and the order-40 contact bound imply `B^(1/10)<24H`, contradicting the anchor range. Its vanishing and the nonzero label `b` give the retained Lucas lower bound. A nonzero numerator of `n-beta`, using order 12, instead implies `log B<(31/200)|a|`, contradicting that lower bound. Finally `2X^2+3` integral implies `X` integral by the reduced-denominator argument. The lowered precision is therefore checked at its actual downstream consumers.

Completeness has one compatible order of choices. Fix the integer input. Carry out the stronger old scalar preparation; choose the positive shared index divisible by 64 and the sufficiently divisible scalar index divisible by 6. Fix the resulting odd Lucas labels and all root points. Choose the positive rational square scales. Enlarge the continuity modulus for the new rational functions at these prepared points, and enlarge the finite prime cutoff for the fixed root denominators. Only then choose the simultaneous contact primes. The old separation conditions make the congruences modulo `Pi^19 M_-`, `M_+`, and `L` compatible. The real-progression choice then avoids the finite forbidden loci and realizes the required primary depth and alternate comparisons. The same reductions give all five half-trace guards. Finally obtain the pole witnesses and apply the exact coordinate changes. No new demand is imposed on already chosen primes, and no independently chosen incompatible scalar witness is reused.
