# V13 local monodromy application before Pan

Date: 2026-11-08. Reviewer: `/root/restart_hodge_gluing`. No shared source edits or new formal proofs.

## Bounded result

The actual local implication required in both V13 height arguments is valid: a continuous finite-coefficient-field representation r_ell whose underlying Q_ell representation is de Rham is potentially semistable. It applies to the actual two-dimensional Tate multiplicity representation, not to a replacement representation. A single local extension works for all coefficient embeddings. No effective bound on that extension, no semistability descent, and no integral comparison are used or obtained.

A final published statement with the needed hypotheses and definitions has been read: Pierre Colmez, *Espaces vectoriels de dimension finie et représentations de de Rham*, Astérisque 319 (2008), 117–186, Theorem 0.1 and §0.2, printed page 118, with local-field setup in §0.1 on pages 117–118. This is an independent proof of the same monodromy theorem, openly archived by Numdam. It gives a published source for the exact implication, but is not presently the manuscript's citation. Adding it alongside Berger is a small explicit source-adoption action for the integrator, not an edit made by this review.

The 2002 Berger author text and current author errata have also been read. The final Inventiones typeset full text was not accessible through the publisher's normal subscription route, so the author-to-final comparison for that particular PDF remains open. Publication metadata and a matching published abstract are not substituted for that comparison. The application is supported by the separate published Colmez theorem; this is not a claim of byte identity between any author PDF and a journal version.

## Exact V13 objects and connecting argument

- First height route: lines 795–815 define the finite coefficient field and rho_ell = beta^−1 a_sigma mu_sigma sigma, then use matrix units to give rho_ell ≅ r_ell ⊕ r_ell. On G_(L') it is the actual Tate action. Lines 815–818 invoke Berger; lines 832–844 consume the local property at ell=5 in Pan's theorem.
- Companion height route: lines 7214–7230 construct the analogous actual rho_ell and r_ell, with de Rhamness and weights supplied by the same lemma. Lines 7238–7246 consume the property in the published Pan formulation. The subsequent finite-twist argument is separate.
- Lemma `lem:de-rham-descent`, lines 16472–16539, explicitly uses a finite coefficient extension C_ell/Q_ell and the isomorphism V_ell(A) tensor C_ell ≅ r|_(G_F) ⊕ r|_(G_F). Its conclusion is that the underlying representation of r is de Rham and has weights 0,−1 at every coefficient embedding. Proper-smooth comparison, the Tate/cohomology duality, closure and finite de Rham descent are separately reviewed predecessors, not consequences of the monodromy theorem.

For the monodromy step, write E=C_ell and W=Res_(E/Q_ell)(r|_(G_(Q_ell))). Then dim_(Q_ell) W=2[E:Q_ell], W is continuous, and the preceding lemma supplies de Rhamness. Apply the published theorem to W to obtain a finite local extension L_local/Q_ell over which W is semistable. This already is potential semistability of the E-linear representation in the usual underlying-representation definition.

For a coefficient-period formulation, multiplication by every e in E commutes with the Galois action on W. Hence it acts on D_st,L_local(W), and the full semistable comparison is E-linear by functoriality. The comparison therefore retains the original E-action and r rather than replacing it by a trace, determinant, different summand or unrelated Q_ell representation. Equivalently, after extending coefficients and splitting the coefficient algebra, its rank-two factors give all coefficient embeddings over the same L_local. No choice of a different local extension for each coefficient embedding is required.

This local extension is not the global field L' with the height-route degree bound. The proof neither identifies them nor charges a new field degree to the isogeny budget: Pan requires the local existence property, not a bounded global extension realizing semistability. At ell=5 the distinct Tate weights remain 0,−1. The monodromy theorem does not change their sign convention and does not supply oddness or irreducibility.

## Nine application obligations

1. **Source hypotheses.** Required: finite-dimensional continuous Q_p representation of a local Galois group with full de Rham period dimension. Constructed: W above, from the actual finite E-valued r_ell and the de Rham lemma. Match: finite E gives finite Q_ell dimension, and the lemma proves de Rhamness rather than only Hodge–Tate weights. Evidence: V13 795–815, 7214–7230, 16472–16539; Colmez §§0.1–0.2.

2. **Constructed objects.** Required: identify the representation supplied to the theorem. Constructed: the same Tate multiplicity summand selected by matrix units after splitting B, with constant coefficient action. Match: scalar restriction forgets only E-linearity; it changes neither the underlying Galois action nor the representation subsequently extended to Qbar_5. Evidence: the two exact construction passages and the E-linear comparison above.

3. **Domain and index coverage.** Required: every local coefficient instance consumed by Pan. Constructed: a fixed finite E at each ell, with every coefficient embedding included in W. Match: the theorem is pointwise for each such W; actual consumption is ell=5. No uniformity over all ell, families or conductor supports is asserted. Evidence: de Rham lemma and the two Pan call sites.

4. **Transition and comparison maps.** Required: preserve the source representation through scalar restriction, local extension and period comparison. Constructed: W=Res_(E/Q_ell) r, restriction to G_(L_local), and the natural E-equivariant semistable comparison. Match: the commutant E survives functorially; no semistability descent to Q_ell is claimed. The earlier finite-extension step descends de Rhamness only. Evidence: displayed connecting argument and V13 16513–16527.

5. **Identity of the consumed object.** Required: potential semistability of r_5 itself, not merely its double, determinant or geometric restriction. Constructed: apply the theorem directly to the underlying r_5 after de Rham descent. Match: the comparison's E-action reconstructs the same r_5, and potential semistability is exactly the local geometric condition in the published downstream statement. Evidence: Calegari pages 611–612 and V13 7238–7246.

6. **Coefficients and normalization.** Required: distinguish rational p-adic periods from integral lattices and preserve Hodge–Tate convention. Constructed: V_ell(A), Q_ell and finite E, not T_ell(A) as an integral representation. Match: all scalar restriction and period comparisons are rational. Tate weights are 0,−1; neither the corrected N scalar nor an integral determinant/KS image is consumed. Evidence: V13 lemma, Berger errata and published Colmez definitions.

7. **Local conditions.** Required: the theorem must apply at the actual local base and give the extension property requested downstream. Constructed: Q_5 has perfect finite residue field; any auxiliary geometric completion F/Q_5 in the de Rham lemma is finite. Match: de Rham descent is performed first; the monodromy theorem then supplies some finite local extension without a reduction-type or ordinary assumption. Ramification away from five is established elsewhere. Evidence: source local setup and manuscript call sites.

8. **Exceptional cases.** Required: no hidden weight, p=5, coefficient, or ramification exception. Constructed: dimension 2 over E is positive; E is finite; p=5 lies in Pan's stated p≥5 range. Match: monodromy itself imposes no residual-image restriction or strict negativity of weights. Negative weights in an intermediate Berger construction are handled by twisting inside the source proof, not imposed on this application. No claim of crystallinity or semistability over the original base is made. Evidence: Berger Theorem 0.7/Corollary 5.22 versus intermediate Theorem 0.5; Colmez Theorem 0.1.

9. **Downstream consumption.** Required: the local geometric condition for Pan, combined with separately supplied irreducibility, oddness, distinct weights and finite ramification. Constructed: this review supplies only potential semistability of the already de Rham r_5. Match: this is exactly one Pan premise. It gives no modularity theorem by itself and no PEL, Arakelov, sourceArithmetic or final 318/326 conclusion. Evidence: the published downstream statement and separate Pan/Brinon–Conrad/Scholze–Milne review boundaries.
