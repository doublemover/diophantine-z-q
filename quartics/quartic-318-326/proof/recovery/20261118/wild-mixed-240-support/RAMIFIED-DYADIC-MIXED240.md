# Unequal first-three dyadic coefficient classes: exact initial singular support outside T=0

For q∈{±1,±2}⁴ assume q₀,q₁,q₂ are not all the same; these are 240 of the 256 ordered types modulo dyadically unramified quadratic multipliers. Write p_i=v₂(q_i)∈{0,1}. Over the dyadic fibre the **original projective normalization** has the following precisely classified singular parameter values **outside T=0**:

* For 216 types it is already regular everywhere outside T=0, including infinity.
* If the first three q_i are all odd with mixed signs, and q₃ has odd valuation, the only other singular parameter value is T=1 (12 types).
* If the first three q_i all have odd valuation with mixed signs, and q₃ also has odd valuation, the only other singular parameter value is infinity (12 types).

In the last two cases there really are singular points above the stated extra parameter, not just a failed smoothness test. The assertions extend by finite étale scalar comparison to the corresponding arbitrary rational squareclasses. No count of points or explicit subsequent resolution over T=0 follows from this note. “Outside T=0” always refers to the *dyadic fibre*, and “regular” to the total space.

## Extract the real coefficient algebra after an étale parameter cover

Let d_j=b_j with b=(0,32,76832,−343). On the finite chart invert T,T−32,T−76832 and adjoin h_j²+h_j=−b_j/(4T) for j=1,2, so s_j=1+2h_j are unit square roots of 1−b_j/T. This is finite faithfully flat étale, including at every dyadic point with T≠0. On the reciprocal patch around s=0 (s=1/T, invert ∏_{j=1}³(1−b_j s)) use h_j²+h_j=−b_j s/4 instead. The generic parameter field E has degree 4 from the two branch ratios, remains geometrically a field after any constant extension, and is the same on the Laurent overlap.

Writing r_i²=q_i(T−b_i), the generic pullback contains constant generators γ_j=r_j/(r₀s_j), γ_j²=q_j/q₀. The two-root constant algebra splits as 4/[K:Q] copies of E·K for K=Q(√(q₁/q₀),√(q₂/q₀)). For rank one this is the elementary duplication of a trivial or duplicate quadratic radical; for independent rank two it is the biquadratic field. The two remaining radicals for r₀ and r₃ are independent over E·K by their separate odd valuations: T=−343 is unramified for E and isolates r₃, and T=∞ is unramified for E and has odd pole for T. This accounts for all the generic factors, each of degree 4[K:Q] over E. The intrinsic normalization commutes with this **parameter-étale** change; the following regularity calculation on the normal components of the pullback is not descent through a ramified scalar cover.

All q ratios belong to the squareclass plane generated by −1,2. Under the unequal hypothesis K is one of the four fields in the table. In O_K localized at 2 there is one dyadic prime and residue F₂; the indicated monic Eisenstein equation gives a uniformizer π with ramification index e=[K:Q]. Its generated local order is a DVR: its maximal ideal is (π), the constant 2 is a unit times πᵉ from the equation, and it is a domain finite over Z_(2), hence equals the localized maximal order. Set η=2/πᵉ. In the last column, reduce modulo 2, i.e. modulo πᵉ, in F₂[π]/(πᵉ).

| K | uniformizer | equation | e | η mod 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q(i) | 1+i | π²−2π+2=0 | 2 | 1+π |
| Q(√2) | √2 | π²−2=0 | 2 | 1 |
| Q(√−2) | √−2 | π²+2=0 | 2 | 1 |
| Q(ζ₈) | 1−ζ₈ | π⁴−4π³+6π²−4π+2=0 | 4 | 1+π² |

Indeed for the quartic field η is the negative inverse of 1−2π+3π²−2π³, which reduces to (1+π²)⁻¹=1+π². This is the square (1+π)² modulo π⁴, whereas the Gaussian 1+π is not a square modulo π². The fields here are actual subfields of a parameter-étale pullback component. No assertion is made that ramified scalar extension of a regular surface is regular.

## A direct integral presentation on each component

Use scaled units c_i=q_i/π^(e p_i) (i=0,3). On the finite component put U=r₀/π^(e p₀/2), V=r₃/π^(e p₃/2). Then U²=c₀T and V²=λ U²+μ where
    finite:    λ=c₃/c₀,        μ=343 c₃ .
On the reciprocal component take U=s r₀/π^(e p₀/2), V=(r₃/r₀)π^(e(p₀−p₃)/2), giving U²=c₀s and
    reciprocal:  λ=343 c₃/c₀²,    μ=c₃/c₀ .
These are units in the coefficient DVR and have residue one. The integer exponents exist since e is even. Both sets of functions recover r₀,r₃ generically. Modulo 2 each c_i is η^{p_i} since the rational unit parts of our representatives are signs.

If λ and μ are squares modulo πᵉ, choose explicit units A,B with A²≡λ, B²≡μ mod πᵉ: their residues are powers of the displayed root of η (in the Gaussian case this applies when its exponent is even; take root 1). Put m=e/2 and Z=(V−AU−B)/π^m. The integral equation is
    Z²+(2/π^m)(AU+B)Z+[(A²−λ)/π^(2m)]U²
           +(2AB/π^(2m))U+(B²−μ)/π^(2m)=0 .
Over the coefficient ring before the h_j adjunctions eliminate T (respectively s) in favour of U. Modulo π the U derivative is precisely 2AB/πᵉ, a unit, because the Z-linear coefficient vanishes, and the U² derivative vanishes in char two. Thus the π-divisor is smooth over the residue field; π is a nonzerodivisor by monicity, so the total-space rings at every vertical point are regular. The two parameter-étale h_j remain étale after this elimination. The ring so given over the parameter-étale base with the chosen constant factor O_K, U, Z is finite of the indicated generic rank, has exactly the field just computed (explicit inverse V=AU+B+π^m Z), and embeds there by free monic adjunction. Char zero is smooth by the branch check or the étale comparison. Hence it is the actual normalized component everywhere on these patches.

In particular for either real/imaginary √(±2) case, and the biquadratic case, **both** λ and μ have such square approximations on both patches. This covers all different valuation classes among the first three entries (a rank-one generator then has odd valuation, or K is biquadratic). Even if all first-three valuations agree, unequal first-three class may give the remaining Gaussian field, treated now.

## The nonsquare Gaussian residue: where the actual singularities are

In O=Z_(2)[i], π=1+i and e=2. Each normalized unit c_i has residue modulo π² equal to (1+π)^{p_i}. For a given patch let l,m₀∈{0,1} be the exponents of η in λ,μ reduced mod 2. They are
    finite:       (l,m₀)=(p₃ xor p₀, p₃);
    reciprocal:   (l,m₀)=(p₃, p₃ xor p₀).
If l=m₀=0, use the preceding smooth integral chart. Otherwise put W=V−U−1 (no division by π). Then on the ring with U²=c₀T (or c₀s) the remaining monic equation is
    W²+2(U+1)W+(1−λ)U²+2U+(1−μ)=0 .
On π=0 we have W=0. The combined coefficient ((1−λ)U²+2U+(1−μ))/π reduces modulo π to the NONZERO polynomial P(U)=l U²+m₀. Where P(U) is a unit, the defining equation has independent linear term π times a unit in the regular ambient O[U,W], giving total-space regularity. Where P(U)=0 in the residue field, the closed point W=0 is genuinely singular: in the regular ambient local ring with maximal ideal (π,U−u,W), u=0 or 1, each term of the equation belongs to its square. The surface dimension is two but its embedding dimension is three. The h_j adjunctions are étale and surjective, so they preserve and reflect these conclusions, including existence of points over each such value on the chart. This monic presentation is still the TRUE normalization although not regular at those isolated closed points: it is a complete intersection, finite free with the correct generic field, regular in codimension one by the computations (P is nonzero), hence normal. The product over factors is the actual normalized étale pullback.

The dyadic parameter is U² on either patch in the residue field. Finite patch concerns U≠0. If p₀=p₃=0 both charts use the smooth case. If (p₀,p₃)=(0,1), finite P=U²+1 gives the extra nonregular point value U=1, i.e. T=1; reciprocal P=U²+1 is nonzero at infinity U=0. If (p₀,p₃)=(1,0), finite P=U² gives no root on this open and reciprocal P=1 gives none at infinity. If (p₀,p₃)=(1,1), finite P=1 gives none, reciprocal P=U² gives precisely the extra boundary value s=0. All nonclosed vertical points are included in the unit or R₁ tests. The reported singular parameter values are independent of the unramified scalar comparison used to pass from arbitrary rational inputs to these representatives.

Finally the Gaussian case occurs exactly when the first three valuations agree but their signs differ: their ratio squareclasses span the line generated by −1. For each prescribed common first-three valuation there are 6 mixed sign triples, and for a prescribed p₃ two fourth signs. This gives 12 representatives in each exceptional row. The remaining 216 have no extra dyadic singular parameter values outside T=0. These are starting-chart statements, not computations of a resolution over the nonregular closed points. The universal algorithm uses their actual reduced subsets, while evaluation of further normalized blowups requires additional work.
