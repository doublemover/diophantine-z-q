# A one-place test and an unmarked lattice-index exit for the actual distinguished lift

Use an original prepared pole with R=b/17=βu², D=1−R=θz² in (0,1), θ>1, v₁₇(R)>0 odd, and K=Q(√β). This includes the indexed scalar a=0 or −1, h=328655617654071015360, β=204618888056724185, with D certified nonsquare at 29. It also applies to any of the original contacts with the stated conditions; in fact the positive odd valuation follows from 17|β and D a square unit at 17, because an odd negative valuation of R would also be that of D. NB: the complementary parameter D stays fixed, not replaced by a model.

Use E:y²=x(x+4)(x−3D/R), E′:Y²=X(X+4R)(X−3D), P=(4D,4D)∈E′(Q), and t:E′_K→E_K given by (X,Y)↦(X/R,Y/(R√R)). The independent actual scalar skew-trace argument gives the exact equivalence between a lift of the distinguished full class δ=(D,1,D) on E and the condition P∈Tr E′(K), since t turns the trace into a difference. Such a lift gives a literal both-nonzero PointPair on the same b. The converse here is an equivalence for this distinguished class, not a necessity for an arbitrary original PointPair.

**Simultaneous 2-saturation by one known local prime.** At k=Q₁₇, put b₀=[β] (odd valuation), c=[3] (nonsquare unit). Then [−1]=1, [D]=1, [3+R]=c, [R]=b₀. Use multiplicative squareclass pairs (x,x+4) for E and (X,X+4R) for E′, with usual tangent substitution at a root. The two root generators 0 and −4 on E give respectively (cb₀,1) and (1,cb₀). On E′, the roots 0 and −4R give (cb₀,b₀) and (b₀,cb₀). In each group these pairs are independent. At the odd p=17 the local index of multiplication by 2 is the size of the rational two-torsion (the open formal group contributes no index), so they exhaust the local Kummer image. The kernel on pairs under k→k(√β) is precisely {1,b₀}². It meets each displayed four-element image trivially. In particular no rational point of either E or E′ acquires a half over K unless it already has one over Q, and restriction Sel₂(F/Q)→Sel₂(F/K) is injective for F=E,E′: the global kernel on pairs is {1,[β]}², all four still distinct at k because v₁₇β is odd.

**All local traces are local doubles.** If a rational 2-torsion point T′∈E′(k) is Tr U for U∈E′(k(√β)), write T=t(T′)∈E[2](k). Then T=tU−σ(tU). Thus the inflation of the E[2]-valued cocycle σ↦T of the quadratic group has trivial image in H¹(k,E), so belongs to the local 2-Kummer image of E. It dies on restriction, hence it is zero by the preceding local intersection. The module E[2] is constant; for nonzero T the cocycle is nonzero. So no nonidentity 2-torsion is a local trace. The four local cosets modulo doubling are represented by rational two-torsion, and every double is a trace, proving Tr E′(k(√β))=2E′(k). Interchanging E and E′ proves the same for E. This does not assert a Hasse norm principle for the elliptic group. Compare Çiperiani–Ozman, *Local to global trace questions and twists of genus one curves*, Propositions 1.7, 1.12 (https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/mirela/TraceTwist.pdf); here the torsion-norm ambiguity is ruled out at a single actual prime without assuming anything about global ranks or Sha.

**A detection criterion requiring no prescribed K-generator.** Write M=E′(K), M⁺=E′(Q), M⁻=t⁻¹(E(Q)), and I=[M:M⁺+M⁻]. These equal the integral plus/minus eigensubgroups, including torsion. Trace induces M/(M⁺+M⁻) ≅ Tr(M)/2M⁺: if Tr(U)=2A with A rational, then U−A∈M⁻. The same statement holds for the difference on M⁻. Thus I is a power of two, and its exponent is not more than either rational rank: locally at 17 all traces are doubles, and E′(Q)/2→E′(k)/2 surjects already on rational two-torsion. Its kernel has dimension rank E′(Q). The identical bound for M⁻ follows by t. More precisely, the trace subgroup modulo 2 is contained in the 17-localization kernel, not assumed to coincide with it.

The given P is locally double at 17 because its E′-Kummer pair is (D,1) with D≡1 mod17. It is not a rational double since D is nonsquare. If one independently certifies rank E′(Q)≤1, that rank is exactly 1 (the explicit point is nontorsion by the earlier marked argument; alternatively no nontrivial 2-torsion is a local double at 17, so the nonzero class of P in the kernel already forces rank). The kernel then has order 2 with unique nonzero class P. Consequently, under just this upper bound, a lift of the distinguished δ exists **if and only if I>1**; the only possible nontrivial index is I=2. Any independently certified extra lattice coset works, and an odd finite-index subgroup containing both rational eigenlattices cannot hide a missing factor of two. Even without the rank bound, I=1 rules out this distinguished realization, whereas I>1 alone need not identify P. No rank upper bound or nontrivial index is certified for the enormous unchanged indexed scalar, and no height search or general rational PointPair exclusion is being substituted.

Source scope: this is a mathematical reduction, not Lean elaboration, and its arithmetic application is dependent on the exact marked-Jacobian and skew-trace papers. For the latter's trace↔class identity one may also argue directly: choose a geometric half H′ of P; the cocycle t(gH′−H′) on constant E[2] represents (D,1,D). A rational E-point with this class and half A gives a K-point A+tH′ with difference tP; conversely from that difference take A=U−tH′, a geometric half of U+σU, and compute the same cocycle on both Galois cosets.
