# Generic partner rank zero; a fixed auxiliary curve for global rank jumps

At a fixed prepared b let B=17b=βu², D=1−b/17=θz², V=B²D, where β,θ are positive squarefree integers and u,z nonzero rationals. Assume θ>1. Preparation means 0<b<17, square reduced denominator, v₂(b)≥3, v₃(b)≥1, v₁₇(b) even ≥2. Thus v₁₇(β)=1 and θ is a 17-adic square. Write E_n:y²=x(x−n)(x+3n). The already source-reviewed full locally marked family is

    e(t)=(1−βt²)(1−9βt²)/(1+3βt²)²,    E=B e(t).

Over Q(t), E_E has rank exactly one and E_EV has rank zero and only its four rational 2-torsion points. This does NOT persist under all rational specializations. At the particular exact prepared b=(204/1073)² with β=17, θ=409, there are infinitely many distinct twist classes on which BOTH globally marked points exist. More generally a *single fixed auxiliary elliptic curve* gives a sufficient test for an arbitrary exact b, with a complete local classification of this test. It is NOT necessary for the unrestricted pair. No selected 15-prime pattern or global rational point on the actual indexed scalar partner follows.

## The function field and the exact rational matching problem

Let C have function field Q(t)(d) with d²=e(t). Its smooth projective model is geometrically integral of genus one (four distinct finite simple branch points) and O_C=(0,1) is rational. Under the quadratic twist isomorphism, sections on E_(n e(t)) inject into rational maps C→E_n satisfying f(t,−d)=−f(t,d); these maps extend on the smooth projective models. A constant such map lands in E_n(Q)[2], since the field of constants is Q. Nonconstant maps, modulo constant translation, are nonzero homomorphisms Jac(C)→E_n; this is the usual universal property of the Jacobian with O_C as base. Since B=βu² and the partner twist parameter has squareclass βθe(t), it suffices to consider n=β and βθ.

The normalized known global first section has constant abscissa β before untwisting; after untwisting its abscissa is β/e(t), nonconstant. (Scale the actual first point on E_E by u.) Thus Jac(C) is Q-isogenous to E_β, and the first rank is at least one. The j-invariant of E_β is 35152/9, nonintegral, hence non-CM. Its endomorphism algebra over Qbar is Q. Consequently the first Hom group has rank one, bounding the rank of anti-invariant sections above. E_(βθ) is the nontrivial constant quadratic twist of this non-CM curve; any Q-isogeny would give a nonzero anti-invariant endomorphism over Q(√θ), impossible. There is no nonconstant second map. All four second 2-torsion points are already defined over the base, so this also gives the exact generic torsion. This is about this explicit parameter t, not about all parameter functions.

For fixed rational t remove singular cases e(t)=0 or ∞. For the desired partner full marking δ=(β,1,β), off branch points its cubic coordinates must satisfy

    x=βU²,   x−n'=V₀²,   x+3n'=βW²,   n'=EV.

Putting a=V₀/(βU), c=W/U yields c²+3βa²=4 and n'/(βU²)=1−βa². At c≠−2 put v=a/(2+c). Then a=4v/(1+3βv²), c=2(1−3βv²)/(1+3βv²), and 1−βa²=e(v). Thus the marked nonzero lift is equivalent to [θ e(t)]=[e(v)] with finite rational v≠0; exclude c=0, e(t)e(v)=0. Conversely this class equality chooses nonzero rational U with n'=βU²e(v); the displayed formulas give the three actual factors and hence the ordinate. Branch representatives in E_n'[2] are not an alternative assertion of nonzero lift: at the previous fresh-prime markers the marked class is already known not to come from torsion. No divisibility restriction is imposed on the search variable v. Original x=BS² with S=U/u.

## A fixed auxiliary curve whose marked lift solves the problem

Use v=−1/(βt), t≠0. Direct cancellation gives

    e(v)/e(t)=[(1+3βt²)/(3+βt²)]² (9−βt²)/(1−9βt²).

Hence this correspondence solves the matching condition exactly when

    H:  Z²=θ(9−βt²)(1−9βt²).

As β nonsquare and positive, no rational finite branch, and for θ>1 neither 0 nor ∞ yields a rational H-point. Consequently no rational H-point is a degenerate candidate. H maps nonconstantly to the fixed curve

    A_N : Y²=X(X−N)(X−81N),   N=βθ,
    X=9β²θt²,  Y=9β²θtZ.

Conversely any rational A_N point with Kummer x-class [θ] (the descent for the isogeny with kernel generated by (0,0), interpreted on its dual quotient) has ordinary nonzero X,Y: O and (0,0) have class 1, the other roots have class [N]≠[θ]. Extract t from X/(9β²θ) and recover Z. In particular this is one explicit isogeny-descent quartic on a fixed auxiliary curve, not a numerical search over free E or a sufficiency claim for a random class of curves. In the usual convention the x map on A_N is the connecting map for the isogeny A_N'→A_N dual to the rational kernel isogeny A_N→A_N'. The H-class is in that dual isogeny's Selmer group exactly when locally soluble everywhere. Selmer membership is not a rational lift.

In fact any rational point of H gives infinitely many twists with global pairs at that exact b. The curve A_N is the twist of A_1 by N with v₁₇(N)=1. The integral model of A_1 has good reduction at 17 since its roots 0,1,81 are distinct there. Over the ramified quadratic extension Q₁₇(√N), rational torsion on A_N maps to torsion on A_1 with inertia acting as negation. The residue field is unchanged, hence reduction equals its own negative. Reduction is injective on torsion here: for the elliptic formal group over the maximal ideal the formal logarithm is injective since normalized valuations v₁₇≥1/2>1/(17−1) (equivalently the terms of degree i≥2 in the integral differential's formal integral have strictly greater valuation than the first). Thus A_N(Q)_tors=A_N[2], and the lifted point P is infinite order. Each odd multiple of P has x-class [θ] by the Kummer homomorphism, so gives an H-point with infinitely many distinct t.

For each fixed c_* in Q×/Q×² the simultaneous conditions e(t)=c_* square and H(t) lead to a biquadratic extension over Qbar(t), ramified over exactly six points: ±1/√β, ±1/(3√β), ±3/√β. The two branch sets each have four members and share only the middle pair; in particular the two squareclasses are independent even over Qbar. At each branch the geometric inertia has order 2, including at the common pair (the ratio of local units is a square over the algebraically closed residue field). Riemann–Hurwitz for degree four gives 2g−2=−8+6·2=4, so the smooth projective fiber has genus 3. By Faltings its rational points are finite. Therefore the infinitely many t produce infinitely many distinct first and second twist classes, each with both marked non-torsion rational points at the chosen b. Nonzero ordinates at abscissa of class β cannot be rational torsion in E_E,E_EV by the previous 17-depth torsion test. Distinct twist classes of E_1 are nonisogenous over Q by the same non-CM argument.

## Exact all-place test on this auxiliary cover

Under the preparation above H is everywhere locally soluble iff β OR θ is square in Q₅. To verify: at 2,3,17 and all other primes dividing β, D hence θ is a local square by the prepared valuations (odd v_p(B) is positive elsewhere by the square denominator); t=0 gives a point. At any other p|θ one has v_p(D)>0 by the same denominator constraint, so B=289(1−D) and hence β is a local square. Here a rational branch t=3/√β is a smooth local point. The real place is immediate. At p outside 2·3·5·β·θ the four branches remain distinct (their squared ratio is 81); the smooth projective genus-one curve over Fp has a point by Hasse and lifts. At 5 a local square θ gives t=0 and a local square β gives a branch. If neither square, both are nonsquare units in squareclasses by the already covered ramified-prime cases. Since β,θ are squarefree integers, they are units. Writing r=βt², (9−r)(1−9r) is a nonzero square unit residue for integral t: modulo 5 it is (2(1−9r))² and a nonsquare β cannot give r=1/9. If v₅(t)<0 divide the product by r²; the residual unit is 9, a square. Thus the extra θ forbids a local point in either chart, also at infinity. At soluble places local smooth neighborhoods give affine, nonzero, nonbranch representatives. This establishes the stated iff, not the triviality of its Sha class.

The auxiliary isogeny Selmer class on this indexed curve is specified, but its global image remains open. For arbitrary other prepared poles a 5-adic failure of H is not a failure of PointPair.
