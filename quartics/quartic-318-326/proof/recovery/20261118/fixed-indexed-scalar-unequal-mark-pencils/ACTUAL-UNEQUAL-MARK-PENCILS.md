# Infinitely many locally soluble unequal-mark pencils at the unchanged pole

Fix an original prepared pole: B=17b=βv², D=1−b/17=θz², V=B²D, with positive squarefree β,θ, 17|β, v,z nonzero rational. The local hypotheses used are: θ square locally at 2,3 and at all primes of β, and β square locally at every prime of θ. These are exactly the consequences used in the fixed matched-cover paper (not merely positivity): in particular at p|θ outside 2,3,β odd valuation of D cannot be negative by the square denominator; then B=289(1−D) is a local square. We use β prime to 6 as in the true scalar and contact preparations. The statement is for any one such fixed b, including the actual indexed scalar, not another pole nearby.

Put x∈6Z, h=1−β(x²+108), k=12/h, c=1+3βk². These k need not be chosen after θ is factored. They are nonzero; 0<βk²<1 since β≥17. Consider the smooth complete binary quartic

    H: Z²=θ c (s²−β)((s−3βk)²−β(1+ks)²).

Every H so obtained has a point over every completion. Infinitely many have pairwise distinct nontrivial c squareclasses; for infinitely many of those, any global H point would give a literal original PointPair with BOTH ordinates nonzero and with unequal second Kummer classes whose ratio is precisely c. On these curves a specific everywhere locally soluble non-torsion Kummer target is identified below; “non-torsion target” means that no rational torsion point represents it, not that it is already rational.

**Source conversion and completeness of the pencil before restriction on k.** For an ordinary gain e, β(1−e)(1+3e)=Y² is parametrized by e(s)=(s²−β)/(s²+3β), Y=4βs/(s²+3β), with s=∞ giving e=1. Its second Kummer class away from the zero ordinate is [β(1−e)]=[s²+3β]. The binary expression extends the ratio test to all s, including poles; at both boundary arguments 0 and ∞ the ordinate vanishes. Write u=(s−3βk)/(1+ks). Then u²+3β=c(s²+3β)/(1+ks)². The relation [e(s)e(u)]=θ is exactly H. Completeness here refers only to allowing all PROJECTIVE k before selecting the locally soluble subfamily: k=(s−u)/(su+3β), with the usual homogeneous interpretation; no rational s is a root of s²+3β. Thus it includes the ordinary unmarked two-gain problem, not just the stronger equal-mark matching family. Given rational s,u choose S with S²=Ve(s)/e(u), E=Be(s), y1=βv³Y(s), y2=β(vS)³Y(u). These are exactly the two original cubics. There is never a rational gain zero or infinite. For finite nonboundary arguments the two second Kummer classes differ by c modulo squares, with no assumption about the individual two classes.

**Local checks without omitted bad places.** The branch roots are ±q with q²=β and the two inverse images under the Möbius map. Its determinant is c>0. A repeated branch point would mean this map sends q or −q to itself (k=0) or its negative (βk²=1). Thus H is smooth. The real place and every place with β square have a rational smooth branch. At 2,3 and p|β, evaluate the leading quartic coefficient at s=∞: it is θ c (1−βk²), a nonzero local square. Indeed h is a unit at each such place, k≡0 modulo 4 and 3, and both factors outside θ are 1 mod p for odd p|β. All other primes with β nonsquare have p≥5, p∤βθ and θ a unit. They fall into exactly these cases:
* If v_p(k)<0, trivialize by dividing (s−3βk) and (1+ks) by k, and c by k². The reduced factors, up to nonzero constants, are s²−β and s²−9β. They are separable, disjoint (p∤6β), and the coefficient is a unit. This smooth genus-one reduction has a point over Fp by Hasse, hence a local lift.
* Otherwise k is a unit, because its numerator divides 12. If c is a unit, the Möbius map is invertible after reduction and the preceding distinctness test still applies (β nonsquare forbids βk²=1). Good reduction again.
* If v_p(c)>0, it is EVEN: c=A²−βL² for A=(1+β(x²+108))/h and L=2x/h, as direct expansion shows. Nonsquare unit β gives an unramified quadratic norm (equivalently the two squares cannot cancel at minimal tied valuation). Divide c by the appropriate even p power. Away from 1+ks=0 the reduced map is constant a=1/k with a²=−3β, and hence a²−β=−4β≠0. The normalized right side is a nonzero constant times (s²−β)(1+ks)². Both quadratic characters of s²−β occur at least (p−1)/2≥2 times among finite s: the sum is −1, by counting (s−w)(s+w)=β. Exclude the at most one forbidden s. Hensel at a nonzero square value gives a local point.
Every local point is smooth in characteristic zero; a local open can avoid all boundaries.

**Forcing genuine variation and a non-torsion alternative.** Outside any finite set containing 2,3,β,θ and divisors of 1+108β, take a prime p≡1 modulo 12β: both β and −3 are squares modulo p by quadratic reciprocity. There are infinitely many by Dirichlet. The numerator M=h²+432β of c before dividing by h² has a simple zero in x modulo p: with j²=−3β, the condition h=±12j is equivalent to βx²=(1∓6j)². These roots are nonzero by the exclusion and the derivative −4βxh is nonzero. CRT gives x∈6Z realizing v_p(M)=1 (avoid the unique zero lift mod p²). Use a fresh p outside all previously obtained c supports to separate the resulting squareclasses. The construction family itself uses only β; θ has finite support, so unboundedly many fresh primes can avoid it even without a factorization. For all such choices v_p(θc) is odd. At s=∞ the extra factor 1−βk² is 4/3 mod p. At s=0 the two factors are −β and β(9βk²−1)≡−4β. None vanishes. Neither s=0 nor s=∞ is a local point. Reversing the correspondence sends (s,u,k) to (u,s,−k) with the same c and same test; equivalently the gain-product squareclass test is symmetric, and all fixed boundary values of u have the same obstruction. Hence every possible global literal pair from H is genuinely both-nonzero, and even its relative mark c differs from 1. It is not enough to assume that some unrelated rank of an elliptic curve is positive.

More precisely the split Jacobian of H has roots 0, r1=−βθc(1−βk²), r2=4β²θck² and r2−r1=βθc². To check, in the quartic g times the four linear factors with leading g=θc(1−βk²), choose branch α1=q as origin and let α2=−q, α3=q(1+3qk)/(1−qk), α4=q(−1+3qk)/(1+qk). In the usual inversion at α1, Y=W times a constant and X is affine-linear in 1/(s−α1); translating α2 to X=0 gives the two roots g(α1−α4)(α2−α3)=4r1 and g(α1−α3)(α2−α4)=4r2. Scale (X,Y) by (1/4,1/8). Over Q(√β) this identifies H with the displayed curve by a map whose pullback of dX/Y is a nonzero rational constant times ds/Z independent of α1. Conjugation thus differs by translation, not negation; it interchanges α1 and α2 and yields translation by T0=(0,0). For the natural covering [2P−D∞] the full class, in standard x,x−r1,x−r2 slot order, is (1,β,β) by the Weil pairing with T0 and T1. This class is locally a Kummer image everywhere by the local proof. A global Kummer lift is equivalent to H(Q) nonempty. It cannot come from torsion for the fresh-prime slopes: at 17, v(r1),v(r2−r1) are odd, so none of the three roots is rationally halved and no rational 4-torsion exists. The first Kummer slots of T0 and T1 are negative (r1r2 and r1), that of T2 is [θc] nonsquare by the fresh prime; and O is not the indicated nontrivial class. Odd torsion is divisible by 2. Thus each fixed slope has an actual rational non-torsion lift on its Jacobian or a specific nonzero Sha[2] class. No choice in this infinite family is asserted to realize the former.

**Explicit pre-existing indexed scalar.** At a=0 or −1, with the true h-index 328655617654071015360 and fixed label of forward-indexed-matching-root-signs-r1, β=204618888056724185, its certified exact source-orbit value is D≡28 mod31 (a unit). Choose x=54. Then k=−12/618767517483533935439 and v31(c)=1. This furnishes one concrete indexed unequal-mark target, not a stand-alone model. There are also infinitely many different marks keeping this very same b and the 31-boundary exclusion: impose x≡54 mod 31² in addition to the fresh-prime conditions. The latter fresh primes need no avoidance of θ for mark distinctness, since 31 alone certifies every boundary exclusion and θc nonsquare. The construction does not use the enormous factorization of θ or treat a root sign as a rank. A point at this indexed b remains unproved.
