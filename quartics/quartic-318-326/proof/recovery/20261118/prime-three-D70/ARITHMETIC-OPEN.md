# The selected untwisted two-step modification on the explicit open D(70)

**Status.** Mathematical argument and three uncompiled Lean components, not a kernel-verified theorem or a change to the manuscript. The local all-stalk theorem and the atlas comparison inputs themselves have source reviews, not Lean acceptance. This argument does not assume a historic, unrecovered properness certificate.

**Claim.** Put A = OriginalIntegerFiniteClosure318.Closure for the constant-one twist, X = Spec A, p = ker(normalizedPoint), X₇₀ = Spec A[1/70]. There is a specific proper morphism b : B₂ → X, an isomorphism away from p. Its restriction to X₇₀ is a proper birational morphism with regular total space. Its base change along Spec Aₚ → X, as a scheme **over this local base**, is the previously constructed gluedSecondModification, with structure morphism toFirstBlowup followed by toPointSpec. This is a claim on the finite normalized affine chart. It does not cover infinity, other twists, or the excluded fibres at 2, 5, 7. It asserts regular local rings of the total space, not smoothness or a reduced normal-crossings special fibre.

## 1. Identify and extend both actual centers

A is finite over Z[T], Noetherian, integral and normal in the specified fraction field. The map from the raw order O into A is finite and identifies A with its normalization in that same fraction field. The selected p is maximal; its displayed residue map surjects onto K = F₃[α]/(α²−2), of order nine. Set B₁ = Bl_p X. Flat base change along A → Aₚ identifies (B₁)_{Aₚ} with Bl_{pAₚ} Spec Aₚ: localization takes each power pⁿ to (pAₚ)ⁿ with its multiplication. Its affine-base Rees Proj with its degree-zero structure map is exactly actualReesProj → Spec PointRing. This is not an identification of Spec Aₚ with an open subset.

In its first U chart the second residual point q is the displayed surjection localResidualPoint : LocalU → K; its defining ideal is localCenter (the *whole* kernel), not a tangent ideal. It lies over p: a map g:Aₚ→K restricts on A to a homomorphism with kernel contained in p. Its image in the finite field K is a finite domain, hence a field, so this kernel is maximal and equals p. FiniteResidualContraction318 proves the implication by a finite-field power argument and specializes it to the literal residual chart map. It proves kernel equality, without supposing two maps to K have identical residue-field automorphisms.

The scheme-theoretic fibre (B₁)_{κ(p)} of the global map and the closed fibre of (B₁)_{Aₚ} are canonically identical, since A/p = Aₚ/pAₚ. So q defines a point Q on B₁ via this common fibre, with the same residue field K. It is closed: its residue field is finite over κ(p) on the finite-type κ(p)-scheme (B₁)_{κ(p)}, and that fibre is closed in B₁ because p is closed in X. Take its **reduced** closed subscheme Spec κ(Q). It pulls back to the reduced point q, not an unspecified thickening: in an affine neighbourhood, all A\p act by units on κ(Q), so its coordinate ring localizes to itself. Set B₂ = Bl_Q B₁. Both centers are of finite type on Noetherian schemes; their blowups are projective, hence proper, so their composite b is proper. Both blowups are canonical isomorphisms over the complement of p in X (there the respective ideals are the unit ideal); this open is dense since A is a domain and p is a non-generic closed point. The blowups are integral by the affine Rees chart description inside the fraction field. In particular b is birational. No assertion that the singular total space of X is resolved everywhere.

## 2. Compare the exact local modification, including all transitions

Flat base change as above identifies (B₂)_{Aₚ} with Bl_q((B₁)_{Aₚ}). On the genuine three-member base cover (PrimeThreeActualTestCover318.baseCover), compare this blowup and gluedSecondModification as schemes over the *same first blowup*:

* U: q has ideal ker(localResidualPoint)=localCenter, and the blowup there is the native Rees Proj of that exact ideal with the structural map toFirstU. PrimeThreeGluedFirstUPreimage318.firstUPullbackIso, with its hom_toFirstU equation, identifies the FULL pullback of the glued morphism over U with precisely this relative native scheme.
* V and W: q avoids both; its ideal restricts to the unit ideal (PrimeThreeGlobalResidualIdealOtherCharts318.globalResidualIdeal_on_other_native_chart). The blowup is the identity. Each full pullback of the glued morphism is also identified over its base with the identity by PrimeThreeGluedOtherPreimages318.otherPullbackIso and its projection equation.
* Every distinct pair of cover members contains V or W. On their intersection both structure maps are isomorphisms, so there is exactly one comparison over that intersection, the inverse of the target structure map after the source structure map. Thus the restrictions of the U/V/W comparisons actually agree. Repetitions are tautological, and the same uniqueness gives the triple compatibility, independent of a choice of coordinates. Glue the isomorphisms and their inverses over the three-piece target cover. This identifies the arrows to the first blowup, hence also their composites to Spec Aₚ.

All stalks on this localized total space are regular by PrimeThreeGluedLocalRegularity318.all_stalks_regular, with no assumption that the original normalization point was regular. If y in B₂ maps to p, choose an affine neighbourhood Spec C of y and its prime P. The pullback neighbourhood is Spec (A\p)⁻¹C. The point lifting y has local ring ((A\p)⁻¹C)_{(A\p)⁻¹P} = C_P, since A\p misses P. This identifies the ORIGINAL total-space local ring at y, not the quotient ring of the fibre. Thus every stalk over p in B₂ is regular. A general fallback would use openness of the regular locus (finite type over Z, hence J-2) and properness of b to remove the closed image of its complement. ProperRegularPrincipalOpen318 gives the last topological principal-open step. The next paragraph obtains a **specific** open without J-2.

## 3. Account for every remaining point of X₇₀

The raw untwisted order O is the tensor product of four monic quadratic factors over Z[T], equivalently the simultaneous presentation rᵢ²=T−cᵢ with c=(0,32,76832,−343); this is not an arbitrary field extension with the same branch set. In a residue field k of O[1/70], if two different rᵢ vanish then T=cᵢ=cⱼ. For every ordered pair i≠j, cᵢ−cⱼ divides both 3²·70¹⁰ and (cᵢ−2)²·70¹⁰. Thus 3=0 and T=2 in k: 70 is nonzero, and a field has no nonzero square-zero elements. All 24 integer divisibilities are checked by the included script; PrimeThreeContactOpen70_318 gives source for the general-field implication. The six positive differences are 32, 76832, 343, 76800, 375 and 77175.

At any prime with at most one vanishing root choose j to be that root, or any index if none vanish. Eliminate T using T=rⱼ²+cⱼ. Over the regular base Z[1/70][rⱼ] the three remaining variables satisfy monic equations rᵢ²=rⱼ²+cⱼ−cᵢ. Localize successively at each of these three roots. At each adjoining step the derivative 2rᵢ is a unit in the localized algebra (2 already invertible); these are standard étale algebras. The final open containing the given prime is étale over the regular base, hence regular. In particular its local rings are normal. Since A is the integral closure of O in the identical fraction field, localization of the normalization over this open equals the open itself. This applies to nonclosed primes too.

It remains to count normalized primes above 3,T−2. In the raw fibre at that point r₁²=r₂²=r₃²=0 and r₀²=2. Hence every prime contains r₁,r₂,r₃; killing them gives the **field** F₃[r₀]/(r₀²−2), so there is exactly one underlying prime, with the stated residue field. The possibly nonreduced fibre has NOT been identified with that field. Passing to A does not split this prime: OriginalPrimeThreeNormalizedLocalIdentification318.actual_normalized_localization identifies A with the RAW order after localizing both at all integer denominators prime to 3, compatibly with the original inclusion (closureToLocal_on_order and closureDenominators_eq_integer). Localizing that common ring retains all and only primes whose contraction to Z is contained in (3). The previously selected p specializes T and roots to precisely these values, so it is the unique normalized prime above this collision. Therefore all x in X₇₀ other than p are regular. For y above such an x, b is an isomorphism there by §1; for y above p use §2. These cases exhaust all primes of B₂ over D(70). Properness survives restriction, as does the identity away from p.
