# A finite derivative certificate on exactly the squarefree tiling twists used by the rank consumer

Write E_d: y²=x(x−d)(x+3d). The original simultaneous support, if instantiated at a source pole, gives two positive squarefree d≡11 mod 12. They are the restricted and full products from the *same* prime assignment. Here is an unconditional, pointwise analytic test for that residue class. We also give a checked, non-indexed, genuinely marked pair at the previously studied prepared rational number b=(204/1073)². No conjectural converse and no prime-twist average are used.

**The fixed modular form.** Let f_1=η(2z)η(4z)η(6z)η(12z), the normalized weight-two form of E_1 of conductor 24. It has no even Fourier terms. The quadratic twist E_{−1}: y²=x(x+1)(x−3) has minimal equation [0,1,0,−4,−4] by x=X+1, conductor 48 (curve 48.a4). Its form is f_1⊗χ_{−4}, with Fricke sign −1, hence functional-equation sign +1 at weight 2. These identifications can be checked in the [LMFDB 24.2.a.a newform, including its eta product and twist table](https://www.lmfdb.org/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/24/2/a/a/), the [48.a isogeny class](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/48/a/), and the [48.2.a.a newform](https://www.lmfdb.org/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/48/2/a/a/).

Since d≡11 mod12, −d is a negative fundamental discriminant coprime to 48. E_d is the twist of E_{−1} by −d. The [coprime primitive quadratic twist formula, §1](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/radziwill/QuadraticTwists.pdf) gives conductor 48d² and sign χ_{−d}(−48)=−1: the 2-part has exponent four, −d≡1 mod3, and the character is odd. At odd m the product χ_{−4}(m)χ_{−d}(m)=(d/m), including zero at a common factor. Both the original and twisted coefficients are zero at even m. Hence the coefficients of E_d are A_m=a_m(f_1)(d/m) for m odd and zero for m even. This takes care of bad factors at 2,3 and primes dividing d; it does not apply the coprime conductor formula to (E_1,direct character) at 2.

**Pointwise certified decision in the nonzero direction.** Put c=2π/√(48d²)=π/(2√3d). With J(x)=∫_x^∞ e^(−t)dt/t, one has exactly

    L′(E_d,1) = 2 Σ_(m≥1) (A_m/m) J(cm).

Indeed the completed function is c^(−s)Γ(s)L(s), the Mellin transform of g(t)=Σ A_m exp(−cmt). Splitting at 1, the sign −1 gives ∫_1^∞ g(t)(t^(s−1)−t^(1−s))dt. Differentiate at 1; since L(1)=0, the prefactor at 1 is c^(−1). Integration by parts gives ∫_1^∞ log(t)e^(−cmt)dt=J(cm)/(cm). Exponential decay allows termwise integration.

Deligne's bound |A_m|≤τ(m)√m (also directly from Hasse and the bad Euler factors) and τ(m)≤2√m imply |A_m/m|≤2. Hence for any integer T≥1, with t=c(T+1), the absolute discarded tail is at most

    B_T = 4 e^(−t) / ( t (1−e^(−c)) ).

This follows from J(x)≤e^(−x)/x and a geometric sum. The finite exact Fourier coefficients use Euler's pentagonal theorem on q⋅∏_(δ∈{1,2,3,6})∏_(j≥1)(1−(q²)^(δj)). The checker forms the four polynomials modulo X^ceil(T/2), X=q², over ℤ. For the remaining real computation the Arb/FLINT ball operations enclose π, J, every partial sum, and B_T. If the partial-sum ball plus [−B_T,+B_T] avoids 0, the analytic rank is exactly one by the sign. Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin then gives algebraic rank one and **finiteness of the whole Sha**, not just a conditional converse or an odd-parity statement. Increasing T and the working precision eventually detects any nonzero derivative; containing zero is inconclusive, not proof it vanishes. Work grows appreciably with d and a tiny derivative may need a larger T. This is not a sublinear-in-d algorithm nor a symbolic nonvanishing theorem.
