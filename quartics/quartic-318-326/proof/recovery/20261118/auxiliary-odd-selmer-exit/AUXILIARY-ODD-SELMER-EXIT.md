# A finite odd-Selmer test that distinguishes the order-eight branches

This concerns the arbitrary nonzero rational twist n of E_n:y²=x(x−n)(x+3n), **under** the three project bounds: finite E_n(Q)/2 of order at most 8, and the all-places 2-Selmer squareclass group of order 8. It is a new sufficient test, not an odd-Selmer calculation on the four indexed curves. Write d for the signed squarefree representative of n. The rational isomorphism coming from n=d·u² preserves every assertion here. The prior reduced-duality result gives two alternatives: rank 1 and Sha[2^∞]=0, or rank 0 and Sha[2^∞]≅Q₂/Z₂.

**Claim.** Let p=5 with 5∤d, or p=11 with 11∤d. If one certifies dim_Fp Sel_p(E_n/Q)≤2 at this *same* n, then the rational and analytic ranks both equal 1 and full Sha is finite. In particular the 2-primary Sha is zero under the original bounds. Conversely the rank-zero Prüfer alternative would force **dim Sel_p≥3** for each eligible p, and at every prime q its q-primary Sha would have positive odd corank. This does not claim the upper bound is true, deduce it from 2-descent, or treat the finiteness of Sel_p as enough.

**1. Common parity.** The standard exact sequence 0→E(Q)⊗Q_q/Z_q→Sel_(q^∞)→Sha[q^∞]→0 identifies the q-Selmer corank with rational rank plus the corank of primary Sha. At q=2 the sum equals 1 in either alternative. [Dokchitser–Dokchitser, Annals of Mathematics 172 (2010), Theorem 1.4](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2010/172-1/p11) proves for every elliptic curve over Q and every q that the q-Selmer corank and analytic rank have the same parity. Thus the root number is −1, and *all* prime-power Selmer coranks are odd. Under rank 0 every primary Sha then has odd corank, not just the 2-primary; by the structure theorem for cofinitely generated primary groups it contains a divisible Q_q/Z_q. No general rational-rank parity theorem without a Sha condition is being invoked.

**2. The exact family meets the odd-converse hypotheses.** The polynomial model has discriminant 2304d⁶, a p-adic unit if p in {5,11} is prime to d. For E_1, direct finite-field counts at 5,7,11 give respectively 8,8,8 points (including infinity), so the traces are −2,0,4. The trace at eligible p twists by the quadratic unit character of d, hence E_d is ordinary at p=5,11 (the sign need not be +). The trace at 7 yields X²+7 on E_1[5]; its discriminant −28=2 mod5 is a nonsquare. The trace at 5 yields X²+2X+5 on E_1[11]; its discriminant −16=6 mod11 is a nonsquare. In each case one Frobenius on the *untwisted* representation has no invariant line over F_p, so E_1[p] is irreducible; tensoring by the quadratic scalar character giving E_d[p] preserves all invariant subspaces. This reasoning does not require 7∤d or 5∤d for the respective irreducibility tests. If absolute irreducibility is desired, complex conjugation's two distinct eigenvalues ±1 already lie in F_p, so a globally invariant line over the algebraic closure would be one of its F_p-eigenlines. The family is non-CM (j=35152/9 is rational nonintegral), though the stated odd-converse implication only needs the ordinary irreducible setting.

**3. The finite test.** For any q the finite and infinite Kummer sequences bound corank Sel_(q^∞) ≤ dim Sel_q: indeed the left is rank E(Q)+corank Sha[q^∞], and dim Sel_q=rank E(Q)+dim E(Q)[q]+dim Sha[q], with the last dimension bounding corank from above. At an eligible p the bound ≤2 therefore gives exactly corank 1 by step 1. Invoke the published good-ordinary p-converse in [Burungale–Castella–Skinner, IMRN 2025, following Theorem 1.2.2(a), immediately after Remark 1.2.3](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~castella/BCS-print.pdf): for p>3 good ordinary and E[p] irreducible over Q, corank one implies analytic rank one. We use the stated rational-curve consequence, not an assertion deduced by ignoring the auxiliary quadratic field in their anticyclotomic main theorem; the stronger integral equalities and large-image (im) condition are not needed. Gross–Zagier and Kolyvagin (with modularity over Q) then give rank one and finiteness of Sha. Thus this criterion uses a theorem at a different, good, odd prime, not an unverified pointwise 2-converse.

Equivalently, on the unwanted rank-zero branch the analytic rank is odd but cannot equal 1 (the forward Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin theorem suffices), so it is at least 3. At every one of the eligible odd primes its Selmer corank is then also at least 3: parity gives an odd number and Burungale–Castella–Skinner excludes 1. A rigorous nonzero derivative at 1 is another sufficient independent exit. Merely knowing the sign, the order-eight 2-Selmer data, the existence of a p-Selmer class, or rank information for a different curve is not such an exit. No eligible indexed numerical twist or concrete odd-Selmer upper bound is supplied here.
