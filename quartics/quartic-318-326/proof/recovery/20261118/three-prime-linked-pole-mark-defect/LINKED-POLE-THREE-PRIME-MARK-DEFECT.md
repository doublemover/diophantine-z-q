# Three fresh split primes suffice for the common point-mark defect on each unchanged pole

**Theorem.** Let B=17b=βv², D=1−b/17=θz²>0, V=B²D, with the positive squarefree β,θ and nonzero rational v,z of an actual prepared pole, including any of the four at the common source h. Use only 17|β, 17∤θ, β a local square at 2 and each prime of θ, and θ a local square at 3 and each prime of β. For any prescribed finite set T of primes containing the divisors of 6βθ there exist infinitely many distinct twist classes n such that E_n and E_{nV}, where E_g:y²=x(x−g)(x+3g), have nontorsion rational points whose abscissas have the **same** global class βΔ. Here Δ>0 is squarefree with at most THREE prime factors, none in T; Δ is a square at every p in T, and each prime dividing Δ splits completely in K=Q(√(−3θ),√(3β)). The two free twists actually have different classes for θ>1. The literal marked equations follow over Q(√Δ) by a common square rescaling of the abscissas. This does not prove the required Δ=1 over Q.

The old five-prime bound sieved two quadratic factors with a negative-anchor pencil. The change is to spend the original source conic identity to make one of the two factors a prescribed square globally, and keep both ordinates nonzero in the resulting positive-anchor pencil. If θ=1, use the equal-gain construction instead: at x=β let e=(s²−β)/(s²+3β), n=βe. Then (1−e)(1+3e)=16βs²/(s²+3β)²; scaling the second copy by √V gives the partner. Choose s giving e a 17-adic unit and odd valuation at any fresh prime splitting in Q(√β) by weak approximation near a simple root of s²−β at that prime. This gives infinitely many classes with Δ=1. In the rest take θ>1.

**The fixed conic.** C: θt²+βs²=1 has the original rational point (z,v/17). Parametrize its projective closure by P¹ over Q. Write its three homogeneous coordinates as integral binary quadratics (U,J,W) without common geometric zero, taking t=U/W and s=J/W. Since β,θ>0 the projective closure has no real point at W=0. Outside finitely many parameters, q=θt² is in (0,1) and 1−q=βs². Put
     
     a=(1−q)(1+3q),   e=−q/(1+2q),   n=a e,   S=Bz/t.

The four factors are
     
     1−e=(1+3q)/(1+2q),     1+3e=(1−q)/(1+2q),
     1−qe=(1+q)²/(1+2q),    1+3qe=a/(1+2q).

Thus at x=a the curves E_n and E_{nq} have rational ordinates a²/(1+2q) and a²(1+q)/(1+2q), respectively. Scaling the second abscissa and ordinate by S²,S³ gives a point on the literal E_{nV}. Their common error class [a/β]=[1+3q] is represented by
     
     H(X,Y)=W(X,Y)²+3θ U(X,Y)².

If Δ=1 the further common square scaling B/a (and its 3/2 power on ordinates, B/a on the free parameter) gives the actual desired PointPair; over Q(√Δ) this works unconditionally. The E_{nV}-point may be equivalently written at B S² after this second scaling.

**A genuine quartic, not a product or conjectural primes.** The four geometric zeros are the conic points t=±√(−1/(3θ)), s=±√(4/(3β)). No zero is at W=0 since U=W=0 has no conic point. The four are simple: t is unbranched at these points since s≠0. Since 3β is positive nonsquare by the odd 17 valuation, and −3θ is negative, the two quadratic fields are independent. K acts through its Klein-four group simply transitively on the four zeros. So the positive binary quartic H is irreducible over Q, with splitting field K and no repeated zero. Enlarge T by any primes dividing its content or discriminant, all primes of bad reduction for the parametrization, and all primes ≤5. A conclusion avoiding this larger set implies the original one.

**Local neighborhoods on this very conic.** We need H square, not just even valuation. At p|β and 3 take (t,s) near (1/√θ,0), where 1+3q=4. At 17 take s nonzero sufficiently small so that q is a unit near 1, 1+2q a unit and v17(n)=v17(βs²) is odd. At p|θ and at 2 take (t,s) near (0,1/√β) where 1+3q=1. At any remaining p≥5 prime to 6βθ, consider the double cover of the smooth conic by the square root of 1+3θt². In characteristic p its four geometric branch points are distinct by the displayed coordinates (4≠0), and its poles have even multiplicity; this gives a smooth projective geometrically connected genus-one reduction. Explicitly it is the intersection θU²+βJ²=W² and Y²=W²+3θU² in P³, smooth by the distinct four singular pencil parameters (equivalently by the Jacobian criterion, including W=0). The Hasse bound gives a smooth Fp-point, Hensel a Qp-point, and the local smooth curve has infinitely many points to avoid the finitely many points with s,t,W,Y zero. Thus a nonempty open set of conic parameters gives nonzero square H at each prescribed place. Weak approximation on P¹ gives one primitive parameter vector A attaining these neighborhoods simultaneously, with q in (0,1); here H(A) differs from 1+3q by W(A)².

**Avoidance in Greaves' sieve without a presumed congruence strengthening.** Complete A to a unimodular integral basis (A,C'). Let m be the positive T-part of H(A): it is a square. Let L be supported on T, v_p(L) arbitrarily large, and define
     
     G(X,Y)=H(A X+L C' Y)/m.

This is an integral positive irreducible quartic: take v_p(L)≥v_p(m)+4. Its leading coefficient H(A)/m is a unit at each p in T; all other coefficients are divisible by p⁴ there. It has no fixed prime divisor: outside T, H is a nonzero quartic form modulo p>5, so not all projective lines are zeros; the substitution is invertible there. At T use (1,0). In particular G is primitive. Apply George Greaves, [Large prime factors of binary forms](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(71)90049-7), J. Number Theory 3 (1971), 35–59: an irreducible integral binary form of degree d≥4 without a fixed prime divisor takes infinitely many values with at most ⌊d/2⌋+1 prime factors counted with multiplicity. For the explicit published statement of this bound see also Irving, §1, [Almost-prime values of binary forms with one prime variable](https://doi.org/10.1112/S002557931400031X). For d=4 this is P3. If p∈T divided X, p⁴ would divide G(X,Y), impossible. Hence X is a p-unit, G is a p-unit, and [AX+LC'Y] is in the same prechosen p-adic neighborhood of [A] provided L was sufficiently deep. Every output pair is primitive since a common factor would occur to the fourth power. Infinitely many distinct projective parameters follow. All real slopes are admissible except the finitely many boundary points by definiteness of the conic.

The original mark error is square-equivalent to G since m is a square. Its positive squarefree part Δ has at most three prime factors, all outside T, and is locally a square on T. At an odd p|Δ the primitive parameter gives a projective root of H mod p via the invertible substitution. At these good primes the four reduced zero points form the same unramified étale scheme as above, with Frobenius acting by signs of √(−3θ) and √(3β), so a fixed root forces BOTH quadratic characters +1. Thus p splits completely in K.

**Infinitude of twist classes and nontorsion.** On the conic [n]=[−βθ(1+3q)(1+2q)], using 1−q=βs² and q=θt². For any fixed squareclass of n the double cover defined by this expression branches at the eight simple points q=−1/3 or −1/2: four over each, and q has only even-order poles. Thus the smooth completion has genus 3 by Riemann–Hurwitz, so Faltings gives only finitely many rational parameters on it. Infinitely many twist classes result. The 17-adic odd valuation of n and the even valuation of V exclude rational order 4 for both curves (at any rational two-torsion root one of the differences to another root has odd valuation). A rational order-3 point would give a rational projective root of the scaled 3-division polynomial 3x⁴+8x³−18x²−9, which has none mod 5. Mazur with full rational two-torsion now leaves only the four rational 2-torsion points. Our nonzero ordinates therefore have infinite order.

All constructions use the same pole b initially fixed. On all four linked rows one can successively add preceding defect primes to T, also enforcing their splitting in later quadratic defect fields by local squareness, with no change in h. If all four Δ are nontrivial the four quadratic fields are independent by disjoint nonempty odd prime supports; the resulting compositum yields all literal nonzero pairs over a totally real multiquadratic extension of degree at most 16, ramifying at at most twelve new primes, and splitting completely at any originally forbidden primes common to the four choices. This is not descent of those four solutions to Q. In particular we do not assert that even one of these unknown Δ equals 1 for the fixed enormous indexed scalar β=204618888056724185.
