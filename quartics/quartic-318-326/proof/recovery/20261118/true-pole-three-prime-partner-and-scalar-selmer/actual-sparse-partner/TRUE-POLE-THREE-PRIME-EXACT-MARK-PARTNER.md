# At each unchanged actual pole the one remaining exactly marked partner can have just three fresh primes

This concerns a NEW free twist choice on the original pair of elliptic curves, not the previously frozen t=18 index. Let B=17b=βv², D=1−b/17=θz₀², V=B²D for an actual prepared pole, with its positive squarefree β,θ, θ>1. Use β≡17 mod24, θ≡1 mod24, 17|β, gcd(β,θ)=1, θ square locally at every prime of β, and β square locally at every prime of θ, as in the original source preparations. In particular the following applies at the actual indexed scalar a=0 or −1, h=328655617654071015360, β=204618888056724185, without changing its b or factoring θ. The other three poles may make their own independent free choices at that SAME h, once supplied. For θ=1 equal twists already give a rational pair and the theorem below is not needed.

**Theorem.** For any finite prime avoidance set, infinitely many positive squarefree Δ>1 with at most THREE prime factors outside this set and 6βθ can be chosen with the following properties. Every prime of Δ splits completely in Q(√β,√(−2θ)). The first and partner curves on the literal unchanged pole can be taken square-isomorphic to E_(βθΔ) and E_(βΔ), respectively, where E_d:y²=x(x−d)(x+3d). Both have analytic root sign −. The first has an explicit nontorsion rational point with full Kummer pair (β,1) in the x,x−d slots. The partner has THIS EXACT full class in its 2-Selmer group, not represented by rational torsion. If this one class lifts over Q a rational both-nonzero PointPair follows on the unchanged pole. Neither an independent mark, nor an extension of Q to repair the mark, is needed. The fresh primes occur in the *partner amplitude*, not in the requested abscissa class. Unlike the t=18 choice, an exact first rank is not asserted.

**Cancel the correct whole factor.** On the fixed rational conic βs²+θz²=1 start at (s,z)=(v/17,z₀) and vary rationally. This leaves the actual source b alone: s is an auxiliary free slope, not a replacement of an indexed circuit coordinate. Put t=s/3 and M=9−βs²=8+θz². The coherent construction gives a global point on E_d at the exact class (β,1), with

    d=β(1−βt²)(1−9βt²)=β θ z² M/9,
    x=β(1+3βt²)²,    y=8β²t(1+3βt²)(1−3βt²).

Indeed x−d=(4βt)² and x+3d=4β(1−3βt²)². The free parameter on the actual first cubic is E=Be with e=d/(β(1+3βt²)²); scaling the displayed x by the square B/x gives exactly B and simultaneously sends d to E. The actual second parameter EV has class [dθ]=[βM]. Thus all original θ-primes are removed from the curve on which a global marking remains to be proved. If a rational point with full class (β,1) exists on the squarefree partner, scale it to the EV-model: its new positive abscissa has the same squareclass as B and hence equals B·S² for a nonzero rational S. This delivers the literal second ordinate. When θ itself is enormous one can parametrize the same auxiliary conic directly as βs²+D z'²=1 using (v/17,1).

**The sieve is applied to a definite quartic.** Parametrize the smooth conic by P¹_Q as (U:Z:W) using integral binary quadratics without a common geometric zero, s=U/W. There is no real zero of W. The integral homogeneous quartic H=9W²−βU²=8W²+θ Z² is positive off the origin. Its four distinct zeros are s=±3/√β, z=±√(−8/θ); W cannot be zero there. The two signs are independent under the Galois group of the indicated biquadratic field since β is positive nonsquare and −2θ negative. They are simple (z≠0 makes s unramified). Hence H is irreducible. Its squareclass at rational parameters is that of M.

Enlarge the avoidance set T to include divisors of 6βθ, primes ≤5, and primes of bad parametrization, quartic discriminant or content. At p|β or p=2,3 choose a p-adic conic point near s=0 (θ square) with M in [9]=1. At p|θ, choose it near z=0 (β square) so M near 8 has valuation zero. At other p≥5, β,θ units, the smooth conic over Fp has p+1 points. At most four are zeros of 9W²−βU², in its projective conic coordinates (U,Z,W). Choose a nonzero value and lift smoothly: whether the chosen point is finite or at infinity, M has EVEN valuation on a suitable p-adic open, since its denominator is a square and the homogeneous numerator is a unit. Here and throughout local conic coordinates can be rescaled integrally and one can avoid finitely many exceptional characteristic-zero parameter values.

Also choose one auxiliary good p>53 outside T. On the conic, adjoining the square root of νM for a nonsquare unit ν gives a smooth connected genus-one double cover in the reduction: its four simple zeros are the branch points, its poles have even order, and the good-prime exclusions ensure the conic parametrization has good reduction. By Hasse, p+1−2√p>8, so we can avoid the four ramification points and the at most four points over W=0. Lift to get M a nonsquare unit at this p and add it to T. This will force Δ>1. Weak approximation on P¹ now produces one primitive integral parameter A with all the chosen local conditions. Let m be the positive T-supported part of H(A); all its valuations are even because H/M=W². At p=2,3 we have more precisely M a local square.

Complete A to an integral unimodular basis (A,C). Choose L supported on T with all its depths as large as needed, in particular v_p(L)≥v_p(m)+4. Then G(X,Y)=H(AX+LCY)/m is an integral positive irreducible quartic whose leading coefficient is a p-unit at p∈T, and all other coefficients are divisible by p⁴ there. It has no fixed prime divisor: outside T use invertibility and the nonzero quartic over Fp with p>5, and at T use (1,0). Greaves' binary-form almost-prime theorem gives infinitely many integral values with at most three prime factors counted with multiplicity (G. Greaves, *Large prime factors of binary forms*, JNT 3 (1971), 35–59, https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(71)90049-7; the P-floor(d/2)+1 statement is spelled out in A. Irving, introduction, https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3592). Every resulting vector is primitive by degree four. If p∈T divided X, p⁴ would divide G, impossible. Hence G is a T-unit and the resulting projective parameter is arbitrarily close p-adically to A by choice of L. Its positive squarefree part Δ is that of H and M, since m is square. The auxiliary nonsquare unit ensures Δ>1. Outside T a prime of Δ gives a projective root of H mod p, forcing both √β and √(−2θ) in Fp by the four explicit coordinates. Only finitely many boundary parameters s,z zero need be discarded. Repeating after adding every previously obtained prime to the avoidance set gives infinitely many distinct Δ (even with disjoint supports), not merely infinitely many rational parameters for one twist.

**Exact partner Selmer marking and signs.** At p|β the squareclass of M is trivial by construction, so on E_(βΔ) the root x=βΔ gives pair (β,1) locally. At 2 the pair is trivial since β is a local square, and at 3 the same root works because Δ is locally square there. At primes of Δ the pair is trivial by splitting in Q(√β). Every remaining odd good prime admits all unramified pairs by good reduction and full rational two-torsion (unit squareclasses in each slot); infinity is trivial. Thus the precise pair belongs to Sel₂. Both squarefree amplitudes are positive and ≡17 mod24 by the dyadic and triadic conditions, so the conductor-24 twist-sign computation of the original free-rank-one paper gives root number − for each. The first point's ordinate is nonzero as t≠0 and 3βt²=βs²/3<1/3. On each twist there is no rational 4-torsion since at 17 differences between distinct roots have odd valuation, so all torsion Kummer images come from the roots and O. At roots 0,d,−3d the first classes are −3,d,−3d, respectively. For both curves d is positive and its support includes an extra prime from Δ, so none of the torsion images has first class β. Hence both any first and any successful partner marked point are nontorsion.

At the named scalar the target curve therefore has at most eleven prime divisors in its positive squarefree amplitude: the eight fixed known β primes and at most three fresh ones, rather than an unbounded unknown support of θ. For an eligible contact the corresponding bound is nine. Given such a choice, the ordinary split 2-descent for the remaining target only requires signed exponents on 2,3 and these at most eleven primes in each slot, i.e. at most 28 binary coordinates for the scalar, 24 for a contact. This is a bound on the size of the linear input, not on the sizes of the fresh primes, not a numeric Δ, not a claim this Selmer space is minimal, and not an exit from the need to prove the partner class rational. The theorem changes the free twists BEFORE asking for both ordinates; it proves nothing about the previous t=18 index.
