# Exact local scalar-pair criterion and the two actual torsion anchors

Let B≠0, D=1−B/289≠0, V=B²D. At a place of Q the original pole equations are
y₁²=B(B−E)(B+3E), y₂²=(Bs²)(Bs²−EV)(Bs²+3EV), with s≠0.
At every p≥5 the following are equivalent:
(1) these equations have a Q_p-point, even allowing either ordinate to vanish;
(2) they have one with both ordinates nonzero;
(3) for EACH of the two separate anchors E=B and E=−B/3 there is a point with y₂≠0;
(4) v_p(B) is even or v_p(B)>v_p(289).
The parameters of the different solutions need not be the same. At p=17 the strict threshold is 2, not 0. This tests the literal related numbers B,V, not an arbitrary independently specified pair of twist constants.

**Necessity.** When v_p(B) is odd, both abscissas have odd valuation. A square x(x−t)(x+3t) at such an x forces v(t)=v(x): otherwise its valuation is 3v(x) or v(x)+2v(t), odd; the case t=0 gives x³, and distinct valuations cannot produce a zero factor. Apply with (x,t)=(B,E),(Bs²,EV); subtraction gives v(V)=2v(s), hence v(D) even. If v(B)<v(289), then v(D)=v(B)−v(289) is odd. Equality with v(289) cannot be odd.

**Endpoint identities.** Write f(t)=(1−t)(1+3t), e=E/B, g=eD(B/s)². The two squares are equivalent to Bf(e), Bf(g) being squares, with y₁=B times a square root of the first and y₂=B s³ times a square root of the second. Conversely for nonzero e,g with [g]=[eD], choose s=B√(eD/g) to recover the same literal V. Brackets are multiplicative squareclasses over the local field. No independent second-twist substitution is made.

**Sufficiency with both fixed anchors.** Fix t∈{1,−1/3}. To obtain (3) set e=t and seek a nonzero g of class [tD] with Bf(g) a nonzero square.
* If D is square in the local field (any characteristic-zero completion, even 2,3 or the reals), choose g sufficiently close to t to be nonzero of its squareclass, with Bf(g) a nonzero square. Indeed Bf(t)=0 and Bf′(t)≠0; the local inverse function theorem realizes every sufficiently small nonzero square in the image. Or use the rational parametrization of the smooth conic z²=Bf(g) through (t,0). This covers v(B)>v(289) at every odd p since D≡1 (mod p).
* If B is square, take g∈[tD] arbitrarily p-adically small and nonzero; then f(g) is near 1 and a nonzero square.
* Otherwise in the only remaining case v(B) is even and its unit squareclass is nonsquare. Then v(D) is even. In fact if v(B)≠v(289) it is 0 or v(B)−v(289); in the equal case D cannot have positive valuation since B/289≡1 (mod p) would make B a square. Take units b₀,d₀ representing [B],[tD]. The smooth proper quartic with affine equation z²=b₀(1−d₀x²)(1+3d₀x²) has good reduction: its four geometric branch points are distinct in characteristic p≥5 and its leading coefficient a unit. Infinity in coordinates (1/x,z/x²) is unramified as well. Its special fibre is smooth, geometrically integral of genus one by the branch count. The Hasse–Weil bound for curves gives at least p+1−2√p>0 residue-field points (it does not presuppose a rational origin). A smooth residue point, including a branch or infinity point, lifts. Its local disk in the generic fibre has infinitely many points, so one can avoid all zeros and poles of x,z. Put g=d₀x². Both the class and nonzero square required follow. See Poonen, Rational Points on Varieties, Cor. 7.2.1 (https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/Qpoints.pdf).

Finally one may move e slightly off t, preserving [e]=[t] and taking Bf(e) to be any sufficiently small nonzero square by the same inverse-function argument. Keep g as above and choose the new s. This proves (2) independent of the anchors, while (3) separately supplies an adelic point on each anchored genus-one model when all completions pass.
