# All four-coefficient twists: an exact dyadic chart algorithm and its termination

**Result and limits.** For arbitrary input q=(q₀,…,q₃) in (Q×)⁴ and b=(0,32,76832,−343), this gives (i) two explicit bounded-denominator descriptions of the original dyadic normalized projective model, (ii) ideal arithmetic that computes its normalized affine charts at any step and the *reduced total-space* singular centers, and (iii) a deterministic geometric rule which terminates with a proper flat regular model of the actual twist over Z. The global stopping proof uses **Lipman's surface resolution theorem**. It does not supply a numerical uniform bound on the number of centers, an evaluated chart-by-chart resolution for the unevaluated mixed types, or a Lean theorem. The included executable performs the starting signed-norm integrality and mixed-Jacobian example checks; it is **not** an implementation or claimed run of the complete ideal/blowup engine. The previous direct chart-by-chart proofs remain stronger wherever their hypotheses hold.

## 1. Two exact normalization formulas, no coefficient splitting

Multiply each q_i by a rational square to get c_i=2^e_i u_i, e_i∈{0,1}, u_i a dyadic unit. Scale the roots accordingly in the same parameter field L. Choose a single finite odd N containing every odd divisor of numerators/denominators of q_i,c_i, the pairwise branch differences and all odd modification primes from the earlier arithmetic model. Use R=Z[1/N] rather than pretending Spec Z_(2) is an open. All u_i are units in R. The finite patch A=R[T] has the raw ring B=A[z_i]/(z_i²−f_i), f_i=c_i(T−b_i), z_i suitably scaled r_i. On the reciprocal patch s=1/T invert d(s)=∏_{i=1}³(1−b_i s) so s=0 remains included. Set A'=R[s,1/d(s)] and
    z'₀=s z₀,  f'₀=c₀s;
    z'_i=(c₀/2^h_i) z_i/z₀,  f'_i=(c₀ c_i/2^(2h_i))(1−b_i s),  h_i=⌊(e₀+e_i)/2⌋,   i=1,2,3.
These are literal elements of L and their indicated squares. Branch valuations on T give four independent squareclasses, and the reciprocal generators recover the same generic field (z₀=z'₀/s, then the ratios recover every z_i). Both rank-16 monic algebras therefore embed there by freeness. Each is regular after inverting 2: on the finite patch the four branch sections are disjoint and simple over R[1/2]; at most one non-étale quadratic factor can occur, with its unit T-derivative giving a smooth coordinate, the others étale. On the restricted reciprocal patch the last three factors are étale, the zeroth is the regular parameter line z'₀²=c₀s when 2 is inverted. In particular these raw rings are then already normal. In either parameter base A the height-one prime (2) has a DVR with v₂(f_i) in {0,1}; on the reciprocal patch subtracting the even 2h_i is essential for this uniformity.

In either patch write Z_I=∏_{i∈I} z_i (primes omitted on reciprocal symbols), F_I=∏_{i∈I} f_i. Its 16 sign automorphisms give Tr(Z_I Z_J)=0 if I≠J, and 16 F_I if I=J. If x=Σa_I Z_I is integral, then Tr(x Z_I)∈A (conjugates are integral; A integrally closed in its field). Thus 16 F_I a_I∈A and the generic vertical valuation of a_I is at least −4−Σ_{i∈I}v₂(f_i)≥−8. At every other height-one prime a_I is integral since B[1/2] is the true normalization with the indicated free basis. The normal parameter base is the intersection of its height-one DVRs. Therefore **2⁸x∈B**, on both patches. The denominators do not include unknown powers of a branch polynomial.

For b=Σ b_I Z_I in B form the degree-16 polynomial
    P_b(V)=∏_{ε∈{±1}⁴}(V−Σ b_I ε^I Z_I)=V¹⁶+Σ_{k=1}^{16} h_k V^(16−k).
All h_k∈A by sign invariance and the monic basis. The exact answer is
    N(B) = { b/256 : b∈B and h_k∈2^(8k)A for every 1≤k≤16 }.
Necessity is integrality of the coefficients of P_{b/256}; sufficiency is that very monic polynomial. More generally for a candidate b/2ⁿ the divisibility threshold is nk. This gives explicit finite membership in the literal integral closure for *every* input, not a squareclass-wise parametrization of its module generators. It computes wild fixed algebras as well: after coefficient splitting the integral closure of either parameter patch in the split compositum intersects L in precisely this algebra (scalar/sign invariants by branch independence); no regularity is inferred by passing to a ramified coefficient extension. On overlaps, integral closure in the fixed L localizes, so the patches really glue. The Python checker computes P_b by four quadratic norm steps on the sparse squarefree-monomial basis, and tests both an integral and a nonintegral half-element for a mixed first-three pattern not covered by the explicit families.

## 2. A terminating generator procedure, including *later* native charts

The bound in §1 suffices for membership, not to materialize a ring presentation. Here is a finite generator routine valid for the initial chart and every unnormalized blowup chart B encountered below. Inputs are finite type R-domains in the exact same fraction field, normal off 2; write ̅B for their finite normalization (finite by excellence). Compute J=√(2B) from the nilradical of B/2. Replace B by the fractional subalgebra
    B_next = (1/2)(2J :_B J).
This is End_B(J) viewed in the fraction field: multiplication stabilizes J exactly when this formula holds, since 2∈J gives 2x∈J⊂B for such an x. Conversely the colon test implies xJ⊂J. Any endomorphism of this nonzero ideal is multiplication by its action on 2 divided by 2 (commutativity and B-linearity); the ideal is finitely generated and faithful, hence such x are integral by the determinant trick. Present its finite generators by generators of that colon ideal divided by 2, using the exact fraction embeddings; for an algebra presentation introduce variables 2U_j−g_j and saturate by 2, i.e. contract the localized defining ideal on 2≠0. This prevents spurious 2-torsion. Distinct steps may require different presentations, all in the same field.

Repeat until the colon equals 2B, equivalently the replacement leaves B unchanged. Here is a direct stopping *and correctness* proof. Because B[1/2] is normal and ̅B/B finite as B-module, 2^l ̅B⊂B for some l (uniformly clear denominators of a finite generating set at this particular step). Its conductor C has 2^l∈C, so J⊂√C, and J^m⊂C for some m. If x∈̅B∖B, take the least n≥1 with J^n x⊂B; choose y∈J^(n−1)x∖B. Then y is integral and Jy⊂B. For j∈J, multiplying a monic equation of y by j to the appropriate powers shows (jy)^r ∈ jB (all lower terms divisible by j and jy∈B), hence jy∈J. Thus y∈B_next∖B. Every nonnormal step is strictly increasing; the chain is contained in the finite normalization of the original B as submodules over that original Noetherian B, so stabilizes. At its first equality the ring is normal. No false uniform eight-iteration bound is inferred; the power-of-two bound in §1 is a lattice bound only. The test is harmless where 2 is a unit, giving the already normal ring.

## 3. Find total-space singular points, not the non-smooth fibre

After normalization each affine is a normal integral arithmetic surface, regular off 2. Use a finite polynomial presentation B=R[X₁,…,X_n]/(g₁,…,g_m); add ordinary inverse variables and their equations for localizations as needed. For each relation define the integral polynomial
    δg=(g(X₁²,…,X_n²)−g(X₁,…,X_n)²)/2 .
Coefficients in R are fixed by the first substitution, and the numerator reduces to zero mod 2. Over B/2 form the matrix with one row per g and columns
    [ δg mod2, (∂g/∂X₁ mod2)², …, (∂g/∂X_n mod2)² ] .
The center ideal is the inverse image in B of the radical of the ideal of its (n−1)-minors in B/2. Use the unit ideal for size zero. Dimension of a closed vertical local ring of this integral finite-type flat relative curve is two (dimension formula over the excellent DVR at 2). In the ambient regular polynomial ring at such a point the dimension is n+1. To verify the matrix test, complete an unramified coefficient extension with the finite residue field of the point, then localize at one rational point above it. Regularity and embedding dimension are unchanged under this coefficient extension at the point (étale hypersurface residue extension, completion regular; equivalently first use finite étale local splitting, then completion). If the residue coordinates are a, translate by their Teichmüller lifts [a]. The classes of 2 and X_j−[a_j] are ambient regular parameters. The linear row of g is
    [ g([a])/2 mod2,  ∂_j g(a) ] .
Here g([a]) is divisible by 2. The Frobenius on the unramified integers sends each Teichmüller lift to the lift of its square, fixes R, and acts by squaring on the residue field. Consequently (g([a])/2 mod2)²=δg(a) since g([a])² is divisible by 4. Applying finite-field Frobenius to the other entries gives precisely our matrix, with unchanged rank. The rows generate the linear parts of the whole ideal since the g's generate it. The local ring is regular iff that rank is n−1; it cannot be larger by the dimension lower bound. Thus these minors detect exactly the singular closed vertical points. Normality gives regularity in codimension one; the singular set is closed and finite by excellence and dimension. The special fibre is finite type over F₂ and Jacobson, so the radical of the indicated minors cuts out precisely its reduced finite support. This argument does not confuse a nonreduced special fibre of a regular ramified surface with a singular total space (see checker). The formulas agree on overlaps because this description by the actual nonregular points is intrinsic. For the general mixed-Jacobian criterion see [Hochster–Jeffries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01996); the elementary local argument above suffices for our unramified coefficient ring.

## 4. Native charts and a noncircular stopping rule

If the singular ideals are units on every patch, stop. Otherwise blow up the globally compatible, reduced finite union of all singular closed points. Given local ideal generators f₁,…,f_s use the genuine projective blowup chart at nonzero scale f_j:
    B[f₁/f_j,…,f_s/f_j] ⊂ Frac(B).
As an ideal presentation substitute new variables and saturate (f_j U_i−f_i) by f_j; scale inversion recovers the previous localized ring, and contraction removes precisely the kernel of the map to the fraction field. Zero generators can be discarded; if the center is empty take the identity chart. These charts cover by the degree-one generators and identify on overlaps by the reciprocal-ratio rescalings. Normalize each by §2 in the same field, then iterate §3. Each iteration uses finitely many ideal operations and gives a proper modification (a finite normalization following a projective blowup), generally not a finite morphism. It is an isomorphism on the previous regular complement and fixes the generic fibre. The fractions and transition maps are part of the recipe, not guessed strict-transform equations.

Why must *this* rule terminate, not just some unknown resolution procedure? The [Lipman theorem, Stacks 0BGP](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BGP), with the [definition of normalized point blowup](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BBR), supplies a finite sequential resolution of our normal excellent surface by normalized blowups of closed points: finite type over the excellent R, normal, finite singular locus and normal completed local rings check its hypotheses. For a given singular closed point x, look at a finite witnessing sequence on Spec O_{X,x}, or restrict a global witness to the successive neighbourhood of x. Any steps away from x are identities there; if x were never blown up, its singular local ring would survive on the regular final model. Hence the first effective step there *must* be the normalized blowup of its maximal ideal. The same argument applies to each singular closed child of that step above x: away-from-child operations do not change its germ, and a regular final model cannot leave that germ unmodified. This recursively embeds every chain of successive singular children from our rule in the witness as a strictly increasing sequence of point-center indices. More explicitly after the forced step over x, restrict the remaining witness to the local ring of the selected child; normalized blowups commute with restriction/localization and other centers disappear. A normalized blowup at a disjoint closed point is an isomorphism near the child and cannot resolve it. Thus no such chain can exceed the witness length. Each level has finitely many closed children because its normal excellent surface has finite singular locus; the first simultaneous blowup agrees near each of its disjoint points with its individual point blowup, and normalization is local. Therefore the simultaneous rule has finite depth and stops. There is no need for, or assertion of, a decreasing multiplicity at each step. Lipman's theorem is a **real external input** for this argument; neither the norm bound nor the idealizer proof alone proves that resolution terminates.

Concretely all the internal operations are standard exact polynomial ideal operations: radical in a finitely generated F₂-algebra, ideal quotient/intersection, saturation by a single generator, equality and presentation of a fractional ring, reduction, derivatives and minors. Over R one may do computations over Z with an extra variable for N⁻¹, using polynomial-ideal operations over a computable PID, and take radicals for the tests only modulo 2. No Teichmüller coefficients need to be computed to run the mixed formula. Different ordered generating lists give isomorphic chart covers of the same intrinsic normalized blowup, so the stopping rule and final modification do not depend on a choice of resolution witness. Each stage can if desired emit its saturated presentations and transitions for verification.

The number of simultaneous dyadic rounds is unchanged by a finite faithfully flat étale coefficient extension near 2: the nonregular loci correspond by étale preservation/reflection; their finite reduced centers pull back reduced, since residue fields are perfect and the base change étale; the blowups and normalizations commute with that base change. A coefficient differing from one of {1,−1,2,−2} by an unramified scalar squareclass therefore has the same round count after a common finite étale comparison. There are at most 4⁴=256 ordered local types. In particular a uniform finite maximum **round count** exists and could be obtained by running the algorithm on those 256 representatives; no numerical maximum, complete table or uniform bound on the computational cost per round was computed here. Residue-field degrees or numbers of closed centers need not remain the same after a twist.

Starting with the actual X_q on the open U=Spec R and fixing its common field at each step gives an integral proper regular modification over U, flat by torsion-freeness. On U∩D(2) it is X_q itself. There the prior arbitrary-twist model over D(2) is also unchanged after our finite choice of N, so glue to obtain a proper flat regular model over all Z with precisely the original parameter map for *every* twist. This is a terminating, exact algebraic prescription with an existence-based termination proof; unlike RAMIFIED-DYADIC-222ODD and its predecessor packets it does not list the actual exceptional divisors or number, degree or multiplicity of centers for each remaining local squareclass pattern. No final semistability, minimality, dualizing/KS, metric/rank or 318/326 equivalence, and no Lean kernel acceptance result, follows here.
