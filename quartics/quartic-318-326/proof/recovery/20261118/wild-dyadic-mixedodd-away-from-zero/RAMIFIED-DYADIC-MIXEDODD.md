# All-odd mixed first-three signs: the original dyadic normalization is regular off T=0

This is an **evaluated partial case**, not an unevaluated call to a resolution theorem. Let the four signed squarefree representatives of q all be odd, and assume the first three are **not all congruent modulo four**. On the true normalization of the original parameter line, the total space is regular above every dyadic parameter point except possibly T=0, **including infinity**. We give the actual normalized rings after a faithfully flat étale parameter cover, with the two wild Gaussian components and their integral coordinate. No number or chart of the points over T=0, or complete explicit resolution over them, is asserted here.

By a finite étale extension of coefficients near 2 we may reduce to q_i=d_i∈{±1} matching the signed-unit residues; the ratios have odd squarefree representatives 1 modulo 4, so their maximal-order compositum is unramified at 2, and actual normalized charts commute with this étale base change. Work first over D=Z_(2), and for this sign representative use the raw roots r_i²=d_i(T−b_i), b=(0,32,76832,−343). At least one of d₁/d₀,d₂/d₀ is −1. A ramified constant Q(i) will occur **inside the normalized components after an étale extension in the parameter variable**. Its existence does not mean one can descend regularity through this ramified constant.

On the finite parameter open invert T,T−32,T−76832, which removes no dyadic point having T nonzero in its residue field (not only closed F₂-points). Over A so obtained adjoin two h_j with
    h_j²+h_j=−b_j/(4T),    s_j=1+2h_j,   j=1,2.
The right sides are integral since 32 and 76832 are divisible by four; the s_j satisfy s_j²=1−b_j/T and are units. Thus A' is finite faithfully flat étale over A: the equations are monic and the derivatives s_j units. Its generic field E is the degree-four biquadratic field from these ratios (valuations at the independent contacts b₁,b₂), and remains a field of the same degree after any constant extension by the same valuations. In the generic pullback of the original 16-root-degree algebra to E we have
    γ_j=r_j/(s_j r₀),     γ_j²=d_j/d₀  (j=1,2).
The algebra of these constants is **two** copies of E(i): each negative sign forces a factor i, giving two copies from the remaining ±1 choice or from the relative choice between two negative signs. E does not contain i by its geometric degree just verified. Over either factor the remaining independent generators U=r₀ and V=r₃ satisfy
    U²=d₀T,     V²=d₃d₀ U²+343d₃ .
Their two squareclasses over E(i) are independent: at T=−343 the extension by s₁,s₂ is unramified, the V radicand has odd valuation while the U radicand does not; at T=∞ the s_j have unit square residues and are unramified, while T has simple pole. Thus two degree-eight factors over E account for the original generic rank sixteen, not an identification of four different roots with one component.

Use O=D[i], the Gaussian DVR at its unique dyadic prime, π=1+i, π²=2i. On each factor of the normalized finite pullback the actual ring is obtained over A' by adjoining O, then U and Z=(V−U−1)/π, with U²=d₀T and
    Z²+(2/π)(U+1)Z+((1−d₃d₀)/π²)U²+(2/π²)U+(1−343d₃)/π²=0.
All coefficients are integral: 2/π divisible by π and 2/π²=−i a unit, and the two even integer numerators are divisible by π². The separate monics give finite free generic rank eight over A' and embed in the indicated field by rank and reconstructing V. Modulo π the displayed equation has U-derivative exactly the unit 2/π²; the linear-in-Z term vanishes and the U² derivative vanishes in char 2. In the polynomial chart with U as coordinate (eliminate T=d₀U²) before taking the two étale h_j the resulting divisor is smooth. Since π is a nonzerodivisor by monic freeness, the total-space local rings above 2 are regular. Adding the h_j is étale and does not alter that conclusion. In char zero the original simple-branch model remains smooth under the parameter étale pullback (equivalently, in the U-chart the two possible simple V branch points are disjoint and have nonzero derivative). Hence each factor is normal. Being finite integral with the matching fraction field, the product of the two factors is exactly the integral closure of A' in the generic pullback. By compatibility of normalization with étale base change, this is the actual normalized original cover pulled back to A'. Regularity therefore descends across the **parameter étale** A'/A. The two choices of γ describe the normalization in the product fields and are not arbitrary sheet choices made over F₂.

On the reciprocal parameter open near s=1/T=0 invert ∏_{i=1}³(1−b_i s). The identical construction takes h_j²+h_j=−b_j s/4 with s'_j=1+2h_j square roots of 1−b_j s. These form a finite faithfully flat étale parameter cover. Again γ_j=r_j/(r₀ s'_j) split the generic pullback into precisely two E(i) components with two independent remaining radicals. In each take U=s r₀, V=r₃/r₀. Then
    U²=d₀s,   V²=d₃d₀+343d₃ U² .
Adjoin O, U and Z=(V−U−1)/π. Relative to the finite formula, the coefficients of U² and of 1 in the divided equation are exchanged:
    Z²+(2/π)(U+1)Z+((1−343d₃)/π²)U²+(2/π²)U+(1−d₃d₀)/π²=0 .
Its π-fibre is smooth for exactly the same unit U-derivative and étale h_j. The generic field and ranks agree by the Laurent identifications and U,V recover r₀,r₃ generically. Normal finite factors thus again compute the actual pullback, now without inverting U near infinity. This establishes regularity on the missing boundary of the finite chart. Descent across the additional dyadically unramified coefficient-ratio cover (for original arbitrary units q_i) is now legitimate because regularity was proved on the normalized sign model and the comparison is étale. All operations are compatible with restriction to a genuine arithmetic neighbourhood of 2 after removing the finitely many odd contact/coefficient primes.

Thus in this mixed class any required dyadic modification is supported **over the single parameter value T=0**, not necessarily at a single point of the cover; in particular none is needed at T=1 or infinity. The example q=(1,−1,1,1) already lies outside the equal-first-three criterion. This does not prove a specific resolution over T=0, a statement about relative smoothness over Z_(2) (indeed π²=2i), minimality, or any final rank/Diophantine implication. The accompanying Lean text supplies only ring identities, not a compiled proof of normalization.
