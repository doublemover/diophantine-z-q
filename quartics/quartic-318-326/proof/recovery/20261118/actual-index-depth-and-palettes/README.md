# A compulsory source modulus from actual scalar-pole solubility

This packet continues the fixed-label obstruction (reviewed manifest
6b54012fc82463ba184a38e263b6f031be91599131d5b07cf19039aac7f9e87c).
It gives exact divisibility constraints on **both original integer
indices** and hence additional tests on the same-index order CRT; it also
quantifies the failure of fixed twist and complementary class palettes.
None of these necessary conditions produces a PointPair, all fifteen
primes, an actual full Values record or a Lean acceptance.

## All-good-prime integer index lock

Adopt the actual notation of that packet: for integer input a let r=inputR(a),
B=17b, H=Norm(chartU(h))≠0, c=Norm(chartU(n−1))≠0 and Q=c/(H r^15).
Fix a prime p∤2·3·7·11·17 with k=v_p(r)>0 odd. On every actual scalar
PointPair the prior local proof gave
v_p(Q)≥ceil((1−3k)/4) and the weighted sum of defects
Σ_{w|p} f_w max(0,k−2v_w(U_h))≤(k−1)/4, where U_j=chartU(j).
In particular **v_w(U_h)>0 at every w|p**. The curve
E:y²=x³−δx, δ=(3−√2)² with norm 49, has good reduction here.
If o_w is the reduction order of P=2R at w, then

    all o_w divide h;
    at least one o_w divides m=n−1;
    v_p(m) ≥ v_p(h) + ceil((57k+1)/8).

Indeed x(hP)=1/U_h² so hP is in the formal kernel. Put
d=v_p(m)−v_p(h). At each w for which mP also reduces to O,
the valuations of U_m and U_h differ by precisely d: both multiples
are in the subgroup generated by o_w P, and the p-adic formal group
logarithm preserves valuation and linearizes multiplication, as in the
preceding proof. Here v_w(p)=1, p≥5, so its higher terms a_j z^j/j
have valuation >v_w(z) even at v_w(z)=1. The parameter valuation is
−v_w(x)/2=v_w(U). At every other w, v_w(U_m)≤0<v_w(U_h).
But v_p(c/H)=15k+v_p(Q)≥ceil((57k+1)/4)>0. Consequently an active
w is necessary and d is positive. The total norm valuation difference
is at most (Σ_active f_w)d≤2d; this gives the bound as claimed, including
asymmetric split-component depths. If precisely one degree-one
component is active, the inactive component contributes at most −1,
so d≥ceil((57k+1)/4)+1 (at k=1: 16 instead of 8). These conditions
are independent of which two twist parameters were chosen. They
allow variations in h,n and do not assert existence with these depths.

For a=0 or −1 the six applicable simple divisors are

from R=(1+√2,2√2) on the literal source elliptic group, orders.py computes

quadratic residue field as appropriate. Every reported order o kills P,
and o/l does not for each prime l|o, independently of how the search
found the number.

    p             5     163    661    733    1489 (+382/−382)    1861
    ord(P mod w)  5      81  21905  44895        178 / 389      27931

Thus h must be a multiple of M=10270488051689719230. Modulo a tested
positive-contact modulus o with h≡1 (mod o), this demands gcd(o,M)=1:
even one shared prime excludes that orientation. In a full CRT check,
adjoin M by taking the lcm with the previous zero-residue modulus,
rather than multiplying unconditionally. Other imposed residues,
square/cube factors and all previous order constraints remain in force.
No all-prime or compatible actual indexed grid is claimed here.

Also n−1 must be a multiple of lcm(5,81,21905,44895,27931)=148327432074315

v_p(n−1)≥v_p(M)+8. For the original positive label

    5·g^8 | Jd.

## The compulsory prime support has normal order 4 log log |a|

Write β(a) for the positive squarefree representative of 17·114444·r(a),
not the earlier detector's inputBeta. Every prime p≠3 dividing β(a)
occurs with odd valuation in the parameter of BOTH elliptic curves in any
actual pair: the cubic forces v_p(e)=v_p(B) and
v_p(eV)=v_p(B)+2v_p(s) as in the fixed-label paper. Moreover 17 always
occurs as r≡5 mod17. These facts do not presuppose that the ordinates
are nonzero. For admissible a:

* The number ω(β(a)) of distinct compulsory candidate primes is of
  normal order 4 log log |a|. Precisely, for each ε>0 the proportion
  of integers with |ω(β)/(log log |a|)−4|>ε tends to zero. Removing 3
  or any fixed finite list changes this count by O(1). The same normal
  order holds for distinct p with v_p(r)=1, not only odd valuation.
  Each such good prime forces p^8 | Jd. Thus the actual original
  multiplier and both possible twist amplitudes have growing
  compulsory prime sets on a density-one set of inputs.
* Any finite collection of rational isogeny classes can contain a member
  of a realizing pair at **only finitely many integer inputs**, allowing
  h,n and the other twist freely. Indeed two nontrivial distinct twist
  squareclasses here cannot be Q-isogenous: j=35152/9 is non-CM, and an
  isogeny to a nontrivial twist would yield an anti-invariant nonzero
  geometric endomorphism in Z. Hence the palette supplies finitely many
  possible twist classes. Its union of prime supports, enlarged by 3,
  supports β(a) whenever either curve is in the palette. There are then
  finitely many [r(a)] and the smooth genus-three curves Y²=d·r(X) prove
  the claim by [Faltings](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01388432) as before.
  No list of all exceptional inputs or bound is supplied.
* At indices satisfying the real guard, actual D=1−b/17 is positive.
  On the density-one input set where it is nonsquare, the two ordinates
  cannot vanish together: e/B and eV/(B s²) would both be in {1,−1/3},
  whose ratios have squareclasses 1 or −3, whereas their ratio has
  class [D]. Thus at least one of the required nonisogenous curves has
  positive rank (both when both ordinates are nonzero), by the
  nonzero-ordinate torsion/17-valuation check in the earlier paper.

Here is the elementary normal-order proof rather than a statistical
extrapolation. In t=a(a+1), the four irreducible factors (s_i t+t_i)
of r(a)/4 are given in the fixed-label packet. Their quadratic
discriminant squareclasses as polynomials in a are respectively

    d_i = 3723, 3723, 7446, −52122.

The four factors have disjoint geometric roots (different t_i/s_i).
Outside a fixed finite set S of primes dividing coefficients, discriminants,
pairwise resultants or 2·3·7·11·17, the number of simple roots modulo p is
ρ(p)=4+Σ_i χ_{d_i}(p)≤8. Each character χ_{d_i} is nonprincipal.
Mertens' theorem in fixed arithmetic progressions ([Williams](https://people.math.carleton.ca/~williams/papers/pdf/057.pdf))
gives Σ_{p≤Y,p∉S}ρ(p)/p=4log log Y+O(1). For 1≤a≤X set Y=X^(1/4)
and let I_p indicate p|r(a). Uniformly in these primes,
E I_p=ρ(p)/p+O(8/X) and E I_p I_q=ρ(p)ρ(q)/(pq)+O(64/X) for p≠q
by CRT. Thus the mean is 4log log X+O(1) and variance O(log log X+
Y²/X). Chebyshev gives the stated concentration on scale log log X.
Each simple root modulo p has precisely one lift modulo p²; the expected
count of p≤Y dividing r to order ≥2 is at most Σ8/p²+O(8Y/X)=O(1).
Removing them changes the sum by o(log log X) in probability by Markov.
There are O(1) prime factors above Y since r(a)=O(X^8). Fixed primes
and multiplication by 17·114444 change the answer by O(1). Replacing
log log X by log log |a| fails to be uniformly additive O(1) only for
negligibly small a (restrict, e.g., to sqrt(X)≤a≤X); the symmetry
a↔−1−a gives the same result for negative inputs.

## No finite palette of complementary squareclasses either

If θ is any fixed **nonsquare** rational squareclass, represented by a
signed squarefree integer, consider good p∤θ with χ_θ(p)=−1 and
v_p(r(a))=1. Since b=114444r times a nonzero rational square, v_p(b)
is odd, for every chart (indeed for a free W≠0). If positive, then
D=1−b/17≡1 (mod p) is square locally; if negative, v_p(D)=v_p(b)
is odd. Neither matches the nonsquare unit [θ] at p. Thus each
such p excludes the proposed complement for all indices, without
requiring PointPair solubility.

The same second-moment argument restricted to those primes yields normal
order c_θ log log |a| for their count, where
c_θ=(4−#{i:[d_i]=[θ]})/2≥1. Indeed multiply ρ(p) by (1−χ_θ(p))/2;
the only extra principal characters are χ_θ χ_{d_i} for identical
squareclasses; the remaining terms have bounded reciprocal prime sums.
The expectation, variance and repeated-prime estimates are as above.
For any fixed finite palette not containing class 1 this gives
density-one simultaneous exclusion; if it includes 1 combine with
the earlier **actual chart-specific** Chebotarev obstruction
(forward-actual-scalar-complement-obstruction-r1, reviewed
c9708a4de89722b642d784789d04bca1a7c4a57e49390f57a81653f834be9b2e).
Unlike the twist/isogeny palette, only density-zero exceptions are
claimed for the complement palette, not finiteness. The proof is not
uniform over classes θ varying with a.
