# A pinned original scalar prime isolates the wanted marking and imposes two independent constraints on the entire partner Selmer group

Fix the unchanged indexed pole at a=0 or −1, h=328655617654071015360, β=204618888056724185, B=17b=βv², D=1−b/17=θz₀². Write E_N:y²=x(x−N)(x+3N), δ=(β,1) in the ordered x,x−N slots. No numerical value of θ or its complete factorization is needed. There are infinitely many distinct choices of the original free amplitudes giving a **rational nontorsion first point with full mark δ** and partner E_(βΔ), with δ everywhere locally soluble. Here Δ is positive squarefree and has the pinned factor 83 and between one and three additional prime factors. Its remaining primes can avoid any preassigned finite set. Both twists have sign −. Their classes, βθΔ and βΔ, do not replace the original pole or common index.

Let r=ω(Δ), so 2≤r≤4. On the partner the FULL subgroup of Sel₂ supported on the old primes 2,3 and primes dividing β (and sign) is exactly ⟨δ⟩, and dim Sel₂≤2r−1≤7, hence rational rank ≤5. If there is just one additional prime, the dimension is exactly 3, and a rational positive-rank proof, **without controlling its generator's mark**, suffices and is necessary for this full mark to lift. This does not assert that such a two-prime output occurs, that the partner has positive rank, or that the former celebrated t=18 index is decided. This new sieve permits four primes in total (only three not preassigned) rather than claiming a strengthened bound of three in total. It is not a solution to the four-pole problem.

**The prime on the actual sequence.** EXACT.json reproduces the genuine original-index computation at 83, not a squareclass guessed from auxiliary poles. The residue ring is F₈₃[√2], and with the source notation the reciprocal abscissas at the two doubled indices are both 66+23√2 of nonzero norm. The division-polynomial recursion checks the supplied common annihilating multiple 11861136 and a unit neighbor product before reducing either index. It gives N=11, Q²=23, W=23, D=61. The β and −2D residues are both nonzero squares. The conic βs²+D z'²=1 has the four points s=±24, z'=±40 mod 83 where M=9−βs² vanishes. These are simple zeros on the conic since s,z'≠0 and 83 is odd; the derivative along s is −2βs. Smooth lifting and varying s thus gives an open with v₈₃(M)=1. Because D is a unit, θ is an 83-unit, and replacing D by θ only rescales z'. The check contains an independent exact replay of the full base Sel₂ input from the preceding scalar sparse Selmer paper, using local doubling-index exhaustion and binary elimination, not an assumption that its non-torsion bit is rational. One basis of Sel₂(E_β) is τ=(−3,−β), γ=(8965,134909253259), δ; at 83 their pairs of unit nonsquare bits are (1,1),(0,1),(0,0).

**Prime forcing in the parameter that already gives a rational first point.** Parametrize the rational conic βs²+θz²=1 starting from the unchanged pole (v/17,z₀). Take t=s/3. The first coherent point on E_d for d=β(1−βt²)(1−9βt²)=βθz²M/9 has x=β(1+3βt²)² and x−d=(4βt)². The ordinary rational scaling by B/x sends the abscissa to B and the amplitude to the actual free parameter, whose V-twisted partner has class βM, as V=B²D. A rational second point with mark δ then gives the literal second abscissa B times a nonzero rational square and a nonzero ordinate. In the preceding quartic sieve write s=U/W, and H=9W²−βU²=W²M, a definite irreducible binary quartic with four simple zeros. In the finite set T put 83 as well as 6βθ, all requested other primes to avoid, and the original bad primes of H and its parametrization. At 83 use the open just proved even if the chosen global parametrization has bad reduction there: we use the characteristic-zero isomorphism with P¹ and control valuations of H, not that reduction. At other places of T use the same local opens as before: local square M at 2,3,p|β, unit M at primes of θ, and even valuation elsewhere. The former auxiliary nonsquare prime to guarantee Δ>1 is unnecessary. Weakly approximate by a primitive integral parameter vector A, avoiding poles and exact zeros, and let m be the T-part of H(A). Thus [m]=83 in Q×/Q×² (its exact 83-valuation may be an odd integer larger than one). Complete A to a unimodular basis (A,C), choose L supported on T with v_p(L)≥v_p(m)+4 and with ample approximation depth. The integral form G(X,Y)=H(AX+LCY)/m is primitive and irreducible, without a fixed prime divisor: at p∈T its leading coefficient is a unit and every lower coefficient is zero mod p⁴; outside T the invertible change and nonzero degree-four form mod p>5 suffice. Greaves' P₃ theorem gives infinitely many G-values with ≤3 prime factors with multiplicity. At a T-prime X must be a unit, since otherwise p⁴ would divide G, so G is a unit there and the output remains in the fixed opens. Hence Δ=sf(83G) has the fixed prime 83 exactly once and at most three further primes, all avoiding T. Every unpinned prime of Δ splits in Q(√β,√(−2θ)) by the four reduced simple zeros; 83 does so directly. Old local-square constraints and the real sign are unchanged. This gives the same ELS δ and actual first rational nontorsion point as in the unpinned argument. Choosing future T to omit none of the prior unpinned primes forces distinct outputs because at least one unpinned prime always occurs, as will follow below.

**A global bound stronger than counting the new bits.** At primes outside 6βΔ, good odd reduction has the entire unramified Kummer subgroup for both curves. At p|6β and infinity the two root-ordered local conditions agree by the local square scaling of Δ. For q|Δ on the target, the three nonzero root images have all the nonzero pairs of valuation parities (root differences have valuation one), exhausting the odd local group modulo doubling. Consequently the kernel of the valuation map to the 2r bits at these q identifies with the base Sel₂ classes whose two slots are both squares at every q. It contains δ and is killed down to its span at 83 by the displayed independent rows. There is no loss of global Selmer conditions here: a base class has old signed support, is in the common old conditions, and is automatically unramified at all good primes.

Let (x,y) be ANY partner Selmer element and (a,b) ANY base Selmer element. Hilbert reciprocity gives product_v (x,b)_v (y,a)_v=+1. This is the Weil-pairing cup product in ordered two-root coordinates: in F₂² the alternating perfect duality pairs the two slots crosswise. Away from Δ both elements belong to the same local Kummer group, a self-orthogonal subgroup for this local pairing (local Tate duality for the self-dual Kummer sequence). Hence all those local factors are +1. For q|Δ both a and b are q-units up to even valuation, and the remaining Hilbert factor has sign bit

    v_q(x) [b/q] + v_q(y) [a/q],

where brackets are the quadratic nonsquare bits. The above product gives a homogeneous linear equation on the complete 2r valuation bits. The equations from τ and γ are independent on the *ambient* bit space, already in the two columns at 83. Thus the image has dimension at most 2r−2. Its kernel has dimension one, proving the asserted bound. This argument does not assume any local-to-global principle for rational points.

The partner's two root classes have distinct independent nonzero valuation signatures at 83, so are independent and disjoint from δ, providing the matching lower bound of 3 on the full dimension and forcing r≥2. If r=2 these three classes exhaust the full group. In particular no 4-torsion is rational (the three root images are nonzero); all odd torsion is twice rational torsion. Thus if a nontorsion rational coset mod doubling exists, its class may be translated by rational roots to δ. Conversely δ cannot arise from torsion. The conclusion does not rely on an unverified 2-converse theorem or on analytic signs proving rational rank.
