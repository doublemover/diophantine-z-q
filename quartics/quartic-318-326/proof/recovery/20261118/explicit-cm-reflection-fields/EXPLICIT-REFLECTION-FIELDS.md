# Which character could account for the intrinsic Heegner nonvanishing?

Use the actual split-refined setup and notation of the prior genus/partial-trace packets: d=βγ≡23 mod24, D=−d, K=Q(√D), C=Cl(K), E=E₋₁, conductor 48, φ the fixed modular map, and w₄₈x₀=σc x₀ for the chosen oriented maximal-order CM point. It does not imply the trivial component is nonzero.

**Identify σ mod squares.** In the ideal-lattice notation of [Gross–Zagier, I.1](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.1007/BF01388809/fulltext.pdf), label x₀ by the inclusion I⊂n⁻¹I, with n n̄=(48), Norm n=48, and cyclic quotient. Complex conjugation gives Ī⊂n̄⁻¹Ī, the Fricke dual inclusion is n⁻¹I⊂I/48, in the same conjugate orientation. Their source ideal classes differ by [n]⁻¹[I]² since [Ī]=[I]⁻¹. Artin action might invert this class depending on convention, but mod squares σ=[n]. Thus for every genus character attached to a signed prime-discriminant divisor δ of D one has χδ(σ)=(δ/48), the Kronecker symbol. On √δ the action of s is sign(δ)·(δ/48). This is invariant under the allowed choices of orientations and basepoint: conjugating either prime factor of n changes its class by a square.

**Quadratic components.** For nontrivial χδ let Wδ=Σ_(a∈C)χδ(a) aφ(x₀). Then bWδ=χδ(b)Wδ and sWδ=−Wδ, the latter by inversion of a and Σχδ(a)=0 in the identity φ(x₀)+sφ(x₀)=T. Hence Wδ is a point on the quadratic twist of E by δ if sign(δ)·(δ/48)=−1, and by D/δ otherwise (interpreted by the twist isomorphism). In fact these are the only two extensions to Gal(H/Q) of its character on C; their values on s are opposite. Equivalently Wδ lands on the factor twist whose root number is −1, by the usual conductor-prime twist root-number formula w(E^(δ))=w(E)χδ(−48). For all ray contacts the distinguished genus character is δ=β>0, and β≡17 mod24 gives (β/48)=−1. Its trace therefore lands on the fixed E^(β)=E₋β(Q), independent of γ, not on the desired E_d(Q).

**The forced quadratic base of a higher component.** For a character ψ of C of order m=2^j≥4, the top quadratic character ψ^(m/2) annihilates C[2]. By the genus packet it is the unique nontrivial such genus character, which on a ray contact is χβ. Thus M=H^kerψ contains N=K(√β)=K(√−γ). Its Galois group over K is cyclic of order m and Gal(M/Q) is dihedral of order 2m. On N, s changes both √D and √β, hence fixes √−γ. Its reflection field F=M^〈s〉 contains the exact J=Q(√−γ). Here and below kerψ includes the odd subgroup of C. The central involution z in Gal(M/K) restricts to −1 on the primitive ψ component. If that component of the CM point is nonzero modulo torsion, apply (1−z)(1−s) to Tr_(H/M)φ(x₀): the result is nontorsion, s-anti-invariant and z-anti-invariant. Indeed s acts by minus on that trace modulo torsion (trace of T), so the ψ-projection is multiplied by four. At m=4, z generates Gal(F/J) after restriction, and the point is on E_d(F) anti-invariant over J after twisting by D (z fixes √D).

**For m=4 enumerate every such field without class field computation.** Assume β has all its primes 1 mod4 as on the three ray contacts. The graph gives (β/q)=1 for q|γ and (γ/p)=1 for p|β. Take integers r,v,t solving

    r² + γ v² = β t²,       gcd(r,v,t)=1,     8|v,     r,t odd,     vt≠0.

They exist by Hasse–Minkowski and weak approximation on the conic: at p|β use (−γ/p)=1, at q|γ use (β/q)=1, at 2 take v=0,t=1,r=√β with β≡1 mod8, at ∞ take a real point, and at the remaining odd primes use smooth reduction. Its rational coefficients are coprime: any prime dividing both r and v divides t, since β is squarefree.

For each positive squarefree divisor of d choose the *unique sign* u with ur≡1 mod4, and form

    F_u=J(√(uλ)),    M_u=F_u·F_(uβ)=J(√β,√(uλ)).

Factors u are understood up to rational squares, so F_(uβ) makes sense without re-squaring divisors. These are quartic and octic over Q: Norm(uλ) is β up to rational squares, hence neither uλ nor uλ/β is a square in J, and β is nonsquare in J. The conjugate field of F_u is F_(uβ) since Norm λ=βt². An automorphism sending √−γ to its negative, √β to its negative, and √(uλ) to ut√β/√(uλ) has order four and fixes √D. Conjugation fixing J and √(uλ) but negating √β inverts this order-four automorphism. Thus M_u/Q is D₄, M_u/K cyclic quartic.

Here are all ramification checks. Because β≡1 mod8 and d≡23 mod24, γ≡7 mod8, so J has discriminant −γ, with 2 split. At both places above 2, uλ≡ur≡1 mod4 is a unit, giving an unramified or split quadratic extension. Away from 2βγ, λ and its conjugate have no common ideal prime, since such a prime would divide both coefficients. Its norm βt² implies even valuations (inert primes cannot divide it). Multiplication by u introduces no new such parity. At q|γ, r is a unit: q|r would force q|t then q|v by squarefreeness, a contradiction. Rational q has even valuation in J, so uλ has even valuation. At p|β the prime splits in J by (−γ/p)=1. The same coprime-ideals argument shows exactly one of its two primes has odd valuation in λ, by the norm formula, and multiplication by u can switch but cannot change the count. Thus F_u/J ramifies at precisely one prime over each p|β, tamely, nowhere else at finite places. F_(uβ) ramifies at its conjugates, so in their compositum M_u/Q every inertia group at p|β or q|γ has order two: at a place over p|β the other quadratic factor is unramified; at q|γ both factors are unramified over the ramified J. K/Q itself ramifies at all such p and q. Therefore M_u/K is unramified and

    Norm_(J/Q) disc(F_u/J)=β,       disc(F_u/Q)=β γ².

All and only the quartic character quotients occur. Two radicals define the same field over J precisely when their ratio is a J-square, i.e. their rational ratio is 1 or −γ modulo Q-squares. For fixed M, in the biquadratic M/J exactly two of its quadratic fields over J have non-square rational norm class β for their radicals: F_u and its conjugate F_(uβ); the third is J(√β). Thus the 2^ω(d) permitted u's yield exactly 2^(ω(d)−2) distinct M (the four rational squareclasses in each orbit under β,−γ are distinct, preserve the mod4 condition, and are represented among the signed divisors after discarding rational squares). This is exactly the number of cyclic quartic quotients of C≅C_odd×(C₂)^(ω(d)−2)×C_(2^e), e≥2, by counting surjections up to inversion. In a given M the reflection s is nontrivial on √β and fixes J; the only two such reflections fix F_u and its conjugate. Thus the concrete list is exhaustive up to Q-conjugacy. Over J the twist by D is the twist by β, since D/β=−γ is square in J. The central anti-projection above now gives

    nonzero primitive quartic CM component ⇒ rank E^(β uλ)(J)>0

for a representative u of its field (replacing by the conjugate yields the conjugate twist, with the same rank). This is a bounded list of explicit elliptic curves over one known imaginary quadratic base. At e=2 there are no higher-order characters. It is not asserted that all these twists have rank zero; indeed the controls show the opposite.
