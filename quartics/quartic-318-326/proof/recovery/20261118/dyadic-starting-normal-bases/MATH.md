# Computed: dyadic integral bases on both initial charts for all 256 types

**What was run.** There are exactly 512 entries in initial-bases-512.jsonl.gz: finite and reciprocal bases for all ordered q in {1,−1,2,−2}⁴. Each has sixteen basis vectors in the original sixteen-monomial field basis, with a common denominator of 256. These are computed integral bases of the two **starting normalizations**, not primitive generators of completed local extensions, and not a run of the blowup/resolution loop. The congruence-only membership description in the algorithm note has thus been replaced, for these representatives, by actual bases. The separate twelve-origin test does compute total-space singularities for the twelve mixed all-odd sign representatives. No Lean has run.

## Coefficients and output conventions

Work over D=Z_(2) and A=D[t]. On the finite chart t=T the four monomials z_j have squares f_j=q_j(t−b_j), b=(0,32,76832,−343). On the reciprocal chart t=s the squares are q₀t for j=0 and q₀q_j(1−b_j t)/4^⌊(e₀+e_j)/2⌋ for j>0, where e_i=v₂(q_i). The associated field elements are s r₀ and (q₀/2^⌊(e₀+e_j)/2⌋)r_j/r₀. This reciprocal input is already smooth in characteristic zero on its **entire** affine s-line: its four single zeros are 0,1/b₁,1/b₂,1/b₃ and are distinct; their slopes are nonzero in Q. The same applies to the finite raw equations. Thus the normality-off-2 hypothesis of the generator algorithm holds over D[t] without first inverting the reciprocal branch factors. Restriction to the smaller reciprocal patch in the theoretical proof just localizes these bases. The full projective line is covered with the original fixed Laurent field action.

Let Z_I=∏z_i for mask I (bit i is root i). In a record, row j of basis_numerators_ascending is the vector of sixteen polynomials whose combination with the ordered Z_I, divided by 256, is normalized basis element j. A polynomial's integer list is in ascending powers of t; [] is zero. A repeatable generator-order and basis choice is made by the script and is not geometrically canonical. The actual normalization is one free D[t]-module of rank 16 on each chart. Only ratios of q_i to these representatives may require an unramified coefficient cover for other inputs; one should not reuse a numerical basis blindly for different coefficients.

## How the normalization engine works without a general computer algebra resolution driver

The starting B has exact multiplication table Z_I Z_J=(∏_{i∈I∩J}f_i)Z_(I xor J). Throughout work with a D[t]-basis and its integer polynomial multiplication table. For J=√(2B) we need B_next=End_B(J)=(2J:J)/2, with the strict-enlargement proof supplied in RAMIFIED-DYADIC-ALGORITHM. All kernel calculations here can be done over F₂[t] with saturated bases:
* In the free residue algebra B/2 let F be square Frobenius. Its nilradical is exactly ker F⁴. Indeed the residue algebra embeds in its generic fibre over F₂(t) by freeness. A nilpotent in a vector-space algebra of dimension 16 has nilpotence index at most 16 (the initial powers before its minimal relation are linearly independent); hence its sixteenth power is zero. Conversely any element with that power zero is nilpotent.
* For residue basis B_i, compute their sixteenth powers by four squarings using only the diagonal of the multiplication table in characteristic 2. The condition Σa_i(t)^16 B_i^16=0 becomes an ordinary polynomial linear system: every entry g(t) uniquely equals Σ_{r=0}^{15}t^r g_r(t)^16 over F₂. Split every coordinate into those sixteen rows and take their ordinary common kernel on the a_i. This is valid over the imperfect rational function field as well; simply treating Frobenius as F₂(t)-linear would be wrong.
* By row and column Euclidean operations over F₂[t], diagonalize the rectangular system far enough to isolate its kernel in the last columns of an invertible change of basis. With a nonzero pivot, divide/reduce its column below and its row to the right, swapping a nonzero remainder into the pivot to decrease degree. Once cleared, recurse on the bottom-right rectangle. Earlier pivots and their zeros stay fixed. No divisibility chain of Smith pivots is needed. Track column swaps and polynomial column additions; their literal lifts with integer 0/1 coefficients are invertible over D[t]. For a kernel of rank 16−r, the resulting J has free basis 2^{d_i}B_i, where d_i=1 on the first r columns, 0 on the rest. This is the actual inverse image of the radical modulo 2, not just its localization at the generic vertical point.
* With t_{ij}^k the multiplication table in this changed B-basis, the matrices of the B_i acting on J have entries 2^(d_j−d_k)t_{ij}^k (integral because J is an ideal; every required coefficientwise halving is checked). Reduce these matrices mod 2. A vector of coefficients is in the ordinary common kernel across all j,k exactly when the corresponding x∈B maps J into 2J. Thus the second kernel gives the subspace of new numerators x with x/2∈B_next. Use a second polynomial column change and replace the trailing kernel columns by half of themselves. If its rank is zero the chart is normal. Otherwise scale the table, checking all exact divisions, and repeat. The stored common 256 numerator basis never needs a nonintegral coefficient by the trace bound at every intermediate ring. The implementation asserts exact divisibility at each rescaling, not a rounded reduction.
* Invertible column changes preserve the lattice. Each newly halved independent kernel column adds 1 to the generic DVR index exponent. Its total is ≤128 by B_final⊂2⁻⁸B_initial; in particular a strict step cannot occur forever. Independently, the idealizer conductor argument proves a nonnormal ring cannot stop early. Integer arithmetic over Z[t] suffices for these presentations and commutes with localization at the odd constants in D. We assert dyadic normality, not a simultaneous normalization at the omitted odd primes.

The run produced strict-enlargement counts 2,3,4,5,6,7 in respectively 40,96,240,96,24,16 charts. The sum of enlarged column ranks (vertical index exponent) ranges from 8 to 28. These are **normalization iterations**, not singular center counts or resolution depths. The 512 records' SHA256 and compressed encoding are fixed in the manifest. run_all.py regenerates the individual JSON records; the compact dataset is their concatenation in lexicographic filename order, gzip mtime zero.

## A first singular center actually evaluated for twelve mixed types

For each q in {±1}⁴ with q₀,q₁,q₂ not all identical, specialize the finite-chart multiplication table at t=0 mod 2. In this 16-dimensional F₂-algebra S, F⁴ kills the nilradical and is an automorphism on the finite product of finite residue fields. Its rank as an F₂-linear map is therefore the dimension of S_red. All twelve computations returned rank **one**. The rational-character search (finite bit-vector enumeration, excluding the zero map and enforcing both squares and multiplication) returned exactly one nonzero character. So the full fibre over this closed parameter value has a unique closed point, residue F₂; it is not asserted to be reduced.

To compute the total-space embedding dimension use the ambient 17 polynomial variables t,Y₀,…,Y₁₅ and equations Y_iY_j−Σt_{ij}^k(t)Y_k and 1−the basis expression for the identity. These present B: products reduce every polynomial to a linear expression (including constants by the unit relation), and the actual basis is free. Locally the unit relation is redundant at the genuine nonzero character: 1_B's basis expression acts as identity on all Y_i by the product relations, and one Y_i is a unit locally. Thus all needed rows come from the multiplication equations. At the rational point use integer lifts 0,1 for Y_i and t=0. For a relation g, its linear row is g(lift)/2 mod2, its t derivative, then its sixteen Y derivatives. The row-rank test gives embedding dimension 18−rank, not dimension of the fibre's tangent space. origins.py obtains **4 if q₃=+1 and 6 if q₃=−1** for all six sign choices of the first three. Local dimension is two by the finite free projection. Thus all twelve are singular there; the independently proved Gaussian etale charts give regularity outside that parameter value including infinity. This is the exact first singular closed center in each of these twelve dyadic starting models. The conclusion and embedding dimension extend to their unramified scalar variations: the unique geometric point descends to a single F₂-point and regularity/embedding dimension are unchanged after the finite étale coefficient comparison. No subsequent blowup of those twelve singularities is evaluated in this packet.

Theoretical ingredients and limitations: Serre normality, polynomial arithmetic and the prior proved idealizer criterion suffice for these starting charts; Lipman's theorem is needed for the separate full *resolution* termination but not for this finite basis calculation. The outputs do not by themselves identify exceptional multiplicities, minimal or stable reductions, KS lattices, ranks or the final 318/326 theorem.
