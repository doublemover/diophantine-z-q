# Neither sign of a zero-ordinate anchor works at the historical five-prime stress pole

This is a pointwise arithmetic result for the earlier **synthetic** prepared pole, not any specified indexed pole. Write m=409·2857·3673·6121·6529, β=17m, θ=131·577·1367·9133, D=20491203655, N=βθ and b*=m(204/D)². The checked preparation identity is D²−12²β=θ. The historical positive anchor at twist N failed by an independently computed Cassels obstruction. Here we exclude the *negative* first-torsion anchor and thus, by exact swap symmetry, **any PointPair with a zero ordinate** at this b*. We do not rule out two nonzero ordinates at a free twist.

**Translate the target.** Normalize the common first abscissa to B=β and the ratio twist class to V=θ by rational square scales. A PointPair on E_a:y²=x(x−a)(x+3a) then seeks a rational point at x=β on E_a and a rational point at x=βr² on E_(aθ), for r nonzero. A zero first ordinate forces a=β or −β/3. For a=−β/3 the second equation, after removing the square (βr)², is

    Y² = β (r²−θ)(r²+θ/3).

In particular r²>θ (θ is nonsquare), and the positive squarefree class d=[r²−θ] satisfies [r²+θ/3]=βd. Brackets mean squareclasses. Clearing projective denominators introduces no new class. At infinity in the r-line the leading coefficient of the quartic is β nonsquare; neither finite zero factor gives a rational ordinate.

**Unique possible full lift.** For an odd p not dividing 3βθ, the two factors differ by 4θ/3, a unit. For integral r at most one has positive valuation, which must be even; for nonintegral r both valuations are even. Thus d is supported on 2,3 and divisors of βθ. At p=3 a nonintegral r would make the product equal β up to local square (β=2 mod3); an r divisible by 3 gives product of odd valuation. For unit r the second factor has class θ/3, forcing [d]_3=[3βθ]_3.

At p|β, negative valuation of r or integral r away from a root leaves the product with odd valuation. Near r²=θ mod p the second factor has class 4θ/3, forcing [d]_p=[3βθ]_p. Near r²=−θ/3 mod p, possible only if −3θ is a square mod p, one has [d]_p=[−3θ]_p. In particular 17|d. At p|θ, r of valuation ≤0 gives [d]_p=1. For valuation ≥1 one has [d]_p=[−θ]_p and this can occur only if −3β is square locally. In particular 131 and 1367 cannot divide d. After these constraints d is 3·17 times a subset-product of 2,409,2857,3673,6121,6529,577,9133. The enclosed integer verifier checks the parity of valuation and Legendre character at each named odd place in all 256 cases. Exactly **d=3β** survives. Necessary conditions suffice for uniqueness; we verify actual local solubility below.

Writing r²−θ=3βu² gives 3r²+θ=s². Projectivize, with z the denominator:

    H1: s²−9βu²=4θz²,    H2: s²−3r²=θz²,    H3: r²−3βu²=θz².

H1 follows from H2,H3. These are the actual full 2-cover equations of E_(−3N), after scaling the original E_(−N/3) by x'=9x,y'=27y. Order the roots (0,9N,−3N). At x'=9βr² when z=1 the three differences are β(3r)², 3(3βu)², 3βs². Thus α=(β,3,3β). The conic Hi forgets the ith root coordinate, so the tangent order below is literal.

**Everywhere local Selmer inputs.** For α take z=1,u=0,r=√θ,s=2√θ at infinity and at 2,3 and p|β (θ is square at each). At p|θ take z=0,r=1,u=1/√(3β),s=√3; 3 and β are squares there. For ε=(−51,−51,1) the Kummer images at infinity, at 2,3,17, and at all other βθ primes are respectively the root point T1, the root point T3, and the origin. Indeed the root-point triples are (−27N²,−9N,3N) and (−3N,−12N,36N²); the verifier checks equality locally componentwise, including the dyadic unit classes. At p∤6βθ both tuples are units. The reduction is good and p odd: local 2-Kummer classes are unramified (finite étale doubling, using the proper model) and the image has order four because the reduction group modulo two has size equal to its full rational 2-torsion and the formal kernel is uniquely 2-divisible. The unramified subgroup also has order four since the torsion is constant. Hence both tuples are locally soluble there as well.

**Cassels row.** Use the full-rational-2-torsion tangent-Hilbert formula in [Shukla, §3.1.1, eqs. 3.1.3–4 and Thm 3.3.7](https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/id/eprint/8348/1/thesis.pdf). In multiplicative notation the pairing is the product of (Li,εi)_v over every i,v; a nontrivial value forbids α from the rational Kummer image. On H1 the rational conic point (s,u,z)=(2D,8,1) gives L1=D s−36βu−2θz. On H2 take (s,r,z)=(−1001545,140716,1) giving L2=−1001545s−422148r−θz. On H3 one can take (r,u,z)=(302690087790674,3819,311373085); the third factor is 1 since ε3=1. These are conic points, not points asserted to lie on the intersection. Multiplying any Li by a fixed nonzero rational is harmless globally by Hilbert reciprocity. Scaling each local common projective representative multiplies the product by a symbol paired with ε1ε2ε3, a square. Smooth local points may be perturbed slightly to avoid the finitely many tangent zeros without changing the two nonzero squareclasses evaluated below.

At other βθ primes −51 is a local square. At every remaining p the diagonal conics H1,H2 are smooth over Z_p, their sections above are primitive and their Li primitive tangent forms (all factors relating the forms to the gradients are units there). At a primitive point of the complete intersection, the projected three coordinates on each conic are primitive: if all three vanished mod p, the remaining quadratic equation with unit coefficients forces the fourth to vanish too. On a smooth conic over Z_p, the primitive tangent line at an integral section intersects it in twice that section: over Q_p this is the tangent divisor, and no vertical component occurs because the special fibre is a smooth conic not contained in the primitive tangent line. Evaluation at another integral section not on the line thus has even valuation. Both odd Hilbert symbols with the unit −51 are +1, even at primes dividing the displayed conic coordinates. This accounts for every place.

The product is (−1)(+1)(−1)(−1)=−1. The only possible full lift cannot be rational, so the negative first-torsion anchor is impossible. For completeness, if the SECOND ordinate in any PointPair vanishes, swap P,Q using a'=aV/r², r'=V/r, y1'=y2/r³ and y2'=y1(V/r)³; the first abscissa remains B. Thus such a pair would yield a first-torsion anchor, positive or negative. For the positive first anchor a=β, write T²=β(r²−θ)(r²+3θ). All factors are nonzero since θ is positive nonsquare. Then P=(βr²,βrT) lies on E_N. Translate by the rational two-torsion point (N,0): the new ordinate is rational and the new abscissa is x'=N(x+3N)/(x−N)=θs², with s=T/(r²−θ) nonzero. Consequently W²=θ(s²−β)(s²+3β) for W the new ordinate divided by θs. This is exactly the historically excluded positive anchored quartic with denominator coordinate 1. No identification of the β and θ abscissa classes without the torsion translation is assumed. The conclusion is only about zero ordinates for this fixed synthetic pole. It does not exclude free-twist solutions, prove rank in the original family, or establish Lean acceptance.
