# An exact class-group invariant on every original support, and a relative 8-rank bit on the three ray contacts

This is a mathematical consequence of the *actual* prime-symbol kernel in r155, not a field-size estimate and not the class group of a covector field. It applies before choosing the optional split-at-2 refinement. Write d for either positive squarefree product of an original simultaneous support. Then d≡11 mod12, D=−d is an odd negative fundamental discriminant, K=Q(√D), and t is the number of distinct odd prime factors p_i of d. The set B of β-prime indices is a nonempty proper subset; β=∏_(i∈B)p_i≡17 mod24. The symbol matrix L (ActualTwoDescentPrimeLaplacian318.laplacian) has, off the diagonal, L_ij=[(p_j/p_i)] in additive bits, and its row sum is zero. Its column sum also vanishes because d≡3 mod4. The exact output ActualTwoDescentFiniteConclusion318.Conclusion.kernel gives ker L=<1,b> with b the indicator of B, **at both** d=n and d=nθ.

**Identify the genus map with the correct transpose.** Write p_i*=(-1)^((p_i−1)/2)p_i, so ∏p_i*=D. Let C=Cl(K), additive notation, and P_i the ramified prime ideals of norm p_i. Gauss genus theory gives C[2]=<P_i> with exactly the all-ones relation (their product is (√D)), and C/2C is detected by the t genus characters χ_j for the prime discriminants p_j*, with exactly their product trivial. Distinguish the ramified ideal index i from the character index j. Put M_ij=[χ_j(P_i)]. For i≠j this is [(p_j*/p_i)]=[(p_i/p_j)]=L_ji by quadratic reciprocity; one can evaluate at P_i even though p_i|D, since p_i is prime to p_j*. Both sides have zero row sums fixing the diagonals. Thus M=Lᵗ. With character values recorded as a column, the genus map on ambiguous-ideal exponent columns is Mᵗ=L. This orientation matters for the *marked* ambiguous class. See [Stevenhagen, Rédei reciprocity, §§2–4](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004121000335).

Consequently C[2]∩2C has dimension one. Its unique nonzero element is the literal class b₀=[∏_(p|β) P_p]. In particular

    Cl(Q(√−d))[2∞] ≅ (Z/2)^(t−2) ⊕ Z/2^e   for some e≥2.

This gives **4-rank exactly 1**, not 0, on each actual output. It applies also to the fourth scalar twist pair: the set B need not consist of 1-mod-4 primes. In particular a 4-rank-zero hypothesis on the class group of this same K is never met by these supports.

**One computable bit gives 8-rank.** Pick r∈ker Lᵗ outside the all-ones line, and write δ=∏_(r_i=1)p_i*. The genus character χ_δ is the unique nontrivial character of C/2C annihilating the image of C[2]: the latter has codimension one by the result above. The other choice r+1 defines the same character. Pick a primitive integral solution with W>0 odd of

    β U² + γ V² = W²,    γ=d/β,    gcd(W,2d)=1.

Existence is unconditional here. The row equations Lb=0 say (γ/p)=1 at every p|β and (β/p)=1 at every p|γ. At those places one may take the nonvanishing unit coordinate and square root, with W a unit. At 2 take V=0 using β≡1 mod8; over R take a point with W>0. At all other odd places the smooth nondegenerate ternary conic has a finite-field point lifting by Hensel. Hasse–Minkowski plus weak approximation on this rational conic gives a rational solution satisfying all the unit conditions; clear denominators primitively, taking local projective neighborhoods with W a unit in primitive coordinates. Nonzero U,V can be obtained at the same time.

For α=β U+V√D, Norm α=βW². At p|β the unique prime has valuation 1, and there is no other support above 2d. Away from 2d, a prime dividing both α and its conjugate would divide U and V, contrary to primitivity. Thus (α)=(∏_(p|β)P_p) a² with a integral prime to D, Norm a=W. In C, 2[a]=b₀, so b₀∈4C iff [a] belongs to 2C+C[2]. The character just found tests exactly that quotient, giving

    e≥3  iff  χ_δ(a)=(δ/W)=+1;    e=2  iff  (δ/W)=−1.

This is an 8-rank test from a ternary norm conic plus one ordinary Jacobi symbol, not a large class-group computation. It does not settle e beyond the 8-rank threshold. Nor should δ be replaced indiscriminately by β: for the legitimate small graph on ordered primes (79,11,19,13), β=209, ker Lᵗ picks δ=2717 (or its complement) and not β.

**A relative sign at the actual contact poles.** Suppose *each* p|β is 1 mod4. Reciprocity gives (L+Lᵗ)b=0, since the reciprocity correction is the off-diagonal product of parity bits and the bits at B vanish. Thus r=b works, δ=β. Write ε(γ)=+1 when the class group at D=−βγ has 8-rank 1 and −1 when it has 8-rank 0, where its actual kernel hypotheses are understood. A primitive θ conic

    βa² + θe₀² = ℓ²

gives the exact comparison for the two original support products,

    ε(γθ) = ε(γ) (ℓ/β).

Indeed choose the β,γ solution (U,V,W) above. Multiply W+U√β and ℓ+a√β in Q(√β). The resulting coefficients W′=Wℓ+βUa, U′=Uℓ+Wa satisfy W′²−βU′²=γθ(Ve₀)². Signs can be chosen so W′>0. Primitivize all three by their positive gcd g. For odd p|g, p∤β since W and ℓ are units at β, and (β/p)=+1: an inert p dividing this product in the real quadratic field would divide one original factor and hence both of that factor's rational coefficients. The primitive triples have gcd(W,U)=gcd(ℓ,a)=1, since γ,θ are squarefree and the equations force any common prime to divide the third coordinate. The prime 2 has χ_β(2)=1. Hence (g/β)=1. For any primitive solution to the new β,γθ equation, its W-coordinate is coprime to odd dθ by squarefreeness of both disjoint factors. If W is even, the same ideal factorization computation works after dividing α by a rational power of 2: 2 is unramified, and one may remove the minimum exponent at its two primes if split, or the whole ideal power if inert (principal (2)). The remaining exponents at split 2 are even by the norm parity; the half-ideal's norm differs from W only by a power of 2. This does not change χ_β. Thus ε(γθ)=((W′/g)/β)=(W/β)(ℓ/β), since W′≡Wℓ modβ and β≡1 mod8. The normalization of the conic or sign of a does not affect this result.

This is not a blanket hypothesis on all four β. At the three *ray-prepared* contact poles, MergedContactPrimePattern318.actual_squareclasses gives b_j=(∏_(k=1)^5 p_jk)z_j² with five distinct positive primes, and the separate per-form 1-mod-408 ray (SelectedRayEmbeddings318.ray_mod_eight and SelectedPoleFullSource318) ensures none equals 17 and each is 1 mod8. Thus β_j=17∏p_jk qualifies. Bare Values without those rays would not suffice. Moreover the actual prepared pole itself gives the comparator without a separate search: write z_j/17=a/ℓ in lowest terms with ℓ>0. The equality 1−b_j/17=θ·(rational square) gives ℓ²−βa²=θ e₀² with e₀ integral because θ is squarefree. The triple is primitive. Its ℓ is prime to βθ by the equation and squarefreeness (β,θ disjoint); it is odd since β,θ≡1 mod8. The relative sign is therefore the explicit product of ordinary residues of this actual denominator over the six β primes. No fixed value of this denominator bit or existence of all the input data is asserted. For the scalar pole one always has the general δ-test, but not this β-character simplification unless its individual primes satisfy the extra hypothesis.

This is a theorem about the imaginary quadratic Heegner fields of the precise indexed support construction. It is not a theorem about Sha(E_d), an odd-Selmer class-group bound, a derivative, or a nonvanishing Heegner trace. It identifies an exact global obstruction to using the common zero-4-rank shortcut and replaces the next field-theory uncertainty with one explicit bit at each curve (one relative input per eligible pole).
