# A fixed-window quantitative Chebotarev polynomial lower bound, and use with the Gaussian obstruction sieve

**Status and scope.** This is a paper-level shortcut to the lower half of the simultaneous-prime theorem in quartic_v10 (a0950ac7, lines 16500ff), not a Lean proof. We need only a fixed sufficiently small positive window constant, not an asymptotic for arbitrary shrinking windows. The genuine extra input is Frei–Tinková, *Linear constellations in primes with arithmetic restrictions*, arXiv:2408.17441v3, Theorem 1.4 (unconditional prescribed Artin symbols, **not** their primitive-root theorem). Frei's TU Graz publications page lists the work accepted in Mathematika, to appear. The published Tao–Ziegler *Polynomial patterns in the primes*, Forum Math. Pi 6 (2018), Theorems 9,11,13 and Corollary 3, provide polynomial transference. We do not assume a polynomial Chebotarev theorem. Companion audits are separately identified.

**Fixed-window lemma.** Fix distinct integral c_1,...,c_k, a finite Galois K/Q, a union C_j of conjugacy classes for each j, and D a multiple of the conductor of the maximal abelian subfield of K. Write mu_j(b)=0 on nonunits and otherwise the conditional fraction of the abelian fibre lying in C_j. Let P_j=u+c_j w², and let L_j be log p on unramified primes in C_j, zero elsewhere. Set
S=(D/phi(D))^k E_{u,w mod D} prod_j mu_j(P_j) prod_{p not dividing D} b_p,
where b_p=E_{u,w mod p} prod_j (p/(p-1))1_{p not dividing P_j}.
If tau>0 is fixed and sufficiently small depending only on the coefficients, and T=floor(tau sqrt N), then
liminf [sum_{N/2<=u<=N,1<=w<=T} prod L_j(P_j)]/(NT) >= S/10.
The factor 1/10 is deliberately wasteful and **independent of the field and cutoff**. The threshold N may depend on all fixed data. All coefficient differences nonzero modulo all but finitely many primes, so b_p=1+O_k(p^-2) there; w=0,u=1 gives a positive factor at every remaining p.

**1. Centered linear cubes at frozen sieve level.** Use exactly the ordinary squarefree primorial W=prod_{p<=v}p of Tao–Ziegler, taking v beyond every divisor of D. Let n=floor(N/W), work cyclically with representatives 1,...,n, and let I=[5n/8,7n/8]. For unit b mod W set
 f(x)=1_I(x) phi(W)/W L(Wx+b),  m(x)=1_I(x) mu(Wx+b mod D),  g=f-m.
The mask is *not* treated as a constant over the W progression when D contains higher prime powers. For any fixed cube order a, fixed exponents 0<C1<C2<C3, A=W^(1/rho) with fixed rho>0, uniformly for q<=A^C1 and n A^-C3 <= J <= n A^-C2 we have
 limsup_{N to infinity} ||g||_{U^a_{q[J]}}^(2^a) <<_{a,rho,C1} 1/log v.
In this display K,C,D are fixed as v tends to infinity. The bound on the finite-N remainder need NOT be uniform in K or D.

To see this, write the box defining the local Gowers powers using x in [n], h_i,h'_i in [J], vertices x_omega=x+q sum_i omega_i(h_i-h'_i) (translate the base x from the alternative 0/1 increment convention). Split into convex polytopes according to torus wrap integers l_omega and the interval tests on all representatives x_omega-l_omega n. The common 1_I on EVERY term allows restriction to those polytopes where all vertices belong to I. Split input variables into residue classes modulo D, fixing each mu. Expand the centered product. For a subset S of f vertices the weighted affine forms are W(x_omega-l_omega n)+b, after affine substitution of the congruence class. Distinct vertices have linearly independent pairs of homogeneous gradients; the forms have fixed finite complexity and size bounded RELATIVE to n, including the O_v(n) intercepts. Apply FT Thm 1.4, Eq.(1.15), on each convex polytope, dilating the ambient box by a fixed constant where needed. Finite unions of conjugacy classes follow by summation. Removing higher prime powers costs O_v(n^{-1/2}(log n)^O_a(1)) after normalization by n J^(2a): a single nonzero affine form has O_v(n^{1/2}) possible powers and O_v(n^{2a}) points per value. The ramified-prime contribution is negligible. The case S empty is lattice point approximation. The FT error o_v(n^(2a+1)) divided by n J^(2a) tends to zero at fixed v, including arbitrary moving J in this range: dimensions and coefficient bounds are then fixed, and FT is uniform over convex domains and relative sizes.

For each active form the finite abelian factor and the ordinary factors at primes dividing W give precisely mu(Wx_omega+b-Wl_omega n) after normalization by phi(W)/W. Indeed on a fixed unit abelian residue D the rational prime density multiplier is (D/phi(D))mu; primes of W outside D each give p/(p-1); every p|D divides W. The substitution of all inputs in fixed D-classes adds no new prime to this list. At p outside Wq the hyperplanes of S are pairwise independent mod p, so inclusion-exclusion through pairs gives 1+O_a(p^-2), uniform in the constant terms. At p|q, p>v the bound is 1+O_a(1/p), since each form has a unit x coefficient (modulo p); all zeros combined have density at most |S|/p. Summing these errors costs O(1/log v), since log q << log W << v and each such p>v. The total normalized local product is thus the product of the mu's at active vertices times 1+O(1/log v), uniformly in convex cells and subsets (zero fibres give zero). The coefficients supplied by the inactive vertices complete the same full product of mu's in every term. The signed main volume terms cancel identically; the sum of normalized volumes is at most 1. The number of cells and fixed classes is finite at frozen v and upper complexity, so their FT remainders tend to zero before v is increased. This proves the cube bound.

**2. Polynomial transfer at the full square-root window.** Put u=Wx+b, w=Wy+c, 1<=b,c<=W. The residues b_j of b+c_j c² lie in [W]; only tuples with all b_j units matter, as all actual primes counted here exceed W on I. Define R_j(y)=c_j Wy²+2c_j cy+(b+c_j c²-b_j)/W. Size of every coefficient is O_c(W), and every difference has degree 2. We can take y in [M0] with M0=floor(T/W)-1 so w<=T, losing nothing for the lower bound. For every y there and N large, |R_j(y)|<=n/32 on choosing tau small depending only on max|c_j|; this will prevent wrap for nonzero contributions.

For clarity the following are the specific Tao–Ziegler parameters (preprint numbering 2.5,3.1,3.9,4.1, respectively published 9,11, Cor.3,13). Take d=d0=2, one step variable and k functions. In Thm 13 the required fine-scale norms use M1=floor(M0/A), box orders bounded in terms of k and polynomials of degree exactly 1 and coefficients O(A^C), with C depending only on k. Choose integer B0>C+2; choose cube order a sufficient for every resulting bounded concatenation; then rho sufficiently small for both levels and set A=W^(1/rho), A'=A^B0, rho'=rho/B0, so A'=W^(1/rho'). Here M0~tau sqrt(n/W) and M1~tau sqrt(n/W)/A, in particular n^0.49<=M1 and M1<=sqrt n at each frozen v for n sufficiently large. For v large M1>=sqrt n/A^3. In particular the necessary coarse lower bound log^(1/rho)n and upper bound A sqrt n hold, and the model fine-scale lower bound log^(1/rho')n and upper bound A' sqrt n hold. The bounded concatenation needs n>=M1²/A', also true. The dense model test step length J=floor((A')^(-2a) M1²) is between n A^(-2B0a-7) and n A^(-2B0a) and its steps q are at most (A')^a. Thus the linear cube estimate applies. The functions f are nonnegative bounded by the ordinary prime weights truncated at n^rho and at n^rho' since I is interior; m and centered factors obey the bounds for Thm 13.

For each local norm in Thm 13 use Cor.3 at (rho',A',M1) to obtain an O_{rho',k}(1)-bounded model f' with f-f'=o(1) in both that averaged polynomial-box norm and all these global step-q Gowers norms. Apply the triangle inequality and section 1 to f'-m; its global norms are o(1). Divide by a fixed constant depending on rho' and k to make it 1-bounded. Thm 9 converts uniform smallness of its global norms to smallness in the required averaged local norm; its required smallness threshold is independent of A'. Use Cor.3 again to return to f-m. Thm 13 now makes each cyclic pattern containing at least one centered factor o(1); each remaining factor is bounded by ordinary prime weight +1. The o(1)'s here denote the usual sufficiently slow joint limit v(n)→∞ of TZ: at every frozen v there are finitely many b,c,q and the thresholds are chosen to satisfy all linear tests; one can take the successive limsup or diagonal. The number of coarse forms and expansion terms is fixed. This reasoning uses the **arbitrary dominated function** dense-model conclusion, not the special model 1 for the ordinary prime weight. There is no requirement of quantitative Chebotarev uniformity as the ray conductor grows.

**3. Lifting back and normalization.** The mean product on the torus is nonnegative; restrict x to [11n/16,13n/16], length n/8+O(1), where all x+R_j lie in I. Conversely any nonzero prime-weight product on I has x genuinely in [N/(2W),N/W-O(1)] and no wrapping because |R_j|<=n/32. Therefore the actual prime sum dominates this portion of the model main term minus the replacement error. For each unit tuple of b_j we multiply normalized counts back by (W/phi(W))^k. Upon averaging over b,c mod W, the total multiplier of uniform replacement errors is bounded by prod_{p|W} b_p=O_c(1), not by a growing power of log v. At fixed v, averaging x in the central interval and y in [M0] is equidistributed modulo D; the means are just mu_j evaluated at u+c_j w². Full averaging b,c mod W and x,y mod D gives the uniform distribution of u,w mod lcm(W,D) (one can use WD equally). Hence the main normalized lower bound is at least (1/8) (D/phi(D))^k E_{u,w mod D} prod mu_j(P_j) prod_{p|W,p not dividing D} b_p. Floors and endpoint errors vanish at frozen v. Sending v to infinity proves the weaker stated factor 1/10. This also justifies taking tau fixed arbitrarily small after fixing the coefficients. It does NOT yield an error term uniform in independent shrinking tau or increasing K.

**4. Consequence upon the companion audits.** For each fixed cutoff L freeze its Gaussian ray field and conjugation-stable allowed set, choosing the normalized generator 9 mod16, 1+3i mod7, i mod M and no small neighbouring-norm factors. The separate RAY-LOWER-FACTOR audit shows S >= c_data (log L)^(-4k). The separate Gaussian-tail audit bounds the log-weighted contribution of all remaining bad tuples in the same N/2<=u<=N, 1<=w<=tau sqrt N window by C_data tau N^(3/2)/(L log L) + O_tau(N^(3/2-1/800)). Choose L so large that the latter leading constant is less than half of c_data(log L)^(-4k)/10, then freeze L. Take fixed tau>0 sufficiently small for all contrast/ratio thresholds. Lower order N remainders then vanish. This gives arbitrarily large values in the manuscript simultaneous-prime theorem, with all same-/different-sign odd common divisors excluded across distinct positions, as well as opposite signs within a position. Replace c_j by q²c_j and w by qw to force any prescribed q|w. A repeated coefficient is a single position with retained row incidences, not a second distinct prime. Neither a pair of rational elliptic poles nor the entire quartic 318 theorem is claimed. The different ray in the earlier Gaussian squarefree packet has a different dyadic profile; do not merge the ray conclusions.
