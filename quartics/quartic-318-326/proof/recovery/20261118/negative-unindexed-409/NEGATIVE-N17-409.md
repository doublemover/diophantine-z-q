# A direct negative-anchor obstruction at the same prepared rational as the positive obstruction

Let β=17, θ=409, N=βθ=6953 and b=(204/1073)². Work on the **negative** elliptic anchor E': y²=x(x−9N)(x+3N), i.e. the curve for parameter −3N, with root order (0,9N,−3N). The statement here is that no point on E'(Q) has first descent coordinate [17] (including the ordinary torsion exceptions). It is NOT a general rank-zero claim and does not rely on PARI.

**Step 1: exact local groups and the two lifts.** Use full rational-two-torsion descent coordinates [x], [x−9N], with the usual value at a root equal to the product of its differences from the two other roots for the vanishing coordinate. At infinity use (1,1). At p outside 2,3,17,409 all roots and differences are p-integral with unit differences. If x has negative valuation then it is even, by the cubic, and otherwise at most one of the differences has positive valuation and that valuation is even. Thus any rational image has signed squarefree coordinates supported on these primes. On this good locus the complete local Kummer image consists of the four pairs of unit squareclasses: its size is four and it is contained in those pairs by the same valuation argument. At the real place and 2,3,17,409 the checker obtains local images by evaluating the two coordinates at true local points (exact rational x with square cubic in Qp or R, and all root conventions). The number of distinct pairs reaches 2 at infinity, 8 at two, and 4 at each odd prime. These are upper bounds by the usual local index formula for an elliptic curve with full rational E[2]: the kernel has size four and on a sufficiently small formal group multiplication by two has index |2|_p^(−1), while at infinity E(R) has two components and doubling is onto the identity component. Thus the computed set is complete, independent of the older special-purpose matrix. Scanning the 32 possible second coordinates for given first coordinate [17] yields exactly (17,3),(17,1227). The pair D=(1,409) is another everywhere locally soluble Selmer class, and these candidates are A and A+D for A=(17,3).

**Step 2: exhibit a nonzero Cassels pairing.** For D the three squareclass coordinates are (1,409,409). Writing x=(409a/t)², the projective cover is the intersection given by c²−b₁²=204t², c²−409a²=51t², b₁²−409a²=−153t². Here b₁ is a projective coordinate, not the prepared rational b. The three tangent conics omit a,b₁,c respectively. On them use the respective rational basepoints (b₁,c,t)=(1,1,0), (a,c,t)=(1,35,4), (a,b₁,t)=(1,16,1). The corresponding primitive linear forms are L1=c−b₁, L2=35c−409a−204t, L3=16b₁−409a+153t. Rescaling a tangent by a fixed nonzero rational changes its global product by 1. The projective first tangent is legitimate (or replace it with any finite rational tangent using the conic parameter and Hilbert reciprocity). By Cassels' tangent formula for the split-cubic two-descent (Shukla 2024, equations 3.1.3–4 and Theorem 3.3.7, https://epub.uni-bayreuth.de/id/eprint/8348/1/thesis.pdf), pair these forms against the ordered three entries (17,3,51) of A.

At 2,3,17 take the actual local projective point a=1,b₁=√409,c=−√409,t=0. Choose the square roots via the residue witnesses in REPLAY.json. Their residues determine all three tangent squareclasses; for 2 the actual lift from a solution mod 2^18 is within 2^17 and the displayed precision comfortably suffices. None of the three L_i vanishes at the point. The Hilbert triples are (+,+,−), (+,−,−), (+,−,−) respectively. At 409 all of 17,3,51 are square units. The cover has a point there (e.g. t=1,a=0 with b₁²=−153,c²=51); any tangent zero can be avoided by a nearby point on the smooth local cover. At infinity the second arguments are positive, so all three symbols are +.

At every other finite p, choose primitive integral local homogeneous coordinates away from tangent zeros (local solubility was proved in Step 1). Each omission triple remains primitive: the two remaining square equations and p∤2·3·17·409 imply that divisibility of three coordinates by p forces that of the fourth. Each tangent conic is smooth over Zp, and the chosen tangent basepoint and line primitive; their reductions give a nonzero tangent with multiplicity two. The relative Cartier divisor of that primitive line on the conic is twice the section (over the generic fibre by tangency and degree two, with no vertical component because the smooth irreducible special conic is not contained in the line). Its nonzero evaluation at another integral section has even p-valuation. Since 17,3,51 are p-units, all three local symbols are +. No denominator-prime exception remains.

The global pairing is therefore −1. Rational Kummer images are in the radical, so A is not a rational image. Also D has zero self-pairing, since the elliptic Cassels–Tate pairing for the canonical principal polarization is alternating ([O] is a rational polarizing divisor); thus ⟨D,A+D⟩=−1 rules out the only other possible lift. This proves the asserted negative-anchor obstruction for precisely the same b,β,θ as the separate positive-anchor construction. For the literal PointPair y1²=F_E(B), y2²=F_(EV)(B S²) with B=βu²≠0, V=θv²≠0, S≠0, a vanishing y1 forces E=B or E=−B/3. In the latter case EV=−3N(uv/3)²; dividing coordinates of the second point by (uv/3)² and (uv/3)³ gives an E' point with first coordinate 9β(S/v)², contradicted above (the x=9N torsion value would require θ square; x=0 and x=−3N also impossible). In the former case the same scaling with uv gives a point on E_N with first coordinate class β, equivalent to class θ by adding its rational (N,0), whose first coordinate is N. This is ruled out by the separate positive-anchor proof. Vanishing y2 reduces to vanishing y1 by switching the pair: E_new=EV/S², S_new=V/S, y1_new=y2/S³, y2_new=y1(V/S)³, at identical B,V. Thus at this specific prepared b no PointPair can have a zero ordinate, whereas the two-seed construction explicitly realizes a pair there. No all-index original result, actual selected pole, or Lean acceptance follows.
