# A genuine parameter modification for the 108 remaining odd-last cases: resolve its whole boundary and remove one radical on the other chart

Take the original contacts b=(0,32,76832,−343). This note treats exactly the 96 genuinely mixed-parity first-three types with q₃=a=±1, and the 12 all-even first-three types with mixed signs and fourth a=±1. Other odd-last types were handled separately. All claims transport to their independently dyadically unramified coefficient variations. The construction is **not** an identification of a base parameter ideal with the singular-point ideal: it is an alternative explicitly defined proper birational model of the original arithmetic normalization.

On the parameter line blow up the ideal (4,T) (extended as the unit ideal at infinity), with true affine charts T=4t and TR=4; both follow by scale cancellation over the DVR polynomial ring. Normalize this new proper surface in the original function field. It is finite over the base blowup by excellence, proper over the original line, and maps to its original normalization X_q by integrality in the same field; this map is birational and proper, unchanged away from (2,T). The two charts overlap where tR=1. At all originally unaffected dyadic points the old model was regular for the listed 108 types. We normalize and resolve the **entire missing boundary** of the finite t chart, and show that on that chart only a concrete three-radical arithmetic genus-one model remains. Generic smoothness and the auxiliary parameter-étale changes below concern genuine localizations near the dyadic fibre, not unconditional étaleness at horizontal characteristic-zero branches.

## On T=4t: a common étale comparison in both directions

Write c_i=b_i/4 (i=0,1,2), so c=(0,8,19208). Put z_i=r_i/2 in the generic field; z_i²=q_i(t−c_i) and z_i are integral on this new affine. The fourth has r₃²=343a(1+4t/343). Adjoin ρ=1+2h, h²+h=t/343, finite étale near the dyadic fibre, and invert ρ there. Then r₃/ρ=α, α²=343a; conversely r₃=αρ. This identifies the normalized pullback with the normalizations over the local maximal orders of this constant algebra of the three-radical field, with the same parameter-étale adjunction. The generic field comparison follows by the explicit reciprocals, the distinct simple branch valuations for each z_i, and the fourth branch t=−343/4 present in the parameter-étale field but not among the other three; the latter parameter field is a geometrically integral rational quadratic function field (t=343h(h+1)). Normalization commutes with étale base change. The remaining generic degree-eight cover of the rational line with three finite branch points and infinity has genus one: all four geometric ramification inertia groups have order two, giving 2g−2=−16+4·4=0. This does not identify it with its Jacobian or presume a rational section.

We can, on either side, adjoin β²=−343 via (1+β)/2, an unramified coefficient comparison at 2 (indeed −343≡1 mod8); use either dyadic localization. If a=−1, α=±β and the three-root base is just the unramified coefficient DVR on either label. If a=+1, i=α/β has square −1; its local maximal order is Gaussian with uniformizer π=1+i, π²=2i and 2=ηπ², η=−i. These are constant factors **inside the parameter-étale pullback**, not the result of descending regularity through a ramified constants extension. Normalizing a candidate three-root order is an outstanding calculation in this note.

## On TR=4: normalized factors at the only new boundary T=R=0

All R≠0 points are already on the finite t chart. All points T≠0 on this side retain original regularity. Around the corner adjoin the unit roots s_i=1+2h_i of 1−(b_i/4)R, i=1,2, by h_i²+h_i=−b_i R/16, integral étale near 2. The r_i/(r₀s_i) square to q_i/q₀. The parameter change R=4/T is birational; the same two branch-ratio Kummer fields and independent remaining branch valuations as in MIXED240 give this precise decomposition. Also apply the unramified β comparison and localize above each dyadic prime; any splitting gives equivalent étale factors. In the mixed parity cases K=Q(√(±2)) or Q(ζ₈); in the all-even mixed-sign cases K=Q(i). Write 2=ηπ^e with e=2,2,4 respectively for the quadratic, Gaussian and quartic fields (quartic has e=4); use the uniformizers in MIXED240. In √(±2) choose π=√(±2), so η=±1. ### μ has a square root in the chosen coefficient DVR

This holds for all K if a=−1 by β, and also for a=+1 whenever K contains i (Gaussian or quartic), using iβ. Choose b²=μ a unit. Add Y=(A−b)/U, integral by Y²+b VY=λ; its special value over the corner is a unit. Thus V=(λ−Y²)/(bY), and the *exact* remaining surface is
    U(λ−Y²)=2bY .
The inverse A=b+UY and this V formula recover every ratio. This hypersurface over O[U,Y,Y⁻¹] is in the actual field by the generic rational substitutions; π-torsion-freeness follows also from its nonzero reduction modulo π, which permits cancellation. The following local finite enlargements suffice.

If λ is square modulo 2, choose g∈O with g²≡λ modπ^e and put p=Y−g. The √(±2) cases have λ=±1; in the quartic case η modπ⁴=(1+π)² modπ⁴ by MIXED240, and c₀ is a sign times a power of η. Thus this square approximation exists. With C(p)=(g²−λ+2gp)/π^e one gets
    U p²=π^e (−η bY−U C(p)).
The parenthesis is a unit at the corner. As e is even, add Z=π^(e/2)/p, integral by Z²=U/(−η bY−U C). Now pZ=π^(e/2) and
    U=−η bY Z²/(1+C(p)Z²) .
Conversely this expression and the displayed inverses recover all generators on a neighbourhood of the sole p=Z=0 corner, the denominator a unit there. This proves the new ring is the ordinary node chart pZ=π^(e/2) in the same field, finite over the old localized model. The chart is normal (hypersurface, only possible nonregular point is closed); hence it is the actual normalization there. It is regular already when e=2, while e=4 has smooth tangent conic pZ=π², resolved by one ordinary point blowup. All possible primes above the corner have these p=Z=0 values by the displayed equations, so none is omitted.

The remaining Gaussian case has p₀=1, λ=±i not a square modπ². Take p=Y−1, t=λ−Y², which in the regular local O[Y] has a unit π-linear term since vπ(λ−1)=1 and 2∈(π²). Thus t,p are regular parameters. In the DVR modulo t, π lies in (p²), and p²+2p=λ−1 gives π of exact order two in p. Lift π=B t+D p², D unit; B,D depend only on the regular base. In the regular ambient with extra U the exact equation U t=η bY π² becomes
    [U−η bY(B²t+2BDp²)] t=η bY D² p⁴ .
The bracket is a true parameter replacing U (unit slope) together with t,p. This hypersurface is already normal with one isolated singularity, settled by two ordinary point blowups of XY=unit·p⁴. All scaled charts follow by the regular ambient divided-equation test, including the exponent-two conic.

### a=+1 and K=Q(√(±2)): μ not extracted in O

Here e=2, η=±1, λ=±1. Use b=β with b²=−μ. Put Z=(A−b−U)/π, integral by the monic equation
    F(Z)+U G(U,Z)=0,
    F=Z²+ηπ b Z+η b²,   G=ηπ Z+[(1−λ)/π²]U+η b .
All coefficients are integral and G is a unit at the corner. Before adding V this single equation in O[U,Z] defines a regular two-dimensional base with parameters π,p=Z−b there: modulo π the U derivative is ηb, and the residue Z equals 1. The saturated chart algebra including V is the hypersurface F(Z)V=−η G π² over that regular base (use U=−F/G, UV=2). The term ηG is a unit from the base, so division rescales the parameter V by a unit if necessary. It is the actual field presentation: after inverting π and the relevant units all substitutions are reversible; the equation is nonzero modulo π in the regular residual local polynomial ring because F modulo π has exact order two in p there. Thus π is a nonzerodivisor and one gets the true subalgebra without hidden vertical component.

At π=p=0, F(p+b) has a nonzero π-linear term ηπ b² since (1+η)b² is divisible by π². Thus t=F,p are regular parameters. Modding out t yields a DVR with π∈(p²) and the exact relation gives π of order exactly two (p² survives, the other terms of possible order two are the unit-linear π). Writing π=B t+D p² with D unit gives Vt=unit·π², hence after shifting/rescaling V precisely XY=unit·p⁴ as in the Gaussian calculation. This chart is normal by isolated singularity and hypersurface S₂, hence after adjoining integral Z gives the correct finite normalization locally, with two ordinary point blowups resolving it. All other directions of those two blowups are regular.

The curve-node and conic blowups used here are at the whole reduced set of the indicated corner singularities or their successors, independent of the chosen chart-étale labels. They descend along the parameter and coefficient étale covers (and normalization commutes therewith). Thus the modification of X_q has resolved all boundary points T=R=0 and leaves the finite t chart unchanged (tR=1 excludes R=0). Counts here are per normalized factor, not assertions that all factor-labels are independently rational on the original base. Only the genus-one three-root problem on T=4t remains for these 108 in this note; no complete resolution, semistability, smooth fibre or Lean acceptance is inferred here.
