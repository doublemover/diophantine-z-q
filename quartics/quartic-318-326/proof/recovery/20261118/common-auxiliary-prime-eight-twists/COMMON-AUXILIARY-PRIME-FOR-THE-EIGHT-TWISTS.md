# One eligible prime after fixing all four original pairs of twists

The degree-24 p=5 class-group test does **not** apply at a twist ramified at 5. For example the scalar first class for integer input a=0 is β=17 times the squarefree part of 11·integerR(0), with integerR(0)=4·5·163·661·733·1489·1861. Both 5 and 11 divide β, so in the squarefree support construction they divide n as well. They cannot be used as good primes there. This is an example at that specific integer input, not the scalar first class at every input. Here is a uniform alternative which does not guess any of the indexed numbers.

**Fix the project variables first.** At the same original pole tuple b_i (i∈Fin 4) take its actual classes C_i with C_i.first_class: 17b_i=β_i c_i² and C_i.second_class: (17b_i)²(1−b_i/17)=θ_i e_i². In the original simultaneous construction n_i is the product of the restricted final support and n_i θ_i is the product of the full final support of that *same* output; each is squarefree and positive by injectivity of its prime list. Work with the exact eight curve parameters D={n_i,n_i θ_i}, allowing coincidences. Retain the established numerical hypotheses on every curve (finite E(Q)/2 of order ≤8 and all-places Sel₂ of order exactly 8) and the actual two MordellWeilControls per pole. A cardinality of an unbound signed space without its actual-comparison theorem would not suffice.

**Unconditional common-prime lemma.** Choose one rational prime p≡5 mod 28 larger than every member of D using Dirichlet (gcd(5,28)=1); one can more economically use *any* prime not dividing those integers and congruent to 5,13,17 mod 28. The least eligible prime is therefore computable from a concrete tuple, even without factoring large n_i. The source component constructs such a prime (the first progression, not necessarily the least) for the eight actual natural parameters; it does not compute their values nor assume an odd-Selmer theorem. Repeated parameters cause no problem. Choosing p after D avoids the error of assuming one named prime can be good for all possible source assignments.

For any chosen p in these progressions, p≥5, p≡1 mod4, and (−7/p)=(p/7)=−1. On every E_d, d∈D, reduction at p is good. At that prime the full rational two-torsion injects on reduction, so 4 divides #E_d(F_p). This excludes trace zero since p+1≡2 mod4. By Hasse, |a_p|≤2√p<p, hence p is ordinary. Moreover p cannot divide this group order: a positive multiple of 4p would exceed p+1+2√p for p≥5. The formal kernel at p is torsion-free on pZ_p (formal logarithm); hence #E_d(Q_p)/p=p.

On E_1 Frobenius at 7 has trace 0 and determinant 7. Its characteristic polynomial has nonsquare discriminant −28 mod p, proving irreducibility on F_p. Quadratic twisting is scalar, so this applies to every E_d, even with 7|d. The square of any Frobenius lift at 7 acts as −7I on E_d[p], a nonidentity scalar for every eligible p. We have therefore discharged the local and residual hypotheses of the published odd good-ordinary converse on all eight, with no imported pointwise 2-converse.

**A number field attached to each true twist.** For each d∈D choose any nonzero F_p-covector λ of V_d=E_d[p] and take K=Qbar^Stab(λ); degree at most p²−1. Each strict Selmer class trivial locally at p injects as an unramified character of its S-ideal class group, where S consists of primes dividing 6dp and may include other good q with p∤#E_d(F_q). Thus

    dim Sel_p(E_d/Q) ≤ 1 + dim_Fp Cl(O_(K,S_K))/p.

Here are the checks for general p, rather than simply invoking the separate degree-24 computation. The map c↦λ∘c|_(G_K) targets homomorphisms to *constant* F_p. If its value vanishes, the span of conjugates of λ annihilates c|_(G_{Q(E_d[p])}) and spans the full dual by irreducibility. The original cocycle descends to the finite image on V_d, whose H¹ is zero because the central scalar −7I−I is invertible (compare values of c on zg and gz with z=−7I). At 2 the additive potentially good component bound 4 and connected special fibre size 2 give a zero local p-image; at 3 type I₂ or additive gives component size ≤4 and smooth identity-component size in {2,3,4}. At any remaining q|d the ramified quadratic twist of a good representation has −I on inertia and hence no local p-torsion. The formal q-adic subgroup is pro-q and local [p]-index equals the p-torsion order when q≠p. At good q≠p Kummer classes are unramified and zero if p∤#E_d(F_q). Thus each evaluated strict character is unramified everywhere, trivial on all decomposition groups above S; global class field theory gives the class-group bound. The p-local Kummer image has dimension one, already checked. This works also for a rational twist whose signed squarefree representative is used instead; retain exactly the rational isomorphism and its index in that case.

These fields are effectively specified without assuming transitivity for every p. With V_d viewed as the geometric p-torsion, form the finite étale Q-algebra of pairs (P,ζ) with P∈V_d nonzero and ζ primitive of order p. In the model y²=f_d(x) take equations ψ_p(x)=0, y²=f_d(x), Φ_p(ζ)=0. Take invariants under (P,ζ)↦(aP,ζ^a) for a∈F_p^× via the group law. Its geometric points are the covectors, through λ(Q)=log_ζ(e_p(Q,P)), and the invariant algebra has dimension p²−1. Any field factor is a valid K; a point field using μ_p-valued characters instead is NOT the same argument. For p=5 the preceding packet gives one explicit degree-24 polynomial. This general construction need not be computationally small.

**Exact remaining finite condition and consumer.** For every d∈D it suffices to certify dim Cl(O_(K_d,S_d))/p≤1 for at least one of its covector fields, or directly dim Sel_p(E_d)≤2. The auxiliary sets S_d may differ as permitted. The same p works for the eligibility hypotheses; this does not imply it works for the unknown eight upper bounds. Under those bounds Dokchitser–Dokchitser parity and the Burungale–Castella–Skinner good-ordinary p-converse give analytic rank 1, and Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin give the infinitude needed by ActualPreparedFourRankConsumer318.from_classes for both twists at every index. That constructor takes the same C_i,n_i and realizes each original b_i via its retained β_i. This is not a rank assertion for ANY unspecified d: we have produced the common prime and a concrete finite kind of test, **not those class-group certificates**, the original 15 prime values, the shared h/CRT data or the completed pole tuple.
