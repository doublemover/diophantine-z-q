# Two genuine rational points with a common mark off by at most five fresh primes

Fix **the unchanged pole** b from any one of the four linked prepared source rows. Put B=17b=βv², D=1−b/17=θz², V=B²D, with β,θ positive squarefree, v,z nonzero. We use β square locally at 2 and all θ primes, θ square locally at 3 and all β primes, 17|β and 17∤θ; these follow respectively from source preparation and the 408 contact ray as in the earlier indexed free-rank packet. For general finite places away from 6βθ no special residue is needed. We may assume θ>1: if D is square the direct equal-gain square-complement construction already gives a literal both-nonzero pair with Δ=1. For infinitely many twist classes in this special case take x=β, e=(s²−β)/(s²+3β), n=βe: since (1−e)(1+3e)=16βs²/(s²+3β)² the ordinate is nonzero rational and the scaled partner is identical. Weak approximation does this successively, avoiding the finite supports of all previous classes; both points have infinite order by the 17-adic argument below.

**Result.** For any finite set of forbidden primes T containing the divisors of 6βθ, there are infinitely many distinct common twist squareclasses n on which both original curves E_n and E_{nV} have rational non-torsion points. Their abscissas represent the SAME rational squareclass [βΔ], where Δ is positive squarefree, has at most **five** prime divisors, all outside T, and is a square in every Qp with p∈T. This is a bound on the single common global error, not two unrelated local-point assertions. One may apply it independently on all four fixed linked poles, even exclude all primes in the preceding defects at the later rows. A literal realization requires Δ=1; the almost-prime theorem does not imply that, nor require that any one of these Δ is known numerically for the enormous indexed scalar.

**Exact points first.** With t∈Q nonzero and 0<q=θt²<1 set

**Removing contributions at the forbidden primes, not assuming primitive sieve output.** Let m_i be the positive T-part of |F_i(U)|. Their product is an integer square by even valuations of the local product; both m_i are odd. Complete U to a determinant-one integral basis (U,W). Put

    Q_i(X,Y)=s F_i(UX+LWY)/m_i,

where L is supported on T with all its p-adic orders arbitrarily large and s∈{±1} is the SAME sign for both i. Taking each order much larger than v_p(m_i) ensures integral coefficients in the pattern a_i X²+2b_i XY+c_iY² (including the halves of the mixed coefficients), and both a_i=s F_i(U)/m_i are units at every p∈T. Before choosing s, the two leading coefficients have the same residue modulo four because F_i(U)≡1 and m1m2 is an odd square. Choose s to make both 1 mod4. Negative individual forms cause no sign change in their product. The forms are primitive: at T this follows from the leading unit, outside T from the integral invertible basis change and the original unit diagonal coefficients up to factors in T. They are irreducible (θ positive nonsquare and −3θ negative), with distinct roots hence nonzero resultant, and both constant-direction coefficients c_i are nonzero since W is a nonzero rational vector. Their discriminants are multiplied by L²/m_i² relative to the original forms, so every p in T divides the discriminants if L is sufficiently deep.

Apply Marasingha, [On the representation of almost primes by pairs of quadratic forms](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa124-4-3), Acta Arithmetica 124 (2006), Theorem 1.1. His nonzero exceptional integer is E=6 Res(Q1,Q2) a1a2 c1c2 disc(Q1)disc(Q2). At p|E in T the vector (1,0) gives two units. At p|E outside T there are at most four bad projective lines because the original quadratics remain nonzero modulo p and the basis transformation is invertible. As p≥5, at least one good line exists. CRT gives one z0 modulo |E| realizing those good residues, choosing (1,0) at T. The proof actually counts in the lattice coset Ψ=z0 mod E (equation (2), §3, not a new fixed-congruences extension of the theorem). At p∈T that forces X a unit, and replacing U by UX+LWY leaves the local ratio slope arbitrarily close to that of U when v_p(L) was chosen deep; hence all the desired LOCAL class assertions survive, not just the parity of valuations. Choose a small closed box with interior around (1,0) in the real (X,Y)-plane, so the resulting slope stays inside 0<q<1; dilating the box does not change the slope interval. The stated quantitative result, in that coset and region, yields ≫ H²/(log H)^O(1) lattice points of size O(H) where Q1Q2 is a positive P5 (prime factors counted with multiplicity), none dividing E. The coarse log-power lower bound follows equally from its displayed Euler product outside the finite exceptional primes, with dimension bounded by four. A fixed projective slope accounts for only O(H) lattice points; therefore infinitely many distinct slopes occur. The original product differs from Q1Q2 by the square m1m2, so its positive squarefree representative Δ has at most five prime divisors, all outside T. Distinct n classes occur as well: n(t)=(2+θt²)(3+θt²)(1−θt²) has six distinct simple geometric roots. In a fixed squareclass the resulting squarefree sextic defines a smooth genus-two curve, which has only finitely many rational points by Faltings.

There is no torsion shortcut here. The true 17-adic condition for these n and nV is odd valuation. In their rational-square-equivalent integral squarefree models each has no point of order four since at each nonidentity root a difference with another root has odd 17-adic valuation. Furthermore the split curve E_1 has no rational cyclic subgroup of order three, nor do its quadratic twists: the x-coordinate divided by g of such a point would be a rational root of 3T⁴+8T³−18T²−9, with no projective zero mod5. By Mazur's rational torsion theorem, full rational two-torsion plus neither 3- nor 4-torsion leaves only the four rational roots/origin. Both displayed points have nonzero ordinate, hence infinite order.
