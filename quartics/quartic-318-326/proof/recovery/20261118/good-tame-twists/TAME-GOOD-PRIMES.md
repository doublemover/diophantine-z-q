# Ramified twists at good-contact odd primes: exact centers and a uniform open

**Result at mathematical/source level.** Let X_q be the original normalized two-chart model for r_i²=q_i(T−b_i), b=(0,32,76832,−343), q_i∈Q×. Let K=Q(√q_i:0≤i<4) and Δ=|disc K|. Combining this calculation with SIGN-DESCENT yields a proper flat regular modification of X_q over **Z[1/gcd(210,Δ)]**, with unchanged twisted generic fibre. In particular the fixed open Z[1/210] works for every twist; individual unramified primes among 2,3,5,7 are covered as well. At p∤210 ramified in K, let I={i:v_p(q_i) odd}, which is nonempty. If |I|=2 or 3 the original normalized model is already regular at p. If I={j} or I contains all four indices, precisely eight geometric ordinary double points occur, over T=b_j or over infinity respectively; blowing up their entire reduced union resolves them. Eight geometric points does not assert eight rational points. No claim about ramified 2,3,5,7, minimality, smooth special fibres or formal acceptance.

## 1. A genuine normalized quotient, not ramified base change of an arbitrary normalization

Fix p∤210 and D=Z_(p). The five horizontal branch sections (four finite and infinity) are disjoint: the nonzero differences between the four finite b_i have prime factors only 2,3,5,7. The untwisted normalized model is smooth over D, a stronger local input than regularity alone. On the finite chart there is at most one vanishing root at every point; eliminate T at that root (any one if all are nonzero), and the other three unit roots give standard étale extensions of a polynomial D-algebra. This verifies smoothness and normality of the raw finite order, hence equality with its normalization in its field. At infinity use the *actual* restricted reciprocal chart: s=1/T, x²=s, y_i²=1−b_i s (1≤i≤3), y_i units after inverting d(s)=∏(1−b_i s). It is étale over a localization of D[x]. These opens cover the normalization.

Multiplying q_i by rational squares replaces it locally by p^{e_i}a_i with e_i=0 or 1 and a_i∈D×. This identifies the same generic twist field and hence the same normalized parameter charts; no integral isomorphism of raw denominator-cleared orders is needed. Make the finite faithfully flat étale extension D→D₁ adjoining roots of all four units a_i (2 and all roots units). Work componentwise, localizing at primes above p if necessary: p remains a uniformizer. If I is empty the field of twist scalars is unramified here, by these same étale unit adjunctions. Conversely, if e_i=1, a quadratic extension with this odd valuation is ramified since its value group contains half an integer. Thus these e_i detect the required ramified cases.

Assume I≠∅. Set D₂=D₁[π]/(π²−p). At primes over p these are DVRs (Eisenstein, generated maximal ideal (π), integral); elsewhere the extension is étale. On the base-changed untwisted model let σ act as π↦−π, u_i↦(−1)^{e_i}u_i, fixing the parameter and D₁. This is an order-two action. The untwisted model after the **ramified** scalar change is still normal here for a specific reason: the established smoothness over D base-changes to smoothness over the regular D₂, thus regular total space. It is finite over the parameter and is the normalization in its base-changed untwisted field (the four linear contacts remain independent by their valuations). This step would not follow just from regularity of an untwisted model at a collision.

On either parameter affine chart the ring of σ-invariants is precisely the integral closure of the D₁-parameter ring in the twisted function field: the functions π^{e_i}u_i times the chosen scalar unit roots are invariant and have the desired squares. Over Frac(D₁)(T), adjoining the ramified constant and all untwisted roots has degree 32 (π gives degree 2; over its constant field the four contacts independently give degree 16); the fixed field of σ has degree 16, equal to the field of the four independent twisted roots. An element of this fixed field integral over the parameter belongs to the upstairs normal ring and is fixed; the converse is immediate. The identifications commute with T↔s⁻¹, so the quotient is the original parameter-preserving twist normalization after **étale** base change to D₁. We did not identify the twisted normalization with ramified base change of X_q. Further flat changes of the parameter ring commute with these invariants since they are the kernel of σ−1 on the finite algebra.

## 2. Enumerate fixed sheets, including multiple minus signs and infinity

Work étale locally over the parameter line over D₁. To mark the signs of unit square roots, adjoin their square roots to the *parameter* ring before splitting the cover. These extensions are finite faithfully flat étale on the respective opens and are given the trivial σ-action, so their sheets are honestly labeled. Where no branch vanishes the untwisted cover separates into sixteen sheets. Every nonzero e acts without fixed labels there. Above a finite branch T=b_j, put t=T−b_j. The other three factors T−b_i are units on an open containing this branch and can be split as above. Over the resulting étale parameter algebra A there are eight sheets, each with ring
  B=A[π,x]/(π²−p, x²−t),  x=u_j.
The free A-basis is 1,π,x,πx. A sheet label consists of the three retained root signs i≠j. A label is fixed by σ iff e_i=0 for every i≠j; since I is nonempty this means I={j}. Then σ acts there by (π,x)↦(−π,−x).

At infinity put t=s and use the ratio chart above. After splitting the three units 1−b_i s on the parameter ring, the eight labels are the signs of y_i=u_i/u₀. Their multipliers are (−1)^{e_i+e₀}. Hence a label is fixed iff all four e_i agree; in the nontrivial case I is the full set. Here x=s u₀ and x²=s=t; its sign is −1. The sheet ring and action are exactly B and the same diagonal involution. These cases exhaust the finite and reciprocal charts. In particular a two-coordinate sign or three-coordinate sign does *not* have any fixed sheet at the five branch sections.

For an exchanged pair of labels, projection from the σ-invariants of B₁×B₂ to B₁ is an isomorphism (inverse b↦(b,σb)); thus the quotient on that orbit is regular, since these are étale base changes of the smooth-over-D₂ untwisted cover. For a fixed label at a branch the invariant ring is
  B^σ = A ⊕ A·(πx) = A[z]/(z²−p t).
Indeed 2 is a unit, so the coefficients of π and x in the free basis must vanish, and the two invariant basis vectors are independent. This is an exact algebra on the whole chart, not only its tangent cone. It is an étale pullback of the node ring N=D[t,z]/(z²−pt) because A is étale over an open of D[t] (translations on finite charts, the original parameter at infinity). Since D₁ is étale over D, this assertion includes the constant extension.

## 3. Resolve the exact singular loci and return to the arithmetic model

At (p,t,z) this node has embedding dimension three and dimension two: in the regular ambient D[t,z] the equation belongs to the square of that maximal ideal. The same holds after any of the local étale extensions at points above it. Away from that closed point N is regular (over t invertible one may use the nonzero linear p term in the ambient at special-fibre points; over p invertible eliminate t; z≠0 implies p,t≠0). Thus the computed charts account for every singular point. To count *geometric* points above the relevant base branch, choose an algebraic closure of F_p with one marking for each adjoining unit root. In this fixed trivialization there are eight distinct invariant sheets, each giving one point over t=0. Marking different lifts of the same geometric base point does not multiply that count; base change to that same geometric point identifies the fibre. For singleton I all other branches, including infinity, have only exchanged pairs, and for full I only infinity contributes. The geometric points form a reduced finite union over the perfect residue field. Independently of the chosen markings it is the reduced nonregular locus over p, hence intrinsic on X_q. Since every point is closed in the finite fibre above the branch, the union is globally closed.

On N the blowup at the reduced maximal ideal (p,t,z) is regular. Its two p and t charts suffice because the Rees directions satisfy Z²=P T. In the p chart write z=pV, t=pV² to get D[V]; p is a nonzerodivisor and inversion restores N[1/p]. In the t chart z=tV gives D[t,V]/(tV²−p); t is a nonzerodivisor (primitive irreducible linear polynomial in t over the UFD D[V], with coefficients V² and p coprime). Inverting t restores N[1/t]. These are the actual ratio subalgebras, not unverified strict transforms. The second chart modulo t is F_p[V], hence regular local rings along the exceptional divisor lift to regular total-space local rings; the first chart is already regular. On the étale charts the pulled-back reduced center is exactly the pullback of (p,t,z), since its residue extension is étale over a perfect field and the same support has just been computed. Flat base change commutes with blowups; étale-local regularity descends. Blow up the reduced union once; away from it the map is an isomorphism.

For the global join, let S=Spec Z[1/gcd(210,Δ)] and use the actual normalized X_q over S. The sign-descent construction over the smaller U=Spec Z[1/Δ] used only finite reduced vertical centers at the retained unramified primes in {2,3,5,7}. They extend successively as the same reduced finite closed points of the corresponding global fibres: the fibres over those rational primes are unchanged on U⊂S, and each is closed in the full scheme over S. Those modifications are identities at each newly added p|Δ, p∤210. Now blow up the finite union of the disjoint singular centers just described at those new primes; omit empty centers. Localizing coefficients at p leaves the affected total-space local rings unchanged and identifies the closed fibres and their reduced centers. Outside these fibres the previous proof applies. The resulting integral modification is proper (normalization finite over the projective line and subsequent blowups projective), flat over S (torsion-free over the localized PID) and has unchanged generic fibre. The recipe is uniform over Z[1/210]; it covers all Z for any twist whose scalar field is unramified at 2,3,5,7. For example q=(13,1,1,1) has Δ=13 and one extra reduced blowup center of geometric size eight at 13. For q=(13,13,1,1) the same discriminant has an exchanged-pair action at 13 and requires no additional blowup there. No smoothness claim for these potentially multiple special fibres.

**Evidence boundary.** Paper/source calculation, not a formalized global theorem. The Lean candidate handles only Boolean fixed-label conditions and basic chart equalities. Its accompanying finite check tests all 15 nontrivial masks and all five geometric branch labels. No assertion about ramified bad-contact fibres or the final Diophantine equivalence.
