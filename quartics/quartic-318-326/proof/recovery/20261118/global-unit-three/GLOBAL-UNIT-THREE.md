# From finite real detection to H³ of the rational algebraic-closure units

**Classical arithmetic reduction; no Lean proof of real detection is asserted.** Let G=Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ) and give A=ℚ̄^× its usual Galois action and discrete topology. For every locally constant inhomogeneous 3-cocycle c:G³→A there exists a locally constant 2-cochain B:G²→A with ∂B=c. This concerns absolute continuous cohomology, not the abstract group cohomology of G, and does not say every 3-cocycle was already a boundary in its original finite Galois field.

**Step 1: retain the actual finite stage and obtain a power boundary.** The compact profinite space G³ and the discrete target give finite image, and c factors in all three arguments through some common finite quotient Q=G/U: cover G³ by finitely many products of opens on which c is constant, refine around points by product cosets of open normal subgroups, take a finite subcover, and intersect the resulting subgroups. Intersect U further with an open normal subgroup fixing all c-values, e.g. for the normal closure of the field they generate. This retains argument factorization and makes the finite field K=ℚ̄^U contain all values with the actual quotient action. The finite descended cochain C:Q³→K^× is closed, by lifting any quadruple and using equivariance. Write m=|Q|>0. In the formulas below products and powers are in the commutative coefficient group; no trivialization of the action is made.

Set S(g,h)=∏_{t∈Q} C(g,h,t). Multiply the 3-cocycle equation over t. The genuine action g distributes over the product; substituting kt for t permutes exactly the same finite index set. One gets ∂S(g,h,k)·C(g,h,k)^m=1, hence ∂(S⁻¹)=C^m. For each of the finitely many pairs choose b(g,h)∈ℚ̄^× with b(g,h)^m=S(g,h)^(-1), possible in the algebraic closure. Inflate b as a *function of the arguments* on G²; the differential must now use the genuine G-action, not a purported action of Q on chosen roots. Still (∂b)^m=∂(b^m)=c^m: the equality follows from pointwise powers, the displayed product equation, and equivariance on K. Thus z=c/(∂b) is an actual μ_m-valued continuous 3-cocycle. Its continuity follows from continuity of the discrete G-module action (each algebraic unit has open stabilizer); b itself is locally constant. No claim that z factors through the original Q is needed.

**Step 2: kill the real class in a finite coefficient enlargement.** For complex conjugation j the action on μ_d is inversion. For the cyclic group C₂, the natural two-periodic resolution gives H³(C₂,M)=ker(1+j)/(j−1)M. For M=μ_d this is μ_d/(μ_d)². The map induced by μ_m↪μ_{2m} on H³ is zero: every element of μ_m has a square root in μ_{2m}. This holds also for even m, when H³(C₂,μ_m) itself can be nonzero. Thus the μ_{2m}-valued class of the *same z* vanishes at the real place.

**Step 3: apply finite-module real detection; take the cochain, then restore the original values.** Apply [Milne, Arithmetic Duality Theorems, I.4.10(c), β defined in I.4.8](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/adt1.pdf) to K=ℚ, S=all places, r=3, M=μ_{2m} with its possibly nontrivial cyclotomic action. It is a finite continuous G-module of order 2m, invertible in the S-integers ℚ; there is just one real place. The localization is injective, so the full μ_{2m} class of z is zero. In the continuous cochain complex this means there exists a locally constant d:G²→μ_{2m} whose coboundary is z, with z included by the literal roots-of-unity inclusions. Include d in A and set B=b·d. Then ∂B=∂b·z=c, and B is locally constant. No Sha or tameness assumption is needed for this **unit-valued** statement; a binary-valued primitive still requires vanishing of the actual binary real class, as in the separate Sha application.

**Status and remaining arithmetic.** The finite-stage averaging, finite coefficient enlargement, real map and final equality are mathematical, using the stated external global duality theorem. They do not supply its implementation in the pinned Lean environment, establish Weil comparison, construct a justified sum of invariants, or prove the quartic rank. A bare appeal to H³(ℚ,ℤ/2)=0 would be wrong: that group has a real obstruction. Applying the finite-module theorem directly to the infinite module A would also be wrong; Steps 1–2 are essential.
