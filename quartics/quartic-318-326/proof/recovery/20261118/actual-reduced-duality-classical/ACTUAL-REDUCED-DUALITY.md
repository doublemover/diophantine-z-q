# Passing published Cassels–Tate duality to the actual reduced primary quotient

**Paper theorem.** Fix a nonzero rational n. Assume the hypotheses of the repository's exact consumer: the rational double quotient is finite of cardinality at most 8 and the all-places image has cardinality 8. Then `ActualReducedDuality n hn` holds by classical Cassels–Tate duality for the specified elliptic curve, and the repository's reduced primary Sha is trivial. The full primary Sha is trivial or isomorphic to one dyadic Prüfer group. The second branch is not ruled out. The classical global result is an external theorem; this is neither a formal proof of Cassels–Tate duality nor an identification of any proposed explicit cochain pairing with it.

**1. Fix the groups, not just their sizes.** The project curve is the smooth Weierstrass curve E: y²=x(x−n)(x+3n) with its rational origin at infinity; its discriminant is 2304 n⁶, nonzero by hypothesis. The full geometric point group with its coordinatewise action is precisely the discrete Galois module E(overline F), at F=Q and at each completion. The project defines continuous full 1-cocycles as the locally constant crossed homomorphisms with that action. Their map into ordinary group H¹ has exactly the principal crossed homomorphisms as kernel (`actual_full_class_zero`); each such boundary is locally constant by `fullBoundary`. Thus its *image*, and not the whole ordinary group H¹, identifies with the classical continuous H¹(F,E). This argument is specific to degree one: coboundaries there use single geometric points, not unrestricted functions on the Galois group.

The restriction is literal precomposition with the chosen restriction of automorphisms, followed by the geometric point map along the corresponding chosen embedding of algebraic closures. It commutes with principal cocycles by `actual_full_restriction_boundary`. By checking the real place and every rational prime, the project's `actualSha` is therefore the classical kernel on continuous H¹ into all completions. At the real place positive-degree Tate and ordinary cohomology agree in degree one. We may choose the project embeddings in the classical definition as well. The subgroup `actualShaPrimary` consists of elements of this group killed by a power of two, not just by two.

**2. The external input and the polarization.** Use [J.S. Milne, Arithmetic Duality Theorems, 2nd ed., I.6.26(a), with I.6.13(a) and the start of §6](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/BSDseminar/refs/MilneADT.pdf). Set K=Q, S=all places, m=2. This S omits no places, contains all the bad and archimedean places, and 2 is invertible in the S-integers Q. This theorem is already stated for the m-primary parts of Sha(E), Sha(E-dual): each radical of the canonical pairing is its corresponding maximal divisible subgroup. No full-Sha finiteness is needed. To identify E with E-dual take its canonical principal polarization from the rational divisor [O]. Alternation, especially at 2, is essential and does **not** follow from antisymmetry alone: here the divisor is rational, so its polarization obstruction vanishes. [Poonen–Stoll, The Cassels–Tate pairing on polarized abelian varieties, §§4–6, Theorem 5 and Corollary 7](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/sha.pdf) gives alternation; equivalently use Cassels' elliptic-curve case recalled in their introduction. Pulling the second slot back under this polarization transports both radicals to the same maximal divisible subgroup of P=Sha(E)[2^∞]. Convert the target Q/Z to the repository's `AddCircle (1 : ℚ)`: the subgroup generated by 1 in Q is exactly the integers; `Multiplicative` changes only notation.

**3. Identify the quotient and descend both variables.** In the stated bounds the repository's `actual_sha_maximal_divisible` proves that its stable subgroup D=`actualShaDivisibleCore` is itself divisible and contains every divisible subgroup of P. Consequently D equals the just-identified arithmetic radicals. This uses both directions of maximality and does not assert that intersections of all power subgroups of arbitrary abelian groups are divisible. Write B for the pulled-back primary pairing. For each x, y↦B(x,y) descends along P→P/D because it kills D; that assignment is still a homomorphism in x since this can be checked at all quotient representatives. Its kernel contains D by the left radical. Descend the first slot. The resulting curried bilinear pairing has value B(x,y) on the classes of x,y. It is alternating, and a class annihilating all classes lifts to an x annihilating all of P, so x belongs to D and its class is zero. This is precisely `ActualReducedDuality` on the actual reduced group, not on a newly proposed Selmer quotient.

**4. What follows and what does not.** The earlier bound gives that P/D is finite cyclic. An alternating pairing on a cyclic group is identically zero, whereas this pairing has trivial radical. Thus P/D is trivial. The repository's `actual_sha_zero_or_pruefer_of_duality` now yields precisely its zero-or-Prüfer alternative under the same three numerical hypotheses. Neither duality nor this argument excludes nonzero divisible Sha. A rank-one rational point, finiteness of Sha, a concrete formula or comparison for an independently constructed local-invariant pairing, and verified Lean acceptance all remain separate.

The supplied quotient component encodes the two-slot factorization and exact radical test; it assumes, rather than declares, the primary pairing with those properties. The separate native H³ Milne consumer specializes the reconstructed canonical class projection and the earlier bounded real-detection theorem. Its only global arithmetic premise is the finite binary real-detection statement, not the Cassels–Tate result used above. These are two distinct uses of published arithmetic duality; existence of a cochain primitive by itself does not prove pairing nondegeneracy.
