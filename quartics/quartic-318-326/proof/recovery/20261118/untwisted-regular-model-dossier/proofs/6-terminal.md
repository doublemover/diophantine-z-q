# Resolving the last dyadic point in the untwisted model

**Claim, at paper/source level only.** The single remaining point P* of DYADIC-OPEN.md (SHA 61169a1417e346b517af2c52d4427af71f337a911ba3f59158cc58093292e052) is resolved by the following sequence of thirteen ordinary closed-point blowups. Together with the earlier explicitly matched finite and reciprocal normalizations, six odd-prime point blowups and the eight disjoint ordinary dyadic nodes, this constructs a proper flat regular model over Z of the original constant-one-twist generic curve. It is not a Lean-compiled global theorem, a minimal/semistable assertion, a treatment of arbitrary twists, or any height/rank/Diophantine conclusion.

Work on the actual normalized finite chart over D=Z_(2), localized near P*, with u=r₀, h=(r₁−u)/2, k=(r₂−u)/2, w=r₃−u−1. The matched presentation and residue field are those of DYADIC-OPEN. Let L=(1+w)u+w−171; L is a unit at P*. Shrink to D(L). There 2L=−w²; in particular 2 is NOT an exceptional direction independent of w. The equations become h²+uh=w⁶/L³ and k²+uk=2401 w⁶/L³, since 19208=8·2401. These are exact in the ring, not binomial approximations. The residue of P* is F₂.

## I. Three ordinary blowups with two genuine charts at each step

On the distinguished w chart after n steps, n=0,1,2,3, write u=wⁿS, h=wⁿA, k=wⁿB. Put Lₙ=(1+w)wⁿS+w−171. The ring is presented with Lₙ inverted by
  w²+2Lₙ=0,    A²+SA=w^(6−2n)/Lₙ³,    B²+SB=2401 w^(6−2n)/Lₙ³.
For n=0 the point to blow up is the given origin. For n=1,2 the only possible remaining point is w=S=A=B=0. Its ideal (w,S,A,B) is the reduced point ideal since 2=−w²/Lₙ and the residue is F₂. At n=0,1,2 its Rees Proj is covered by the w and S charts: multiplying the two quadratic equations by the Rees variable squared shows that if w·t and S·t are in a homogeneous prime then the squares of A·t and B·t are also in it. Indeed 6−2n≥2, and Lₙ is a unit. All degree-one generators would then lie in that prime.

On the w chart set S=wS', A=wA', B=wB' and divide the last two equations by w² to get the displayed formula with n replaced by n+1. On the S chart set w=Sz, A=Sa, B=Sb. Its presentation is
  S²z²+2Lₙ(Sz,S)=0,   a²+a=S^(4−2n) z^(6−2n)/Lₙ³,
  b²+b=2401 S^(4−2n) z^(6−2n)/Lₙ³.
Here Lₙ(Sz,S) means substitution for w in its displayed polynomial; in the right sides the same substituted unit is used. These are the native ratio charts. For either chart, inverting the scale restores the preceding chart after inverting that scale, by the explicit reverse substitutions. The scale is a nonzerodivisor: start in the polynomial ambient ring with Lₙ inverted; impose the scale, then the first equation (modulo scale it is 2 times the unit Lₙ), then the two monic polynomials in the two separate ratio variables. They form a regular sequence. At each prime containing all of them regular sequences permute in the Noetherian local ring, putting the scale last proves nonzerodivisorship there; away from scale it is automatic. This proves injectivity into the localization, and the indicated generators give exactly the ratio subring. On their overlap z is the inverse of S' and a=A'/S', b=B'/S'. Thus the coverage does not invert any exceptional coordinate throughout a chart.

On the S chart modulo S, 2=0 and Lₙ is a unit; both monic equations have derivative 1 in their own variable. This is étale over the regular localized F₂[z], even for n=2 where the right sides need not vanish. The quotient is regular and the scale is a nonzerodivisor, so the original total-space local rings at all these directions are regular. On the w chart modulo w with n+1=1,2 one obtains A(A+S)=B(B+S)=0; for n+1=3 one obtains A²+SA=B²+SB=1. If S≠0 in a residue field, both derivatives are units; regularity again follows first for the divisor chart and then for the total space. If S=0, the first two stages have exactly the indicated F₂ origin. After the third stage there is just the point q: w=S=0, A=B=1 with residue F₂. This is a statement about primes; squared equations are not silently treated as reduced schemes. All points off the previous blowup centers retain their established regularity.

## II. Exact split product in a regular mixed-characteristic ambient ring

Write L=L₃. Near q, A and L are units, and G=L⁻³. Temporarily keep just the first root A: let C be the D-algebra on w,S,A with A,L inverted, w²+2L=0 and A²+SA=G. Here w is a nonzerodivisor by the same regular-sequence test, and C/(w)=F₂[A,A⁻¹], with S=A⁻¹−A. Thus at q's projection C is a two-dimensional regular local ring with parameters w and p=A−1. No formal-coordinate theorem or unramified coefficient field is used.

Introduce d=B/A−1, t=1+G/A², e=d−2400, and v=e+t+4800. Substituting S=(G−A²)/A in B²+SB=2401G and dividing by A² gives precisely d²+t d−2400(t−1)=0. Equivalently
  e v = −2400·2401 = H w¹⁰,    H=75·2401/L⁵ a unit.
The sign and unit follow from 2400=75·2⁵ and 2=−w²/L. Conversely B=A(1+e+2400), so this is an isomorphism of the actual hypersurface presentations, not just the location of its critical point. Use the regular local ambient ring T=C[e] localized at (w,p,e); v is the function just defined, not an additional independent algebraic generator. Its maximal ideal modulo its square has basis p,e,v: since 2∈(w²), we have L≡1+w, G≡1+w, A⁻²≡1 and t≡w mod the square. Hence v≡e+w mod the square. Thus e,v,p form a genuine regular system of parameters for T.

The following order computation, not just tangent-cone information, controls the next two blowups. In the DVR T/(e,v) with uniformizer p, w lies in (p²) by that linear comparison. Therefore 2=−w²/L lies in (p⁴), and t=−4800 gives L³ A²=−1/4801. The odd unit −1/4801 is 1 modulo p³ there. Modulo p³, L≡1+w (the 171 shift and the w³S term cause no error) and A²=(1+p)²≡1+p². Consequently 1≡L³ A²≡1+w+p² mod p³. In particular w has order EXACTLY two in T/(e,v), not merely ≥2. Lift the unit coefficient to T, obtaining

## III. Two crossing-direction blowups and the final eight

Here is the chart test applicable throughout this section. At the closed point of a regular local ambient threefold with parameters X,Y,Z, blow up its maximal ideal and work on its ordinary three affine ratio charts. On the Z chart, for example, coordinates are Z,X/Z,Y/Z. Modulo the scale, the chart is κ[X/Z,Y/Z] (the associated graded of a regular local ring is the polynomial ring on its parameter classes; the exceptional divisor is its Proj). Ambient charts have nonzerodivisor scale; at the origin in the exceptional affine plane the scale and its two ratios form regular parameters. The same holds cyclically in the other charts. For a surface equation with leading order two, substitute and remove the square of the scale. If the resulting equation mod scale is nonzero in that polynomial ring, scale remains a nonzerodivisor modulo the divided equation: from s r=f' a reduce mod s, deduce a is divisible by s and cancel s in the ambient ring. Inverting the scale restores the old original hypersurface where that parameter is inverted; the chart generators give its actual blowup ratio algebra. This proves equality with the ordinary blowup of the surface rather than presuming a general strict-transform comparison. A regular quotient by this nonzerodivisor at a point of the divisor implies regularity of the original local ring (lift regular generators of the quotient maximal ideal and add the scale). Each open overlap is the canonical rescaling of the same ratio coordinates; restriction of an earlier ambient coefficient uses its actual pullback on the new chart.

First blow up the point of T/(ev−Hw¹⁰) at e=v=p=0. In the e and v directions the exceptional equation after division is a linear coordinate (respectively v/e or e/v), giving regularity everywhere in those charts over the center. In the p chart write e=pE₁, v=pV₁. Then w=pZ₁ where Z₁=αE₁+βV₁+γp, and the exact divided equation is
  E₁V₁ = H p⁸ Z₁¹⁰.
Over p=0 only E₁=V₁=0 can fail to be regular; the divisor E₁V₁=0 is regular everywhere else. The remaining ambient local parameters are E₁,V₁,p with residue field F₂. Blow up this point. Its tangent equation is again E₁V₁ since p⁸Z₁¹⁰ has order at least 18 there. The E₁ and V₁ charts have linear exceptional equations as before. On the p chart put E₁=pE₂,V₁=pV₂; then Z₁=pZ₂, Z₂=αE₂+βV₂+γ. The equation is now
  E₂V₂ = H p¹⁶ Z₂¹⁰.
Off E₂=V₂=p=0 the exceptional divisor is regular. At this sole point Z₂ is a unit (γ is a unit, and α,β are pulled back from T). Thus near it the actual equation is XY=p¹⁶D₀ for a unit D₀ in a regular local ambient ring with parameters X=E₂,Y=V₂,p.

For exponent k=16,14,…,4, blow up the origin of XY=pᵏD₀. The X and Y charts have linear exceptional equations; in the p chart the exact equation becomes X'Y'=p^(k−2)D₀ with the pulled-back D₀ still a unit. Only the origin of this chart can remain nonregular. These are seven blowups, reaching exponent 2. The eighth blowup there has exceptional conic XY=δZ² over the residue field, δ the nonzero residue of D₀. It is smooth **in characteristic two**: in the X or Y chart the other ratio appears linearly; in the Z chart X'Y'=δ forces both coordinates nonzero. All three chart equations have nonzero reduction, so the chart saturation and quotient-regularity test above applies throughout, including non-rational points. This completes the thirteen stages 3+2+(7+1). Smoothness here is a property of this last exceptional conic, not a claim that the whole arithmetic special fibre is smooth or reduced.

## IV. From local centres to the actual proper model

At every stage only one residual origin can remain in the full exceptional fibre of the preceding center, and its residue field is F₂. It determines a closed reduced point of the global model: the fibre over the preceding closed point is closed of finite type over a finite field. Its reduced ideal restricts to the displayed maximal ideal, including when coordinates are known only in a local ring. Flat base change along localization of the ambient base at the preceding point recovers the blowup with these centers; fibres agree there and all stalks at points over that closed point are unchanged by this localization. Equivalently these coordinates are regular on a suitable neighbourhood after shrinking at units only. Thus the local checks cover every global point affected by each operation, not a formal completion or just points rational over F₂. Away from these centers the previous model is unchanged. The prior finite and reciprocal comparison had already certified every other dyadic point, all odd primes, and the generic fibre. The composite over Z is proper; the blowups remain integral and preserve the generic fibre, hence dominance and torsion-free (therefore flat) local rings over Z. Its total space is regular everywhere.
