# Opposing-majority quarter models: the common first boundary and all 12 with odd majority

The remaining 24 types after URMATCH have q₃=−1, unequal first-three valuation parity, and opposite signs d and −d at its majority indices j<k. Let ε=0 or 1 be the majority parity and a the minority sign (at m). Use the working s=2^ε t, e=2^ε(0,8,19208), scaling the minority root by 2 if ε=1, just as in URMATCH. The three-radical function field over the u-line (u first working root) is
    y²=−u²+2^r C,   w²=2ad u²+2^l D,   s=e_j+d u² ,
where r=3+ε for j=0 and 8+ε for pair12, l=4+ε. The constants are C=d(e_k−e_j)/2^r, D=a(e_j−e_m)/2^(l−1). For j=0 these are dN and −aN' with {N,N'}={1,2401}; for j=1 these are 75d and a. We will always start from a proper parameter blowup then normalize in this field; this is deliberately not claimed to be the canonical normalized singular-point blowup.

## Common first blowup of the actual parameter (2,u), all 24

The u-line maps by s=e_j+d u² finitely to the s-line including infinity. On it blow up (2,u) and normalize. The 2-chart u=2S reduces the two exponents by two, since y/2 and w/2 are integral there. On the other chart 2=uR, take Y=y/u,Z=w/u, each integral by its square:
    Y²=−1+2^(r−2) C R²,   Z²=2[ad+2^(l−3)D R²].
All points R≠0 are on the 2-chart. Near R=0 put τ=1+Y, τ²=2f, f=τ−1+2^(r−3) C R² a unit. Put h=0 or 1 with h≡2^(r−3)C mod2. Adjoin the integral B=(1+hR)Z/τ−1, with
    f(1+B)²=(1+hR)²[ad+2^(l−3)D R²] .
The difference f minus the right hand side is τ+2J(R) for an integral polynomial J since l≥4 and the indicated reductions coincide mod2. Thus B²=τ times a unit near R=B=τ=0; explicitly fB²=−τ[1+(τ/f)(J+fB)]. Before reintroducing u this is a regular two-dimensional base with parameters R,B: over D[R], adjoin τ and B by unit-leading monics, generically independent since the two original radicals are independent over Q(R); at the residue τ,B=0 only these R,B are needed for the maximal ideal, and 2=τ²/f. It follows that 2=B⁴E with E a unit in this base. The remaining surface is exactly u R=B⁴ E. This finite enlargement contains Y and Z by the displayed inverses; after inverting 2 all substitutions are reversible. The hypersurface presentation over the regular base sits in the stated field, e.g. R is a nonzerodivisor after imposing uR−B⁴E by reduction modulo R in the regular base polynomial ring. It is normal with only the isolated corner node. Hence it is the true finite normalization there. Two ordinary origin blowups resolve its exponent-four product, including every exceptional direction via the regular-ambient chart test. At u≠0 on this boundary R=0 the same expressions are regular (R solved in terms of B); the construction covers the whole dyadic u≠0 locus by localizing there as well. Indeed the base functions at R=0 have the same residue independent of u.

For ε=1 this also resolves the rational parameter t=e_j/2+d u/R. On u,R directions of the first corner blowup one of u/R,R/u exists; on its B side u=B U,R=B V, with UV=B²E. Blow up the crossing once more; again one ratio is available on U,V directions, and on the B direction U=BP,V=BQ, PQ=E a unit so u/R=P/Q. These cover the entire fibre. The 2-chart has t=e_j/2+2d S²; away from the original dyadic u=0 use 1/t=2/s (and at s=∞), or the characteristic-zero formula. Starting with the proper u-model as above, the map is proper and birational to the appropriate normalized finite t-cover on preimage, by the graph criterion and common-field normality. It is unchanged on finite dyadic t≠0: such t can lie only over the resolved corner with u/R unit (e_j/2 is even); on the first exceptional stage the directions with both u,R homogeneous nonzero are impossible by tangent uR=0. At the second stage such directions have B nonzero by its smooth conic and lie on the final B chart. There PQ=E and t=e_j/2+d P/Q; at B=0 the residue of E is fixed, so t≠0 has finite fibres (P²=(t−e_j/2)E/d). Thus the proper birational map to the finite **normal** t-model there is quasi-finite, hence finite and an isomorphism. Points away from the modified dyadic center are already generically or originally unchanged. For ε=0 no extra marked-map check is needed: nonzero t on the original dyadic line maps to u≠0 and we did not blow up there. All further 2-chart operations below for ε=1 map to t=0. We now handle the twelve ε=0 types, leaving ε=1 separately.

## ε=0, j=0: resolve the new central quartic

On the 2-chart after setting Y=y/2,Z=w/2 the equations are
    Y²+S²=2C,   Z²=2ad S²+4D .
On S≠0 put τ=Z/S, f=ad+2D/S² a unit, τ²=2f, and V=Y/S so V²=−1+2C/S². The integral B=(1+V)/τ satisfies f B²−τ B+1−C/S²=0. Put z=S B−(S−1), giving
    f(z+S−1)²−τ S(z+S−1)+S²−C=0 .
On τ=0 the residue z vanishes. At S≠0 the linear τ coefficient at z=0 reduces to S(S−1) (the other terms at z=0 are divisible by 2, including f−ad), with simple root S=1. Hence all points except that simple smooth-conic node are regular here. Its tangent contains τ(S−1) with unit coefficient and z²; all other terms at that point contribute τz or τ² (terms depending on S only are even polynomials), so the conic is smooth. The monic finite ring over the regular (S,τ) base is normal by isolated nonregularity, hence actual; one ordinary blowup resolves. The base is regular by τ=0 regular divisor; no extraction of i required.

Near S=0 instead let τ=Y−S, f=C−S²−Sτ, τ²=2f with f a unit, and b=(1+S)Z/τ−S, integral with
    f b²+2f S b−τ S³+2L=0,
    L=[(C−ad)/2−ad S−(1+ad)S²/2]S²−D(1+S)² .
At a generic dyadic S, b² has independent linear τ term proportional to S³, giving a regular height-one localization; where 1+S,f are units and S≠0 the preceding chart also applies. The unique origin is singular. Thus this free monic over the regular S,τ base gives a true normal order, containing the original roots and with unchanged generic field.

Blow up the reduced origin on τ,S directions and normalize. On τ side S=τP, b=τV (ordinary ratio); write Θ=1+2P(P+1), f=C/Θ, τ²Θ=2C. The quantity e=Z/2=f(V+P)/(1+S) is a unit-slope replacement for V. Its equation is Θ e²=DΘ+K P², K=ad C=aN. Adjoin J=(e−1−P)/τ. After substituting q=P+1, the exact unit-leading equation is
    C J²+τΘ q J+[q(q−1)−C']q²=(D+K)(1−2q)/2,
    C'=D+(K−1)/2,  Θ=1+2P+2P².
Indeed its constant polynomial before the q substitution is Θ[(1−D)/2+P]+[(Θ−K)/2]P². Since K+D=a(N−N'), the RHS is Uτ⁸ with U a unit, constant modulo τ² on any later q=τⁿQ chart. Mod τ the q derivative of the quartic is q², so only q=0,J=0 can be singular on this divisor. On the S side τ=SR,b=SB, f=C−S²(1+R). The native ratio B satisfies f²(1+B)²=(1+S)²[ad f+D R²]. Adjoin J'=(B−R)/S. Its constant coefficient prior to division by S² is f²(1+R)²−(1+S)²(ad f+D R²), divisible by S² using 2=S²R²/f and even C−ad,1−D. The coefficient of J' after division is integral as 2/S∈(S). At S=R=0 the divided constant vanishes in the residue, and its derivative in R is a unit: modulo (S,R²) it is −f(1+R)−ad f, i.e. R (the remaining constant is even). Therefore this side corner is regular. Both finite integral extra scales contain the ordinary ratios; they are monics over the true regular base blowup charts, free and correct after scale inversion. Isolated nonregularity gives R₁/S₂, so exactly the two normalized blowup charts.

At the main origin the quartic is exactly the central cubic terminal form of URMATCH, with the cubic coefficient B₀ replaced by the local unit q−1 (and the leading coefficient C a unit). For clarity, if C' even, write C'=τ²c using 2=τ²Θ/C. After unit division one has shape J²+τ M qJ+(B₀q+τ² B₁)q²=Uτ⁸. The one ordinary then one normalized cycle of GAUSSIAN54 gives J'²+M P J'+(B₀P+B₁)P²=Uτ² on its main chart, with regular nonoverlap sides via B₀ times the side-base parameter. One last ordinary blowup has residue Z²+M̄ QZ+B̄₁Q²=Ū (smooth at Q=0 by Ū≠0), other side étale. If C' odd shift J by q: the q² coefficient is now q(q−1)+C−C'+τΘ=qB₀+τ L with L unit, while the mixed coefficient is τ times a unit. Blow up on τ,q; the q-side R=0 regular, τ-side q=τP,J_shift=τV gives V²+τ M P V+τ(B₀P+L)P²=Uτ⁶. The nonzero simple root in P gives one smooth-conic node. Near P=0 put E=B₀P+L unit and perform three ordinary τ,P_old blowups as in URMATCH: V_n²+τ M QV_n+τ E Q²=Uτ^(6−2n). For n=1,2 only Q=0 continues, each side corner is a smooth conic; at n=3 the side has an additional quadratic R² term but is still smooth. On the final main line V₃=1 and the τ-linear constant after shift is Ē Q²+M̄Q+constant, separable. U is an odd constant times Θ-powers and (1−2q), hence no unforeseen Q dependence at first order. Each simple root resolves by one smooth-conic blowup. This uses actual units, not polynomial equality with the other contact configuration.

## ε=0 pair12

The first 2-chart has Y²+S²=2⁶C, Z²=2ad S²+4D, D=a. For S≠0, τ=Z has τ²=2f, f=ad S²+2D unit. B=(Y−S)/τ satisfies f B²+τ S B+S²=2⁵C. Its residue B=1 and shifting B−1 gives nonzero linear τS, so here all points are regular and this finite monic is the true normalization. Blow up (2,S) on the parameter, taking its normalization; on 2 side S=2s,
    y'²+s²=2⁴C,  z'²=2ad s²+a,   y'=Y/2, z'=Z/2 .
On nonoverlap S side 2=SR one has (Y/S)²=−1+2⁴ C R²; by parameter-étale extraction of the ratio to −1 one gets the true local Gaussian coefficient i (same reversible radical comparison as ODDBRIDGE). Write π=1+i, η=−i. The other root W=Z/S satisfies W²=2ad+D R². Take β²=D=±1 in Gaussian O and adjoin B=(W−βR)/π by its monic B²+ηπβR B=η ad. At R=0, p=B−1 has p²=π times a unit since η ad≡1+π modulo π²; the terms with coefficient 2 or πR do not change that unit there. Hence before S is reintroduced the base is regular in p,R (finite free monic over O[R], 2=unit p⁴). Adding S R=2 gives the exact normal product S R=unit·p⁴ in a regular ambient, resolved by two ordinary blowups as in the common boundary above.

On this second 2 chart with s≠0 use τ=y'−s, τ²=2f, f=−s²−sτ+8C unit; B=(z'−1)/τ is integral with f B²+τ B=(a−1)/2+ad s². Let k∈{0,1} reduce (a−1)/2 and put v=sB−(s+k). The equation is f(v+s+k)²+τs(v+s+k)−s²[(a−1)/2+ad s²]=0. Its residue v=0; for s≠0 the τ-linear term in its constant reduces to s(s+k)(s+k+1). The other polynomial in s alone has all coefficients even so contributes no τ-linear term. This has its only nonzero root s=1, simple, yielding a smooth conic v²+τ(s−1)+λτv+μτ² after scaling. Thus this normal monic's one node resolves, with all other s≠0 regular.

At s=0 for a=−1 let π=z'−1, f=−1−π−d s², so π²=2f and the base π,s is regular. B=(y'−s)/π is integral, with fB²+πs B+s²=8C. Shift v=B−s; then f v²+π(1+π)s v−d s⁴=Cπ⁶/f³ exactly. The repeated generic vertical square in this expression alone would not prove normality; an explicit generic test is given below. Use its reduced point blowup followed by normalization at the origin as follows, without presuming the preceding order normal. On π side s=πP,v=π²V get f V²+(1+π)P V−dP⁴=Cπ²/f³. P≠0 étale, P=0 sole origin. On s side π=sR,v=s²Z get f Z²+R(1+π)Z−d=C s²R⁶/f³; at s=R=0, Z=1 and the R derivative is a unit. The monics give actual normalizations of the ordinary ratios by saturation and isolated bad locus. On the π side blow up the origin again on π,P: P=πQ,V=πW gives f W²+(1+π)QW−dπ²Q⁴=C/f³ with smooth residue, including Q=0,W=1 via Q derivative. The P side has unit W'-linear coefficient and is étale. For the initial B-order one can also check R₁ at generic s by shifting B by s/(1+s), as in PREFIX24: the τ-linear constant here comes both from f's −π term and from πsB and reduces to s³/(1+s)², nonzero generically. Thus this is indeed the correct normalization before those blowups; alternatively blowing up the finite order and normalizing yields the same proper birational regular modification, and normalizing outside the isolated center agrees with the preceding O(s≠0) checks.

At s=0 for a=1 use instead another parameter blowup (2,s), then normalize. On its 2 chart s=2t', z'²=1+8d t'² is split by an étale (z'−1)/2 monic. The remaining Y'=y'/2 satisfies Y'²+t'²=4C. The t' side has smooth-conic tangent V'²+t'R+C̄R² (2=t'R). All resolve in one more ordinary blowup. On the other parameter chart 2=sR, (y'/s)²=−1+4CR² gives Gaussian i by the same étale extraction. On s side π=sP,R=ηsP²,L=sZ adjoin W=Z/P by the monic W²+η P W=dη. It is integral and contains the old ratios; the base relation sP=π gives regular ambient. At s=0 the exceptional curve W²+PW=1 is smooth, including P=0 by the P derivative. On R side π=RP,s=ηRP²,L=RZ, Z²+Z=dηP² is étale. This resolves all nonoverlap points; away from the corner finite normalization of that raw order is understood. Precisely, the normalized blowup of its reduced closed corner gives a proper birational map to its finite normalization, is an isomorphism elsewhere after normalizing, and therefore glues with the true finite t' chart. No regularity is inferred for the nonnormal order by itself.
