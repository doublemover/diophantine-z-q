# Choose which actual matching slope succeeds, while keeping the first class and arbitrarily many local data

Fix a positive squarefree β with 17|β, β≡17 mod24 and all its primes 1 mod4 (in particular any ray numerator 17 times five distinct primes 1 mod408). For either choice k=4 or k=12, there exist infinitely many distinct squarefree complements θ>1 and stand-alone prepared poles b with B=17b of squareclass β, D=1−b/17 of squareclass θ, such that:

* H_k(Q) has infinitely many points and its rational Jacobian has positive rank; infinitely many distinct twist classes on E_n and E_nV give literal PointPairs at this same b with BOTH ordinates nonzero and the full markings. Here V=B²D and E_n denotes y²=x(x−n)(x+3n).
* The other of H₄,H₁₂ has no rational point despite local solubility. More strongly the entire opposite half of slopes 2^h or 3·2^h (h≥2) has the nonzero pair of Tate–Shafarevich classes proved in the companion arithmetic. We are not asserting global points at all slopes on the successful half.
* One may take 0<b<17, b with square reduced denominator and exact depths v₂(b),v₃(b),v₁₇(b)=(4,2,2). Alternatively, for any given eligible prepared b* of the same first squareclass and any finite set of places, such b approximate b* arbitrarily closely and preserve its local complementary squareclasses there. In this alternative, at 2,3,17 copy the actual depths of b* (they may exceed the displayed default). Thus either success/obstruction ordering occurs within every such finite set of neighborhoods, not necessarily AT b*.

This result varies the global scale; it does not show that the already specified scale of an indexed pole lies in the produced infinite set. Keeping β is not keeping its 15 Lucas-linked source data.

## Choose a locally specified class with a global point on the chosen slope

Set m=βk² and φ(t)=(t+k)/(1+βkt). Put
    f(t)=(1−m)(1−9βt²)((1+βkt)²−9β(t+k)²).
The quartic H_(k,θ) is y²=θ f(t). For a rational t with f(t)>0 take θ to be the positive squarefree representative of [f(t)]. The quartic has a rational point with this abscissa; y≠0 after avoiding the branch. Moreover
    f(t)=Norm[(1+k√β)(1+3t√β)((1+βkt)+3(t+k)√β)].
Writing the element in brackets as u+w√β gives a rational point on the conic L²−βA²=θZ². This is not an assertion that all locally eligible θ occur.

Impose θ locally square at 2,3 and all p|β, hence θ≡1 mod24 and coprime to 6β. At 2 and p|β, t near zero works (at 2, m a multiple of 16). At 3 use t near zero if k=12. If k=4 take t integral with v₃(1+βkt)=1. Then q₁≡1, q₂/9≡1−β≡2, (1−m)≡2 mod3; f(t) is a local square. At infinity choose any sufficiently small interval around t=0, where f(0)=(1−m)(1−9m)>0. Never choose the finitely many degenerate t where t or φ(t) is a pole, zero, or a rational boundary for the marked points.

For the approximation alternative, write b*=17βr*², D*=θ*z*² with θ* positive squarefree. At each requested place choose instead any smooth local point of y²=θ*f(t), avoiding the finite bad list and branches. It exists by the local-solubility proof of the slope arithmetic for the input b*, including whichever of k=4,12 was chosen. Every nonempty smooth local curve neighborhood is infinite. On an open neighborhood of this abscissa f(t) has class θ*. At 2,3,p|β the targeted class is already square. Include the real positivity constraint. Rational weak approximation now enforces all finitely many conditions simultaneously.

Infinitely many distinct θ can be constructed while retaining these open sets. To avoid any finite previously used list of θ, select a new odd prime P not dividing any of them, 6βk or the branch discriminant. One can choose P at which q₁=1−9βt² has a simple root: take an integer t₀ divisible by every forbidden prime, large enough so that |1−9βt₀²|>1 and select a prime factor. It is not forbidden. At its simple root q₂ is nonzero since the binary quartic branch sets are disjoint modulo P. Perturb t₀ mod P² so that v_P(f(t))=1, impose this single additional open condition in weak approximation, and now P|θ. The other local constraints are unchanged (exclude those primes when choosing P).

## Choose a true prepared scale

The nonempty smooth conic L²−βA²=θZ² is Q-rational. On its chart L≠0 put r=A/L, so that 1−βr²=θ(Z/L)². Set b=17βr². For the default depths choose v₂(r)=2, v₃(r)=1, v₁₇(r)=0 and r integral at all p|β, with 0<r<1/√β at infinity. These are nonempty local open sets on the conic: θ is a local square at those finite places, and 1−βr² is a local square for any such choice (at any other p|β the same holds for every integral r). Rational parametrization and weak approximation yield rational r in all these sets. The reduced denominator of b is square because a denominator of r at primes away from 17β contributes an even exponent, while no denominator occurs at primes of 17β. The displayed depths follow from 17|β with β squarefree. For approximation, at each requested place use r* itself: since θ has the same local squareclass as θ*, 1−βr*²=θ times a local square. Approximate this local conic point; at 2,3,17 and p|β preserve the exact valuations and integrality of the prepared target as well. In particular b can be arbitrarily close to b* while b and b* globally have different complementary squareclasses.

Upon writing r=A/L in lowest terms, L>0, set e=Lz for the constructed rational z satisfying 1−βr²=θz². This is an integer with L²−βA²=θe² (a rational square times squarefree θ which is integral forces denominator one). This has gcd(L,βθ)=1: if p|L and p|β then v_p(L²−βA²)=1 contradicting p∤θ, and if p|L and p|θ then p∤β forces p|A. These are precisely the conic/first-class hypotheses of the opposite-sign calculation. Since H_k is soluble, its Cassels row vanishes; consequently the opposite half of the slope family realizes the nonzero sign. No Selmer-converse assumption is involved.

## A positive rational rank and infinitely many marked twists at each constructed pole

We can exclude finitely many t once before choosing the data. Over Qbar, all these H_(k,θ) become the fixed H_(k,1) via (t,y)↦(t,y/√θ). For a point P of H_(k,1), the divisor map π(P)=[2P−D∞] to J_(k,1) is nonconstant of degree 4; with a ramification point as origin it is doubling. The hyperelliptic involution sends π to −π since P+ι(P)∼D∞. If H_(k,θ) has a rational point Pθ above t, π(Pθ) on its own Jacobian is rational and, after the above quadratic isomorphism, goes to a point of the fixed J_(k,1) of field degree ≤2.

The fixed curve has only finitely many torsion points of degree ≤2: choose two distinct primes of good reduction; at each prime away from a given torsion order's primary characteristic its primary component injects into the reduction over F_(p²) (the residue degree is at most two). The two fixed orders therefore bound the exponents of every primary component, hence bound the total order independently of the quadratic field. There are only finitely many points on a fixed characteristic-zero elliptic curve of bounded order. The preimage under π and its projection to t therefore exclude only finitely many t. Choose outside them. Then π(Pθ) has infinite order, H_(k,θ) itself has infinitely many rational points by its marked elliptic torsor identification, and infinitely many t-values avoid the finite exceptional list.

For completeness the marked twist classes do not collapse to a finite set. Put e(t)=(1−βt²)(1−9βt²)/(1+3βt²)². Fix a squareclass c. The joint cover y²=θf(t), z²=c e(t) is geometrically connected biquadratic. The branch sets share exactly the pair ±1/(3√β). The other pairs are φ⁻¹(±1/(3√β)) and ±1/√β; these are disjoint since φ fixes ±1/√β and the original four branches are distinct. There are six branch points in all, each inertia 2 in characteristic zero. Riemann–Hurwitz gives genus 3 and Faltings finiteness applies to its smooth projective normalization. Therefore infinitely many of the H-points give distinct [e(t)]. For each, [e(φ(t))]=[θe(t)] by the earlier matching identity. The two explicit marked points of that construction give the literal nonzero ordinates on the twists with parameters E=B e(t), EV. Both are non-torsion by the earlier 17-adic twist test, or directly since v₁₇(E) is odd if [e(t)] is a 17-unit of even valuation: when t is integral e(t)≡1 mod17; when v₁₇(t)<0 the leading terms give e(t) of valuation zero. Here v₁₇(B)=1+v₁₇(b) is odd and V=B²D has even valuation, and the point is not 2-torsion. E_1 has good reduction at 17 and rational torsion on an odd-valuation twist maps to 2-torsion in the unchanged residue field over the ramified quadratic extension; the formal log is injective on its maximal ideal at 17, also for 17-primary torsion. Thus there are infinitely many distinct marked rational-rank-positive twist pairs for each of these prepared poles. This is single-pole and does not produce a common original assignment.
