# The actual modular function field on the exact generic elliptic curve
Write k=Q(j), j transcendental. This is also the fraction field of Z[X,1/(6NX(X−1728))] by transitivity of localization. Van Hoeij–Smith, *A Divisor Formula and a Bound on the Q-Gonality of the Modular Curve X₁(N)*, Research in Number Theory 7 (2021), article 22, §3 eq. (22), use exactly y²=x³−3j₀x−2j₀ with j₀=j/(j−1728). Their q_N for N>2 is the primitive x-coordinate polynomial, irreducible in Q[x,j₀]; their discussion immediately following (22) presents X₁(N) by that equation. Since q_N has positive x-degree and is irreducible, its image in k[x] is irreducible (Gauss) and presents the generic field. For N=2 one uses x³−3j₀x−2j₀ instead: as a polynomial linear in j₀ it is primitive over Q[x] since gcd(x³,3x+2)=1, so irreducible; it cuts out the three nonzero order-two points. For N=1 the modular curve is the j-line itself; the identity mark lies at infinity and its affine coordinate is not used. See [authors’ §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.13644) and [published-version record](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10310037).

Thus for any affine mark P of exact order N>1 in the common finite Galois splitting field L/k, the choice of its x(P) embeds the rational function field of the *point-marked* X₁(N) as F₁=k(x(P)). The earlier two sections’ q_N-root description and generic irreducibility, not an arbitrary numerical index or a specialized degree, are the outside inputs. For H⊂(Z/N)ˣ, the point-marked coarse X_H is the quotient of X₁ by its diamonds; on the characteristic-zero generic field this gives F₁^H. See [Mascot §2.1–2.2](https://d-nb.info/1266847782/34) and the prior exact character-kernel comparison for the point versus dual-isogeny conventions. A generator x(P) under pullback of the point-marking diamond u goes to x([u]P); using inverse diamonds changes nothing in the fixed field. In particular the central sign acts trivially, and one must not simply divide by |H| on the coarse curve.

**Arithmetic verification.** For σ fixing x(P), the conjugate point equals P or −P: the x-projection on an elliptic curve identifies precisely those alternatives. Thus x([m]P) is fixed by σ for all m and lies already in F₁ by finite Galois correspondence. For each unit u modulo N, [u]P has order N and x([u]P) is a root of the same irreducible q_N, so there is a unique k-homomorphism F₁→F₁ carrying the generator to that element. The inclusions and finite dimensionality make the homomorphisms automorphisms; they coincide with the coarse diamonds by the stated moduli-generator description. If σP=[u]P, σ and the diamond u have the same restriction into L on every element of F₁ by checking the generator. Consequently F₁^H embeds in the field fixed by this literal point-orbit stabilizer. The reviewed generic orbit degree bound yields [Q(X_H):Q(j)] ≤ [SL₂(Z):Γ_H] ≤ N[U:H]∏_{p|N}(1+1/p). There is no equality assumption between this coarse field and the point-orbit field, no appeal to an assumed analytic-fibre injection, and no enlargement of the arithmetic base Q(j) to C(j).
