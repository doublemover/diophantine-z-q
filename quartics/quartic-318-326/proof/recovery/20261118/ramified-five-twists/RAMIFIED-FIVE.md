# All ramified twists at 5: twelve semistable nodes upstairs, then the exact tame quotient

**Paper/source result.** With the same curve, coefficients q_i and Δ=|disc Q(√q_i)| as in TAME-GOOD-PRIMES, the explicit proper flat regular modification now exists over **Z[1/gcd(42,Δ)]** for every q. In particular it exists uniformly over Z[1/42]. For 5 ramified, identify I={i:v₅(q_i) odd}. A 12-node resolution on an auxiliary ramified cover is required by this construction for every such I. The resulting finite quotient has exactly eight *geometric* singular points when I is a singleton or all four indices, and none when |I| is 2 or 3. One final ordinary blowup of the reduced eight-point union resolves the first alternative. These last eight are on the intermediate quotient, not on the original raw or normalized 5-fibre, and the twelve auxiliary nodes are not twelve blowups on the twisted surface. No assertion of semistability of the final twist model. The unresolved cases of this uniform argument are the ramified fibres at 2,3,7.

## 1. Exact semistable input for the untwisted curve at five

Let D=Z_(5); write p=5. For the **untwisted** model use the actual blowup at the F₂₅ collision point from FIVE-OPEN. At 5 its closed fibre is reduced nodal and its relative structure is smooth except at the following twelve geometric points, each étale-locally xy=p. Here the count concerns singularities of the fibre, not singularities of its already regular total space.

Off the central collision the unit-root finite charts are étale over D[r_j] and the near-infinity ratio chart is étale over localized D[x], so are D-smooth. On the central blowup's p-chart U=r₁/p, V=r₂/p, W=r₃/p and b=r₀. The equations are
  V²−U²=−3072,    W²−U²=15,    b²=p²U²+32.
Where U≠0 in residue characteristic five, W and b are units. Taking V as coordinate, solve successively for the unit roots U,W,b, giving a D-smooth chart even when V=0. At U=W=0, V²=3, b²=2 in the geometric residue field, so exactly four points. Set X=W−U, Y=(W+U)/3. Then XY=5 **exactly**. The linear coordinate change is invertible (2 and 3 units), and V,b are unit-derivative quadratic adjunctions. This is the required étale nodal chart. On the other, u=r₁-chart outside the p-direction overlap, take P=p/u, V'=r₂/u, W'=r₃/u. Above the collision u=P=0, V'²=W'²=1, b²=2; there are eight more geometric points. The chart is étale over uP=5 at these points since V',W',b are units. All other points are covered by the p-chart or the unchanged complement. The two sets of nodes are disjoint.

## 2. Remove unit factors étale-locally, resolve the ramified scalar change

Set e_i=v₅(q_i) mod 2, a_i=q_i·5^(−v₅(q_i))∈D×, and write ε_i=(−1)^e_i. Rational square rescaling replaces the generic field equations by r_i²=5^{e_i}a_i(T−b_i) without changing the original normalization (b_i here are the four branch constants, not the retained root b). Take D₁=D[α_i:α_i²=a_i] as the full four-factor tensor algebra, a finite étale torsor under G₀={±1}⁴; dependencies among squareclasses merely make it disconnected. Set D₂=D₁[π]/(π²−5). Localize and work componentwise as necessary: the local rings above 5 are DVRs with uniformizer π, and away from 5 this extension is étale. Let H={1,σ} act semilinearly by π↦−π, u_i↦ε_i u_i on the original *untwisted* field and its equivariant blowup, writing u_i for the four untwisted roots. G₀ changes signs of the α_i and compensates by the same signs on u_i. The actions commute and the functions r_i=α_iπ^{e_i}u_i are fixed.

On the D₂ base change of the untwisted modification every smooth point stays smooth over D₂. The 12 former fibre nodes now have total-space equation XY=π² up to the indicated étale maps, and all are genuine total-space singularities (hypersurface in the square of the ambient maximal ideal). Blow up their reduced union. Three true charts of Bl_(X,Y,π)(XY=π²) are:
* scale π: X=πξ, Y=πη, ξη=1, a smooth torus chart over D₂;
* scale X: π=XP, Y=XP², with XP=π, regular total space;
* scale Y: the symmetric chart.

For each scale, inverting it gives the old ring with it inverted. The π-chart is Laurent-polynomial; on the X chart X is not a zerodivisor because XP−π is primitive irreducible over the DVR, and similarly for Y. These are therefore the actual ratio algebras. The second chart's exceptional divisor is regular (mod X its ring is a residue polynomial ring), and likewise on the third. All comparisons and center ideals pull back étale: a reduced nodal center over a finite perfect residue field has reduced étale preimages. This constructs a projective regular arithmetic surface W over D₂ (componentwise) with compatible H×G₀ action. The reduced 12-point union is distinguished by the singular locus of the base-changed total space, so it, unlike its individual geometric labels, is preserved by all these actions.

## 3. Enumerate every geometric fixed point of σ on W at five

The action fixes no geometric point over the fraction field of D₁, since it changes π there. On the geometric special fibre use one marking of the α_i and of the explicitly labeled roots. Write 0,1,2,3 for the respective indices of b=r₀,U,V,W on the central p-chart; the distinction between ε₀ and the three central signs matters. The following calculations include all masks I≠∅.

**Smooth points away from the 12 exceptional conics.** Off the old triple branch, signs act freely on the sixteen nonbranch sheets. Above the distinct finite branch T=0, a fixed point occurs exactly for I={0}: the other three roots are nonzero and independently labeled, giving eight geometric points. A smooth parameter is u₀, anti-invariant. At infinity use the three unit ratios u_i/u₀ and x=s u₀. Exactly I={0,1,2,3} fixes the eight labels, with x anti-invariant. These are the only other branch sections in the 5-fibre outside the central T=2 collision. On the central p-chart where U≠0, W and b are nonzero too, so a fixed point requires ε₀=ε₁=ε₃=+1. Nontriviality means I={2}, and V=0 is necessary and sufficient. Then U²=3072, W=±U, b²=2, giving eight geometric points. The coordinate V is an anti-invariant smooth parameter. The complement of these regions over the collision consists of the original 12 nodes, already replaced by conics.

**Conics from the four p-chart nodes.** Labels b,V are nonzero; the node is preserved iff ε₀=ε₂=+1. Otherwise σ exchanges distinct conics and no point on them is fixed. For a preserved node:
* If I={1,3}, both W,U change sign, hence X,Y,π all change sign. The exceptional conic is fixed pointwise. On the π-chart ξ and η are invariant while π changes sign. On an X-chart with π=XP, X changes sign, P is invariant, and on the Y-chart similarly. These are fixed *curves*, not isolated quotient singularities.
* If I={1} or {3}, X↦dY, Y↦d⁻¹X, d=3ε₃∈D×. The two points with π-direction zero are exchanged. On the π-chart σ(ξ)=−d/ξ. Exactly ξ²=−d gives fixed points: two distinct geometric roots at each of the four conics. There the parameter v=(ξ+d/ξ)/2 is anti-invariant, vanishes in the residue field, and has derivative (1−d/ξ²)/2=1 there. Thus v is a smooth fibre coordinate, with π also anti-invariant. This yields eight isolated fixed points. There is no other nontrivial preserved-node case.

**Conics from the eight u-chart nodes.** Their nonzero labels b,V',W' transform by ε₀, ε₂/ε₁, ε₃/ε₁. A nontrivial mask preserves a node iff I={1,2,3}. Then u,P=p/u and π all change sign. As in the fixed-conic case above, the conic is fixed pointwise with invariant direction parameter and anti-invariant normal parameter. Otherwise the conics occur in exchanged pairs. This exhausts every chart above five. Labels in this count are geometric: no rationality of the nodes, their fixed points or conics is being assumed.

## 4. Take the actual quotient, verify every local ring, and descend the centers

W is projective over the affine D base (finite maps, the initial finite normalization of the projective line, and blowups are projective). The quotients by H and H×G₀ are therefore schemes with finite surjective quotient maps; on invariant affine opens the rings are the invariants. This standard finite-group quotient theorem applies to quasi-projective schemes; see [Rydh, Theorem 2.3.2](https://davidrydh.se/papers/hilbchowsymdiv-20080416.pdf), or SGA1 V.1.8. Finite-type separated quotients so obtained are proper over D when W is proper (universal closedness descends through a finite surjective cover). Taking the quotient by G₀ after H is the effective finite étale descent along the G₀-torsor D₁/D: checking after pullback to D₁ splits that torsor into the disjoint G₀ copies and recovers W/H. In particular G₀'s order causes no new inertia at a point. This reasoning works for disconnected D₁.

For precision on the total-space calculation one may make an unramified scalar extension at a closed point so the relevant geometric residue labels are rational. Finite invariants commute with flat scalar extension and completion (kernel of the action differences). Over a given closed point the completion of a finite algebra is the product of the completions over its points, by the semilocal decomposition. On an exchanged pair the invariant product is one of its regular factors by projection. On a fixed point it is the invariant completed local ring. Thus local computations after this étale splitting neither assume a geometric orbit is rational nor identify only the closed-fibre ring. It suffices to check all closed points of the special fibre (all other vertical points specialize to them and regularity localizes), and the already smooth generic fibre.

At an isolated fixed point from §3 the completed local ring of W is D̂₂[[v]] after an unramified extension making its residue point rational, with v the anti-invariant coordinate just specified (u₀, x, V, or (ξ+d/ξ)/2). Indeed the charts are étale over a smooth polynomial/torus coordinate there and v vanishes at the residue point with unit derivative; completion at (π,v) is that of the polynomial coordinate ring, with identical residue field. The involution changes both π and v and is trivial on D̂₁. Taking even and odd coefficients in the power series, the invariants are EXACTLY
  D̂₁[[A]] ⊕ z D̂₁[[A]]  ≅  D̂₁[[A,z]]/(z²−5A),    A=v²,  z=πv.
Indeed D̂₂ is free on 1,π over D̂₁, and invariant coefficients of even v-powers lie in D̂₁ while those of odd v-powers lie in πD̂₁. This is an actual ordinary node of the total space (dimension two, embedding dimension three).

At a point of a pointwise fixed conic, use the π-chart torus or an end of the conic in the X- or Y-chart. On the torus the relative coordinate ξ is invariant and π changes sign, so the completed invariant ring at a rationalized label is a regular one-variable power series ring over D̂₁ (subtract any D̂₁-lift of its residue). At an end, the actual chart is étale over D₂[X,P]/(XP−π); σ(X)=−X and σ(P)=P. The end is X=P=0. Its completion, after making its label rational, is the completion of that displayed ring, since étaleness and equality of residues identify completions equivariantly with the shown coordinate action. Eliminating π gives D̂₁[[P,X]]/(P²X²−5); even powers of X give the invariants D̂₁[[P,A]]/(P²A−5), A=X². One can see the equality before quotienting by splitting the ambient series into its even and odd parts; the relation is even. This last local ring is regular because 5 is a parameter of D̂₁ and the defining equation has nonzero linear part −5. At the other end use the Y-chart. These computations explain why a whole fixed conic is not a singular center in the quotient.

Consequently the reduced nonregular locus of W/(H×G₀) over 5 is finite, of geometric cardinality eight or zero as stated. It is a union of closed points over F₅, cut out downstairs by their maximal ideals; after unramified base change its reduced structure pulls back to that same geometric union since F₅ is perfect. Blow it up. At the eight possible points the completion just computed is the completed node z²=5A. This ordinary node is resolved by a single blowup as in TAME-GOOD-PRIMES §3: its 5-chart is D[V], its A-chart D[A,V]/(AV²−5), both regular along their exceptional directions. This comparison with completions really tests the actual blowup: first identify the base-changed completed-node blowup with that of the polynomial-node local ring after flat completion. At a point above the old closed point, the corresponding map from a polynomial-node blowup stalk to its completed-base-change stalk is regular because completion of the excellent base local ring is a regular morphism (and base change/localization preserve regular morphisms). Thus the latter stalks are regular. Flat descent along the faithfully flat local completion of the *actual* quotient stalk, and the preceding finite étale scalar extensions, proves regularity of every affected original blowup stalk. Away from the reduced union the quotient was already regular. No unproved identification of the blowup of an arbitrary completion with the completion of a specific blowup chart is used.

## 5. Morphism to the original model and global arithmetic open

The composite covers/modifications have the twisted generic fibre after quotient. Over D₁ the field fixed by H is generated by α_iπ^{e_i}u_i as before: upstairs degree 32 over the parameter field, four independent twisted squareclasses give a fixed subfield of degree 16, and H has order two. G₀ descent restores the specific rational twist field. The regular W also maps to the original normalization X_q over the parameter: any element of that field integral over a parameter affine algebra belongs to every normal local ring of W over that affine, hence is regular there. These maps glue over the common parameter overlap and are invariant, so descend to the quotient. The resulting map of the resolved quotient to X_q is proper (source proper over the base, target separated), birational, and an isomorphism generically. This does NOT require ramified base change of the singular original finite normalization to commute with normalization.

To glue over arithmetic opens, use a genuine open neighbourhood of 5 rather than treating Spec Z_(5) as open. Invert 2,3,7, all primes ≠5 in the numerators and denominators of the q_i, and every other prime dividing Δ; this gives B=Spec Z[1/N] with 5 not inverted. All a_i are now units. The p-chart, reciprocal chart and reduced F₂₅ center of FIVE-OPEN define the same untwisted blowup over B, smooth outside 5 (all other collisions excluded). The D₁ and D₂ algebras above, the 12 reduced centers over 5, the group actions, projective quotients and final reduced centers are defined over B by these same formulas. Away from 5 both constant extensions form étale torsors and no center contributes; there the quotient identifies with the original X_q exactly by the parameter-normalization comparison. On B∩Spec Z[1/gcd(210,Δ)] in the ramified-5 case, the earlier model also equals X_q: all other places at which that construction used centers are inverted in B, and 5 is omitted by the earlier open. Thus the two proper models glue by this common isomorphism over the actual intersection of base opens. Their union is Spec Z[1/gcd(42,Δ)]. Properness is local on the base, the generic fibre is unchanged, and flatness follows by torsion-freeness over a localized PID. When 5 is unramified the preceding proof already covered it. This completes every valuation pattern at 5; it does not treat ramified 2,3 or 7.
