## 2. Prove target density without implicit analytification

Over a finite affine base chart B is finite free over ℂ[x]: over the previous coefficient field it is torsion-free and finite over the PID E[x], and scalar change is flat. For each exceptional fibre x=x₀ and actual point y, choose an element b of B whose values on this finite fibre are 1 at y and 0 at every other point. This is the exact quotient-and-Chinese-remainder separation of its maximal ideals; nilpotent thickening is harmless. The actual characteristic polynomial of multiplication by b has 1 as a root at x₀ (it acts as 1 on the residue at y). At a sequence x_n→x₀ avoiding the deleted values, enumerate all d complex eigenvalues with multiplicity. Monicity, coefficient convergence and a Cauchy root cap give one convergent subsequence of the d-tuples. The limiting product of linear factors is exactly the special characteristic polynomial; hence some entries converge to 1. At each n that entry is evaluation of b at some actual point y_n (multiplication by b−λ is noninvertible iff b−λ lies in a maximal ideal of the finite ℂ-algebra). The same monic bound applies to each of the finitely many B generators; extract a subsequence of the actual whole geometric evaluations. Their limit gives a point in the special fibre because the algebraic relations are closed, and b=1 forces it to be the exact y. Thus no branch may lack an ordinary converging sheet. This is the elementary compactness proof of the root-continuity assertion and requires neither Rouché nor an asserted complex target chart.

The image of F is closed (analytic source compact, target Hausdorff) and contains the whole ordinary dense locus, so F is surjective.

## 3. Prove injectivity, order one, and target coordinates from the same extension

The analytic sum of e_c is d as well: on finite disjoint filled disks each degree is the exact local power e_c, and an ordinary fibre counts those degrees.
