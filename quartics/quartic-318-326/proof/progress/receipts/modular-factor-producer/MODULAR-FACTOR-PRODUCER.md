# Actual rational modular cover and quaternionic surface factor

## Scope and conclusion

This is a mathematical theorem-application proof for the precise modular object needed by the five-contact argument. It is not a Lean construction of modular curves, Jacobians or Tate modules. It discharges the existence/model/factor antecedent; it does not assert the complete `FiveContact49Height` theorem.

Start with the final **primitive weight-two newform** f of level N from the modularity/conductor producer, its nebentypus ε, the finite normal field L'/Q already constructed there, and the non-CM absolutely simple quaternionic abelian surface A/L. The accepted Tate realization is

    V_l(A) ⊗ Qbar_l | G_L' ≅ r_l ⊕ r_l,

where r_l is the constituent attached to f with the fixed coefficient embedding. We prove that the **point-of-order-N** curve X_H/Q, H=ker ε, and J_H=Jac(X_H) have all the following properties:

1. X_H, its map to the j-line, and the relevant diamond quotient are defined over Q.
2. The same actual r_l occurs in V_l(J_H) after coefficient extension. No extension of the arithmetic base field is made here.
3. There is a nonzero L'-homomorphism A→J_H. Its image has dimension two; J_H is L'-isogenous to A×Z for an abelian variety Z/L'. Thus g(X_H)≥2.
4. Γ(N)⊂Γ_H⊂Γ_0(N), −I∈Γ_H, and

       deg(X_H→X(1)) = [SL_2(Z):Γ_H]
                      = N·|im ε|·∏_{p|N}(1+1/p).

There is no factor of two, coefficient-field degree, or cyclotomic degree in this formula or in the base field L'. The Jacobian's principal polarization is canonical; no polarization-degree bound for A or Z is asserted.

## 1. Exact rational model and the model-convention correction

Mascot, §2.1, equations (2.1.5) and (2.1.8), specifies the point-level model and its rational diamond action. The determinant image of the defining subgroup U_H is all of (Z/NZ)^*, since diag(a,1)∈U_H for every unit a. Its constant field is therefore Q. Section2.2, equation (2.2.1), states the Q-isogeny decomposition of this same model's Jacobian into newform factors. These are the relevant assertions, not a choice of a connected full-level component over a cyclotomic field.

There is a genuine convention distinction in the manuscript's citations: Baker–González-Jiménez–González–Poonen §3.1 uses the **μ_N-embedding** model, characterized by rational Fourier expansions at ∞. Its cusp ∞ is Q-rational. Mascot's point-level cusp ∞ generally is not Q-rational. The two models must not be identified by the identity on the upper half-plane. No argument below needs that identification or a Q-rational cusp on the point model.

Here is the explicit rational transport. For a point-level object (E,P), let C=⟨P⟩ and φ:E→E/C. Cartier duality and the canonical principal polarizations identify ker(φ^∨) with C^D. The marking P identifies C with Z/NZ, hence identifies C^D with μ_N. Consequently

    (E,P) ↦ (E/C, μ_N ≅ C^D ↪ E/C)

is a morphism of the two moduli problems over Q. Applying the same construction to the dual isogeny gives its inverse, up to the harmless sign of the marking on the coarse curve. It extends uniquely to the smooth projective compactifications. Denote this Q-isomorphism by F:X_1^pt(N)→X_1^μ(N).

Replacing P by hP changes the induced dual marking by h^−1. Thus F conjugates the two diamond actions by inversion. In particular it identifies their H-quotients, because H=H^−1. This construction uses the group schemes Z/NZ and μ_N over Q; choosing an individual primitive root of unity is unnecessary.

Over C, quotienting the lattice by the subgroup generated by 1/N and marking the dual kernel identifies F with the Fricke map τ↦−1/(Nτ), with the usual weight-two pullback factor. F is a rational isomorphism **between the two models**, not a claim that Fricke is a rational automorphism of either model with its original marking convention.

## 2. Same newform factor, all coefficient conjugates, and diamonds

On X_1^μ(N), take Shimura's quotient q_μ:J_1^μ(N)→A_f/Q. Its pulled-back differential space is

    W_f = span_C{ f^σ : σ:Q(a_n(f))→C }.

This is the entire coefficient-conjugate span, not just the chosen eigenline. The quotient and its rationality are exactly Shimura Theorem1(i),(iv); the modern Tate-module description appears in the proof of Baker et al. Proposition3.2.

Li's original printed page296 defines coefficient conjugation K and Fricke H_N, then proves for a normalized primitive newform F that F|H_N is a nonzero scalar multiple of F|K. Applying this to every f^σ proves W_f|H_N=W_f. Therefore the transported quotient

    q_pt = q_μ ∘ F_* : J_1^pt(N)→A_f

is Q-defined and has the same differential span W_f in the point-model analytic uniformization. Its target is the original A_f, not a potentially unrelated twist. This also verifies the Fricke-stability proviso in Shimura's Euler-factor assertion. Mascot (2.2.1) independently records the resulting factor directly on the chosen point model.

For every h∈H and every σ, the diamond eigenvalue on f^σ is σ(ε(h))=1; an inverse-diamond convention gives the same answer. Hence

    (q_pt ∘ ⟨h⟩)^* = q_pt^*

on all differentials of A_f. A characteristic-zero homomorphism of abelian varieties with zero differential is zero: its connected image would otherwise be a positive-dimensional abelian variety with nonzero tangent space. Thus q_pt∘⟨h⟩=q_pt. This is a statement about actual homomorphisms over Q, not just a single complex eigenline.

Let H_eff be the image of H in Aut(X_1^pt(N)), let m=|H_eff|, and let π:X_1^pt(N)→X_H be the quotient. The usual pullback/norm identities on Jacobians are

    π^*π_* = ∑_{h∈H_eff} h,       π_*π^* = [m].

They hold for this finite quotient even at its ramification points. Therefore

    (q_pt π^*) π_* = [m] q_pt.

The right side is surjective. It follows that q_pt π^*:J_H→A_f is surjective. Poincaré reducibility gives a Q-isogeny factor A_f of J_H, and consequently its Tate representation is a direct summand up to Q_l-isogeny. The action of −1 on the curve is already trivial; averaging over H or H_eff gives the same rational idempotent, and m is used in the norm identities. No duplicate central factor occurs.

## 3. Realizing the actual surface over the already controlled field

The original A_f carries the two-dimensional constituent r_l attached to f, after choosing the coefficient embedding used by modularity. The preceding Q-isogeny-factor construction therefore puts r_l inside V_l(J_H)⊗Qbar_l. Restrict to G_L'. The accepted relation V_l(A)⊗Qbar_l≅r_l⊕r_l gives

    Hom_{G_L'}(V_l(A)⊗Qbar_l, V_l(J_H)⊗Qbar_l) ≠ 0.

One may project from one copy of r_l and then include it in J_H. Coefficient extension commutes with this Hom space: the invariants are the kernel of linear equations on the finite-dimensional Q_l-space Hom(V_l(A),V_l(J_H)); finitely many equations span the same constraint space. Hence the Q_l-Hom space itself is nonzero. This coefficient-field argument does **not** replace L' by an extension containing Fourier coefficients.

Faltings' Hom theorem, over the number field L', now gives

    Hom_{L'}(A,J_H) ⊗ Q_l ≠ 0.

Choose a nonzero actual homomorphism. The connected component of its kernel is an abelian subvariety of the absolutely simple A, so it is zero; the kernel is finite and the image B has dimension2. Poincaré reducibility over L' gives a complement Z to B. Composing A→B with B×Z→J_H yields the desired L'-isogeny A×Z→J_H. The case in which A is not absolutely simple belongs to the separately handled CM branch, not this argument.

In particular g=dim J_H≥2. Neither [Q(a_n(f)):Q] nor any coefficient denominator appears. A_f itself may have large dimension; one only extracts the actual dimension-two image of A after restriction to L'.

## 4. Degree, central quotient, and geometric cover conditions

Weight two gives ε(−1)=1, so −1∈H and −I∈Γ_H. The reduction map

    Γ_0(N) → (Z/NZ)^*,  [a b; c d] ↦ d mod N

is surjective. For a unit residue d, choose integers a,b with ad−bN=1; the matrix [a b; N d] witnesses surjectivity. Its inverse image of H is Γ_H, so

    [Γ_0(N):Γ_H] = [(Z/NZ)^*:H] = |im ε|.

The standard first index can be counted without a sign ambiguity. Cosets of Γ_0(N) correspond to primitive bottom rows modulo N up to unit scaling. At p^a there are p^(2a)−p^(2a−2) primitive rows, and the unit action is free with p^a−p^(a−1) elements. The quotient therefore has p^(a−1)(p+1) points. The Chinese remainder theorem multiplies these local counts, giving

    [SL_2(Z):Γ_0(N)] = N∏_{p|N}(1+1/p).

Since the source and target groups both contain the central −I, their SL_2 index equals the effective PSL_2 index and hence the complex covering degree. This also equals the degree of the rational j-map, because field extension does not change the degree of a finite morphism. The forgetful map is defined over Q on the chosen point moduli problem. Its possible branch values are the cusp and the two elliptic values; no additional branch locus is introduced by the rational-model choice. Thus it is a degree-d Belyi map after the usual rational normalization of the j-line.

Γ(N) is contained in Γ_H. The smooth compactification is the standard compactified congruence curve. The genus formula gives

    g = 1 + d/12 − e_2/4 − e_3/3 − c/2 ≤ 1+d/12 ≤ d,

where the last inequality follows from g≥2 (in particular d≥12). This supplies the cover, level, degree, genus and central-sign antecedents used by the cusp/Wronskian and vertical-support producers.

Combined with the actual conductor bounds N≤CR², rad(N)|R and the determinant-derived t≤4608R, the already kernel-checked `EulerIndex318.index_bound` and `ModularIndex318.index_from_determinant` give d<2^14 C R^(97/32). The finite index estimate is a separate Lean result; this note constructs the actual objects to which it applies.
