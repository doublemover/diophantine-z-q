## Independently checked mathematical points

1. **Same field throughout the CM construction.** The quaternion division algebra and CM type force the geometric isogeny class to be a square of a CM elliptic curve. All geometric endomorphisms being defined over L makes the rational idempotents, elliptic images, and factor homomorphisms defined over L. Differentiation embeds the imaginary quadratic endomorphism field in L. Passing to maximal order can be done over the same L: the subgroup generated by `(f alpha_i)(E[f])` has complex lattice description `O_F Lambda / Lambda`, so its quotient has maximal multiplier order. No extra field degree enters the later isogeny estimate. These are source-level arguments, not hidden Lean constructions.

2. **The discriminant factor is four.** The actual fundamental discriminant characterization controls the odd exponents and the possible 2-adic exponent three. Thus `|D_F| <= 4 rad(|D_L|)` is correct, including the factor `2^3/2`. The Lean module derives this from `Int.IsFundamentalDiscr` and prime support; it does not assume the desired inequality.

3. **The eta estimate concerns the actual infinite product.** Each factor is nonzero when the q-parameter has norm below one. Reverse triangle and the logarithm bound control finite products by the geometric series. Multipliability and nonvanishing justify passing through log to the infinite product. The actual `ModularForm.eta` is unfolded, including its exponential factor. Reduced imaginary part gives the strict correction bound `32/225`. There is no supplied eta-product estimate or unverified logarithm at zero.

4. **Original height normalization is consistent.** I reread the original PDF text of Barquero-Sanchez et al., Research in Number Theory 3 (2017), article 13: the opening convention and printed page 14 equation (6.2), with `Delta=(2 pi)^12 eta^24`, give the individual-embedding summand `-log(2 pi)-log|eta|^2-(1/2)log(Im z)`. The later hypothesis about coefficients over Q(j) belongs to class averaging, which this argument does not use. I am not inheriting a visual-review claim from the author's note.

5. **The two field degrees are correctly separated.** An arbitrary everywhere-good-reduction extension is used only for the stable-height embedding average, with parameter q. The original field degree d, bounded by 576, alone appears in the isogeny expression. No bound of 576 is asserted for the good-reduction extension. Product additivity and the actual period average give `hAprime < 6 R`.

6. **The original GR bound is squared.** I reread the original GR PDF text at Theorem 1.9(1): for dimension two `Xi=(14^32 d max(1,log d,hAprime))^8`, and the applicable general bound is `degree <= Xi^2`. Both isogeny directions are available. Using base and extension field both equal to L preserves d. The separate MM improvement is unused. Half-log variation therefore contributes exactly eight times the logarithm of the base. The formal inequalities lead to `14 R+840 <= 854 R`, with strictness from the CM estimate, for `R >= 1`.

7. **No normalization factor is lost at the endpoint.** The 854 R result is for the surface. The separate metric comparison is responsible for the factor two and additive constant in the contact-height estimate `1708 R+120`.
