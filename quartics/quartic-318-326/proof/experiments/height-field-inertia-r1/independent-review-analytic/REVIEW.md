## Source and consumer comparison

1. The ring is the actual integral closure of Z in the previously constructed
   maximal residue field. Inertia is Mathlib's actual subgroup defined by all
   differences s(z)-z belonging to the specified prime ideal. For an integral
   root z with z^2=b, the square identity gives s(z)=z or -z. The negative case
   puts 2z in the ideal. Primality and nonvanishing of 2 force z into it, contrary
   to the assumed nonvanishing of b. No separate local inertia witness is assumed.
2. Normalization covers every nonzero integer b of even p-valuation. The exact
   finite multiplicity decomposition is b=p^(2m)c with p not dividing c. The
   constructed root x/p^m stays in the same field, has square c, and is integral
   because its square is integral. This supplies the integral root used above.
   The contraction equality P under Z = (p) supplies the required two residue
   nonvanishing statements. Recovering x uses the actual rational scalar p^m.
3. The four-root application scales all four original quotient-field roots by
   the same nonzero rational d. It proves each original root fixed after canceling
   d. The original nested tensor generator extensionality and the actual quotient
   map's surjectivity then prove the whole field automorphism is identity. This
   is not a statement about a merely related root tuple or a larger splitting field.
4. The literal specialization is t=32u/v with the original unit tuple. Independent
   inspection of that tuple and the formulas confirms the scaled radicands
   -448uv, -192v(u-v), 1344v(u-2401v), and -21v(32u+343v). All four indices and
   all five forms are covered, including v. Nonzero forms supply every denominator
   and radicand nonvanishing hypothesis. Primitivity and squareclass independence
   are not used or needed for these stronger support conclusions.
5. Exclusion from the literal odd-contact set forces even valuation of every
   form: an odd valuation would be positive, give actual divisibility, and place
   p in that set. The constant factors have prime support only among 2, 3 and 7,
   all excluded by the stated bad set. Addition of the two even form valuations
   gives even valuation of each radicand. The proof preserves odd-contact support,
   rather than replacing it by the full radical or asserting absent divisibility.
6. The Galois action on the ring of integers is the restriction of the same full
   Q-automorphism group. The primary IsGaloisGroup.of_isFractionRing theorem
   requires integral closure and an integral extension, both supplied here by the
   original integer ring and number-field structure. The previously proved normal
   splitting property belongs to this exact field. Finite/flat integer extension
   and separable residue fields supply the cardinality-of-inertia theorem's actual
   hypotheses. Its common ramification index is then identified with the actual
   index of the chosen P, producing index one from the trivial group.
7. The primary ramificationIdx_eq_one_iff theorem additionally requires essential
   finite type and separable residue fields at the contraction. These are supplied
   by the finite integer extension and the finite perfect residue field of (p).
   The source explicitly transports along the actual contraction equality before
   applying the criterion. Thus Algebra.IsUnramifiedAt concerns the original
   integer ring at every specified P, not an etale surrogate over Q.
8. The downstream support statement is the contrapositive of this same actual
   index-one result, with all quantified primes above p retained. It is only an
   inclusion of possible ramified primes; it does not assert ramification at every
   listed prime. Exceptional primes and zero forms remain excluded as stated.
9. No accepted predecessor or manuscript was changed. All five copied predecessors
   are byte-identical to the reviewed splitting packet. The historical
   progress/receipts/fiber-field-bridge/FIBER-PRODUCER.md, especially sections 7-8,

   direct square-root argument is formally about the smaller actual four-root

   nor replace the historical good-reduction argument.
