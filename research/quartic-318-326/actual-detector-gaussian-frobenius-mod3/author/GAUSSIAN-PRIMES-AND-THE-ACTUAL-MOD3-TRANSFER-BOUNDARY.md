# The actual Gaussian prime selector and the mod-3 Heegner transfer boundary

The original linear-\(q\) detector and its two selected level-48
twists can be refined by arbitrary determinant-\(+1\) residual
Frobenius classes at their genuine Gaussian contact/denominator primes.
These classes do not provide a transfer of the inert-13 or inert-19
Heegner value between twists. No trace or rational point on a selected
original twist is computed here.

## 1. Exact residual representation for the literal base

Let \(E_0:y^2=x(x+1)(x-3)\) and
\(L_3=\mathbf Q(E_0[3])\). Its mod-3 representation
\(\rho:G_{\mathbf Q}\to \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\) is surjective.

First \(\# E_0(\mathbf F_7)=8\), so \(a_7=0\) and the Frobenius
polynomial \(X^2+7\) is irreducible modulo 3. Thus \(\rho\) is
irreducible. At 3 the model has \(\Delta=2304\), \(c_4=208\),
and is minimal since \(v_3(\Delta)=2<12\). The node has two
distinct rational tangents \(y=\pm x\), hence the reduction is
split multiplicative. The Tate parameter has valuation 2.
Over \(\mathbf Q_3(\mu_3)\) its valuation is 4, not divisible by
3, so its cube root gives cubic ramification. The Tate
3-division sequence then supplies a nonidentity transvection
in inertia. Irreducibility gives a conjugate with a different
fixed line. Relative to those two lines, their powers generate
both elementary unipotent subgroups and hence
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\). The determinant is the
cyclotomic character, giving full \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\).

The only abelian subextension of \(L_3/\mathbf Q\) beyond
\(\mathbf Q\) is \(A=\mathbf Q(\mu_3)\). Indeed the commutator
of \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\) is \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\):
commutating \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\) with elementary
unipotents generates both elementary subgroups. This also
proves that the only quadratic subfield of \(L_3\) is \(A\),
not \(\mathbf Q(i)\). Every homomorphism from
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\) to a group of order 2 is trivial,
since it is generated by order-three unipotents.

## 2. Actual prime moves are free in this residual field

For each fixed cutoff of the original Gaussian argument, let
\(G/\mathbf Q(i)\) be its full Gaussian ray class field of
rational-integer modulus, so \(G/\mathbf Q\) is Galois. Include
any rational abelian wheel data if needed; it remains abelian
over \(\mathbf Q(i)\). The conductor includes 3; the quadratic
extension \(\mathbf Q(i,\mu_3)/\mathbf Q(i)\), tame at 3 and
unramified elsewhere, is included. Therefore \(A\subset G\).

In fact \(L_3\cap G=A\). For \(M=L_3\cap G\) we have
\(M\cap\mathbf Q(i)=\mathbf Q\) since \(L_3\) has only the
quadratic subfield \(A\). Thus
\(\operatorname{Gal}(M\mathbf Q(i)/\mathbf Q(i))\simeq
\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbf Q)\) is abelian, as \(M\mathbf
Q(i)\subset G\). The preceding abelianization identifies \(M\).

The Galois group of \(L_3G/\mathbf Q\) is the fibre product over
\(\operatorname{Gal}(A/\mathbf Q)\). The commutator of this
fibre product contains \((\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3),1)\):
lift an element \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)\) by some element of
\(\operatorname{Gal}(G/\mathbf Q)\) restricting nontrivially to
\(A\), then commutate with the elementary unipotents in the
kernel. Consequently its maximal abelian subfield is exactly
\(G^{\mathrm{ab}}\); there is no hidden extra cyclotomic
restriction on the old affine-linear form values.

Now at each **distinct** genuine prime form of the published
linear-\(q\) proof, choose any \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\)
conjugacy class \(C_j\subset\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbf F_3)\).
Merged equal prime forms get one choice, not two contradictory
ones. The old Gaussian norms are \(1\bmod3\), so these choices
agree on \(A\). For any old Gaussian class restriction \(C\),
adjoining the specified Frobenius class gives the
conjugacy-invariant product set \(C\times C_j\) over \(A\).
The fibres have exactly 24 possible residual elements,
so the class density in this set is \(|C_j|/24\) times
the old one. This ratio is independent of the cutoff.
The finite replay lists all such classes and their sizes;
for instance trace zero and trace different from zero each
have nonempty choices.

Use [Frei–Tinková v3 Theorem 1.4 and formulas (1.10),(1.15)](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17441v3#S1.SS2)
with the finite union of resulting classes. Its local
compatibility uses the unchanged maximal abelian subfield,
and all old positive local factors remain, with the fixed
multiplicative factor \(\prod_j|C_j|/24\).
The bad-neighbor upper bounds from the original proof bound
a larger set, so are unchanged. Hence one can choose the
cutoff large after this fixed loss and then take the
coordinate range large at that fixed cutoff, just as in the
original proof. The old Gaussian representative orientations,
nonparallel forms, common source \(h\), and exact Ready
outputs are still available. In particular the original
Gaussian congruence/order conditions alone cannot determine
even the individual \(a_{p_j}(E_0)\bmod3\) at the selected
contact/denominator primes.

This is an actual refinement freedom, **not** a relation
between such Frobenius choices and the mod-3 Heegner coefficient.

## 3. No ordinary residual transfer between these exact twists

Any quadratic self-twist \(\chi\) of \(\rho\) is first trivial
on \(\ker\rho\): the trace of identity is 2, nonzero modulo 3.
The only possible remaining nontrivial character on the full
image is determinant. But a matrix of determinant \(-1\)
and trace 1 is, for example, \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\).
Thus \(\rho\) has no nontrivial quadratic self-twist.

The same argument holds after any imaginary quadratic
base change \(K'\). The residual image upon restriction is
either the full group or its determinant-one index-two
subgroup. In the second case there is no nontrivial
group character of order 2 at all. For two twists with
distinct positive ratio class \(\theta\), their relative
character remains nontrivial on \(G_{K'}\) because
\(\mathbf Q(\sqrt\theta)\) is real. Twisting both
representations back to \(\rho\) therefore proves they are
not isomorphic even over a common imaginary quadratic base.
There is no mod-3 residual identification with a different
quadratic twist supplied by the Gaussian ray.

Thus the usual residual-identification hypothesis of
[Kriz–Li's Heegner logarithm congruence, Theorem 2.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03172)
cannot be obtained in this way. That theorem concerns
\(p\)-adic logarithms, not automatically this finite
inert-13 or inert-19 point receiver, but even its first
hypothesis already fails for the proposed quadratic transfer.
The mod-2 residual equivalence of the same two twists is
irrelevant to this objection.

One can make the two twists isomorphic over the real field
\(F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt\theta)\), but this simply joins their
two distinct eigenspaces: by Artin formalism
\(L(E_{n_1}/F,s)=L(E_{n_1}/\mathbf Q,s)L(E_{n_2}/\mathbf Q,s)\).
The two actual selected signs are both negative, so this
product has at least a double zero. This is not the
ordinary rank-one base-change situation. It supplies no
comparison of the two finite nonzero tests and does not
exclude some stronger theorem for their joint order-two
leading term.

The special case \(\theta=1\) needs no transfer, and other
non-residual comparison methods are not ruled out here.
