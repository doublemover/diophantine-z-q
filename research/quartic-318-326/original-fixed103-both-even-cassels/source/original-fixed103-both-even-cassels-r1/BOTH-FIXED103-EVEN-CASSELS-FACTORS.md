# Both even factors for the fixed-103 original detector selector

**Input and scope.** Start with one original detector Ready pole after the fixed-103 source protection, its exact six genuine primes \(d\), the prepared \(\beta=17d\), and the actual positive squarefree companion \(\theta=[1-b/17]\). The old protected data and mark are as in the immutable *A fixed final prime on both literal detector twists* selector. In particular \(\theta\equiv1\bmod24\), it is coprime to \(\beta\cdot103\), and the true aggregate mutual symbols against \(\beta\) are positive. The \(\theta\) primes may have arbitrary individual types and true graph. Keeping this same pole, \(q_0=103\), and both native size-eight marked Selmer groups, the following modified helper choice gives
\[
\operatorname{rank}E_{-c}(\mathbb Q)=\operatorname{rank}E_{-c\theta}(\mathbb Q)=0,\qquad
\Sha(E_{-c})[2^\infty]\simeq\Sha(E_{-c\theta})[2^\infty]\simeq(\mathbb Z/2)^2,
\]
where the two actual odd twists are \(E_{1751c}\) and \(E_{1751c\theta}\). This is an algebraic claim. A separate rank-zero preprint implication gives both even central values if adopted. No odd weighted phase, odd rank, PointPair, numeric key or new source specialization is supplied.

## 1. True directed even descent, including arbitrary \(\theta\) prime types

Let \(C>0\) be odd squarefree with \(C\equiv1\bmod24\). On
\(E_{-C}:y^2=x(x+C)(x-3C)\)
use ordered coordinates \((x+C,x-3C)\). As in the exact even-auxiliary predecessor, the rational 2-torsion has images \((C,-3C),(1,-C),(C,3)\); the independent sign/3 bits of the second coordinate normalize each pure Selmer class uniquely to a pair of positive squarefree factors of \(C\). At real places the first coordinate is nonnegative (using the exceptional torsion value at the root); good odd places away from 3 and \(C\) allow exactly the unramified classes. At 3 the exact Tate torsion table forces the first coordinate square and allows all second classes. At 2, \(C\equiv1\bmod8\) gives the original \(E_{-1}\) model, and its local double-quotient has eight elements. Its four torsion classes have first coordinate 1 and all second odd unit classes; a formal point with \(z=-x/y=2\) has first unit class 5. This gives exactly first local classes \(\{1,5\}\), any second odd unit class, and no odd valuation at 2. These are the same local computations as for the known first even factor.

Index the primes \(p\mid C\), let \(r_p=[(-1/p)=-1]\), \(\lambda_p=[(3/p)=-1]\), and let \(L\) be the **directed** additive Legendre Laplacian (row denominators, off-diagonal \([(\ell/p)=-1]\), diagonals giving row sum zero). At \(p\mid C\), rational 2-torsion exhausts the local quotient (an open pro-\(p\) group is uniquely 2-divisible; the finite quotient has doubling index equal to kernel size). Its four classes are distinct by their two valuation bits. With valuation pair \((X_p,Y_p)\) the first stripped torsion class is that of \((C/p)^{X_p}\), and the second that of \(3^{X_p}(-1)^{Y_p}(C/p)^{Y_p}\), where \(C/p\) is the integer quotient. Thus the exact prime equations on global exponent vectors \(X,Y\) are
\[
                 L X=0,\qquad M Y+\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)X=0,
                 \qquad M=L+\operatorname{diag}(r).
\]
Quadratic reciprocity gives \(L^T=L+rr^T+\operatorname{diag}(r)\), and since \(r^T\mathbf1=0\), \(1^TL=r^T\), \(1^TM=0\), \(M\mathbf1=r\). The two equations imply \(r^TX=0\) and \(\lambda^TX=0\), which are exactly the combined 2,3 restrictions on the first coordinate (at 3 the exponent is \((\lambda+r)^TX\)). No local condition remains. In addition
\[
                         \ker(M^T)=\ker L.
\]
Indeed \(M^T=L+rr^T\), and a vector in either kernel has \(r^TX=0\) by summing its corresponding equations. This identity does not assume symmetric \(\theta\) rows.

We arrange \(C=C_A b_0\) with no cross edges, \(b_0\) an isolated type-five prime, \(C_A\equiv b_0\equiv5\bmod24\), and \(\ker L_A=\langle\mathbf1_A\rangle\). The left kernel of \(M\) consists exactly of the two component indicators. Their pairing with \(\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)X\) forces both constants of \(X\) to vanish, because independently \(\sum_{p\mid C_A}\lambda_p=\lambda_{b_0}=1\). Thus the pure group is exactly dimension two, with representatives \(\alpha_0=(1,b_0,b_0)\) and \(\alpha_a=(1,a,a)\), where \(a\) is the support product of the unique nonzero right vector \(Y_A\) of \(M_A\). If every main prime is 1 modulo 4, then \(a=C_A\). Otherwise no assumption about its mod-8 class is made.

## 2. Independently adjust the odd and even companion graphs

Start from the fixed-103 selector just **before** its companion-pivot repairs: it already has first odd invertibility, a connected old even main core, a type-five leaf there, a type-five singleton \(b_0\) after removing 17 and 103, and an old unmarked type-one helper \(v\) in the main core. Appending \(\theta\) changes no old genuine diagonal because \((\theta/p)=+\) at each \(p\mid\beta\); old helpers except named attachments have zero \(\theta\) edges. The resulting sharp marked kernel stays exact by \((\beta/p)=+\) at each \(p\mid\theta\). Replace the earlier companion repair with two independent gadgets.

For each gadget take four fresh type-one helpers, numbered \(0,1,2,3\), with internal path \(2-1-0-3\). An attachment mask has bit \(i\) for an edge to helper \(i\); the masks to the current old unmarked \(v\), to \(103\), and to a selected \(\theta\) vertex \(j\) are respectively:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc|cccc}
 &v&103&j&\text{first even}&\text{first odd}&\text{companion even}&\text{companion odd}\\
\mathrm O&13&15&15&0&0&0&\text{diagonal flip at }j\\
\mathrm B&4&7&12&0&0&\text{edge }v-j&0 .
\end{array}
\]
With \(j\) absent in the first arms, use no \(j\) edges at all. In even arms 103 is absent; in odd arms its edge contributes to the helper diagonal before the 103 row/column is deleted. No helper has an edge to a marked vertex or the isolated \(b_0\). The helper sharpening entries are zero (type one). The internal block has row masks off its diagonal \((10,5,2,1)\) and diagonal the total incident parity. The replay gives all eight inverses and three entries of each Schur update, including the original external diagonal increments. This is ordinary elimination, also valid on the nonsymmetric outside core.

First use flags \(\mathrm O\) to set each successive actual companion **odd** \(\theta\) pivot to one, eliminating the old invertible sharp core first. Each flag adds only its own diagonal parameter, so the construction works for any actual \(\theta\) off-diagonals. It changes neither first odd nor either even core. Next choose \(\mathrm B\) flags independently for companion **even** control. Delete the row/column of old \(v\) within the main component. The old block is invertible before extending by \(\theta\) by the original first-even connected graph calculation, and it stays the same because each genuine old row's total \(\theta\) incidence is zero. The gadgets \(\mathrm B\) add precisely one controlled diagonal for each \(j\) in this \(v\)-deleted matrix. Eliminate the old block and successively choose each \(j\)'s pivot to be one. All actual \(\theta\)-to-old and \(\theta\)-to-\(\theta\) off-diagonals are left as given. Thus the companion even main \(L_A\) has rank exactly one less than its dimension, while both \(\mathrm O\) and \(\mathrm B\) leave both first-arm properties intact. No cross edges to \(b_0\) are introduced. All these helpers are \(1\bmod24\), so the required component residue persists. Empty \(\theta\) simply needs no gadgets.

## 3. The companion Cassels sign and an exact sign switch

Here take \(C=C_A b_0\) to be the even companion and use the pure space of §1. Compute the pairing in the order \(\langle\alpha_0,\alpha_a\rangle\): unlike the first-even symmetry calculation, the *test* class \(a\) need not be the whole main support. Every prime dividing either component is a square unit at the other component in the aggregate sense needed below; in fact this follows from the absence of individual cross edges with the type-five singleton.

The quadratic norm \(\xi^2-b_0\zeta^2=-C_A\) is soluble over \(\mathbb Q\). At \(p\mid b_0\) the target is a square unit by no-cross and \((-1/p)=+\); at \(p\mid C_A\) the extension splits; elsewhere at odd primes the unit target is a local norm; at 2 the quadratic extension for \(b_0\equiv5\bmod8\) is unramified; at infinity it splits. Local-global for the norm conic and weak approximation allow \(\xi\in 8\mathbb Z_2\), \(\zeta\) odd, since \(C_A/b_0\) is a square unit at 2. The first covering, for \(\alpha_0\), has the same ordered conics as in the known even factor with component parameters \(b_0,C_A\). The rational tangent factors, after dividing by the two rational constants as in that calculation, are
\[
       L_2=\zeta z_1-\xi z_3-C_A t,\qquad
       L_3=\zeta z_1-\xi z_2-2C_A t .
\]
The ordered Cassels pairing is \(\prod_v(L_2 L_3,a)_v\); the first test entry is 1 so the third tangent is irrelevant. At \(p\mid b_0\), \(a\) is square. At \(p\mid C_A\), take \(t=0,z_1=\sqrt{b_0},z_2=z_3=\pm1\); then \(L_2=L_3\), even when \(p\mid a\). At 3, \(C_A\equiv2\bmod3\), so \(t=1,z_1=0,z_3=\sqrt{-C_A},z_2=2z_3\) gives \(L_3=2L_2\); both 2 and \(a\) are units at this odd prime. At good odd primes outside \(3C\), the simultaneous primitive normalization of the two tangent centers changes their product by a square; each is a doubled tangent divisor on the smooth conic, giving even evaluation order against the unit \(a\). At infinity \(a>0\). Choose signs or a local perturbation when needed to avoid an exact zero.

At 2 take \(t=1,z_1=\sqrt C,z_3=0,z_2=\sqrt{-3C_A}\). Both square roots are available by the residue conditions. The exact identity \((\zeta\sqrt C)^2=C_A(C_A+\xi^2)\) with \(v_2(\xi)\ge3\) lets us choose the sign \(\zeta\sqrt C\equiv-C_A\bmod16\) (compare odd squares modulo 64). Then \(L_2/2\equiv-C_A\bmod8\), \(L_3\equiv-3C_A\bmod8\). Thus \(L_2L_3\) has squareclass 6, and
\[
              \langle\alpha_0,\alpha_a\rangle=(6,a)_2=(-2/a).
\]
The symbol is positive for \(a\equiv1,3\) and negative for \(a\equiv5,7\bmod8\). If all main primes are 1 modulo 4 then \(a=C_A\equiv5\bmod8\), already negative.

If some main prime is 3 modulo 4, then \(Y_A\ne0,\mathbf1_A\), since \(M_A\mathbf1_A=r_A\ne0\). We can obtain unmarked helper representatives of differing \(Y_A\) bits without changing the four cores:

- An old helper is its own representative. For any genuine prime \(i\), use two fresh type-one helpers, each attached to \(i,17,103\) and no other vertex. They have no internal edge. In either odd core the two unit helper pivots and their identical even marked incidence sum to zero net Schur update. In either even main core these are simply ordinary leaves at \(i\), so each copies its \(Y_A\) bit.
- For a \(\theta\) vertex \(j\), take two copies of \(\mathrm B\) at \(j\). There is no net update (the even bridge toggles twice); in the companion even solve, numbered helpers 0 and 2 are exactly copies of \(Y_j\), as read from the two attachment columns of the inverse in the replay. In the first even group all four internal helpers copy \(Y_v\). If all non-gadget old main and \(\theta\) values had been constant, all type-one gadget equations would extend that same constant (row-sum-zero), so nonconstancy is detected among such external vertices or an already existing helper.

Attach two more type-five prime leaves, one at each chosen unmarked representative of values 0 and 1. In either even matrix each new leaf copies its parent in the right kernel, and leaf elimination does not alter either main cofactor. The pair keeps the main residue modulo 24 and its total \(\lambda\) unchanged. For the companion test \(a\) its factor contributes exactly one new class \(5\bmod8\), which flips \((-2/a)\). It does not change the first even factor proof: its main has no 3-mod-4 prime and still has product \(5\bmod24\), so that even pairing is negative as well.

For either odd factor, one such type-five leaf at unmarked parent \(w\) has unit pivot 1 and, after cancellation of the two ordinary diagonal contributions, adds \(e_w e_{17}^T\) to the deleted sharp core. If \(\mathbf b\) is the exact \(\beta\)-indicator, then \(\mathbf b^T B=e_{17}^T\) on the current invertible sharp core \(B\): unsharp \(L\mathbf b=0\) also gives \(L^T\mathbf b=0\) by reciprocity, since every prime in \(\beta\) is 1 mod 4, and \(\mathbf b^T\lambda=1\) with only 17 on its support. Hence \(e_{17}^TB^{-1}e_w=\mathbf b_w=0\), so determinant and marked kernel are unchanged. This applies successively to both leaves and does not require symmetry of the \(\theta\) block. Add the pair only if the initial sign needs the flip.

Every genuine prime and actual \(\theta\) factor is already fixed. The new helpers are all 1 modulo 4, so each required symmetric bit against any old prime is obtained by a single rational residue; any two fresh helper bits are imposed when choosing the later one. Independent type \(1\) or \(5\bmod24\), avoidance of the old support and any separately protected small primes, and an optional square condition at an auxiliary prime outside that support are compatible by CRT and Dirichlet. The new helpers never change the true fixed origination of the pole, \(d,\beta,\theta\), source polynomial, or \(q_0=103\).

## 4. What this yields on the fixed base, and the remaining odd input

Both even curves now have a nondegenerate pairing on their exact pure dimension-two spaces. Rational Kummer classes lie in the radical; hence both rational ranks are zero, and the pure classes are \(\Sha[2]\) for each. An order-4 element of \(\Sha\) would double to a nonzero element of \(\Sha[2]\) pairing trivially with all of \(\Sha[2]\), contradicting nondegeneracy. This proves the stated finite 2-primary groups. The full \(2^\infty\)-Selmer coranks are zero, and the two-division fields are rational. Thus [Burungale–Tian, arXiv:2611.11566v1, Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11566v1#ThmtheoremA), **if invoked as a separate rank-zero preprint theorem**, implies \(L(E_{-c},1)L(E_{-c\theta},1)\ne0\). No rank-one converse is used.

The same fixed conductor-free base \(K_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-1751})\) can therefore be used on both arms: their quadratic ring-class conductor characters \(\chi_c,\chi_{c\theta}\) have their actual exact conductors \(c,c\theta\), both coprime to \(6\cdot1751\), and auxiliary factors \(E_{-c},E_{-c\theta}\). The usual split-level Rankin factorization and Gross–Zagier receiver are those of the first-even predecessor, with \(c\theta\) substituted in arm two. The source protection and selector still leave genuinely primitive conductor data. Both odd weighted phases remain undetermined. The simultaneous use of a shared fixed CM base plus two nonzero even factors does not itself prove either odd trace nonzero or a marked PointPair.
