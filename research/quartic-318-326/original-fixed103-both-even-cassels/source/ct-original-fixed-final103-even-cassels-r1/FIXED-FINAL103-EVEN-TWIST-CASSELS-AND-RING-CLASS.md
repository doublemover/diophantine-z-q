# The fixed-final-prime selector has a nonzero even auxiliary factor

Let $q_0=103$ and use the actual fixed-final-prime selector. In its first arm write $n=1751c$ with $c=c_1c_2>0$. The prime support of $c$ is in two graph components with no cross edges. The binary Legendre Laplacian on each component has kernel precisely the constants. In each of the two components exactly one prime is $5\bmod24$, and all others are $1\bmod24$; in the constructed second component $c_2$ is simply the isolated type-five prime. Hence $c_1,c_2\equiv5\bmod24$, and $c\equiv1\bmod24$. We prove an exact nondegenerate first Cassels pairing on $E_{-c}$, giving rank zero and $\Sha(E_{-c})[2^\infty]\simeq (\mathbf Z/2)^2$ without a rank converse. A separate recent rank-zero theorem gives the required nonzero central value if invoked. This is not positive rank of $E_n$ or of $E_{n\theta}$.

## 1. Exact pure 2-Selmer group of the even auxiliary twist

Use $B=E_{-c}:y^2=x(x+c)(x-3c)$ with ordered roots $-c,3c,0$ and the ordered first two squareclasses $(x+c,x-3c)$. Full local Kummer coordinates use the actual exceptional root products. The ordered torsion images of $(0,0),(-c,0),(3c,0)$ are
$$
       (c,-3c),\qquad (1,-c),\qquad(c,3).
$$
At good odd primes the valuations are even by the three distinct reduced roots. At a prime dividing $c$ the complete local quotient by doubling is its rational 2-torsion image: an open odd-residue-characteristic formal group is uniquely 2-divisible, and the finite quotient shows the local doubling index is the size of its rational kernel. The four listed classes including identity are distinct by their first two valuation bits. At 3 the curve is split multiplicative with Tate depth two ($c\equiv1\bmod3$); this gives a local doubling quotient of size four. In the same torsion table the first coordinate at 3 is always a square and the second exhausts all four local classes. The local torsion itself has order at most four: the Tate valuation modulo two has torsion kernel only $\{\pm1\}$ in $\mathbf Z_3^\times$. Hence the global rational torsion is exactly its four named 2-torsion points.

At 2 the twist by $c$ is locally trivial since $c\equiv1\bmod8$. The local doubling quotient of $E_0$ has size eight, since its sufficiently deep logarithm group has rank one over $\mathbf Z_2$ and its rational 2-kernel has order four. The four local torsion classes have first class 1 and second classes all four odd unit classes. An actual formal point with parameter $z=-x/y=2$ has $4x=1\bmod8$ by $1/z^2=x-2-3/x$, so the stripped units of both $x+1$ and $x-3$ are $5\bmod8$. Thus its Kummer class is the other coset, and every local Kummer coordinate has even 2-valuation. In particular the torsion classes account for all possibilities with first class locally square.

Consequently any global Selmer class, modulo the three nontrivial rational torsion representatives, can be uniquely written as two positive squarefree integers supported on $c$. Indeed the real first coordinate is always positive, its 3-valuation is zero, and neither coordinate has a 2-bit. In the second coordinate remove sign and 3 using the independent torsion bits $-1$ and $3$. This uniquely normalizes torsion because the torsion classes have distinct second-coordinate sign/3 pairs. Write the resulting coordinates by exponent vectors $X,Y$ on the primes of $c$. Let $L_c$ be the binary row-sum-zero Legendre Laplacian and $\lambda_p=[(3/p)=-1]$. Stripping the local torsion table at each prime $p\mid c$ gives exactly
$$
        L_c X=0,qquad L_c Y+\operatorname{diag}(\lambda)X=0.
$$
For example, the first stripped symbol of the torsion point of valuation $(X_p,Y_p)$ is that of $(c/p)^{X_p}$, and the second is $((c/p)^{Y_p})(3/p)^{X_p}$. This uses $-1$ and 4 square at every such odd prime. At 3 the first local-square condition is $\lambda^{\mathsf T}X=0$. Whenever it holds the first 2-adic coordinate is also square, since only the type-five primes are nonsquare modulo 8; either second odd unit is allowed. At other good odd primes the whole unramified 2-Kummer subgroup is allowed: the local group has four classes by good reduction and rational 2-kernel, and the smooth proper Kummer map is unramified. This checks both directions of the displayed local conditions.

Now $L_cX=0$ makes $X$ constant on each component. Pairing the second equation with either component indicator forces that component's constant to be zero, since exactly one $\lambda_p=1$ occurs there. Thus $X=0$ and $Y$ is independently constant on each component. The exact dimension-two pure Selmer group is generated by the two triples (the third is the product of the first two)
$$
                \alpha_1=(1,c_1,c_1),\qquad
                \alpha_2=(1,c_2,c_2).
$$

## 2. Two simultaneous tangent forms and their single dyadic sign

The $\alpha_1$ covering is
$$
 z_1^2-c_1z_2^2=4c_1c_2 t^2,\quad
 z_1^2-c_1z_3^2=c_1c_2 t^2,\quad
 z_2^2-z_3^2=-3c_2 t^2 .
$$
The norm equation
$$
                   a^2-c_1 b^2=-c_2
$$
has a rational solution. At odd primes dividing $c_1$, the target is a square unit by the no-cross hypothesis and $-1$ is square. At primes dividing $c_2$ the extension is split by the same no-cross hypothesis. At other odd primes the norm on units of an unramified quadratic extension is surjective (or the extension is split); at 2, $c_1\equiv5\bmod8$ gives the unramified quadratic extension and the target is an odd unit. At infinity $c_1>0$ is split. The quadratic norm principle (equivalently Hasse–Minkowski for this conic) proves the global assertion. Choose the solution by weak approximation on this rational conic so that $a\in4\mathbf Z_2$ and $b\in\mathbf Z_2^\times$: there is a local seed with $a=0$, $b=\sqrt{c_2/c_1}$ since the ratio is 1 modulo 8. A nonzero rational $a$ is automatic for a rational solution since $c_2/c_1$ is not a rational square.

The conics omitting $z_2$ and $z_3$ have exact rational points $(z_1,z_3,t)=(c_1b,a,1)$ and $(z_1,z_2,t)=(2c_1b,2a,1)$. Their tangent functions, divided by the indicated nonzero global constants, are
$$
         L_2=bz_1-az_3-c_2t,\qquad L_3=bz_1-az_2-2c_2t .
$$
The third conic is split at $t=0$ and does not enter since the first coordinate of $\alpha_2$ is 1. The full split-root [Cassels tangent formula](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1998.001) for this ordered pair is
$$
        \langle\alpha_1,\alpha_2\rangle
        =\prod_v(L_2 L_3,c_2)_v.
$$
Choose local points separately at each place, avoiding any exact zero by the named sign or an arbitrarily small perturbation; the local invariant is unchanged.

- At any $p\mid c_1$ the second Hilbert variable is square. At $p\mid c_2$, take $t=0$, $z_1=\sqrt{c_1}$ and $z_2=z_3=\pm1$ using local squarehood of $c_1$. Then $L_2=L_3$ and their product is a nonzero square with a choice of sign. At infinity $c_2>0$.
- At 3 take $t=1,z_1=0,z_3=\sqrt{-c_2},z_2=2z_3$. It satisfies the actual covering since $-c_2\equiv1\bmod3$. Then $L_3=2L_2$, so the product is 2 times a square; its Hilbert symbol with the unit $c_2$ is positive.
- At an odd good prime off $3c$, use a projectively primitive integral local tuple. Each projected conic triple is primitive as well, by the other equation with unit coefficients. The two rational tangent centers may need to be scaled to primitive integral points; the same scalar normalizes both at such a prime since 2 and $c_1,c_2$ are units. Scaling the two tangent values together changes their product by a square. On each smooth split conic the primitive tangent cuts twice its section, so its valuation at a primitive point is even (locally a unit times the square of a parameter, including at points reducing to that section). This makes the symbol against the unit $c_2$ positive.
- At 2 take $t=1,z_1=\sqrt c,z_3=0,z_2=\sqrt{-3c_2}$, all the displayed units being squares by $c\equiv1$, $c_2\equiv5\bmod8$. The second tangent $L_3$ is odd. The first tangent is $b\sqrt c-c_2$. Both choices of the sign of $\sqrt c$ give even nonzero values whose difference has valuation one, and whose product is $-c_2 a^2$ by the norm equation. Their valuations sum to an even number, and their minimum is exactly one, so both valuations are odd. Since $c_2$ is the unramified 5-class, the dyadic symbol is therefore **minus**.

This gives a nondegenerate alternating form on the exact two-dimensional pure Selmer group; it is not just an upper estimate or one local symbol without the others. Rational Mordell–Weil classes are in the radical. Its nondegeneracy gives rank zero and identifies the pure group with $\Sha(B)[2]$. Moreover an element of order four would double to a nonzero radical element, by the bilinearity of the Cassels–Tate pairing with multiplication by two. Hence $\Sha(B)[2^\infty]=\Sha(B)[2]\simeq(\mathbf Z/2)^2$.

## 3. The live weighted rank-one side is still a different input

To infer analytic information about the **even** factor, invoke separately [Burungale–Tian, Theorem A, arXiv:2611.11566v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11566v1). Its premises hold: the two-division field is rational (in particular abelian), and the full $2^\infty$-Selmer group is finite by the preceding exact arithmetic. The theorem gives
$$
                     L(E_{-c},1)\ne 0 .
$$
This invokes the newly stated rank-zero result of that independent manuscript, not the pointwise rank-one converse. The algebraic rank and 2-primary Tate–Shafarevich assertions above are independent of this analytic import.

The base field for the first selected odd twist is now the *fixed* $K_0=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-1751})$, with conductor $c$ variable. Both 2 and 3 split in $K_0$, and $c$ avoids $2,3,17,103$. The real genus character obtained by restricting the quadratic character of $\mathbf Q(\sqrt c)$ to $K_0$ has exact ring class conductor $c$: at every prime $\ell\mid c$ the local norm is surjective on residue units in the unramified quadratic case, and the split case is the product norm. The nontrivial quadratic residue character is trivial on central rational units (their norm is a square) and on $1+\ell\mathcal O$, but nontrivial modulo $\ell$. This is the actual weighted ring class character, not a replacement fundamental source discriminant. Its Rankin $L$-series factors as
$$
                L(E_0,\chi_c,s)=L(E_n,s)L(E_{-c},s).
$$
The Heegner hypotheses for the split-level formula of [Cai–Shu–Tian, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1733) apply to this fixed field and the conductor. The same modular map is used, with the conductor-$c$ CM orbit and quadratic weights. The weighted point lies rationally in the two twist eigensides over $K_0(\sqrt c)$; the even side is rank zero, and this field splits at 3, so its total rational $E_0$ torsion is exactly the four named 2-torsion points by the same local Tate bound. Thus one nonzero odd residue phase of that **weighted** point would certify nontorsion of the first original $E_n$ and the mark via its actual size-eight Selmer group. The even factor alone does not provide such a phase or rank, and this paper does not claim the same even-factor calculation for $E_{-c\theta}$.

This gives a fixed base of 48 ideal classes; the new conductor fibres and their weights still depend on the actual very large $c$. Nothing in the argument permits replacing them by the pure Hecke average or omitting the marked vertical kernel information. A concrete certified primitive weighted phase remains the precise arithmetic input.
