# Arbitrary mod-13 Frobenius is compatible with the exact one-I ray, but it does not supply the two seed coefficients

This is an exact scope result for the genuine paired arithmetic ray, not new evidence that the two needed negative coefficients either vanish or survive. Use the existing fixed scalar B, p=114649, literal unknown θ and a fully completed common q ray; optionally impose the already proved simultaneous r₈=0 refinement.

## The exact residual representation has full image

Use the actual A=E₋₁:y²=x(x+1)(x−3). Its mod-13 representation ρ on A[13] has image GL₂(F₁₃):

* A has #A(F₇)=8, hence Frobenius at 7 has trace zero and characteristic discriminant −28≡11 mod13, a nonsquare. The representation therefore cannot have a rational invariant line.
* At 3 it has split multiplication: c₄=208 is a unit, the reduced node of y²=x²(x+1) has tangents ±x, and the given discriminant 2304 has valuation 2, which is already minimal. The Tate parameter has valuation 2. In the Tate-curve description of A[13], inertia at 3 acts unipotently and nontrivially on the 13th root of the parameter, since adjoining it over the maximal unramified field has ramification degree 13 and the 13th roots of unity are unramified. Thus it supplies a nonidentity transvection. This is the standard elementary local Tate-module calculation ([Silverman, Advanced Topics, V §3](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-0851-8)).
* Some image conjugate moves the transvection's fixed line by irreducibility. In a basis of the two fixed lines the cyclic groups generated by these two nonidentity elements are exactly the upper and lower elementary additive groups over the **prime** field F₁₃: repeated powers range over all scalars. They generate SL₂(F₁₃) by elementary row reduction. The determinant, the mod-13 cyclotomic character, is onto, so the image is all GL₂(F₁₃). In particular the representation is absolutely irreducible.

The already proved full Brandt Hecke correspondence on the actual order-13 marked line gives a_q(g)≡a_q(A) mod13 for every good q; no trace at a handful of test primes is substituted for that all-prime operator identity. For the Hecke reduction below, it suffices to use this equality and the literal field Q(A[13]), rather than a new comparison of characteristic-zero Hecke lifts.

## Independent of both genuine 8-rank toggles

Let M be the abelian field of the completed ray, enlarged to include Q(ζ₁₃), and L=M F₀ F₁ the composite with the two dihedral governing fields from the exact r₈ proof. Its Galois group is solvable. The intersection L∩Q(A[13]) is exactly Q(ζ₁₃): it contains the determinant field, and any solvable quotient of GL₂(F₁₃) kills its perfect SL₂(F₁₃) subgroup. For example SL₂ is perfect because commutators with diag(2,2⁻¹) yield every upper and lower elementary generator (the relevant multiplier 2²−1, or its inverse analogue, is a unit).

Choose any Frobenius conjugacy class of Gal(L/Q) that satisfies the original whole ray and the two desired r₈ tests. Its action on ζ₁₃ is one fixed δ∈F₁₃×. For **every** matrix C∈GL₂(F₁₃) of determinant δ, the two automorphisms agree on the precise intersection and hence lift together to the compositum. Chebotarev on this exact compositum yields infinitely many single rational primes q realizing that pair, preserving all old ray and governing conditions. Thus one can impose any trace t simultaneously with both original arithmetic selectors, using for instance C=[[0,−δ],[1,t]]. One can even impose any permitted conjugacy class, not just its trace. If desired the determinant residue q mod13 can itself first be fixed to any compatible unit by enlarging the abelian field; the previous central-involution argument still applies. Nothing here prescribes a numerical q.

## Why this cannot transport the seed

By the exact direct/negative comparison, what is required is the pair of fundamental-index coefficients A⁻₃₇(Rq), A⁻₃₇(Rθq) of the same weight-3/2 source. Its unramified half-integral Hecke operator T(ℓ²) has Fourier terms at indices ℓ²m, m, m/ℓ², with a diagonal quadratic-symbol factor. Thus at good primes both these operators and all their finite compositions, with the usual diagonal twists, stay within the same squareclass of m. In particular Chebotarev supplies the eigenvalues for this recurrence, not its initial value at a new squareclass. Applying Shimura at a varying fundamental index gives the same residual GL₂ eigenpacket but the first coefficient of that Shimura series is the very unknown normalization A⁻₃₇(Rq) or A⁻₃₇(Rθq). Replacing the normalized eigenform coefficient a_q(g) by either of those two initial coefficients is not a Hecke identity. Nor would introducing a fresh ℓ² at the seed change the twist.

For this actual ray R and θ are fixed and q is always a fresh prime outside both supports. The two squareclasses are distinct and unbounded; there is no old finite calibration in either squareclass (including the old substitute pairs), and no finite union of fixed squareclasses can acquire them by these recurrences. The Galois theorem does not say a global relation between initial values is impossible, or that specific normalized mod-13 nonvanishing cannot be proved. It shows exactly what is missing from the proposed finite-Hecke/Chebotarev tactic: a genuinely new fundamental coefficient or a global simultaneous nonvanishing theorem. An integral-weight prime-coefficient Frobenius theorem by itself is not that input.
