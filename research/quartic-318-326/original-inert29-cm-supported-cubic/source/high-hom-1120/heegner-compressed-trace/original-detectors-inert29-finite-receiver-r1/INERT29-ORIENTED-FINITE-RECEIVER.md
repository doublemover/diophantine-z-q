# A 68-coordinate inert-29 receiver for the fixed level-48 detector trace

This note takes the exact balanced two-eights construction and the raw
level-48 map as input. It supplies no detector pole, selected pair, CM
multiplicity, nonzero trace, or rational point.

## 1. One good reduction per exact arm

Let \(D=-n<0\) be one selected fundamental discriminant with
\(D\equiv1\pmod{24}\), \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt D)\), and \(H\) its Hilbert
class field. Fix a CM type and the level ideal
\(\mathfrak n=\mathfrak p_2^4\mathfrak p_3\). Define
\[
q_{\mathfrak a}=(\mathbf C/\mathfrak a\longrightarrow
\mathbf C/(\mathfrak a\mathfrak n^{-1})),\quad
P=\sum_{[\mathfrak a]\in\operatorname{Pic}(O_K)}\phi(q_{\mathfrak a}),
\qquad S=P-cP
\]
where \(c\) is the nontrivial automorphism of \(K\). The exact raw
map to \(E_0:y^2=x(x+1)(x-3)\) and its Fricke action are
\[
C:Y^2=X^8+14X^4+1,\quad
x=\frac{Y+X^4+1}{2X^2},\quad y=x(X-1/X),
\quad w_{48}(X,Y)=((X+1)/(X-1),4Y/(X-1)^4).
\]
At \(X=0\), or at infinity using \(Y/X^4\), the plus branch maps
to \(O\) and the minus branch to \((0,0)\). Chord addition gives
\(\phi w_{48}=T_3-\phi\), \(T_3=(3,0)\). Conjugation and the Fricke
involution both flip the two level arms, up to a permutation of the
class index. Therefore \(cP=h(D)T_3-P\) and \([2]S=[4]P\). This
shortcut is for the raw map, not an arbitrary rational translate.

At a good \(\ell\nmid6D\), take all reductions at one chosen place
of \(H\). Nonzero \([4]\bar P\) implies \([2]S\ne O\) globally; as
rational torsion on the selected twist is killed by 2, the
anti-invariant descent of \(S\) on that exact twist is nontorsion.
For clarity the descent is \((x,y)\mapsto(Dx,D\sqrt D\,y)\).
Both arms need a nonzero. On the verified two-eights family, rank
at least one exhausts the exact Selmer group by the rational Kummer
injection. It supplies the specified nontorsion \((\beta,1)\)
class, not necessarily the class of the initial witness. For
\(B=17b\), write the marked abscissas \(B r_i^2\).
Since \(n_2/n_1=B^2\delta\eta^2\), dividing the two sets of
coordinates by \((r_1^2,r_1^3)\) and
\(((r_1\eta)^2,(r_1\eta)^3)\) gives the literal
\(\operatorname{PointPair}(b)\) on the same pole and index.
The degree-two graph is an independent sufficient route to these
equations; nothing follows about its Jacobian here.

If \(\ell\) splits, the \(H\)-residue degree over the chosen
\(K\)-place is \(f=\operatorname{ord}[\mathfrak l]\), by unramified
class reciprocity. A complete oriented orbit can be supplied
as one representative per Frobenius cycle, with its actual length;
the class action has order \(f\) before forgetting the marking.
Add each cycle as \(T_f(Q)=\sum_{k=0}^{f-1}F^k\bar\phi(Q)\)
using \(T_{2m}=T_m+F^mT_m\). The resulting trace lies in
\(E_0(\mathbf F_\ell)\). It is not enough to certify an arbitrary
Frobenius-invariant sum of chart points. For example an ordinary
oriented seed and a complete prime-to-\(48\ell\) ideal-class action
can certify the actual reductions. At \(\ell=29\) the image of
multiplication by 4 in \(E_0(\mathbf F_{29})\) is generated by
\((23,7)\) of order 3.

## 2. Inert multiplicities and the correct orientation

If \(\ell\) is inert, \((\ell)\) is a principal prime of \(K\), so
has identity Frobenius in \(H/K\). All reductions are over
\(\mathbf F_{\ell^2}\). Frobenius \(F\) over \(\mathbf F_\ell\) on
that residue field restricts to \(c\) on the reduced \(K\)-place,
so termwise weights
\[
\omega(q)=[2](1-F)\bar\phi(q)
\]
sum to \([2]\bar S=[4]\bar P\) in the Frobenius-minus group.

At a supersingular marked label \(q=(A,C_{48})\), write
\(R_q=\operatorname{End}(A,C_{48})\).
[Cornut–Jetchev Corollary 4.1 and Remark 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.0725v1)
identify the maximal-order CM points reducing to \(q\) with
\(R_q^\times\)-classes of conjugate pairs of optimal embeddings
\(O_K\hookrightarrow R_q\). The residue choice in §3.6 Remark 1
selects exactly one embedding in each pair: its differential on
\(\operatorname{Lie} A\) agrees with the fixed reduction of the
characteristic-zero CM type. Indeed the two differentials are
distinct at an inert prime and conjugation by an automorphism of
\((A,C_{48})\) preserves the scalar on the tangent. Specialization
of the normalized characteristic-zero embedding has just that
differential. The conductor here is 1, prime to \(48\ell\).

Retain a normalized embedding \(\iota\) only if
\(C_{48}=A[\iota(\mathfrak n)]\) as the actual cyclic subgroup,
not merely as an isomorphic quotient. The 16-part needs the full
line, not only its 2-torsion. Reduction commutes with these
prime-to-\(\ell\) kernels. Conversely on the corresponding maximal
CM lift the stable cyclic subgroup lies on exactly one split arm
at each prime, and étale specialization distinguishes them.
The retained multiplicity \(m(q)\) counts the **one exact class
orbit**. Counting both tangent embeddings or all level
orientations and dividing uniformly is not this certificate.

A lattice issuer can enumerate trace-zero
\(v\in (2R_q+\mathbf Z)\) of norm \(|D|\) with
\((1+v)/2\in R_q\); this last element is the image of
\((1+\sqrt D)/2\), and maximality makes the embedding optimal.
Perform the chosen residue-Lie and both level-arm tests before
quotienting by \(R_q^\times\). The abstract ternary quadratic
form without these readings is insufficient. A certified
prime-to-\(48\ell\) ideal action from one actual seed instead
allows aggregation without constructing these lattices.

The involution \(g=F w_{48}\) preserves \(m\): a lift of \(F\)
acts as \(c\) on \(K\), flipping the two ideal arms, and
\(w_{48}\) reverses them back, with a permutation of classes.
Moreover \(\omega(Fq)=-\omega(q)\) and
\(\omega(w_{48}q)=-\omega(q)\), since \(T_3\) is
\(\mathbf F_\ell\)-rational. Thus \(\omega(gq)=\omega(q)\).
The counts need not be separately invariant under \(F\) or
under \(w_{48}\).

## 3. Complete finite arithmetic at 29

Use \(s^2=2\) and the coarse \(X_0(48)\) chart from
[Colò, Appendix A, pp. 247–248, Figure A.3](https://www.leonardocolo.com/documents/thesis/PhD_Thesis.pdf).
With \(y_{48}=(Y+X^4+1)/2\) its forgetful tower is
\[
t_{16}=X-2+3X/y_{48},\quad
t_8=t_{16}^2/(t_{16}+2),\quad t_4=t_8^2/(t_8+4),\quad
t_2=t_4^2/(t_4+16),\quad j=(t_2+256)^3/t_2^2 .
\]
A pole is retained as infinity. At \(X=0\) or infinity,
\(t_{16}=-2\) or a pole, and either case gives \(j=\infty\).
For finite \(X\ne0\), \(y_{48}\ne0\) by
\(y_{48}^2-(X^4+1)y_{48}=3X^4\).
The degree-eight polynomial has no multiple root outside
\(2,3\): its derivative is \(8X^3(X^4+7)\), and evaluating at
\(X=0\) or \(X^4=-7\) gives \(1\) or \(-48\).
The leading coefficient gives two smooth points at infinity.
Hence the standard good-reduction model and the given
forgetful tower reduce here, with no re-identification of the
generic modular curve by a new coordinate choice.

The complete replay examines every point on \(C(\mathbf
F_{29^2})\), the Hasse invariant on each possible \(j\), and the
full elliptic group. The supersingular \(j\)'s are \(0,2,25\).
The chart has 908 points including 224 supersingular marked
labels: 32 above 0, and 96 above each of 2 and 25.
The Frobenius-plus and Frobenius-minus elliptic groups have
orders 24 and 36. Multiplication by 2 has cyclic image of
order 9 in the minus group, generated by \(G=(14,13s)\).
For \(i=0,\ldots,8\), the numbers of supersingular labels
with \(\omega(q)=iG\) are
\[
(96,32,0,16,16,16,16,0,32).
\]
There are 12 fixed labels and 106 pairs under \(g\), of which
8 fixed labels and 60 pairs have nonzero weight.
Consequently each inert arm needs **68 certified counts modulo 9**
on the active representatives in the exact JSON. Check
\[
\sum_{\text{active }q} |g\text{-orbit}(q)|\,m(q)\,i(q)
\not\equiv 0\pmod9.
\]
One need not certify counts on the zero-weight labels merely
for this sum. Chart membership, a prescribed sum of unverified
counts, or the presence of a chart label of nonzero phase does
not supply the needed oriented multiplicities at an actual
\(D\). There is no runtime or nonvanishing claim about that issuer.

Common 29 is usable only if \(29\nmid n_1n_2\). When it also
avoids the fixed data when choosing \(q_0\), the extra CRT can
choose one splitting sign, but the two signs multiply to
\((\theta/29)\). The two arms can both be inert at 29 only
when \((\theta/29)=+1\); a mixed pair uses the two different
clauses above. The general common good-prime test does not
require equal splitting and still needs actual nonzero data.
