## 4. Every competing full lift differs by a pure second class

The first coordinates \(\beta\) and \(\theta\) corresponding to the same boundary quartic differ by the first-coordinate value of the rational torsion \(T_d\). After fixing the first coordinate to \(\beta\), the difference of any two full lifts is therefore a positive pair \((1,s)\), i.e. the ordered class \((1,s,s)\). At 2 the primal local equation for the first slot \(1\) forces \(v_2(s)=0\) and the odd \(s\equiv1\bmod4\); at 3 the second slot is square. At odd \(p\mid d\) the first-slot equation forces \(v_p(s)=0\) whenever \((-3/p)=-1\), because the pure second local torsion pattern with odd second valuation has first slot \(-3\). Thus a competing \(s\) is a positive squarefree divisor of \(d\) supported entirely on primes split in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\), with \(s\equiv1\bmod4\).

For such \(s\), the remaining exact local second-slot rows are equivalently
\[
                  X^2-sY^2=-\beta\delta/s,\quad X,Y\in\mathbf Q.
\]
Indeed the local row is \(s\sim1\) at \(p\mid d,\ p\nmid s\), or \(s\sim-d\) at \(p\mid s\). The symbol \((s,-d/s)\) gives exactly these two conditions by the odd depth-one formula. At all other odd places the norm symbol vanishes, including at 3; at 2 both classes are units and \(s\equiv1\bmod4\), hence it vanishes there, and at infinity \(s>0\). Replacing \(d\) by the raw \(\beta\delta\) changes the right-hand side by a rational square. This compact criterion neither lists actual unseen split primes nor proves any nontrivial competing lift.

All four visible \(\theta\)-primes are inert in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\); the five old split columns are \(163,661,733,1489,1861\). The exact fixed rows have rank five on just those pure second columns. Therefore **if no unseen \(\theta\)-prime splits in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\)**, the full lift is unique modulo rational torsion, for any unseen prime count. In that case a nontrivial evaluated pairing against it would indeed reject the positive zero-ordinate boundary. Uniqueness also holds under the different one-unseen hypothesis, since the aggregate kernel then contains every possible pair and its first projection has rank four. We do not assert either hypothesis for the actual scalar. Without uniqueness or a check of all competing lifts, rejecting the specific full lift does not reject the quartic.
