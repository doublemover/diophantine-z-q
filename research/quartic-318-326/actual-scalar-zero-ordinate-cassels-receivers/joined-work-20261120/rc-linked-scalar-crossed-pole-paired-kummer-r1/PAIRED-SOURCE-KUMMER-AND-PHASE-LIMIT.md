# The paired source's actual two-division constraint at crossed poles

Keep \(h,i=mh\), the actual two split source curves, and the
same reduced denominator. At every additional odd denominator
prime \(q\), the previous crossed-pole proof gives
\[
 (2/q)=(-1/q)=(7/q)=(-R/q)=+1 .
\]
Let \(s^2=2\) in \(\mathbb F_q\), put \(d_\pm=3\mp s\),
and use the literal source points
\[
 R_\pm=(1\pm s,\ \pm2s),\qquad
 E_\pm:y^2=x^3-d_\pm^2x.
\]
After swapping the two completions if necessary,
\([h]R_+\) has exact order 4 due to the first pole and
\([i]R_-\) has exact order 4 due to the opposite pole.
Consequently the literal 2-primary parts of the orders
of \(R_+,R_-\) are \(2^8,2^{24}\).
This note imposes one further exact constraint on such
a prime; it does not exhibit one.

## Equality of the actual two-division marks

As \(d_+d_-=7\), the ratio \(d_-/d_+\) is the square
\((d_-/\sqrt7)^2\). The corresponding square-by-cube
scale identifies the two curves over \(\mathbb F_q\),
preserving the three corresponding root labels.
The products of the corresponding full two-division
factors of \(R_\pm\) are exactly
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 \text{root label}&0&d_\pm&-d_\pm\\ \hline
 (x(R_+)-e_+)(x(R_-)-e_-)&-1&-4&16 .
 \end{array}
\]
All are nonzero squares since \((-1/q)=1\).
Thus the actual two-division squareclass triples agree,
independently of the sign choice of the cube scale.
For the classical full-root criterion, a point lies in
\(2E(\mathbb F_q)\) iff its three abscissa differences
are squares; see [Bekker–Zarhin, §2.1](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3117847_3/component/file_3276671/content).
Only this elementary finite-field criterion is used.

The \(\mathbb Z[i]\)-orbit of the 2-primary part of
\(R_-\) has Gaussian annihilator \((1+i)^r\)
with \(r\ge47\), by the earlier exact order argument.
Its subgroup killed by \(2^{23}\), i.e. by
\((1+i)^{46}\) up to a unit, has size \(2^{46}\).
That is the entire geometric \(E[2^{23}]\), since in
odd characteristic the multiplication kernel has
order \(2^{46}\). Hence **the entire \(E[2^{23}]\)
is rational over \(\mathbb F_q\)** on both models.

The 2-primary part of \(R_+\) has order \(2^8\);
it is divisible by \(2^{15}\) using multiplication
inside this full geometric \(E[2^{23}]\).
The odd component is divisible by \(2^{15}\) in
the finite group as well. Thus \(R_+\in2E_+(\mathbb F_q)\).
The squareclass equality proves
\(R_-\in2E_-(\mathbb F_q)\). A rational half of
\(R_-\) has 2-primary order \(2^{25}\).
Taking its Gaussian cyclic module now gives
\[
                2^{49}\mid\#E_\pm(\mathbb F_q).
\]
In particular by the exact Gaussian group-order norm
used in 225c9a68, the prime must have the shape
\[
 q=(1+2^{24}u)^2+(2^{24}v)^2,\qquad
 u\equiv v\bmod2,\qquad v\ne0.
\]
Indeed \(\pi-1\) for the compatible signed Gaussian
generator has \((1+i)\)-valuation at least 49.
This implies \(q\equiv1\bmod2^{25}\), strengthening
the previous necessary Gaussian form without any
additional prime search. The earlier independently
checked sparse lower bound remains unchanged here.

The same first squareclass yields
\(1+s\) and \(1-s\) both square in \(\mathbb F_q\).
Thus \(q\) also splits the literal quartic
\[
                         X^4-2X^2-1.
\]
It is a genuine source-specific congruence restriction,
not inferred from the lower bound or signatures alone.

## Why these more exact restrictions still do not give the 83 phase

The splitting field of that quartic is generated by
\(a=\sqrt{1+\sqrt2}\) and \(i\); its roots are
\(\pm a,\pm i/a\). Its discriminant is \(-2^{10}\)
(by the discriminant of \(X^4+bX^2+d\)), so the
splitting field is unramified at every odd prime.
The Gaussian ray class field of modulus \((1+i)^{49}\)
is also unramified at odd primes and is Galois over
\(\mathbb Q\) because the ideal is preserved by
complex conjugation. Their compositum \(L\) is
therefore Galois and unramified at odd primes.
A rational prime fully splitting in \(L\) is a
candidate satisfying both new conditions.

For any finite set of odd test primes (including
the six odd factors of \(R\) and 7), consider the
multiquadratic extension generated by their
quadratic fields of discriminant \(p^*=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p\).
It intersects \(L\) trivially: otherwise a nontrivial
quadratic subfield in the intersection would have a
product of these odd discriminants and would ramify
at some odd prime. Thus any compatible pattern of
\((q/p)\) at the odd tests can be specified together
with complete splitting in \(L\), using ordinary
Chebotarev for the compositum. Impose \((q/7)=+1\)
and the one product condition \((-R/q)=+1\).
The individual \(83\) sign remains free at the
congruence-only level. This is the same precise
distinction as d8ad7727, now for this stronger
actual Kummer restriction. It does not assert
that any such prime has the two prescribed actual
residue orders, let alone the retained transverse
depth. In particular no value of \((Z_{\rm exc}/83)\)
is deduced.

## The smallest still-missing actual arithmetic

For a supplied \(q\) satisfying both residue poles,
one can compute their local logarithm depths using
only the common residue group count \(n=\#E_\pm\).
It is prime to \(q\) by Hasse and evenness
(\(0<n<2q\), so otherwise \(n=q\)).
On each good source model, \([n]R_\pm\) lies in the
formal group. Subtract the unique prime-to-\(q\)
torsion lift of the raw residue point; its logarithm
is exactly \(\log([n]R_\pm)/n\).
Because the fixed \(h,m\) are \(q\)-units, the
actual crossed depths \(a,b\) from d8df8aa3 are
therefore the valuations of these two *raw* formal
logarithms. In particular \(b=4a-\epsilon\ge3\)
is a condition on the opposite raw point, not an
artifact of the enormous translated multiplier.
The earlier analytic unit normal form then gives
the exact finite truncation test for the sign when
a candidate \(q\) is known.

What is missing globally is the parity of the
\(83\)-nonsplit primes of this fixed paired division
data that also pass the truncated transverse-distance
test. Equivalently it is the actual cross-support
symbol \((M_C/M_L)\) in c10703bc.
Neither the common two-division mark, the stronger
Gaussian form, the rational torsion aggregate, nor
the exact gauge of the alternate cover evaluates it.
