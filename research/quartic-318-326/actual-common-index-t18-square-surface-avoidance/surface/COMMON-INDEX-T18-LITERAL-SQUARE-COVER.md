# The literal source of the \(t=18\) square test is a double cover of a surface

Fix the original input-zero labels and physical anchor, and the previously fixed first point for \(t=18\). This note treats the explicit **sufficient** plus-sign specialization, not the complete secant equation. Nothing below chooses a key, evaluates a new index, or proves that there are finitely many native solutions.

## 1. Exact descent to the literal restriction of scalars

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), \(\omega=3-\sqrt2\), and use the stored source elliptic curve and point
\[
  E:y^2=x_E^3-\omega^2x_E,\qquad R=(1+\sqrt2,2\sqrt2).
\]
Write \(\sigma\) for conjugation in \(K\). The native chart is \(U(P)=2y_E(P)/(x_E(P)^2+\omega^2)\). The exact duplication identity is \(U(P)^2=1/x_E([2]P)\) as a rational function. With \(R_s=4376874610839020\), \(L_s=114444R_s\), and the fixed nonzero inverse norm \(I_s\) of the second label, put
\[
  \lambda=\frac{12}{1+L_s},\qquad \gamma=\frac{I_s^2}{R_s^{31}}>0 .
\]
Let \(\mathcal A=\operatorname{Res}_{K/\mathbb Q}E\). Over \(K\) it is \(E\times E^\sigma\). In this description let \(x=U(P_1)^2/R_s\) and \(y=U^\sigma(P_2)^2/R_s\) be the two rational functions from the two elliptic arms. Under the descent datum the two functions are exchanged. The following rational function is therefore defined over \(\mathbb Q\):
\[
  c=\lambda J,\qquad J=(1+x)^2(1+y)^2+\frac{\gamma}{xy}.
\]
At the native points \(P_h=([h]R,[h]R^\sigma)\), this is exactly the prescribed scalar \(c(h)\): the native \(N\) is \((1+x)(1+y)\), and \(H_h^2=R_s^2xy\) in \(R_sQ_h^2=I_s^2/(H_h^2R_s^{29})\). The chart is used only where the source has its stated nonzero norm and real guard. In particular every genuine \(P_h\) is a \(\mathbb Q\)-point by Weil restriction, and its \(c(h)\) is **rational**, not a freely chosen value in \(K\).

Use the fixed secant constants \(A>0,a_{\rm sec},B_0>0\) from the previous paper. Normalize \(\mathcal A\) in the double cover with equation
\[
        Z^2=Q(c):=(2a_{\rm sec}c+2A)^2+B_0c^2 .
\]
At each admissible \(h\) the right side is finite and positive, so a \(\mathbb Q\)-point above \(P_h\) is precisely the optional rational square-match certificate. A square over \(K\) alone would not suffice. Changing the anchor representation while keeping the fixed labels makes no change to \(\gamma\) or this function. The analogous minus-sign pencil is obtained by changing the sign of \(2A\); the discussion below is for the stated plus sign.

## 2. This is not the single native elliptic curve

The stored multiple is nontorsion (\([2]R\) is the exact generator whose nonzero integer multiples are proved nonzero). The cyclic subgroup generated by \((R,R^\sigma)\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) is Zariski dense in the two-dimensional product. Here is the extra independence check; the two curves do have the same geometric \(j\)-invariant.

The ratio \(\omega^\sigma/\omega=(\omega^\sigma)^2/7\) has squareclass \(7\) over \(K\). The usual short-model scaling \(E^\sigma\to E\) dividing \(x_E\) by this ratio and the ordinate by its \(3/2\)-power produces an isomorphism defined over \(K(\sqrt7)\). Its conjugate under a sign change of \(\sqrt7\) is its negative. There is such an automorphism fixing \(K(i)\), because the only three quadratic subfields of \(K(i)\) have squareclasses \(2,-1,-2\). The endomorphisms of a characteristic-zero \(j=1728\) curve are generated by the order-four automorphism and are defined over \(K(i)\). Composing with the displayed isomorphism now shows that this Galois action negates **every** homomorphism \(E^\sigma\to E\).

If the Zariski closure of the cyclic subgroup were proper, its identity component would be an elliptic subgroup surjecting onto both factors, since both projected points are nontorsion. A nonzero multiple \(m(R,R^\sigma)\) would lie on it. If its projection to \(E^\sigma\) has degree \(d\), composing the dual of that projection with the other projection gives a homomorphism \(\alpha:E^\sigma\to E\) with
\[
                 [dm]R=\alpha([m]R^\sigma).
\]
Applying the automorphism above negates the right side and fixes the left. This contradicts nontorsion. The same argument applies to every nonzero progression step, and translates preserve density.

In particular there is no elliptic **orbit curve** containing any whole nontrivial progression. At the level of formulas neither arm can be discarded. In fact the argument on homomorphisms gives \(\operatorname{Hom}_K(E^\sigma,E)=0\) and the same in the other direction; any elliptic quotient of this product **over \(K\)** must factor through one arm. The ramification calculated below is not vertical or horizontal, so the quadratic cover is not a pullback from either such arm. It is of course a pullback from the rational \(c\)-line; that does not interpolate the native point \([h]R\) together with its conjugate.

## 3. Exact divisorial branch locus

Write \(c_\pm\) for the two roots of \(Q\). Its discriminant is \(-16A^2B_0\ne0\) and its leading coefficient is \(4a_{\rm sec}^2+B_0\ne0\). The identities \(B_0=3\beta(2k/A)^2\) from the fixed first point show that the roots split over the imaginary quadratic field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3\beta})\). Geometrically the only branch divisors are the reduced pullbacks from the two finite fibres \(J=c_\pm/\lambda\) in the torus \(xy\ne0,\infty\). This specifies divisorial branch, and says nothing about singularities or exceptional curves of any resolution.

To verify the multiplicities, the torus fibre \(J=t\) has polynomial equation
\[
                    xy((1+x)^2(1+y)^2-t)+\gamma=0 .
\]
No component can be vertical or horizontal: after fixing a nonzero finite coordinate the polynomial in the other coordinate has nonzero constant term \(\gamma\) and cannot vanish identically. Every component is generically reduced. Indeed at a critical point of \(J\) neither \(1+x\) nor \(1+y\) can vanish, because the derivatives of \(\gamma/(xy)\) would not then be cancelled. Comparing the equations \(x\partial_xJ=y\partial_yJ=0\) gives \(x=y\), and then \(2x^3(1+x)^3=\gamma\). The critical locus in the torus is finite, which excludes a repeated curve component in characteristic zero. The pullback from the two torus factors to the elliptic arms is étale at the generic point of every such component, since its own ramification maps to vertical or horizontal lines. Thus the zero order of \(Q(c)\) is one on these divisors. At a generic point of any boundary divisor \(x=0,\infty\) or \(y=0,\infty\), \(J\) has a pole: at zero the reciprocal term dominates and at infinity the squared term dominates. Such poles give even order for \(Q(c)\). The double extension is geometrically nontrivial by the exhibited simple zeros, and no other codimension-one place ramifies.

## 4. The generic fixed-arm slice has genus 25, not a finiteness result for \(h\)

After fixing the second elliptic arm at a geometric point with generic \(y\), the \(x\)-values over either \(t=c_\pm/\lambda\) are the roots of
\[
          x^3+2x^2+\left(1-\frac{t}{(1+y)^2}\right)x
                      +\frac{\gamma}{y(1+y)^2}=0 .
\]
There are three distinct generic roots for each \(t\): as a rational function of \(y\) the discriminant has a double pole at \(y=0\) from the term \(-27[\gamma/(y(1+y)^2)]^2\), with all other terms of pole order at most one. The two sets of roots are disjoint since the levels are distinct. They avoid the branch values \(0,\infty,\pm1/(R_s\omega)\) of the map \(E\to\mathbb P^1_x\): for a fixed nonzero finite branch value, \(J(x,y)\) has a pole in \(y\) at zero and is not identically \(t\); zero and infinity are not generic roots of this cubic. The map \(E\to\mathbb P^1_x\) has degree \(8\), from degree \(4\) multiplication by two, degree \(2\) projection \(x_E\), and a fractional linear change. We obtain precisely \(6\cdot8=48\) simple geometric branch points for the double cover of this genus-one slice. Its smooth projective normalization therefore has genus \(25\) by Riemann--Hurwitz. This holds outside a finite set of second-arm values (and identically with arms exchanged).

This does not put the native \(h\)'s on one genus-25 curve. Fixing one arm itself leaves at most one \(h\), since \(R^\sigma\) is nontorsion. In fact, even an arbitrary infinite set of distinct orbit points is Zariski dense in the surface. Any irreducible curve of geometric genus at least two has finitely many such points by Faltings on its normalization over a number field of definition. An abelian surface has no rational curves, and its genus-one normalizations map to translates of elliptic subgroups. Two distinct native points on one such translate would put a nonzero orbit step in the subgroup, contrary to the independence proof. Thus a genuine theorem that the matching surface does not have Zariski-dense rational points would imply finiteness of these optional square matches; this note does not provide it. The existing prepared conditions neither change the dimension of the source nor supply a rational match.
