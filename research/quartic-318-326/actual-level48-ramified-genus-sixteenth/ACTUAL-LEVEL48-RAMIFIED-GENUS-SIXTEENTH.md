# The unweighted detector trace is a rational sixteenth multiple

Keep the actual selected detector and literal Selmer-eight basis of the rational-eighth theorem. The original marked support has 17 and six genuine primes $1\bmod24$. Fix one genuine prime $p_\beta\ne17$ as comparison. Suppose the selected twist also retains one old helper $r\equiv1\bmod24$ with $(17/r)=-1$ and has the separate final prime $q_0\equiv7\bmod24$. Both old first-arm selector phases and their companion extensions have such an old helper: bit at the 17-coordinate is one in all the original helper columns. In this scope **the same raw trace lies in $16E_n(\mathbf Q)$**. This prevents the previously natural next test on an eighth from marking, not a statement of vanishing or rank. No degree-eight CM intersection receiver is assumed.

## 1. A shorter unselected twist is already a rational double

Remove any subset of the **other five** genuine primes, not $p_\beta$, and put $n'=n/\Delta$. Keep $17,p_\beta,q_0,r$ throughout. The shorter twist is still positive squarefree and $23\bmod24$. It need not possess the selected Selmer basis or any known rank. Let $P'=P(n')$ be its literal unweighted level-48 trace with the induced oriented level. For every $p\mid n'$, $p\ne17$, the earlier ramified pairing applies to $P'$ without Selmer hypotheses: the class number $h'$ is divisible by four and $\chi_{17}$ flips the paired indices. Thus $\bar P'=O$ there and its rational Kummer valuation parities at such $p$ are zero by the formal anti-invariant parameter. The rational torsion on each of these shorter twists is $(\mathbf Z/2)^2$ from the exact split Tate bound at 3.

Let $(d_1,d_2)$ be the rational squareclasses of its two Kummer coordinates $(x+3n',x-n')$. At good odd $p\nmid 2\cdot3\cdot n'$ both valuations are even: integral points can lie near at most one of the three distinct roots, which forces its valuation even by the cubic square equation; for nonintegral points all three valuations agree and their sum is even. Together with the ramified formal result, the support of both classes lies in the signed products of $2,3,17$. Also $d_1$ is positive by the real locus; the torsion-free nonzero branch has no Kummer endpoint ambiguity, and the identity gives the trivial class.

At any odd ramified $p\ne17$ (and $p\ne3$), all four local Kummer classes come from rational 2-torsion. Indeed an open formal subgroup is uniquely 2-divisible and the quotient is finite, so the index of doubling equals the size of the rational kernel. The four root torsion classes already have distinct valuation pairs $(0,0),(1,1),(1,0),(0,1)$. It follows that a class of valuation pair $(0,0)$ is the identity local class, not merely an unramified unit class. At $r$ the signs of $-1,2,3$ are all positive and $(17/r)=-1$, so 17 is absent from both $d_i$. At $q_0$ the signs of $-1,2,3$ are respectively minus, plus, minus. Thus the first class must be $1$ or $2$, and the second has its $3$ and negative exponents equal.

At 3, the split local Tate model also has quotient by doubling of size four: the valuation modulo 2 contributes two and $\mathbf Z_3^\times/(\mathbf Z_3^\times)^2$ contributes two. The four rational 2-torsion classes already exhaust it. The ordered second torsion coordinate is always $1$ or $-n'$, and $-n'$ is a square in $\mathbf Q_3$ since $n'\equiv2\bmod3$. This forces the second class to be $1$: 3 cannot divide it, its sign is already tied to that exponent, and 2 is a nonsquare unit at 3. Thus the only remaining possible pair apart from identity is $(2,1)$.

The latter is impossible at 2. If $v_2(x)<0$ the common valuation of the three factors is even, so both coordinates have even valuation. If $x$ is even integral both translated roots are odd units. If $x$ is odd integral its valuation is zero, and the product of the two coordinate valuations must be even in parity sum (use the square equation), excluding the unequal pair $(2,1)$. Even using only the valuation condition at 3 leaves the potential $(2,2)$ as well; then the two translated roots must both have valuation exactly one, since their difference has valuation two. Their stripped unit squares would both be $1\bmod8$, but differ by $2n'\equiv6\bmod8$, impossible. This local proof does not equate the 2-adic logarithm with the global Kummer class. The global two-division kernel therefore gives an actual rational half $H_\Delta$ of $P'$ on $E_{n'}$.

## 2. Only the one relevant reduction character of the half is needed

Apply the **first-half coherent modular-unit argument**, not its Selmer-size conclusion, to the shorter twist. Its required stronger class exponent also holds: the ramified classes at $17,p_\beta,r$ generate $C_2^3$ because their product still omits $q_0>4$, so every nontrivial subset norm is nonsquare and less than $n'/4$. As in the earlier proof this makes $h'/4$ kill the whole class group. All other inputs of that modular-unit argument are unchanged: the level splits at 2,3, the raw trace was just shown rationally double, and the eta ideal uses the connected/étale arms of this same smaller quadratic field. On either 2-arm the exponents are interchanged, not a new residue sign. The norm-one ideal has a generator ratio in $K'=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-n'})$ supported only at 2 and 3 after division by its global unit $\pm1$. At each retained ramified comparison prime the ratio is one modulo the prime, since conjugation is trivial on its residue field. Hence the coherent half-root $\rho_{H_\Delta}=\sqrt{(x(P')-n')/x(P')}$ has equal signs there (in rational twist coordinates). This statement uses no class independence between an old mark and torsion on the shorter curve.

Specifically let $\lambda:E_0[2]\to\mathbf F_2$ denote the valuation parity of the first Kummer coordinate after twisting at an odd ramified place: $\lambda(T_0)=\lambda(T_{-1})=1$, $\lambda(T_3)=0$. These labels are on the common $E_0$. They are independent of the ramified prime. The reduction of $H_\Delta$ is in $E_0[2]$ because $2H_\Delta=P'$ reduces to zero. Subtracting the corresponding rational named 2-torsion once gives a formal anti-invariant point with even first parity. Thus the first-half comparison from $\rho_{H_\Delta}$ says exactly
$$
 \lambda(\bar H_\Delta\text{ at }q_0)
 =\lambda(\bar H_\Delta\text{ at }p_\beta).
$$
The other reduction coordinate need not agree. If $P'=O$, choose $H_\Delta=O$, or any rational 2-torsion with the same sign at both. Changing the globally chosen half changes both signs by the same torsion functional. No nonzero assumption on any shorter trace is used.

## 3. Orthogonality with five distinguished characters

Now use all five genuine primes different from $p_\beta$ as the distinguished set and the common genus field $H_0$ obtained by adjoining their square roots to $K$. Its unramifiedness at 2 and 3, the pure-character descent of $R_\Delta$ and the independent character slice are as in the rational-eighth proof; the same proof gives $E_0(H_0)[16]=E_0[2]$. Let $A$ be the full indexed sum on the all-positive slice. One has
$$
 32A=P(n)+\sum_{\Delta>1}R_\Delta,
 \qquad 4\bar A=O\text{ at both retained comparison primes}.
$$
The second equality follows again from stability under the genuine inertia–Fricke index reflection, no nonvanishing requirement.

For $w=\omega(\Delta)\ge1$, in the nontorsion branch the weighted height ratio gives $v_2(m_R/m_{P'})\ge w+1$. The free coordinate $m_{P'}$ is even by §1. Thus $m_R$ is divisible by $2^{w+2}$, in particular by eight. Choose $R_\Delta=8Q_\Delta+T_\Delta$ on the **actual shorter rational twist**, with $T_\Delta\in E_0[2]$, taking $Q_\Delta=O$ in the torsion branch. The ratio between the free coordinates of $Q_\Delta$ and the chosen rational half $H_\Delta$ is $(m_R/4)/m_{P'}$, with nonnegative 2-adic valuation. Therefore there are odd $a$, integer $b$, and one rational named 2-torsion point $T'$, with
$$
 aQ_\Delta=bH_\Delta+T'.
$$
After reduction, projection to the 2-primary subgroup and division by odd $a$ shows the 2-part of $\bar Q_\Delta$ is in $E_0[2]$ and has the same $\lambda$ at both primes by §2. This uses the same $T'$ in the actual group, not a local sign convention.

Write $P(n)=8C$ via the proven rational eighth. Orthogonality gives
$$
 8(4A-C-\sum Q_\Delta)=\sum T_\Delta\in E_0[2].
$$
The parenthetical point is one element of $E_0(H_0)[16]=E_0[2]$. Reducing at the same two places and taking 2-primary parts shows the reductions of $C$ belong to $E_0[2]$ and have equal $\lambda$. There is no odd reduction part because $8\bar C=\bar P(n)=O$. Thus the Kummer class of the rational eighth on the original twist has equal first parities at $q_0,p_\beta$. In the actual Selmer-eight basis this says exactly that it is a torsion class. Subtract the corresponding rational 2-torsion and take a rational half; multiplication by eight erases the correction. This gives the claimed exact rational sixteenth.

This does not compute any raw trace, say that it is nontorsion, or decide the first possible marked division beyond the eighth. It holds on both selected detector twists whenever the old helper and mark hypotheses are met. It does not address an arbitrary shorter twist without the retained old helper, or a different weighted orbit intended as an independent producer.
