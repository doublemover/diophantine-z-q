# An exact same-pole direction kills the entire contact-supported Selmer block

Take one genuine original linear-\(q\) Ready detector pole \(b\), not a change of the source variables or of the pole consumed by the final theorem. Write \(\mu=b/17=A/D^2\) reduced, \(C=D^2-A>0\), and assume \(\delta=C/D^2\) nonsquare (the rational-square case already has a direct PointPair). The first squareclass is
\[
      \beta=\operatorname{sf}(A)=17\prod_{i=1}^6 p_i ,
      \qquad p_i\equiv1\pmod {408},
\]
where the six distinct primes are the five contacts and the one order-only denominator prime, all of depth one in \(A\). Assume the actual small-prime and denominator Ready premises. The \(p_i\) are known from a proposed full key, not supplied numerically here.

We construct an allowed direction for the direct norm quartic on this same **exact pair of global squareclasses**. On the auxiliary zero-direction model all 2-Selmer classes supported solely on \(\beta\) are trivial. Here supported means all three Kummer squareclasses admit representatives divisible only by primes of \(\beta\), with real signs allowed. Thus the actually identified \(\beta\)-supported Cassels obstruction block is removed; the construction says nothing about Selmer classes with additional primes or the existence of a rational point on that auxiliary quartic.

## 1. Exact squarepair and reduced-denominator transport

Use a direction \(k=D^2m\) in the original norm-one convention
\[
 \gamma=(1+\sqrt\delta)\frac{1+k\sqrt\delta}{1-k\sqrt\delta}
           =a+c\sqrt\delta .
\]
For integral \(m\), put
\[
 u=1-D^2Cm^2,\qquad v=1+2Cm+D^2Cm^2,\qquad w=1+2D^2m+D^2Cm^2 .
\]
The real and \(\sqrt\delta\) coefficients are \(a=v/u,\ c=w/u\). The transformed auxiliary pole has
\[
 \mu'=\frac{\mu}{a^2}=\frac{Au^2}{D^2v^2},\qquad
 \delta'=1-\mu'=\frac{Cw^2}{D^2v^2},
\]
indeed \(D^2v^2-Au^2=Cw^2\). These are literal global square ratios. The original direction quartic and the zero-direction quartic at \(\mu'\) are isomorphic by \(t'=at,\ W'=W/a\); a verified PointPair on the latter scales to the unchanged \(b\) by the exact two-class transport theorem.

Take \(m\equiv0\bmod 2ACD\). Then \(u,v,w\) are units at every old prime dividing that product. They are pairwise coprime: away from \(2ACD\), common zeros of \(u,v\) force \(Cm=-1\), where \(u=-A/C\ne0\); common zeros of \(u,w\) force \(D^2m=-1\), where \(u=A/D^2\ne0\); common zeros of \(v,w\) imply \(2Am=0\), giving \(v=w=1\). Therefore the exact reduced integers are
\[
                 A'=Au^2,\qquad D'=Dv,\qquad C'=Cw^2 .
\]
Here \(v>0\) since \(D^2v=A+C(1+D^2m)^2\). For sufficiently large positive \(m\), \(uvw\ne0\), and \(0<\mu'<1\). Along the positive \(m\)-progression \(\mu'\to\mu\), so any fixed real open guard can be retained. Arbitrarily deep old finite-place guards follow by making \(m\) sufficiently divisible at those finitely many places, before choosing the fresh primes. In particular \(A'\) has the same depth-one six primes, the same positive odd 17-depth, and the same small-prime Ready depths.

One may equally do this using an **unreduced** integer presentation \(A_0/D_0^2\), \(C_0=D_0^2-A_0\), with positive \(A_0,C_0,D_0\). Since the actual reduced denominator is already square, \(D_0=\lambda D,\ A_0=\lambda^2A,\ C_0=\lambda^2C\) for an integer \(\lambda>0\). Use the raw quantities throughout the formulas and protect the raw product \(2A_0C_0D_0\). The same pairwise coprimality away from the old support shows the only old normalization is \(\lambda^2\), giving the same reduced-value claims with \(u,v,w\) computed from those raw quantities. Neither branch requires factoring \(C\) or extracting \(\theta\); the prime tests can read \(A,C,D\) or the corresponding raw quantities modulo each candidate prime. This is not a bit-cost estimate for recovering a compact pole or a guarantee that a good p is small.

## 2. The exact local axes to kill

Use the integral Jacobian of the auxiliary zero-direction quartic, in root order
\[
               0,\quad 4(A')^2,\quad A'(A'+3(D')^2).
\]
The 2-Selmer class of that quartic is \((1,C',C')\) in these coordinates. By the independent fixed-pole contact calculation in [the exact tangent and local admission packet](../../../rc-native-helper-20261120/ct-original-k0-contact-cassels-r1/ACTUAL-CONTACT-CASSELS-REJECTION.md), any positive \(\beta\)-supported Selmer triple has no factor 17 and each coordinate's contact mask belongs to the same Laplacian kernel \(K\subseteq\mathbf F_2^6\). This contact necessity also follows directly: at each old depth-one prime its four-element local Kummer image is generated by the two torsion triples \(([3A'],1,[3A'])\) and \((1,[3A'],[3A'])\). It holds equally for all auxiliary \(A',D',C'\) because the depths and the \(\beta\) residues are unchanged.

For completeness, signs on \(\beta\)-supported representatives do not enlarge this block. Over \(\mathbf R\) the first coordinate is positive; the other two have the same sign. At 2 the first is square (all its prime factors are \(1\bmod8\)); a negative second would be in the class of \(-1\). Then a square abscissa must have valuation at least \(2v_2(A')\), as otherwise \(x-4(A')^2\) is also square by the ratio \(1+8\mathbf Z_2\). But at such depth the third factor has class \(-3\), not \(-1\), since \(A'\) is a local square up to the square \(D'^2\), \(D'\) is odd, and \(A'+3(D')^2\) has class \(3\). The exceptional root triples have respectively first class \(3\), second class \(-3\), first class \(3\), and the origin is trivial. Thus all signs positive. At 17 the local torsion triples above apply also at positive odd depth, but \([3A']\) after stripping that odd depth has nonsquare unit since \(3\) is nonsquare, \(A'/\beta\) square, and the six contact primes are \(1\bmod17\). A positive divisor of \(\beta\) has square unit part at that prime, so every coordinate omits 17.

For convenient local tests, if two roots are congruent modulo an odd \(\ell\) and the difference between their common residue and the separate root is a **nonzero square**, the separate Kummer coordinate is square for every local point. Indeed inside the cluster it has that square residue; away from the cluster the two near roots give factors of square product, including for an abscissa of negative valuation. The full product is a square. At roots use the same residue property or the value of the exceptional product of the other differences. This is the local admission constraint used below, not a Selmer rank inequality.

Let \(r=\dim K\), which is 3 or 4 for the actual two allowed six-prime graphs. It suffices to prescribe \(r\) dual linear functionals detecting all masks in \(K\) on the **first** slot, and independently \(r\) on the **third**. Then the second slot is forced trivial by the product-square equation.

## 3. Two disjoint lists of fresh CRT primes

Choose new primes \(\ell\equiv1\bmod24\), prime to the old protected moduli, with arbitrary prescribed characters \((p_i/\ell)\), and with \((\beta/\ell)=+1\). This is possible because \((17/\ell)\) can be chosen to compensate the product of the six characters. For the *second* list also prescribe \((C/\ell)=+1\). The nontrivial squarefree part of \(C\) is disjoint from \(2,3,\beta\) by \(A+C=D^2,(A,D)=1\) and the Ready depths; hence its character is independent of the previous choices. Formally each finite set of characters is a compatible set of unit residue classes by quadratic reciprocity and CRT, followed by Dirichlet's theorem. Equivalently the relevant linearly independent quadratic squareclasses admit the desired Frobenius by Chebotarev. The actual value of \((C/\ell)\) can be tested by Euler's criterion without knowing those factors. Choose all primes distinct, and choose their restricted functionals to be a basis of \(K^\vee\) on each list.

**First list (kill slot 1).** At a prescribed \(\ell\), force \(w(m)\equiv0\bmod\ell\) via its simple root: discriminant \(4D^2A\) is a nonzero square. Refine \(m\bmod\ell^2\) to give exact depth 1 (exclude the one lifting residue). Then \(C'\) has depth 2, \(A',D'\) are units, and the two upper roots meet at \(4(A')^2\) with square difference from zero. Hence the first coordinate must be square at \(\ell\). Its contact mask is orthogonal to the chosen dual row.

**Second list (kill slot 3).** At each new \(\ell\) of this list force \(u(m)\equiv0\bmod\ell\), \(u(m)\not\equiv0\bmod\ell^2\) via its simple roots (since \(C\) is square and nonzero at \(\ell\)). Then \(A'\) has depth 2 while \(C',D'\) are units. Rescale \(x,y\) by \(\ell^2,\ell^3\) in the Jacobian equation. The first two roots meet at zero, while the separate third root has residue \(3(D')^2(A'/\ell^2)\), of class \(3\beta\). The difference from it has class \(-3\beta\), square because \(\ell\equiv1\bmod24\) and \(\beta\) is square there. The third coordinate must therefore be square at \(\ell\). Its contact mask is orthogonal to the corresponding dual row.

All choices of \(m\bmod\ell^2\) and old saturation coexist by CRT, with arbitrarily large positive \(m\). There are \(2r\), hence at most **eight**, fresh primes; each separate unit condition kills one linear coordinate functional. The other three transformed fields need no independent Selmer assumption. Combining the two bases kills both contact masks and therefore the entire \(\beta\)-supported local-to-global Selmer block, not just the subfamily \((d,1,d)\).

This genuinely avoids the previously identified contact-only Cassels witnesses without changing the final literal \(b\) reached by back-transport. It does not prove this auxiliary class lies in the full Cassels radical. Its own possible outside-support partners include primes dividing \(C'\); primes at these particular chosen sites have \(\ell\equiv1\bmod24\), which kills the local unit contribution \( (2/\ell)\) from the two clustered ramification bits in the tangent product. Other sites and their true global Selmer admission remain to be controlled.
