## 2. One actual \(D=210\) surface and the \(+64\) comparison

Vertically work **before** ramified trait pullback:
at a height-one component of a regular semistable atlas the
reduced fibre makes the base prime a uniformizer. In a dualizing
frame the fundamental differential induces a relative derivation
which preserves the DVR and kills that prime, so
\(v(df)\ge v(f)\) for any nonzero rational \(f\).

\(a\le b'\le d\le h\); the highest gives \(v(dt)\ge h\).

## 3. CM case for this very fibre

Use the full rational level-3 normal fibre splitting field \(F_3\)
of this same \(s\), **not** the optional small non-CM Brauer
field. The effective level kernel has order36 and the
Atkin–Lehner group has order16. The original nonbranch
whole fibre is a torsor for their constant normalizer
extension. Its faithful Galois permutation group is in the
opposite regular action, hence \([F_3:\mathbb Q]\le576\).
Off \(S\) the actual even-contact tame charts give
unramified fields and good models. In particular
\(\operatorname{rad}|\operatorname{disc} F_3|\mid S\).
These are the earlier precise fine-fibre and support
proofs, not an inference of support from degree.

All geometric endomorphisms of this \(A\) are defined over
\(F_3\). Indeed Galois acts through a finite group on their
free integral lattice, and the full \(A[3]\) being rational
implies that the difference of an endomorphism and any
conjugate factors through \([3]\), so the action is identity
modulo 3. The torsion-free mod-3 congruence kernel in
\(\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)\) kills this finite action.
Division QM and CM force \(A\) geometrically isogenous to
the square of a CM elliptic curve: the geometrically
simple four-dimensional rational homology case would have
endomorphism algebra exactly the quaternion division
algebra and would not be CM; two distinct elliptic factors
or a square of a non-CM elliptic curve cannot contain that
division algebra. Images of multiples of matrix idempotents,
and the induced factor maps and endomorphisms, are now
defined over \(F_3\). The CM quadratic field \(K_0\) embeds
into \(F_3\) by its action on the differential of the
same elliptic factor. Pass that factor to maximal order
by its \(F_3\)-defined finite subgroup corresponding to
enlarging its complex lattice to \(\mathcal O_{K_0}\Lambda_{\rm ell}\).
Thus \(A'\), the square of this maximal-order curve, is
\(F_3\)-isogenous to the actual \(A\). The discriminant
tower formula and fundamental-discriminant exponents give
\(|D_{K_0}|\le4S\).

At reduced CM periods, \(\sqrt3/2\le \Im z\le\sqrt{|D_{K_0}|}/2\).
Use [Silverman's individual-embedding formula, reproduced as
(6.2)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-017-0077-7#Sec6)
over a further good-reduction **height averaging** field.
The finite discriminant term vanishes there; no assumption
about class-number weights or an equation defined over
\(\mathbb Q(j)\) is needed. The summand in original
normalization is
\(-\log(2\pi)-2\log|\eta(z)|-\tfrac12\log\Im z\).
Putting \(r=|e^{2\pi iz}|<1/16\), the Euler product bounds
\(-2\log|\eta(z)|\le\pi\Im z/6+2r/(1-r)^2\).
In particular the summand is \(<\sqrt{|D_{K_0}|}+1\),
so the elliptic height is \(<3S\), and \(h_0(A')<6S\).

For [Gaudron–Rémond, Theorem 1.9(1)](https://smf.emath.fr/system/files/filepdf/smf_mem_176_0.pdf#page=16)
take both fields to be \(F_3\), and center the dimension-two
estimate at \(A'\). The actual chosen \(F_3\)-isogeny
\(A'\to A\) has degree
\(\delta\le(14^{32}D\max(1,\log D,h_0(A')))^{16}\)
for \(D=[F_3:\mathbb Q]\le576\). This is the general
squared bound, no MM specialization. Raynaud variation on
this chosen isogeny then gives
\[
 h_0(A)<6S+8(96+7+2+\log S)\le854S.
\]
Here \(\log14<3,\log576<7,\log7<2\), and
\(\max(1,\log D,h_0(A'))\le7S\).
By (2), \(\log\pi<2\) and \(S\ge B_0\),
\[
                    h(s)<1708S+68<S^6/2,
\]
contrary to (1).
