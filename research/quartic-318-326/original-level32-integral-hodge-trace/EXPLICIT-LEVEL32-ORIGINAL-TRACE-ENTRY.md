# An explicit nonzero constant entry in the original finite trace certificate

The original level is \(M=2^{28}3^6S^2\); \(32\mid M\). Use the original modular map, its whole signed monic equation \(p\), and the unchanged field \(F=L(X_1(M))=L(J)[x]/(p)\). The actual map \(\pi:X_1(M)\to X_0(32)\) sends \((E,P)\) to the cyclic subgroup generated by \((M/32)P\). This is a morphism on the original curve, not a replacement of it. The target has the genuine \(\mathbb Q\)-structure of [Tian, Proposition 2.1](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/232/8762-35_Ye_Tian_Congruent_Numbers_and_Heegner_Points.pdf#page=6).

Use the explicit modular coordinates \(x_0=\sqrt{\lambda(4\tau)}\) and \(y_0^4=1-x_0^2\), with branches \(x_0=4q+\cdots\), \(y_0=1+\cdots\), from [Wen-Ching Winnie Li, Ling Long and Fang-Ting Tu, §3.1 and (3.1)](https://sigma-journal.com/2018/090/sigma18-090.pdf#page=15). These coordinates are rational functions on the actual \(X_0(32)_{\mathbb Q}\): \(x_0=4\eta(2\tau)^4\eta(8\tau)^8/\eta(4\tau)^{12}\) has rational Laurent coefficients at its rational cusp, and the chosen binomial expansion of \(y_0=(1-x_0^2)^{1/4}\) does too. The rational \(q\)-expansion principle identifies the \(\mathbb Q\)-functions. Fix the nonzero differential \(\omega_{32}=dx_0/(4y_0^3)=f_{32}\,dq/q\). Set \(u=4x_0\), \(Y=2y_0\); hence
\[
 Y^4=16-u^2,\qquad \omega_{32}=\frac{du}{2Y^3}.
\]

## Literal modular \(J\), not a different rational map

Write \(k(\tau)=\theta_2(0\mid\tau)^2/\theta_3(0\mid\tau)^2\) and \(\lambda=k^2\). The doubled-nome identities [DLMF 20.7.12](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.7.E12) and [20.7.14](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.7.E14), evaluated at zero argument, give
\[
 \lambda(\tau)=\frac{4k(2\tau)}{(1+k(2\tau))^2}.
\]
There is no independent square-root sign, since \(k\) is the theta ratio and agrees with the indicated cusp branch. The Legendre equation for \(\lambda(\tau)\) gives the standard \(J=256(1-\lambda+\lambda^2)^3/[\lambda^2(1-\lambda)^2]\). Substituting the first doubling formula makes it
\[
 J=\frac{16(1+14v+v^2)^3}{v(1-v)^4},\qquad v=\lambda(2\tau).
\]
Substitute \(v=4x_0/(1+x_0)^2\), then \(u=4x_0\), to obtain the exact identity
\[
 A(u)=u^4+240u^3+2144u^2+3840u+256,\quad
 D(u)=u(4-u)^8(4+u)^2,\quad J=A(u)^3/D(u).
\]
In particular \(J=q^{-1}+744+\cdots\), with the same normalization used in \(p\). The cusp widths of this rational function on \(X_0(8)\) are \(1,8,2,1\), at \(x_0=0,1,-1,\infty\), respectively.

## A polynomial multiplier with no uncertain denominator

Let \(A_0=\mathcal O_L[J]\). In \(A_0[T]\), set \(P(T)=A(T)^3-JD(T)\). It is monic of degree 12, \(P(u)=0\), and \(Y\) is also integral over \(A_0\) from the displayed fourth-power relation. At the three roots of the denominator,
\[
 P(0)=2^{24},\qquad P(4)=2^{48},\qquad P(-4)=2^{36}.
\]
For \(a=0,4,-4\), define the actual polynomial \(Q_a(T)=(P(T)-P(a))/(T-a)\) in \(A_0[T]\). Evaluating at \(u\) gives
\[
 -Q_0(u)=2^{24}/u,\quad Q_4(u)=2^{48}/(4-u),\quad
 -Q_{-4}(u)=2^{36}/(4+u).
\]
Consequently \(2^{480}/D(u)=-Q_0(u)\,Q_4(u)^8\,(-Q_{-4}(u))^2\), with \(480=24+8\cdot48+2\cdot36\). Differentiating \(A(u)^3=JD(u)\) over \(L\) yields \(dJ=(P'(u)/D(u))\,du\), where the derivative in \(P'\) holds \(J\) fixed. Thus the following explicit polynomial expression in \(u,Y,J\) is an element of the true normalization \(B=\operatorname{Nor}_F(A_0)\) after pullback along \(\pi\):
\[
 b=2Y^3\,P'(u)(-Q_0(u))Q_4(u)^8(-Q_{-4}(u))^2
   =2^{480}\,dJ/\omega_{32}.
\]
Its integrality holds without postulating mixed-characteristic smooth reduction at \(2\). It does not require computing a minimal polynomial of the enormous original field.

## Actual Hodge row and literal entry

Use the same global invariant lattice \(H\) of the modular Jacobian \(J_X\). Choose a positive rational integer \(r\) clearing the finitely many coefficients of the inverse image of \(\pi^*\omega_{32}\) in \(L\otimes H\) under the existing generic Abel–Jacobi identification. Then there is a genuine \(h\in H\) with \(\omega_h=r\pi^*\omega_{32}\); it is nonzero. For its literal coefficient \(c(h)=\omega_h/dJ\), multiplying by the displayed \(b\in B\) gives exactly \(c(h)b=r2^{480}\). Hence the checked integer polynomial (in fact a constant) in the original \(p\)-certificate is
\[
 t=[F:L(J)]\,r\,2^{480}=\deg(p)\,r\,2^{480}\ne0 .
\]
Both \(h\) and \(b\), as well as the earlier \(1\)-column, may be included in their finite generating families. Only \(r\) comes from the one actual Hodge lattice; the literal dyadic multiplier is completely explicit. This statement does not compute the chosen Hodge lattice, enumerate the rest of the matrix, or change the arithmetic abelian surface \(A\) used in the opposite comparison.
