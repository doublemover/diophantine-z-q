# A 2-torsion pairing excludes every odd degree on the archived raw companion

Keep the fixed original archived input $a=0$, $h=473592745039516333133760$, $\beta=204618888056724185$, and the literal $\delta=1-b/17$. The [published raw reparametrization](https://github.com/doublemover/diophantine-z-q/blob/26396907b6850a8659e6ecaab31c6b056d66d136/research/quartic-318-326/original-a0-variable-first-no-raw-section/source/original-a0-variable-first-no-raw-section-r1/VARIABLE-FIRST-PARAMETER-NO-RAW-SECTION.md) is
$$
 f(X)=(X^2-\beta)(X^2+3\beta),\qquad
 W^2=\delta f(t)f(t').
$$
Here $\beta$ is positive and nonsquare, and $\delta$ is nonsquare, for example by the [already certified odd valuation at 6551](https://github.com/doublemover/diophantine-z-q/blob/f767adfb8eba61b98fc865e82f73776f4f095799/research/quartic-318-326/actual-scalar-specialization/ACTUAL-THETA-MODULAR-ROWS.md). No number or pole is changed.

**Theorem.** If $t,t'\in\mathbb Q(z)$ are both nonconstant and the raw equation holds with $W\in\mathbb Q(z)$, then **both** projection degrees are even. In particular, no bidegree-$(3,3)$ raw rational parametrization exists on this fixed scalar. This excludes both previously studied cubic strata; it does not decide whether an isolated rational point exists.

## 1. Why the pullback subgroups have trivial cross-pairing

Let $H$ be the smooth completion of the common double cover with $u^2=f(t(z))$ and put $v=W/u$, so $v^2=\delta f(t'(z))$. The double cover is geometrically integral: otherwise $f(t(z))$ would be a square in $\overline{\mathbb Q}(z)$, making a nonconstant map from the projective line to the smooth genus-one curve $C_1:u^2=f(t)$. There are two nonconstant maps over $\mathbb Q$,
$$
 g_1:H\longrightarrow C_1,\qquad
 g_2:H\longrightarrow C_\delta:\ v^2=\delta f(t').
$$

The two target Jacobians are rationally nonisogenous. Explicitly, on $C_1$ take
$$ x=2(t^2+\beta+u),\qquad y=2tx .$$
The product identity $(t^2+\beta+u)(t^2+\beta-u)=4\beta^2$ gives $y^2=x(x^2-4\beta x+16\beta^2)$, birationally with inverse $t=y/(2x)$ and $u=x/2-t^2-\beta$. This elliptic curve has $c_4=-512\beta^2$, discriminant $-196608\beta^6$, and $j=2048/3$. It has no complex multiplication since its rational $j$ is not integral. The Jacobian of $C_\delta$ is its nonsquare quadratic twist: the ordinate sign on the double cover induces $[-1]$ on the Jacobian, because any point and its involution image sum to a fibre divisor class. Over the quadratic extension the geometric Hom group is generated by an isomorphism since the geometric endomorphism ring is $\mathbb Z$; the nonsplit twist acts by negation on this group. Its rational part is therefore zero. These are the same target data as in the [previous cubic genus-three proof](https://github.com/doublemover/diophantine-z-q/commit/7f5c576f190ea398fa152f92814482b2e9cdc3e4).

The composition $g_{1,*}g_2^*:J(C_\delta)\to J(C_1)$ is consequently zero. For the canonical principal polarizations, pullback and pushforward are adjoint, so on geometric 2-torsion
$$
 e_{2,H}(g_1^*a,g_2^*b)
 =e_{2,C_1}(a,g_{1,*}g_2^*b)=1.
$$
No target 2-torsion class used here must be rational, and no assertion of injective pullback is needed. In particular if $H$ had genus one, the two nonconstant maps would give nonzero isogenies on Jacobians, contradicting the zero composition. Thus $H$ has genus at least two.

## 2. Pullback of a target root pair, with ramification

Work over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ for this calculation. Let $\mathcal R$ be the geometric branch set on the $z$-line of $H\to\mathbb P^1_z$, and $D_H$ the degree-two class of a fibre. Identify a point of $\mathcal R$ with its unique Weierstrass point above it when writing a divisor on $H$.

The square identity gives equality of the odd supports of $f(t)$ and $f(t')$ as rational functions on the projective source line. A pole of a parameter gives order a multiple of four, so these supports consist exactly of the source points over target roots at **odd** multiplicity. They may include source infinity. The same description is valid for either parameter independently.

On either target, for a pair of distinct root points $R_1,R_2$, consider the 2-torsion class $[R_1+R_2-D_i]$, where $D_i$ is the double-cover fibre class. Under $g_i^*$ its even-subset representative consists of precisely the common branch points above those two chosen roots. To verify this, take a target root and a point on the parameter line above it with multiplicity $m$. If $m$ is odd, the common double cover has one ramified point $Q$ above it. The local multiplicity of $g_i$ there is $m$: in the square with the two double-cover maps, both have local degree two. Thus its divisor contribution is
$$mQ\sim Q+\frac{m-1}{2}D_H.$$
If $m$ is even, the source double cover is unramified at that fibre. The two points each have local degree $m/2$ under $g_i$, so together contribute $(m/2)D_H$ and no Weierstrass term. This counting applies at source infinity using a local coordinate there. The sum of multiplicities over each target root is $d_i=\deg t_i$. Also $g_i^*D_i\sim d_iD_H$. Subtracting this class from the two fibre pulls leaves
$$
 \sum_{Q\in S_i}Q-\tfrac12|S_i|D_H ,
$$
where $S_i$ is the stated even subset. Indeed each chosen root has an odd-point count congruent to $d_i\bmod2$.

For the canonical hyperelliptic 2-torsion model the even subsets of $\mathcal R$, modulo taking complements, give exactly these divisor classes, and their Weil pairing is
$$ e_{2,H}(S_1,S_2)=(-1)^{|S_1\cap S_2|}. $$
See [Morgan, Section 8.1](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1112/plms.70066). One may move the geometric source infinity off the branch set to use the even-degree equation in that description; the class of a double-cover fibre is unchanged. Complementing either subset does not change the intersection parity because the other subset is even.

## 3. One mixed pair and one full pair read the projection-degree parity

Label the two quadratic root pairs
$$
 A=\{+\sqrt\beta,-\sqrt\beta\},\qquad
 B=\{+\sqrt{-3\beta},-\sqrt{-3\beta}\}.
$$
Their splitting fields are distinct quadratic fields (one real and one imaginary). In their biquadratic compositum the two swaps are independent, so choose Galois automorphisms inducing each single swap and fixing the other pair. Both parameter maps are rational, so these automorphisms permute the common branch set and act compatibly with the labels in both coordinates.

On $C_1$ choose the mixed pair $\{a_0,b_0\}$ with $a_0\in A$, $b_0\in B$. On $C_\delta$ choose the full pair $A$. The common branch points above $a_0$ in the first coordinate that go to $B$ in the second coordinate are even in number: the automorphism fixing $A$ and swapping $B$ bijects the two second-coordinate root fibres among those points while fixing $a_0$. The total number above $a_0$ is $d_1$ modulo two by the previous multiplicity calculation. Hence the number going from $a_0$ to $A$ is $d_1$ modulo two. The number going from $b_0$ to $A$ is even, using the other independent swap fixing $B$.

Therefore the two selected pullbacks pair by
$$ e_{2,H}(S_1,S_2)=(-1)^{d_1}. $$
Section 1 forces this to be $+1$, so $d_1$ is even. Exchanging the coordinates proves that $d_2$ is even. The argument does not assume simple individual parameter fibres, distinct pencils, the two degrees equal, or both odd.

## Scope

Both parameters must be nonconstant rational functions over $\mathbb Q$ on this fixed archived raw surface. In particular this rules out both the eight-branch and twelve-branch cubic cases, not just a first reduction cell at 6551. It does not exclude an even-even family, a parametrization over an extension with different Galois/endomorphism conditions, or any individual rational companion point. No new prime, source index, or local coefficient scan is used.

Check the frozen proof with `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS`; the cited standard pairing formula and elementary divisor calculations suffice to reproduce the argument.
