## Odd orders and the joint genus

First fix one exact coherent \(h\) with its chart and norm guards,
as in the literal complement fold calculation. The calculation
applies as well at the exact archived \(h_*\) used as a reference
for the 673 and additional local return periods.

At **every** one of the five simple contact roots \(C_jt=r\)
of \(F\), the three weighted leaves have pole orders \(1,7,25\).
Indeed each root is simple and \(C_jt-1\) is a unit. The order-seven
coefficient in the first real stage is the same fixed nonzero
\(H_h^2\), and the same initial dominance and thirteen subsequent
paired squarings give orders \(229376\) for the real fold and
strictly less for the imaginary fold. At that same root, the
trace quotient has order \(229376\), and the final regular
divisor \(c_j^5(C_jt-1)^3\) is a unit. Hence, at all five roots,
\[
                 v(b_j)=v(D_j)=1-2\cdot229376=-458751.
\]
The constant term 4, rather than the complement's 1, makes
no difference to this odd valuation.

In the product of the two \(D_j\), use the root \(t=-1/C_j\)
for the **smaller** of \(C_0,C_1\). Then the other quintic
argument is strictly less than \(-1\) and its quintic value is
strictly negative. If that other square multiplier \(S\) in
\(b=F S^2\) has a pole, the other \(D\) has even order. If it is
regular, the other \(D\) reduces to \(4+\nu S^2\) with
\(\nu>0\), hence has order zero. Thus the product has odd order.
Each of the two individual functions already has an odd order.
This proves that their two squareclasses are independent even
after extending the constants to \(\overline{\mathbf Q}\);
the positive nonvanishing used to check a rational coefficient
is established before extending the constants. Independence also
persists over the generic coherent source field, whose relevant
coefficient field is formally real.

The smooth projective normalization of the simultaneous-square
cover
\[
             Z_0^2=D_0(t),\qquad Z_1^2=D_1(t)
\]
at fixed \(h\) is geometrically connected of degree four over the
contact line. No finite extension of that function field whose
degree is not divisible by four can contain both square roots.
The two physical scale squares are distinct (in particular their
exact residue values at 673 differ). Among the four nonzero
contact roots in each scale, the four rational squareclasses
are distinct. A coincidence across the two scales would require
the ratio of two such roots to be a square; identical roots
are also excluded by the distinct scales. Explicitly the negative roots \(-1/2,-1,-32/407\) have
distinct classes by the 2-, 11- and 37-prime valuations, and the
positive root \(1/2399\) is separated by sign. The zero
root is the only shared contact root and is counted just once:
both functions have odd order there, their product has even
order, and geometric local inertia has order two. Infinity
is unramified: the literal folded quotient has
\(b_j=O(t^{-1})\) there, so \(D_j\to4\) with order zero.
There are therefore at least nine distinct branch locations
in the simultaneous cover, with possible additional finite
branch locations away from the contacts. Over geometric
constants, at every location of odd order for at least one
of the two functions the inertia has order two: the
completed geometric rational function field has just its
uniformizer squareclass, since all units have square roots.
If there are \(R\) such locations,
Riemann--Hurwitz gives \(2g-2=-8+2R\), hence \(g\ge6\).
This excludes a uniform shared \(t\)-parametrization, not
rational points of this cover. In particular it does not decide
whether any rational point is an actual Gaussian key, nor does
fixed-\(h\) geometry imply a bound as \(h\) varies.
