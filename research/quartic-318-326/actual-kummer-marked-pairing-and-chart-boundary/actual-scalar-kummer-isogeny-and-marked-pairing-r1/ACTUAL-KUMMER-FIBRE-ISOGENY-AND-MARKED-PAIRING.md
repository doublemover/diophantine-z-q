# All-place isogeny panel and one exact marked Cassels character

Use the [exact frozen scalar fibre](../../../rc-native-helper-20261120/ct-original-scalar-kummer-fiber-r1/ACTUAL-RAW-POINTPAIR-KUMMER-AND-SCALAR-FIBER.md), with the **same** \(\mu=\beta c^2\) and \(\lambda=1+3\mu\). Its fixed curve is
\[
        C_\lambda:\quad V^2=\lambda(t^2-\beta)(t^2+3\beta).
\]
This note does not replace its slope \(t'=1/c\), and does not test rationality of the entire Kummer surface. The exact compact actual \(\beta=204618888056724185\) and place tests for \(\lambda\) are inherited. In particular \(\lambda\) is nonsquare for the actual scalar. We do not assume an available factorization for its reduced numerator.

## 1. Orientation and exact local panel

Put \(L=[\lambda]\) and \(d=[\beta\lambda]=\beta L\), squarefree positive integer representatives. Ready gives \(\lambda\in\mathbf Q_p^{\times2}\) at \(2,3\) and at every prime \(p\mid\beta\), because \(v_2(\mu)\ge4\), \(v_3(\mu)\ge2\), and positive depth of \(\mu\) at the odd support of \(\beta\). Thus \(\gcd(\beta,L)=1\), \(L\equiv1\bmod24\), and \(d\equiv17\bmod24\) since \(\beta\) is square at 2 and a nonsquare unit at 3. The root curves are
\[
 E_d:y^2=x(x^2+2dx-3d^2),\qquad
 E'_d:y^2=x(x^2-4dx+16d^2).
\]
Let \(\phi:E_d\to E'_d\) be the isogeny with kernel generated by \(T_0=(0,0)\), and \(\hat\phi\) its dual. Rational scaling identifies the model with \(d\) to \(E_{\beta\lambda}\). The map \(x=\lambda t^2,\ y=\lambda tV\) on the latter is a \(\hat\phi\)-covering; its class is \(\lambda\) in \(S^-=\operatorname{Sel}^{(\hat\phi)}(E'_d)\). Its everywhere-local proof is in the donor. Its torsor is under \(E'_d\), so its obstruction lies in \(\Sha(E'_d)[\hat\phi]\), **not** in \(\Sha(E_d)[2]\). The dual local images \(\alpha(E_d),\alpha(E'_d)\) are the \(x\)-class maps for \(E_d/\hat\phi E'_d\) and \(E'_d/\phi E_d\) respectively.

* At each odd good \(p\nmid3d\), both images consist of all unit squareclasses. The isogeny on smooth reductions has constant kernel of order 2 and cokernel of order 2 (the isogenous curves have equal finite point counts); its prime-to-\(p\) formal map is an isomorphism. The \(x\)-class is unramified.
* At \(p\ge5\mid d\), \(\alpha(E_d)=\{1,-3,d,-3d\}\) in \(\mathbf Q_p^\times/\mathbf Q_p^{\times2}\). Indeed the local quotient \(E_d(\mathbf Q_p)/2\) has size four, and \(E_d[2]\) injects into it: halving any nonidentity rational 2-point would require each of the other root differences to be square, impossible at odd \(v_p(d)=1\). Their \(x\)-classes are the four displayed entries, allowing repeats. If \((-3/p)=-1\), the displayed classes are all four, and \(\alpha(E'_d)=\{1\}\). If \((-3/p)=+1\), they are just \(\{1,d\}\), and \(\alpha(E'_d)=\{1,2d(1+s)\}\), where \(s^2=-3\) in \(\mathbf Q_p\). For the latter image use the \(E'_d\) 2-point \(x=2d(1+s)\) (which has odd valuation); the other choice differs by a square because the product is \(16d^2\). The size statements follow from the exact short sequence
  \[
   0\longrightarrow E'_d(\mathbf Q_p)/\phi E_d(\mathbf Q_p)
   \xrightarrow{\hat\phi} E_d(\mathbf Q_p)/2E_d(\mathbf Q_p)
   \longrightarrow E_d(\mathbf Q_p)/\hat\phi E'_d(\mathbf Q_p)
   \longrightarrow 0 ;
  \]
  the first arrow is injective since \(E'_d[\hat\phi]=\phi E_d[2]\).
* At \(3\), \(d\) is a nonsquare unit and \(-3\) has odd valuation, so the torsion \(x\)-classes give all four classes directly on \(E_d\). Hence \(\alpha(E'_d)=\{1\}\).
* At \(2\), remove the square unit twist \(d\). On \(E_1\), \(x\)-depth is even if nonzero (for positive depth the other factors are units, and for negative depth the three factors share that depth). The classes \(-3\) at \(T_0\) and \(3\) at the point \((3,6)\) give all four unit classes. The same exact sequence and \(\#(E_1(\mathbf Q_2)/2)=8\) give size two on \(E'_1\); its class \(5\) is admitted since the right side at \(x=5\) is \(105\), a square unit.
* At the real place \(\alpha(E_d)\) is both signs (the bounded component has negative \(x\)); \(\alpha(E'_d)=\{1\}\) since the remaining quadratic in \(x\) is positive.

In particular \(S^-\) is exactly the following binary kernel. Its domain is the signed squarefree divisors of \(3d\), with coordinates for \(-1,3\) and every prime of \(d\). There is just one equation per \(p\mid d\) with \((-3/p)=+1\): the class of a candidate \(e\) equals \(1\) if \(p\nmid e\), or \(d\) if \(p\mid e\). In Legendre coordinates this is
\[
 \left(\frac{e/p^{v_p(e)}}p\right)
     =\left(\frac{d/p}p\right)^{v_p(e)} .
\]
At the other places there are no more equations on this domain. For comparison the positive group \(\operatorname{Sel}^{(\phi)}(E_d)\) consists of positive squarefree \(e\mid d\), with \(e\equiv1\bmod12\), and the full individual third-column local tests for \(\alpha(E'_d)\) just given. Neither exact dimension is computed here.

The actual factors \(5,11,17\) of \(\beta\) all survive in \(d\), and all have \((-3/p)=-1\). Therefore, **regardless of any unseen \(\lambda\) primes**,
\[
           \dim S^-\ \ge\ 2+\#\{p\mid d:(-3/p)=-1\}\ \ge 5 .
\]
The exact sequence from \(0\to E'_d[\hat\phi]\to E'_d[2]\xrightarrow{\hat\phi}E_d[\phi]\to0\) gives a kernel of dimension one for \(S^-\to\operatorname{Sel}_2(E'_d)\): it is the injected \(E_d(\mathbf Q)[\phi]/\hat\phi E'_d(\mathbf Q)[2]\), since \(E'_d(\mathbf Q)[2]\) consists only of the dual kernel. Thus \(\dim\operatorname{Sel}_2(E'_d)\ge4\). Also \(E_d(\mathbf Q)/\hat\phi E'_d(\mathbf Q)\) is a quotient of \(E_d(\mathbf Q)/2E_d(\mathbf Q)\), of dimension at most \(2+\operatorname{rank}E_d(\mathbf Q)\). If rank one were supplied, the **actual** \(\Sha(E'_d)[\hat\phi]\) would therefore have dimension at least two. A rank-one theorem alone, even with Sha finite, cannot settle the specified class.

For scope, \(E_1\) is [LMFDB 24.a4 / Cremona 24a1](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/24/a/4) by replacing the source \(x\) with \(X=x+1\). Its conductor is 24 and sign is \(+1\); this also follows from Fricke on \(\eta(2z)\eta(4z)\eta(6z)\eta(12z)\). Thus \(E_d\) has sign \(-1\) by the coprime twist formula \(w(E_d)=\chi_d(-24)\). Sign alone is not rank. One could use a certified suitable odd-\(p\) Selmer upper bound and published odd-\(p\) converse to establish analytic rank one, but no such bound is given here, and even that would not place \([\lambda]\) in the rational image. The nearby [Wei–Zhai binary-form theorem](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnag278) does not apply: \(D^2+3\beta r^2\), for \(c=r/D\), has discriminant field ramified at 3, while the theorem requires the form discriminant coprime to the reference elliptic conductor (24, or \(24\beta^2\) if \(\beta\) itself is the base twist). It would also not specify this unchanged \(c\).

## 2. Explicit function for the single marked pairing

On \(C_\lambda\), set \(S=t^2,\,T=1\). The pushout conic
\[
 R^2=\lambda(S-\beta T)(S+3\beta T)
\]
has the rational point \((S:T:R)=(\beta:1:0)\); no number-field conic solver is needed. Parametrize from it using \(z=R/(S-\beta T)\). Clearing denominators gives the binary quadratic numerator \(\beta(z^2+3\lambda)\) for \(S\), with denominator \(z^2-\lambda\). Thus Fisher's first pushout function is
\[
 f_2=\beta(z^2+3\lambda)=\frac{4\beta\lambda\, t^2}{t^2-\beta}.
\]
It has the same squareclass as \(\beta\lambda(t^2-\beta)\). One can also see directly that \(t^2-\beta\) has even divisor: each of its two simple \(t\)-line zeros becomes a double zero at a branch point, and both poles at infinity have order two. Its half-divisor gives the nonzero rational 2-class of \(E'_d\): over the algebraic closure \(t^2-\beta\) is not a square in the hyperelliptic function field, since neither it nor its ratio with the defining quartic is a square on the \(t\)-line. Constant \(\beta\lambda\) has global Hilbert product one against any rational \(e\).

Apply [Fisher, *Higher descents*, §§3,5, Eq. (9) and the function \(f_2\)](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~taf1000/papers/higherdesc.pdf). For a true \(e\in S^-\), the sign of the Cassels–Tate pairing (values \(0,\tfrac12\)) against the marked \([\lambda]\) is
\[
 (-1)^{2\langle [\lambda],e\rangle}
 =\prod_v (t_v^2-\beta,e)_v
 =\boxed{\prod_{p\mid L}\left(\frac3p\right)^{v_p(e)}} .
\]
Here the local points are chosen on the literal quartic, away from zeros and poles of the function. At \(2,3,\infty\) and the old support of \(\beta\), \(\lambda\) is locally square, so take \(t_v\) near infinity; \(t_v^2-\beta\) is square. At any remaining \(p\) of even \(\lambda\)-depth, \(e\) is a unit and \(v_p(t_v^2-\beta)\) is even: a pole of \(t_v\) gives even depth, and a positive-depth zero of this factor leaves \(t_v^2+3\beta\) a unit. At \(p\mid L\), \(p\ge5\), the actual identity \(\lambda=1+3\beta c^2\) shows that \(v_p(\lambda)>0\) is odd, \(c\) is a unit, and \(-3\beta\) is square in \(\mathbf Q_p\). Choose the given local point near \(t_v^2=-3\beta\). Then \(t_v^2-\beta\equiv-4\beta\bmod p\) is a unit, and the Hilbert sign at this place is \((-4\beta/p)^{v_p(e)}=(3/p)^{v_p(e)}\), as asserted.

This is a test of **one marked row**; it needs no class-group computation and does not assume finiteness of Sha. In particular **no old-support \(e\) can obstruct**, even if its admission were proven. Its unknown admission conditions can themselves be folded into the equivalence
\[
 \lambda\in N_{\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt e)\,/\,\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})}
              \mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt e)^\times
\]
once the old \(\beta\) rows are checked. Indeed for \(e\) supported on signed divisors of \(3\beta\), the only possible nonzero rational symbols \((e,\lambda)\) occur at \(p\mid L\). Restriction to \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\) kills precisely the nonsplit local obstructions; at split primes these symbols reproduce the hidden admission rows. The quadratic norm criterion over that field proves the equivalence. This is not a claim of an actual norm witness. In particular the tautological \(\beta\) row differs from the mark by the rational torsion class \(d\).

Any **actual** admissible \(e\) with displayed sign \(-1\) certifies the fixed quartic has no rational point. Vanishing for all \(e\) only guarantees that the mark lifts to the image of \(\operatorname{Sel}_2(E_d)\to S^-\), by Fisher's Theorem 2.1 for the first isogeny pairing. It is not a rational point or a zero Sha class. An unadmitted candidate does not give a valid obstruction.
