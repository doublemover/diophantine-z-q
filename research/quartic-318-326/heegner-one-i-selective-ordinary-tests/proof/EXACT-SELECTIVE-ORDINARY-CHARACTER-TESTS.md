# Selective ordinary mirror tests on the actual one-I curve

Keep \(E=E_d\), \(V=E[13]\), and the exact faithful ordinary class
group on \(F^c\). This shrinks the prime *tests* and certifies local
quotient rows, not a global class-group computation. Use arithmetic
Frobenius throughout.

## 1. Exact local readouts and degree-one reduction

At a good rational prime \(\ell\ne13\), let \(g\) be Frobenius
on \(V\). A strict Selmer class is unramified there, represented
on Frobenius by \(v\in V/(g-1)V\). For a nonzero covector \(c\)
of Frobenius-orbit length \(f\), the reflected character's
unramified value at the corresponding mirror prime is

    c((1 + g + ... + g^(f−1)) v),

with one common sign if the Weil pairing is written in the
opposite order. Use the actual covector attached to \(P\)
and the chosen primitive root, not merely the projective line.
Over the common point/cyclotomic completion Shapiro gives the
Weil pairing of the restricted cocycle with \(P\); its value
on \(g^f\) follows from the cocycle law. The extra restriction
degree from the mirror completion divides twelve and multiplies
an unramified value by that degree, so it cancels. Changing the
representative \(v\) by \((g-1)w\) changes the sum by
\(c((g^f-1)w)=0\). Scaling \(c\) scales the readout in the exact
faithful direction.

- If \(g-1\) is invertible, all readouts vanish.
- If the eigenvalues are distinct \(1,a\), one degree-one covector
  line is fixed by \(g\), dual to the 1-eigenline. With \(v\)
  chosen in that 1-eigenline the sum is \(fv\), so other readouts
  are zero or specified multiples of this single value.
- If \(g=1\), two independent degree-one covectors suffice.
- If \(g\ne1\) is unipotent with eigenvalue 1, every nonfixed
  orbit has length 13 and the thirteen-power sum is zero.
  The fixed covector line gives a single degree-one readout.

Thus all good-prime readouts of **actual strict characters**
are determined by degree-one primes at the same rational prime.
One must not impose arbitrary split-prime vanishing: the
1-eigenline supplies a local degree of freedom. At the bad
rational primes the prior local calculations give zero Kummer
image away from 13: additive at 2 and primes dividing \(d\), with
no prime-13 component or reduction contribution, and at 3 the
split Tate valuation and residual multiplicative group have
order two. At 13 strict localization is zero by definition.

Under the GRH assumption of [Grenié--Molteni, Corollary 3.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.02430),
the actual degree-168 ordinary class group is generated by prime
ideals of norm at most \(4\log^2|D_{F^c}|\). The constant-four
clause applies: the actual log discriminant is below 6100, and
the stated threshold \((2\pi e^\gamma)^{168}/e\) exceeds
\(6^{168}/3\). An exact rational atanh series with positive-tail
bounds gives the integer floor **147,866,716**. Any nonzero strict
character is nonzero on at least one such ideal; the local
formula detects it on a degree-one prime over the **same**
rational \(\ell\), with \(13\mid\#E(\mathbf F_\ell)\) and
\(\ell\le147{,}866{,}716\). This is conditional injectivity of
a family of readouts, not an upper bound on their domain.

## 2. A sharp placement barrier and a genuine safe test

Consider good \(\ell\ne13\) where \(\#E(\mathbf F_\ell)\) is
not divisible by 13, so unramifiedness forces zero. To survive
faithful projection on divisors, a mirror prime must split
completely relative to \(K\): a nontrivial relative stabilizer
kills the faithful projection. Thus its covector orbit length
\(f\) equals its projective orbit length. It cannot be one.
If it were two, the covector and its first Frobenius translate
would be independent but both fixed by the square, giving
either an eigenvalue 1 or scalar \(-1\); the latter contradicts
projective length two. Repeated eigenvalues unequal to 1 add no
possibilities. A scalar has nontrivial scalar return at each
line. A nonscalar Jordan block has projective lengths one and
thirteen, and in both the scalar return is the same eigenvalue unequal to one.

Hence only \(\ell\le1951\) can give a smaller norm than
\(1951^3\). The exact point-count and companion-matrix check
gives the minimum **\(1951^3=7{,}426{,}288{,}351\)**.
At 1951 the trace is \(-40\), the point count 1992, and
the distinct eigenvalues have order three, giving four
relative-split projective orbits of length three. A specific
ideal has cubic \(Y\) factor \([1,66,1847,82]\) and
\(Z\equiv1224Y^2+947Y+1704\) modulo that factor and 1951.
The absolute \(Y\) equation is squarefree there and the
checked nonzero root of \(Z^2=13^4dY\) selects an honest
maximal-order prime whose strict readout is zero.

Its norm is more than 50 times the conditional generation
cap. Forced zero from an invertible \(g-1\) alone therefore
supplies **no** nontrivial good test inside that cap on this
actual field. This is not a barrier to other class methods.

## 4. A selective non-splitting test is not global generation

For a putative nonzero ordinary character with associated
affine fields \(U/K,T/K,F^c/K\), one has an exact Euler quotient

    ζ_(F^c)(s) ζ_K(s) / ζ_T(s)
         = product_(p in B) (1−N(p)^(-13s)) / (1−N(p)^(-s))^13 ,

for \(\Re s>1\). Here \(B\) comprises exactly the unramified
\(K\)-primes split in the cyclic resolvent with nonzero
translation Frobenius, equivalently inert in \(T/K\).
If scalar Frobenius is nonidentity its translation can be
conjugated away, and the local orbit lists differ by one
fixed label. At scalar identity with nonzero translation the
affine residue is one 13-cycle, giving the displayed factor.
At a ramified prime, inertia is a nontrivial scalar subgroup
up to conjugation. Its unique common affine fixed point is
preserved by decomposition, which normalizes inertia, so
decomposition is scalar in those coordinates as well.
The affine and cyclic permutation modules then differ by a
trivial label there, even after taking inertia invariants.
All ramified contributions therefore cancel. Log Euler
coefficients for the displayed primes are \(13\log N(\mathfrak p)\)
at powers not divisible by 13, and zero otherwise.

This distinguishes a **supplied** nonzero character without
listing all affine extensions. The conditional finite cap and
new local rows save test work, but they do not bound the number
of independent global characters. A projected ordinary
relation presentation, or an independently proved dimension
bound for the character domain, remains to be supplied.
No rank, class bound, or point is asserted.
