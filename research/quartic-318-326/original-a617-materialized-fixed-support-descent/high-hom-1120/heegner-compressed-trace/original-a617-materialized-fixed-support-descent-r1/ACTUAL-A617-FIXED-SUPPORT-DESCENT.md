# Fixed-support Kummer descent on the materialized original a=617 scalar

This works on the **same numeric h,J** and its positive exact complement δ, not any retuned scalar. The direct readout is that the natural pure-second vector `(1,θ,θ)` fails at 47, and a stronger aggregate calculation excludes `(1,θc,θc)` for every fixed-supported c. The full fixed-support block has dimension three at the fixed places, but its two additional basis vectors are not claimed to be globally admitted. Three exact quartic norm equations are the remaining admission tests on that block.

## 1. Fixed roster and actual aggregate exclusion

Use the exact roots e,f and the actual finite δ residues in `../original-a617-numeric-reconstruction-r2/EXACT-ROOT-SIGN.json` (SHA-256 `c5093c42d1aace2def0059e75b46150540245fb86a3c6194e60d3b91b46aa36f`). Every place outside S has a good baseline model, including primes dividing the denominator of z. This use of S is intentionally **not** the larger tangent-exception roster used for the separate Cassels formula. The source complement δ is positive and a unit at every odd p in S, and a square in Q₂. Its positive squarefree representative θ is consequently 1 modulo 8 and coprime to S.

The full rational 2-descent uses triples of squareclasses with square product, from `(x,x−δe,x−δf)`. At a good odd p with odd δ depth, use the normalized odd twist. The actual local quotient by doubling is generated by the nonzero-root torsion classes Te,Tf: the quotient has order four and their valuation parities `(1,0,1),(1,1,0)` are independent. The third nonzero type is T₀ with parity `(0,1,1)` and triple `(ef,−δe,−δf)` in local squareclasses. Thus a locally admitted pure-second `(1,d,d)` with p dividing d to odd order at such a place requires `(ef/p)=+1`. For a globally admitted vector whose entries have no valuation at p its local image instead has to be the identity.

The squareclass of ef is `(z−4)(z²−40z−32)(7z³+16z²+32z+32)`. The checker matches that class to the product of the ten listed odd fixed support primes, directly by testing a positive rational square ratio. Quadratic reciprocity, θ≡1 modulo 8 and the fixed units give the exact identity

The seven negative terms are at 47, 37649, 6207001, 974953729, 29734087137400621, 25177769110018199947, 142598860296677615953. In particular at least one actual odd prime of θ is inert in Q√ef. This rejects **every** pure-second `(1,θc,θc)` when c is supported on S (and a possible sign), because such a c cannot remove any odd θ prime. No bound on the number of unknown primes is used. Independently, at the literal prime 47, the roots δe and δf have depths 0 and 1, respectively, and θ is a nonsquare unit. For a non-torsion affine local point with square first coordinate x, at depth v(x)≤0 the third coordinate x−δf is square; at depth v(x)>0 even, the third depth is 1 but the second depth is 0. ## 2. Exact finite block and meaning of dimension three

At odd p the local quotient E(Q_p)/2 has size four, and at 2 it has size eight. This follows from the open one-dimensional formal subgroup, on which the index for multiplication by 2 is 1 at odd p and 2 at p=2, and the kernel from two independent rational 2-torsion points. The replay constructs a complete local basis at every p in S; it uses the rational 2-torsion points, then only finitely many explicit x coordinates on the relevant local model when needed. Verification of each affine candidate is exact: test that the cubic value is nonzero and a square in Q_p, then record the first two Kummer classes. In the actual receiver, replacing the twist unit by its archived residue at odd p changes it by a square. An even coordinate scaling of the roots also does not change the ordered Kummer classes. At 2 use the square twist and the normalized explicit root depths. At infinity the allowable signs are `(+,+,+)` or `(−,+,−)` since e<f<0:
only the e-coordinate class must be positive. The witnesses and bases are archived and recomputed in standard-library Python.

Test all squareclass tuples with support in S and a sign, using first and second coordinates as independent variables. Each local class image is a subspace; the exact annihilator rows give a joint matrix of nullity three. Its basis is the known mark η=(1,β,β) and two explicit rows U,V. Adding both necessary whole-θ constraints `(b₀/θ)=(b_e/θ)=+1` leaves exactly that same space. These constraints are necessary for a fixed-supported tuple to be admitted at all odd θ primes, but **are not the pointwise tests**. The first coordinates of U,V are independent squareclasses (one is negative, one is positive nonsquare), so the only fixed-supported globally admitted pure-second vectors are the identity and η, which are already known locally everywhere.

For a tuple supported on S together with either all or none of the odd θ primes in each slot, its valuation parity pattern is constant across θ. One can remove it by multiplying by the Kummer image of one rational torsion point, as the three nonzero patterns are exactly the three torsion patterns. The resulting fixed-supported vector lies in the above three-dimensional finite block. This observation does not bound vectors with different parity patterns at different odd θ primes.

## 3. Each nontrivial fixed-support coset is one exact quartic norm

For W in {U,V,U·V}, write W=(a,b,ab) as rational squareclasses, using the explicit signed squarefree representatives in `EXACT-FIXED-SUPPORT.json`. Set L=Q(√a,√b). The following small correction primes q and local places v are exact:

| Row | q | Place with full decomposition group V₄ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| U | 27953 | 47 |
| V | 8377 | 17 |
| U·V | 12457 | 17 |

At the indicated place the two classes have exact squareclass codes 1 and 2 (the uniformizer and nonsquare unit). This proves [L:Q]=4 and a full decomposition group. The recorded q is prime, 1 modulo 8, and splits completely in L. At every odd prime dividing either signed radical a,b, the q-residue symbol equals the actual δ unit character. The script checks these facts directly; no claim that these q are minimal is used.

At every fixed place, qδ is a norm locally: at 2 it is square; at the odd ramified places it is a square unit by choice of q, and the other odd fixed places are unramified; at infinity it is positive. A square is a local norm here because all local Galois groups have exponent two, by local reciprocity. At an unramified odd place outside S, the unit part is a norm and the norm valuations are the multiples of the local residue degree, which is 1 or 2. Thus the only remaining local test is that every p of odd θ depth split completely in L. The correction prime itself splits so adds no constraint. The [Tate criterion, as stated by Frei–Loughran–Newton, Theorem 6.1](https://pure.manchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/55657924/HNP.pdf) gives the Hasse norm principle here: the cohomological restriction for a place with full decomposition group is the identity. Therefore the exact admission criterion is

```
W is globally admitted  ⇔  qδ is a rational norm from L.
```

Indeed away S∪supp(θ), the good 2-Kummer image is exactly the unramified two-dimensional subgroup, so a fixed-supported W already has valid local conditions there. At a θ prime W has zero valuation parities, so its local tuple must be identity, exactly simultaneous splitting of a,b. Multiplying W by η does not change that test since β splits at every odd θ prime from δ=1−βc². Thus this is the exact coset test, independent of any hidden-support count. An explicit certificate for the right-hand side is a rational solution of

```
(x₀²+a x₁²−b x₂²−ab x₃²)² − a(2x₀x₁−2b x₂x₃)² = qδ.
```

The formula is the tower norm through Q√a. **None** of these three literal norm equations has been solved or obstructed here. No actual additional Selmer vector, Cassels sign on one, rank, graph point, or four-pole witness follows just from this fixed block. Once an admission witness is supplied, its first Cassels row still needs the fixed local terms and any denominator factor; this paper does not evaluate them.
