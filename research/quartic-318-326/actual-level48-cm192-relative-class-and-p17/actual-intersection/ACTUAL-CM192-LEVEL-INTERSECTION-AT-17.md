# A genuine marked CM intersection at 17 after an admissible final prime refinement

On the first arm of the original balanced detector pair, with the allowed choice \(u=1,v=\theta\), one can refine the final prime \(q_0\) so that a point in its **literal oriented level-48 CM class trace** meets the fixed marked CM fibre of discriminant \(-192\) at 17. That collision has normalized intersection multiplicity \(1/2\), or one in the ramified uniformizer. The same selected \(q_0\) still gives both Selmer eights and both original marks. This certifies one genuine term of the signed intersection ideal, **not** its total valuation, a residue sign, trace nonvanishing, a rank, or a rational detector point.

## 1. A short necessary list for collisions at good primes

Keep \(E_0,\phi,P_{\mathfrak a},T,D=-n\) from the marked cyclic-four paper. Specifically \(T=(3+2\sqrt3,2(3+2\sqrt3))\) is the chosen half of \(T_3\), and the fibre in \(X_0(48)\) has \(t=y/x=+2,\ X=1\pm\sqrt2,\ Y=4\sqrt3X^2\). Its two points are individually fixed by \(w_{48}\): the literal formula
\[
 (X,Y)\longmapsto((X+1)/(X-1),4Y/(X-1)^4)
\]
fixes them since \(X^2-2X-1=0\). Each has CM order \(O_8=\mathbf Z+8O_0\) in \(K_0=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\), \(O_0=\mathbf Z[\omega],\ \omega=(1+\sqrt{-3})/2\). Write \(v=4\sqrt{-3}\in O_8\). On either fixed flagged elliptic curve \((A,C)\), Fricke fixedness supplies an endomorphism of degree 48 with kernel \(C\): compose quotient by \(C\) and the identifying isomorphism back to \(A\). Since \(O_8=\mathbf Z[v]\) and \(\operatorname{N}(a+bv)=a^2+48b^2\), the only degree-48 possibilities are \(\pm v\). Hence
\[
 \ker v=C\quad\text{on both fixed flags, including after good reduction.}
\]

At any actual collision of flags in characteristic \(p>3\), the original CM element \(u=\sqrt D\), \(u^2=-n\), acts on the same \(A\) and \(\omega_D=(1+u)/2\) preserves \(C\). Both fields embed in the reduced endomorphism algebra, which is the supersingular quaternion algebra \(B_{p,\infty}\); ordinary collision is impossible for two different CM fields. Put \(s=-\operatorname{Trd}(uv)/2\). This is integral since \(u=2\omega_D-1\) and \(\operatorname{Trd}(v)=0\). Pure quaternions satisfy \(uv+vu=-2s\), so
\[
 \omega_D v+v\omega_D=v-s.
\]
The left side kills \(C\), as does \(v\), while \(C\) is cyclic of order 48 in characteristic \(p>3\). Hence \(48\mid s\). Set \(s=48k\). Then \(v\) and \(u-kv\) anticommute and have squares \(-48,-(n-48k^2)\). The second is strictly negative: reduced norm is positive on nonzero elements of \(B_{p,\infty}\), and \(u\) is not a rational multiple of \(v\) because the two CM fields are different. Thus \(N_k=n-48k^2>0\) and
\[
 B_{p,\infty}\simeq(-3,-N_k),\qquad |k|<\sqrt{n/48}.
\]
Since \(N_k\equiv23\bmod24\), the local symbol at 2 is split (unramified quadratic, \(-N_k\) an odd unit), and at 3 is split (\(-N_k\equiv1\bmod3\)). At odd primes away from 3 the ramification condition is exactly \(\ell\equiv2\bmod3\) and \(v_\ell(N_k)\) odd. It follows that \(p\) is the **only** prime \(\ell\equiv2\bmod3\) occurring to odd exponent in \(N_k\). This yields a candidate-support calculation from less than \(2\sqrt{n/48}+1\) integer trace parameters without enumerating class indices. Its condition is necessary, not a sufficiency test for an arbitrary candidate: local integral flags and some optimal embeddings still need to exist.

## 2. Actually producing an oriented intersection for \(k=0,p=17\)

Take the published balanced selector with \(u=1,\ v=\theta\). No repair prime is required since \(u=1\). After the helpers are chosen, the first twist is
\[
 n=17R q_0,\qquad R=(\beta/17)\prod I_j,
\]
with every prime in \(R\) \(1\bmod24\), and the shared final \(q_0\equiv7\bmod24\). In particular \(R\equiv1\bmod24\), all primes of \(Rq_0\) split in \(K_0\), and \(17\) is the sole nonsplit prime dividing \(n\). All old helper, companion-side, and final-prime bits are retained below.

Fix either of the two actual \(w_{48}\)-fixed flags in \(\phi^{-1}(T)\) and a reduction \((A,C)\) at 17. The underlying \(-192\) CM elliptic curve is connected to a \(j=0\) elliptic curve by a chain of three 2-isogenies, using its three ascending conductor steps. Reduce that chain at 17; after an isomorphism at the \(j=0\) end take \(A_0:y^2=x^3+1\) over \(\mathbf F_{17}\). Its 17-Frobenius \(\pi\) has \(\pi^2=-17\) (17 is inert in \(K_0\), or count the cubes since the cube map is bijective). It conjugates the \(K_0\)-action since it conjugates the cube roots of unity. Transport \(\pi\) through the degree-eight isogeny as a quasi-endomorphism of \(A\); it still satisfies those identities.

In the rational 2-adic Tate module of \(A\), identify the \(K_{0,2}\)-line with \(K_{0,2}\) and its integral lattice, after homothety, with \(O_{8,2}=\mathbf Z_2+8\mathbf Z_2\omega\). This is the actual CM lattice before reduction: a proper ideal lattice in the quadratic order is locally principal, and prime-to-17 Tate modules agree after good reduction. The transported \(\pi\) acts by \(x\mapsto\eta_0\bar x\) for a fixed \(\eta_0\in O_{0,2}^\times\) with norm \(-17\). In fact the relation with \(K_0\) gives semilinearity, \(\pi^2=-17\) gives the norm, and the norm is a unit so \(\eta_0\) is a unit in the unramified quadratic algebra at 2.

The Eisenstein integers \(O_0\) have class number one. Choose \(\gamma_0\in O_0\) of norm \(R\) by taking one Eisenstein prime for each distinct rational split factor. We require a prime \(q_0\) from the existing rational CRT whose factor \(\gamma_q\in O_0\) can be chosen to satisfy
\[
 N(\gamma_q)=q_0,\qquad
  \eta_0\gamma_0\gamma_q\in\mathbf Z_2^\times(1+16O_{0,2}).\tag{1}
\]
This is one ray in the **conductor-16 ring class field of \(K_0\)**, not a new independent condition at a prime of the original curve. Indeed its ring class group is
\[
 S=O_{0,2}^\times/
    \bigl(\mathbf Z_2^\times(1+16O_{0,2})\,O_0^\times\bigr),
\]
because \(O_0\) has class number one. The desired Artin class, up to the uniform choice of reciprocity inverse, is that of \((\eta_0\gamma_0)^{-1}\). A generator of the prime over \(q_0\) in the desired ray can be adjusted by an Eisenstein unit to make (1) exact. The local norm of the desired ray is \(7\bmod8\), since \(N\eta_0=-17,\ N\gamma_0=R\equiv1\bmod8\). This norm is well-defined on \(S\): rational odd units give square norm \(1\bmod8\), principal \(1+16\) units give \(1\bmod8\), and global Eisenstein units have norm 1. The finite replay verifies the eight classes and this quotient directly.

There is no hidden conflict with any fixed representative of the old \(q_0\) CRT. Let \(H_{16}/K_0\) be this ring class field and \(Q=\mathbf Q(\zeta_M)\) the cyclotomic field of that rational CRT, enlarging \(M\) to include 24. By [ring class reciprocity and the generalized dihedral property, Cox §9](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/~ila/Cox-Primes_of_the_form_x2%2Bny2.pdf), \(H_{16}/\mathbf Q\) is unramified outside 2 and 3 and has elementary abelian 2 abelianization (conjugation acts on \(S\) by inversion). Its intersection with \(Q\) is therefore contained in \(\mathbf Q(\zeta_{24})\): every quadratic field unramified outside 2 and 3 has fundamental discriminant dividing 24. The restriction of the desired \(K_0\)-Artin class on this intersection is the cyclotomic action by norm \(7\bmod24\). For an explicit representative, lift the specified unit class at 2 to an \(\alpha\in O_0\) prime to 3, using CRT. Its norm is \(7\bmod8\) and \(1\bmod3\), the latter since reduction of the Eisenstein norm at the ramified prime 3 is a nonzero square. Artin on the cyclotomic piece is given by that rational norm. Thus the two Galois conditions agree on the intersection, and Chebotarev in \(H_{16}Q/\mathbf Q\) supplies arbitrarily large shared final primes satisfying both, avoiding the old finite support. If Frobenius is the inverse in the desired dihedral conjugacy class, take the other prime of \(K_0\) above \(q_0\).

Now use \(\gamma=\gamma_0\gamma_q\), \(u=\gamma\pi\) on \(A\). It has \(u^2=-17N(\gamma)=-n\) and \(uv=-vu\). The rational endomorphism \(\omega_D=(1+u)/2\) is integral at all primes except possibly 2 by construction: away from 2 transport through the degree-eight isogeny introduces only a 2-power denominator. At 2, \(u\) is \(x\mapsto\eta\bar x\) for \(\eta=\eta_0\gamma\), and (1) says \(\eta\) is rational and odd modulo \(16O_{0,2}\). The map \(1+\eta\,\mathrm{conj}\) sends both \(1\) and \(8\omega\) into \(2O_{8,2}\) (use \(\bar\omega=1-\omega\)); hence \(\omega_D\) preserves the Tate lattice. A rational endomorphism with 2-power denominator which preserves this lattice is integral: multiply it by a power of 2 to obtain an endomorphism, which kills the corresponding 2-power torsion and therefore factors through that multiplication map. Thus \(\omega_D\in\operatorname{End}(A)\). The identity \(v\omega_D=(1-\omega_D)v\) shows that \(\omega_D\) preserves \(C=\ker v\), the **actual** cyclic 48-level, not merely \(j(A)\).

This gives an optimal \(O_K\)-embedding in the flagged endomorphism ring since \(D=-n\) is fundamental and its order is maximal. At 17 every such embedding is normalized for Deuring lifting, as the quadratic extension is ramified. By [normalized Deuring lifting, Elkies–Ono–Yang §3](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~tonghaiyang/deuring.pdf), it lifts to a characteristic-zero \(O_K\)-CM elliptic curve; lift the prime-to-17 flag by finite étaleness, retaining its \(O_K\)-stability. Since 2 and 3 split in \(K\), that flag is exactly one of the level orientations \(\mathfrak p_2^4\mathfrak p_3\). Use this permitted orientation for the first arm. Its full class orbit includes the lifted flag. This is a genuine intersection on the new, equally admissible selected twist. It does not choose a different detector \(b\) or alter any of the source polynomial equations. The second arm retains Selmer size eight and its mark, but this argument need not produce an intersection for it.

## 3. Exact multiplicity and the one-prime parity reduction

In fact at any such collision with \(p>3\), \(p\mid n\), the local intersection length with \(T\) is exactly one over the ramified local base. The fixed flag is defined over an unramified extension (its chart coordinates lie in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)\)); the original trace's CM flag is defined over its Hilbert class field, unramified over \(K\). Thus one may compare over a strict local extension with absolute ramification index two and uniformizer \(\varpi=\sqrt D\). The good modular curve is locally the deformation of the flagged elliptic curve: one may adjoin an auxiliary prime-to-\(p\) fine level to remove automorphisms, an étale local step because a flag here has no automorphism other than \(\pm1\). A nontrivial automorphism of order 3 fixing a cyclic 16-level would give an eigenvalue of order 3 on a cyclic 2-primary group of exponent 16, impossible on its order-two line; similarly order 4 would require a unit square \(-1\bmod16\). At \(p>3\) these cover extra elliptic automorphisms. The literal map \(\phi\) is étale at these flags, since its deck sends \(X\mapsto-1/X\) and a fixed point \(X^2=-1\) cannot have \(t=+2\) in odd characteristic.

If the two elliptic sections near the flag agreed modulo \(\varpi^2\), their curves over that quotient would be identified with the constant special curve pulled back from the residue field: the fixed flag is unramified and \((p)=(\varpi^2)\). On this constant elliptic curve any lift of its reduced endomorphism \(u\) as a group homomorphism equals the constant lift. To see uniqueness, the difference of two lifts is a group homomorphism reducing to zero. It factors through an affine neighborhood of the origin (the special fibre is a homeomorphism on underlying spaces), so it is constant by properness and \(\Gamma(E,\mathcal O)=\) the base ring, and therefore zero. The reduced \(u\) acts by zero on the tangent (\(u^2=-n=0\) in that residue field), so the constant lift acts by zero. The original normalized CM lift, however, acts by \(\sqrt D\) (or its negative, depending on the embedding), nonzero modulo \(\varpi^2\). This contradiction proves the intersection has order exactly one in \(\varpi\); it is \(I_{\mathfrak a}=1/2\) with \(v(p)=1\). Thus the constructed term contributes \(+1/2\) or \(-1/2\) to the signed sum in the cyclic-four paper, and its sign is negative with the usual choice of that lifted class as the \(\chi_{17}=+\) seed. Other terms are not excluded.

There is also a parity simplification at 17. Inertia there flips \(\sqrt{17}\), so in \(\tau=r c\) one has \(\chi_{17}(r)=-1\). The exact level-ideal \(\chi_{17}\)-value is also \(-1\). Consequently the class-index involution \(a\mapsto B a^{-1}\) preserves both genus halves at 17. It sends collisions at a chosen Fricke-fixed flag to collisions at that same flag. All two-element orbits give even total contribution in integral \(\varpi\)-order of the signed ideal. Only fixed class indices matter for its parity.

These fixed collision indices are exactly the \(k=0\) ones. With the normalized CM type, the involution sends the reduced embedding element \(u\) to \(v(-u)v^{-1}=u-2kv\) on the fixed flagged curve. The minus comes from restoring the CM type after inertia (\(\sqrt D\) changes sign); the conjugation by \(v\) is the fixed Fricke quotient. If the index is fixed then these two embedding elements agree up to a flag automorphism, namely at most \(\pm1\), so \(k=0\). Conversely \(k=0\) gives identical normalized embeddings, and uniqueness in normalized Deuring lifting, together with the unique prime-to-17 lift of the indicated cyclic subgroup, gives the same class index. Thus the parity of the 17 component can in principle be counted by the anti-commuting norm problem, rather than all signed pairs. The construction in §2 guarantees one solution, **not** odd total parity. It also does not supply any of the other components required for a coherent global residue comparison.
