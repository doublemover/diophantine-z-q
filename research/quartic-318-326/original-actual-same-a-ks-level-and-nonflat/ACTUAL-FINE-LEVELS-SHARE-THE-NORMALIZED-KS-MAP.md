It neither replaces the surface by its coarse isomorphism class nor uses the separate modular Jacobian. The new assertion is actual agreement of the normalized integral determinant maps, including on the nodal overlap, not merely equality of image ideals.

Let $S=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z[1/33]$. Use the full-frame loci on the genuine fine models $X_i$, and the two constructed finite étale maps $g_i:X_{33}\to X_i$ over $S$, $i=3,11$. There are actual Cartesian universal-family squares: $A_{33}=g_i^*A_i$ as polarized abelian schemes, with the same identity and fixed compatible polarization. Here and below “relative dualizing” for each curve means relative to the specified integer base (restricted to $S$ on the overlap).

## Exact functoriality before any height-trait evaluation

For each level put $D_i=\omega_{X_i/S}$ and $H_i=\det(e_i^*\Omega_{A_i/X_i})$. The identity gives the canonical $g_i^*H_i\simeq H_{33}$ (ordinary relative differential base change for smooth abelian schemes, then determinant). For $D_i$ use the canonical $g_i^*D_i\simeq D_{33}$. One concrete normalization is finite duality: the relative dualizing module of a finite étale algebra $B/C$ is $\operatorname{Hom}_C(B,C)$, identified with $B$ by $b\mapsto(b'\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{B/C}(bb'))$. This is an isomorphism by the perfect trace pairing, also where the degree is not a unit. The composition of relative dualizing complexes gives the stated identification over $S$; on the smooth locus it is ordinary differential pullback. These normalizations follow from [Stacks Lemma 49.15.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BVI), [Lemma 49.3.1](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BJF), and [Lemma 48.28.10](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0E30); on an étale splitting cover the trace summand at a single sheet is the identity, so there is no factor of $\deg(g_i)$. Equivalently, a nodal dualizing basis pulls back to the same one by the étale local model identification. This is not a trace of a pullback of a section.

On the smooth locus of $X_i/S$, take the exact sequence of absolute-over-$S$ and relative one-forms of its universal abelian scheme. The base map $g_i$ and its family pullback map are étale; therefore the two absolute-over-$S$ modules and the relative module pull back canonically to the corresponding terms for $A_{33}/X_{33}$. The pulled short exact sequence is precisely the target short exact sequence, with the same two differential maps. By [Stacks Lemma 30.5.2](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02KH), flat base change identifies the $R^1\pi_*$ and $\pi_*$ terms, and the naturality of the connecting homomorphism identifies the map $\phi_0$ of [Yuan §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.11334v2#S1). For example on a separated affine cover over an affine open of the base, all Čech coefficients, restrictions and differences of lifts pull back by flat tensor product; the same ordered difference computes the connecting map. This does not use flatness of a later selected height trait.

The dual abelian scheme and the particular polarization are preserved by the Cartesian family square, by the relative Picard functor definition. With the usual base-change identification of $R^1\pi_*\mathcal O$ with $\operatorname{Lie}(A^\vee)$, the polarization contraction in Yuan's map is therefore the pullback of the same contraction, not a change of the chosen sign. Applying determinant, tensor-square, and the identification of the curve differential with the dualizing line gives, on this smooth locus, literal equality
$$
g_i^*\Psi_i=\Psi_{33}
\quad\text{in }\operatorname{Hom}(H_{33}^{\otimes 2},D_{33}^{\otimes 2}),
$$
where $\Psi_i$ is Yuan's $\psi_3$, not an unspecified isomorphism onto its image. Integral equality follows without any new computation at nodes: the model is flat over the integer base, and two maps of invertible sheaves equal on the generic fibre are equal (in a local trivialization their difference is an integer-torsion element and the coefficient ring has no such torsion). The generic fibre is smooth, and the genuine integral maps are the ones of [Yuan Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.11334v2#S1).

On the original models write $\Theta_i=\Psi_i/210$. Theorem 1.1 identifies the exact integral image with $210D_i^{\otimes 2}$; since 210 is a nonzerodivisor, $\Theta_i$ is the **unique** isomorphism $H_i^{\otimes 2}\simeq D_i^{\otimes 2}$ with $210\Theta_i=\Psi_i$. Dividing this way on the model (not in a special fibre) gives
$$
g_i^*\Theta_i=\Theta_{33}.
$$
Here equality is exact, not up to unit and not only after inverting a bad prime. A map from any subsequently selected height trait can pull back this equality and the line isomorphisms, even if the trait map is not flat or passes through a node. Distinct bases picked for the two occurrences of the same Hodge lattice need not be assumed to coincide: the natural Hodge and dualizing identifications carry them through this $\Theta$, and a change of basis is the usual determinant change on that very lattice.

## Exact degree over the same height number field

Let $T=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_L$, $T_i=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_L[1/i]$. The selected sections on $T_3,T_{11}$ restrict to the projections of the single 33-section on $T_{33}$, by the exact finite scheme construction and extension uniqueness. Pull back $D_i$ by those sections and glue them using the two canonical maps to $D_{33}$. Call the resulting line on $T$ $D$. Similarly the determinant of the same global good abelian scheme over $T$ is $H$, whose two localizations are exactly the height-trait Hodge determinants already identified with the Néron lattice. The integral isomorphisms just proved glue to $H^{\otimes 2}\simeq D^{\otimes 2}$. The complex Petersson metrics agree on the overlap: at each complex embedding use the same lift on the upper half-plane, where both maps forget only an étale basis and $\lVert d\tau\rVert_{\rm Pet}=2\operatorname{Im}\tau$. Thus $D$ is a single metrized line, not separate per-place atlases whose norm comparison needs a choice.

For each complex embedding the pulled-back $\Psi=210\Theta$ is an isometry on the tensor squares for the exact Yuan metric. Consequently $\lVert v\rVert_{H^{\otimes 2}}=210\lVert\Theta v\rVert_{D^{\otimes 2}}$ at infinity; the integral $\Theta$ is unit-normalized at all finite places. Arithmetic degree is minus the sum of the logs of the norms of one nonzero rational section, with every complex embedding counted once (equivalently real weights 1 and complex-place weights 2). By the product formula and tensor-square additivity,
$$
2h_Y(A)=\frac{2\,\widehat{\deg}(D,\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\rm Pet})}{[L:\mathbf Q]}-\log 210 .
$$
Here $h_Y(A)$ is the same stable height in the previously pinned Yuan metric, before any conversion to the GR normalization. The minus sign is from the factor 210 on **archimedean norms** under the integral normalized isomorphism; an independently applied factor at finite places would double count it. One can keep using the older inverse-section inequality and strict constants unchanged. This equality accounts for the common coefficient family; it supplies neither a new height bound by itself nor any statement about the modular Jacobian.
