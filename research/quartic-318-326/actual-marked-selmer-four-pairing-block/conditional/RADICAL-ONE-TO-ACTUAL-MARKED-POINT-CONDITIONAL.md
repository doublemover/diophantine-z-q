## Exact Kummer and finite symplectic argument

Write R₂ = im(Sel₄(E)→Sel₂(E)), the radical of the ordinary pairing
by [Fisher, *On binary quartics and the Cassels–Tate pairing*,
Introduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14977). The finite Kummer
diagram gives

    0 → A/2A → R₂ → 2B[4] → 0.

Indeed the maps on the left and right from Sel₄ to Sel₂ are the
surjection A/4A→A/2A and multiplication by 2 on B[4]. The Kummer
right arrow maps R₂ onto precisely 2B[4], and its kernel is the whole
A/2A because all such Kummer points lift from A/4A. Modding out the
rational torsion Kummer subgroup T gives

    dim(R₂/T) = rank A + dim(2B[4]).                       (1)

The group B has finite 2-torsion, as a quotient of the finite Sel₂.
Its compact Pontryagin dual is a finitely generated Z₂-module:
its quotient modulo 2 is finite, and topological Nakayama applies
(the compact pro-2 module is the inverse limit of its finite
quotients). Equivalently B is a finite 2-group plus its maximal
divisible subgroup (Q₂/Z₂)^b. Its finite nondivisible quotient has
a perfect alternating pairing: this is the elliptic Cassels–Tate
theorem as recorded in [Poonen–Stoll, *The Cassels–Tate pairing
on polarized abelian varieties*, §1](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1999/150-3/p10).
The restriction to the finite 2-primary quotient is still perfect,
because different primary components pair trivially.

Each exact cyclic elementary divisor occurs an even number of
times in such a finite paired group. For completeness, choose a
maximal-order x of order 2^k; perfectness provides y pairing with x
to exact order 2^k. Then the coefficients of any relation between x
and y are divisible by 2^k by pairing with x and y separately.
Their subgroup is a perfect alternating hyperbolic plane of order
2^(2k) and splits off orthogonally. Induct on the remaining group.
Thus the dimension of 2F[4] is 2c, where F is the finite quotient
and c counts its elementary-divisor pairs of exponent at least two.
The divisible summand contributes b dimensions to 2B[4], so (1)
becomes

    dim(R₂/T) = rank A + b + 2c = corank_Z₂ S + 2c.       (2)

The second equality is exactly the direct-limit Kummer sequence
0→A⊗Q₂/Z₂→S→B→0. Thus if the normalized radical is 1-dimensional,
then c=0 and the full 2-primary Selmer corank is exactly one. This
argument is valid even if the divisible part of Sha is nonzero
before applying any converse, and does not compute local root signs.
