# The third factor on a Selmer-four lift: an exact local obstruction at five

Let C₂ be the same two-factor genus-one curve with squareclasses a,b
and original roots s,t; let C₃ add the exact third equation
\(w^2-u=cQ^2,\ u=1/2399,\ c=p_cq_c\) from 48ae5e44.
All three coefficients a,b,c are squares in \(\mathbf Q_\ell\) for
each prime \(\ell\mid B\). Fix any locally soluble 4-cover \(C_4\)
lifting the marked 2-cover, with an actual fixed projection
\(f:C_4\to C_2\). A local origin on each torsor identifies \(f\)
with a translate of [2]; thus its image on local points is one
coset of \(2E_D(\mathbf Q_\ell)\) in the affine \(E_D\)-torsor
\(C_2(\mathbf Q_\ell)\). Do not identify the image with the whole
local point set. This distinction makes the following obstruction
meaningful even after the full Sel₄ lift.

## 1. Ordinary good places and the six moving primes

The smooth projective fiber product \(C_4\times_{C_2}C_3\) has
genus 17. Indeed \(C_4\to C_2\) is étale of degree four and
\(C_3\to C_2\) is the double cover adjoining \(\sqrt{w^2-u}\),
branched at eight geometric points (four for each of
\(w=\pm\sqrt u\)); the poles of \(w^2-u\) have even order.
The pullback is a connected double cover with 32 simple
branch points, and downstairs \(C_4\) has genus one.
At odd \(p\nmid Babc\) everything has smooth good reduction.
For precision, local rational points identify both genus-one
torsors with the same good elliptic model and extend the
translation and [2] integrally; the branch fibers remain
étale and disjoint since all roots and differences are units.
The same connectedness and genus argument works geometrically
there. By Hasse–Weil \(p+1-34\sqrt p>0\) for these \(p>76176\),
so smooth Hensel gives local points. This does not guarantee
any additional prescribed residue ball at such a prime.

At every \(p\mid ab\), the rational 2-torsion deck acts on C₂
and realizes every class of \(E_D(\mathbf Q_p)/2\): the four
torsion parity rows already exhaust that quotient. Starting
from the exactly prescribed local square ball, translate a
local point by this deck group until it lies in the image of
\(f\). This leaves \(w^2\) unchanged and therefore leaves the
third equation soluble, regardless of the lift's phase.
It may change the sign of w, so this argument concerns
unoriented square balls, not the selected representative of
w modulo \(p^2\).

At \(p_c,q_c\), the curve \(E_D\) has good reduction. The two
actual imposed old quotient signs give \(\chi_p(a)=+\),
\(\chi_p(b)=-\) and hence \(\chi_p(D)=-\). The untwisted signs
\((s,t,s-t)\) are \((-,+,-)\), at q_c by the same B-supported
clone. Since \(-1\) is a square, the twisted torsion triples
span only the line generated by \((-,+,-)\). But the Kummer
class of the actual marked \(C_2\) map there is
\(\alpha=(D,b,a)=(-,-,+)\), independent of that line.
For any fixed \(w^2\) in the third target ball, the two old
ordinates are nonzero units and the corresponding point of
\(E_D\) can have either sign of \(y\). With any local origin
on \(C_2\) its projection is \(P\mapsto 2P+Q_0\);
the two sign orbits have \(P+P'=-Q_0+T\) for a 2-torsion T,
and hence differ modulo \(2E_D\) and the deck image by
\([Q_0]=\alpha\). The two orbits therefore fill all four
cosets together. So both new moving primes also always
allow an image point with the correct \(w^2\).

At real places large positive and negative w and the real
rational deck give a point in either local image component:
\(E_D(\mathbf R)/2\) has two components and rational
2-torsion meets both. At 11,37,2399 the exact torsion
parity rows also exhaust the local quotient, so the same
deck argument works starting from the deep denominator
ball for all three equations. At any prime of B the
question whether a lift image permits the third equation
depends only on \(w^2-u\) being square, not on the newly
selected q_c (since c is square there).

## 2. The exact fixed obstruction at five

At five a,b,c are squares. Choose an infinity origin of C₂
above the origin of \(E_D\) and transport to \(E_1\) by
the local scaling with D. Its projection is [2], so for
a point \(P\) of this local elliptic model the actual
image parameter satisfies \(w^2=x([2]P)\) in unscaled
\(E_1\) coordinates. Use the square scale
\(C=(2399\cdot407\ c_1)^2\), writing the integral roots
\[
 S_0=-32\cdot407\cdot2399^2,\qquad
 T_0=2399\cdot407^2\eta^2,\quad \eta=c_1/c_2.
\]
For integral-model coordinate x of P we have the exact
ordinary duplication formula
\[
 w^2 = \frac{(x^2-S_0T_0)^2}{4x(x-S_0)(x-T_0)C}, \tag{1}
\]
away from the torsion points. This follows from the
usual tangent slope on the cubic (no choice of y sign).

The exact p³ replay shortens the four literal labels to
\((98,1,124,1)\) modulo 125. The actual residue point
order is 5, and formal multiplication by 5 raises depth
by at least one, so 125 is a valid period at this
precision. The checker computes these four now small
multiples over the actual rational quadratic field by
exact fractions, then reduces the U charts at \(5^3\).
Their product is \(34+124\sqrt2\bmod125\), so
\(\eta\equiv35\) and \(c_2=(\operatorname{Norm}\prod U)^{-1}
\equiv69\bmod125\). Consequently
\[
 (S_0,\ T_0/25,\ C/25,\ u)\equiv(1,24,11,24)\pmod{25}.
\]

The local roots 0,T₀ form a split depth-two pair. The exact
local image is \((d,1,d)\) for arbitrary local squareclass d,
where \(d=[x]\) in the integral model. All three rational
2-torsion rows are trivial in that quotient: S₀ and the
stripped unit of T₀ are squares, S₀−T₀ is a square unit,
and −1 is a square at five. Thus this local labeling of
the image phase does not depend on which infinity origin
was chosen; deck translations cannot change d.

**Exactly the phase \(d=[5]\) prohibits this third equation.**
If \([x]=[5]\), the only possible valuation of x for a point
on the cubic is one: in the colliding pair, a negative
valuation forces x square, a valuation 2 is even, and any
larger odd valuation makes the two colliding factors have
different parity. So \(x=5k\) with k a square unit. Put
\(b_5=T_0/25,\ C_5=C/25\). In (1) cancel \(5^4\);
the resulting denominator is a unit and the exact residue is
\[
 w^2\equiv
 \frac{(k^2-S_0b_5)^2}
 {4k(5k-S_0)(k-5b_5)C_5}\equiv9\pmod{25}
 \qquad(k\equiv1,4\bmod5).
\]
The finite replay includes every k mod 25 of these signs
(the formula is stable on the full coset). Therefore
\(w^2-u\equiv10\bmod25\) has odd valuation at every point
of this image phase and cannot equal a square times c.
No point of this phase maps to infinity, since those are
deck translates of the local identity.

The other three phases do contain points where the third
factor is square: at \(d=[1]\) use \(P\) sufficiently
near the origin so w has a pole; at \(d=[2]\) use \(x=2\)
on the integral model (the cubic is square since
\(2-S_0\equiv1\) and \(2-T_0\equiv2\bmod5\)); (1) gives
\(v_5(w^2)=-2\). In both cases \(w^2-u\) is square by a
principal unit. At \(d=[10]\) use \(x=10\); this is a
local point because the two colliding factors have the
same valuation and unit class and the third is a square
unit. Formula (1) gives \(w^2-u\equiv1\bmod5\), a square
unit. These points can be chosen affine and away from the
branch locus.

Consequently one must choose a 4-lift/projection whose
local image phase at five is not [5]. The full marked Sel₄
argument gives a lift but does not choose its image phase.
Changes by already exhibited W classes cannot control it:
those classes are local squares at every prime of B.
Neither can the rational 2-torsion deck at five. It would
require an actual localization of another global Sel₂
direction or a pointed refinement of the full Sel₄
governing argument. This is a one-place criterion;
we do not claim that the actual constructed (unspecified)
4-lift is obstructed or that satisfying five suffices for
every prime of B.

## 3. One further fixed place has no obstruction

At 2 the local torsion image has rank two and the remaining
class is represented by \(x=S_0+640\) in the same integral
model, by the exact dyadic calculation of 92707c5c. The four
actual U values each have depth \(3/2\) over
\(\mathbf Q_2(\sqrt2)\). Thus \(c_2\) and \(c_1\) each have
valuation −12 (\(\eta\) is a unit), so \(v_2(C)=-24\).
For \(x=S_0+640\) the numerator of (1) has depth 10 and its
denominator excluding C has depth \(2+5+7=14\).
Therefore \(v_2(w^2)=20\). Since \(u\) is unit class −1,
\(w^2-u\) is square. The neutral torsion cosets have
representatives near infinity, already satisfying the
third factor. Deck translation then covers all local
cosets. This is separate from the exact forbidden
5-adic phase.
