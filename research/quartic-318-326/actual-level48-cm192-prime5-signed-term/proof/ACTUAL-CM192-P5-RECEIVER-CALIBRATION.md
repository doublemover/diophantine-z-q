# The two literal CM −192 collision flags at the first possible good prime

This is an exact finite evaluator at 5 for the fixed target of the original level-48 CM intersection. It computes both literal flags, the actual $2$- and $3$-level conditions, the normalized tangent condition, and the genus sign. It does not assert that a specified original discriminant has a collision at 5, evaluate a signed total, or give point nonvanishing.

## 1. The two literal flags and a common endomorphism coordinate

The normalization is the elliptic target $E_0:y^2=x(x+1)(x-3)$ and its chosen half $T=(3+2\sqrt3,2(3+2\sqrt3))$ of $(3,0)$. Its two marked preimages on the level-48 long chart are $X=1\pm\sqrt2$, $Y=4\sqrt3 X^2$. They are individually Fricke-fixed, so on either flag the cyclic subgroup of order 48 is $\ker v$ where $v=4\sqrt{-3}$. This follows from the exact CM conductor-eight identification and the norm $N(x+yv)=x^2+48y^2$. The chart and forgetful functions are from [Colò, Appendix A](https://www.leonardocolo.com/documents/thesis/PhD_Thesis.pdf). In particular set $y_{48}=(Y+X^4+1)/2$, $t_{16}=X-2+3X/y_{48}$; successively replace $t$ by $t^2/(t+d)$ for $d=2,4,16$, then $j=(t+256)^3/t^2$.

Start on $B:y^2=x^3+1$ in characteristic zero. Take the successive 2-isogenies at roots
$$
 -1,\qquad -3-2\sqrt3,\qquad
 -3-6\sqrt3\mp(6+2\sqrt3)\sqrt2
$$
in the successive quotient coordinates. The upper sign goes with $X=1+\sqrt2$. In a model $y^2=x(x^2+ax+b)$ translated to kernel root zero, the quotient model has coefficients $(-2a,a^2-4b)$. On the first model the analogous translation to $-1$ has $a=-3,b=3$; the next model has $a=6,b=-3$. The replay checks the kernel equations and the identical endpoint $j$-values in the exact characteristic-zero biquadratic basis, not just their reductions. The 2-volcano from $j=0$ has unique descending paths to each of these distinct conductor-eight $j$-values: each subsequent node has its unique ascending subgroup and both descending branches have different terminal $j$-values here. The endpoint curve with its CM action (up to the fixed sign of the square root) therefore agrees with the literal flag, and the subgroup $\ker v$ is intrinsic. Transport through this degree-eight chain, denoted $\nu$, now works in characteristic five. No comparison of the two flags based on their equal reduced $j=0$ is made.

Work in $k=\mathbf F_5[z]/(z^{32}-2)$. Every root of $z^{32}=2$ has order 128; the order of 5 modulo 128 is 32, proving that this is a field. Select $s=z^{16}$ and write $\sqrt3=2s$, $\sqrt{-3}=s$ in the residue field; $\sqrt2=s$ sets the upper flag. When $\sqrt{17}=s$ the spin formulas below have their displayed signs; choosing $\sqrt{17}=-s$ reverses both. The field containing each named root is the common algebraic residue closure; choosing $s=\sqrt3/2$ is harmless, and the two $X$-choices are both included.

Let $\omega=(1+\sqrt{-3})/2$. On $B$ use $\omega(x,y)=(-\omega x,-y)$, with the correct differential $\omega$ at the origin. Let $\pi$ be the fifth-power Frobenius on $B$; point count at 5, or the bijective cube map, gives $\pi^2=[-5]$ and $\pi\omega=ar\omega\pi$. Thus on the full $B[5^{16}-1]$ the arithmetic Frobenius $\pi^{32}$ fixes every point, in particular both torsion groups used below. The action of $\omega$ on either the 2-adic or the 17-adic Tate module is the unramified quadratic action. Any primitive vector $P$ in $B[64]$ and any nonzero $R$ in $B[17]$ therefore give bases $(P,\omega P)$ and $(R,\omega R)$ respectively: the minimal polynomial $X^2-X+1$ is irreducible modulo both 2 and 17.

## 2. Exact level and spin receivers

The replay, using actual elliptic coordinates, gives $\pi P=(22+17\omega)P$ and $\pi R=(2+2\omega)R$. In the basis $[8]P,\omega[8]P$, the unit kernel generators of the two maps $\nu$ have least coefficient pairs $(1,7)$ and $(1,3)$. The other generating pairs for each line are recorded in the JSON. Hence in a homothetic $O_{8,2}$ lattice for the target, $\pi$ acts by $x\mapsto\eta_{\pm}ar x$. Indeed the quotient of the $O_{0,2}$ lattice by the cyclic kernel generated by $w/8$ has superlattice $(w/8)O_{8,2}$ since $w$ is a unit. Transport multiplies the source semilinear constant by $ar w/w$. The constants in the $1,\omega$ basis modulo 64 are
$$
 \eta_{+}=(1,22),\qquad \eta_{-}=(61,38).
$$
Lifts of $w$ changed by $8O_{0,2}$ change the homothety by an $O_{8,2}$ unit. The stated constants use exactly the replay's lifts; the final cyclic subgroup eigenvalues do not depend on a choice of its generator.

For fixed odd fundamental $D=-n$ and an input $u=kv+(a+b\omega)\pi$ of square $D$, put $(e,f)=(a+b\omega)\eta_\pm$ in the $1,\omega$ basis, modulo 64. Equivalently the vectors are
$$
 (e,f)_+=(a-22b,22a+23b),\qquad
 (e,f)_-=(61a-38b,38a+99b).
$$
The condition that $(1+u)/2$ is integral at 2 is precisely
$$
 f\equiv8k\pmod{16},\qquad A=e+f/2\text{ odd}.
$$
Test this in that order, so the half is defined modulo 32. Indeed $\gamma\eta_\pm=A+B'v$ with $B'=f/8$; applying $1+u$ to the basis $1,v$ proves the equivalence. Since $v$ is trace zero, acting on $1/v$ in the cyclic subgroup $\ker v$ gives scalar $(1-A)/2\bmod16$ on its 2-part. The identity $v(1+u)/2=(1-u)v/2-48k$ also verifies preservation of the full flag. At 3, $\ker v$ on $B$ has generator $(0,1)$; $\pi$ fixes it, $\omega$ negates it, and $\nu$ has degree prime to 3. The order-three eigenvalue is consequently $(1+a-b)/2\bmod3$. Match these two explicit eigenvalues with the actual chosen level ideals for $D$, not with unlabelled roots.

At 5 assume $5\nmid D$. The tangent action of $u$ is $4ks$, since $\pi$ has derivative zero and $\nu$ is étale. Match it with the named residue $\sqrt D$ at the chosen place. This is the necessary normalized embedding condition at an inert prime. Every surviving embedding is optimal because $D$ is fundamental. [Normalized Deuring lifting, §3](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~tonghaiyang/deuring.pdf) gives the literal CM-flag index, not a weight from all unmarked curves: lift the stable prime-to-5 cyclic subgroup by finite étaleness, so its two prescribed eigenscalars pick exactly the specified level ideals. The flagged automorphisms are just $\{\pm1\}$; already on a source in this $j=0$ isogeny type, an order-three automorphism would have no invariant line on the 2-division and hence no invariant cyclic 16-subgroup.

For the sign, with $\sqrt{17}=s$, the Miller pairing on $(R,\omega R)$ gives a primitive $\zeta$ whose quadratic Gauss sum is $s$. Since $17\mid D$ exactly once, $u$ acts on $B[17]$ as a nonzero rank-one nilpotent; the degree-eight transport to the flag multiplies the Weil exponent by $8$, a square modulo 17. The spin formula is the quadratic character modulo 17 of $8k+2a+4b$. If it is zero use $-8k+4a+2b$, the determinant on the other basis vector in the inverse-pairing orientation (inversion does not change the character at 17). This fallback is nonzero: a rank-one nilpotent has the property that the determinant of $W,uW$ is zero exactly on its kernel. The Gauss normalization is the same as the absolute Weil genus sign; reversing the named $\sqrt{17}$ reverses the sign.

## Reproduce and scope

Run `python3 replay_cm192_p5_smallest_prime.py` and compare with the exact JSON. It covers both fixed flags at 5, with no numerical original detector, cohomological rank inference, or assertion about signed cancellation. An application still needs admissible input $D$, the two level eigenvalues and the place, plus either a collision representation or a signed aggregate of them.
