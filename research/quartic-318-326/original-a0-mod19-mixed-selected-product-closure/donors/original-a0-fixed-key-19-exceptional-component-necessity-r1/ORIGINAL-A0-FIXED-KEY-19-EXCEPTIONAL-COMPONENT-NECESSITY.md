## A fixed extra cutoff, chosen before the key

In choosing Q before the affine-prime argument, add one fixed 19-adic bound for the contact-independent detector chain coefficients on the **generic special fibre** of the good source model. The model A_R over R=Z₁₉ is the Weil restriction of the good elliptic scheme y²=x³−(3−√2)²x along the unramified quadratic ring Z₁₉[√2]. Its special fibre A_s is geometrically integral, and the generic point of A_s defines a DVR valuation ν with ν(19)=1. The finitely many chain coefficients are literal rational functions on A. Thus each nonzero coefficient has finite ν, and a fixed integer H′ bounds their negative depths; zero coefficients cause no constraint. This is an extra fixed cutoff before the key, not information known for an already locked Q. The prior class at the actual h and its pointwise coefficient cutoff are retained unchanged.

Take an open U of A_R containing the generic point of A_s on which all those normalized coefficient functions are regular. Shrink U where required to invert the real-chain denominator; it is 1 on U_s, so this removes no points of U_s. The trace quotient is then 1/2 there, a unit. ## Dominant special components cannot carry the elliptic image

Fix one of the three literal detector slots and its actual β, δ and k=(1−324β)(1−2916β). Let Y be the normalization of A_R in the generic double cover with equation d²=δ (use the product algebra if the generic square splits). Finite normalization holds because R and its finite-type schemes are excellent. Above U it is the finite étale double cover cut out by d²=δ, since δ and 2 are units on U. On the special fibre it splits into exactly two copies of U_s, with d=1 and d=−1. Thus the two irreducible components dominating A_s are each birational to A_s and there are no other components dominating A_s.

The target curves are the actual fixed zero-second-ordinate targets E_β and E_(−3β), or the fixed-first t18 target E_(βk), with E_m given by y²=x(x−m)(x+3m). They all have good reduction, since m is a 19-unit and the three roots stay distinct. For the actual profile, β mod 19 is 15 and k mod 19 is 14. The exact finite counts of these three targets are respectively 16,16,24, so their Frobenius traces are 4,4,−4 and they are ordinary. The two geometric factors of A_s are isomorphic over the algebraic closure to the j=1728 curve y²=x³−x, with exact trace zero at 19, hence supersingular. A rational map from A_s to the ordinary elliptic curve extends over the smooth abelian source (Weil extension). A morphism is a translation of a homomorphism by rigidity. Restricting a homomorphism from the product to each factor yields zero: a nonzero map between elliptic curves would be an isogeny and preserves ordinarity. Therefore any such rational map is constant, with no separability assumption.

## Why a nonconstant generic map must use the exceptional locus

Suppose a proposed coherent anti-map is nonconstant on an irreducible generic component. The subsequent exact square marker is still required for either zero-ordinate branch, but is not needed for this necessary condition. Form the scheme-theoretic closure Gamma of its generic graph in the proper R-scheme Y ×_R E_R, where E_R is the good target elliptic scheme. This graph closure is proper and flat over R. In affine charts its coordinate ring embeds into its generic localization by the definition of schematic closure (the generic ideal is contracted); it is torsion-free over the DVR, hence flat. We can take only the generic component on which the map is nonconstant if the generic cover is disconnected, or include all components whenever the map is given on them.

Over the finite étale cover of U, which is smooth over R, the generic rational map extends to a morphism by Weil extension and then extends over R by the Néron mapping property of the good elliptic scheme. Thus Gamma over this open is just that morphism's graph, by density of the generic fibre there. The components of the special graph which dominate A_s are exactly the corresponding components birational to A_s (possibly just one sheet if only one disconnected generic component is kept). The maps on them are constant by the previous section.

On the other hand the image of Gamma in E_R is closed by properness and contains the generic target: a nonconstant rational map to the generic elliptic curve has dense image there, and its proper graph image is closed. Hence Gamma surjects onto E_R, including its special fibre and after algebraic extension of the residue field. The geometric special fibre has finitely many irreducible components. At least one must have nonconstant (dominant) image in the geometric special elliptic curve. Such a component cannot dominate A_s and in fact lies over A_s minus U_s, since the graph is already a morphism on both smooth sheets above U_s.

Thus a generic nonconstant marked anti-map would force an exceptional vertical component whose geometric image is the ordinary elliptic target. This is a necessary condition on the proper graph, not a claim that such a component is absent or that every resolution has a specified dual graph. It identifies a proper locus on the good source fibre where new arithmetic geometry is needed. The known finite characteristic-zero polar corners with no branch do not classify this mixed-characteristic boundary, and the smooth 19-adic t18 cell v=2 does not compute it. Even an exceptional component with ordinary image would not prove the existence of the anti-map or its descended square mark. No exclusion of isolated Ready indices follows.
