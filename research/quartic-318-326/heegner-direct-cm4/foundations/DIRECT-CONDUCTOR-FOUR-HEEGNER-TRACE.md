# A direct conductor-four Heegner trace on the literal level-48 quotient

For either member d of the **same selected pair** (n,nθ), this gives an independent Heegner construction on a fixed level with no auxiliary m and no level-2544 modular-coefficient requirement. Its local dyadic test vector and a genuine partial trace are nonzero in the senses stated below. It does **not** prove the full trivial-character trace nonzero, the selected rational point, analytic rank, or the combined original endpoint.

## 1. The actual cyclic level and the minimal dyadic order

Let d>0 be squarefree, d≡11 mod24, and K=Q(√−d). Then −d is the fundamental discriminant, 2 is inert and 3 splits. Let O be the maximal order of K, O₄=Z+4O, and H₄ its ring class field. Put A=E₋₁:Y²=X(X+1)(X−3), literal conductor 48, and use the already verified degree-two X₀(48) quotient f based at ∞. Its Fricke map is f∘W₄₈=−f+T with T=f(0)=(3,0). Indeed in the pinned hyperelliptic model cusp 0 is x=1,y=3, where the raw quotient has r=0,s=1 and hence A₀=(s+1)/r² has a pole; translating the raw origin by the same known f_raw(∞)=(3,0) gives T. This is consistent with [He–Hu–Tian, Corollary 3.2](https://www.sciengine.com/doi/pdf/C674839BE2234E14B7ACCE693F245CF6) on the based optimal quotient.

At 2 choose ω a primitive cube root of unity in the unramified quadratic extension K₂ of Q₂. On the Q₂-basis (1,ω) put
```
L  = Z₂ + 4 Z₂ ω,
L' = Z₂ + (1/4) Z₂ ω.
```
Their quotient is cyclic of order 16. Both multiplier orders are Z₂+4O₂: this is tautological for L, and for L' use its homothetic lattice 4Z₂+Z₂ω and compare coordinates of (a+bω)ω=aω+b(−1−ω) and (a+bω)4. Its stabilizer group in split GL₂(Q₂) consists of integral matrices diagonal modulo 4, the centered conjugate of K₀(16).

This is the minimal conductor among pairs with **the same local multiplier order on both ends**, when the initial lattice is an invertible ideal of that order. In fact if a rank-one ideal of O₂,r=Z₂+2ʳO₂ is the source lattice, and a cyclic quotient of order 16 carries its action extending to the target, the image of 2ʳω in End(Z/16) would solve x²+2ʳ x+2²ʳ=0 mod16. For r=0 there is no root even mod2; for r=1 x must be even, then division by 4 would again give the irreducible quadratic mod2. For r=2 the displayed actual pair works. At 3 choose O₃=Z₃⊕Z₃ and the superlattice for one of its genuine cyclic index-3 branches; at all other primes use identical maximal-order lattices. Intersect the local lattices in K to obtain a pair with nested cyclic quotient of order 48. This is a point P∈X₀(48)(H₄): CM reciprocity on the **nested pair** fixes both lattices for units of the same O₄, not merely their two j values or an invertibility assertion at 2 for a level ideal. Class action then labels P_a by a∈Pic(O₄); use the unaveraged sum over all these classes.

## 2. Exact dyadic projection of the level-48 newvector

Write π for the local component of A at 2. Let K(r)=1+2ʳM₂(Z₂), V=π^{K(2)}, and δ₂=diag(1,−1). The centered K₀(16) maps to the diagonal units modulo 4, generated by δ₂ and central units. Its fixed line contains the actual centered level-48 newvector v. By [Casselman's local newform dimension formula](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/id/PPN235181684_0201?tify=%7B%22view%22%3A%22info%22%2C%22pages%22%3A%5B305%5D%7D) the + eigenspace of δ₂ has dimension 1 (conductor exponent 4). Its − eigenspace is the fixed space of the centered K₀(16) in π⊗χ₋₄∘det. This is the actual opposite twist E₁ of conductor 24, so dimension 4−3+1=2. Indeed χ₋₄ is trivial on K(2) and central scalars and is −1 on δ₂. Hence dim V=3 and tr(δ₂|V)=−1.

In K(1)/K(2), determinant-one modulo 4 is the trace-zero part of M₂(F₂). Modulo central −I it is a Klein four group B. Multiplication by ω, matrix W=[[0,−1],[1,−1]], cycles the three nonzero elements of B, and hence the three nontrivial complex characters. The restriction of V to B thus either has all characters trivial, or exactly one of each nontrivial character. The trivial case is impossible: δ₂ together with B and central units generates K(1)/K(2), so the δ₂-invariant line would contain a nonzero K(1)-fixed vector. Globally, at the same level3 this would give a weight-two cusp form on Γ(2)∩Γ₀(3), of genus zero (PSL index 24, torsion-free, six cusps from three at level2 and two at level3). The compact subgroup's determinant is all units, so there is no missing second component in this genus argument. Thus V has the three nontrivial character lines of B.

The quotient of the torus units O₂ˣ by central units and K(2)∩O₂ˣ has six elements, generated by W and u=I+2W. The latter commutes with B and W modulo4, hence acts by one scalar on V; its square acts trivially. The element b=δ₂u⁻¹ is the nonidentity B element with matrix [[1,2],[2,1]] mod4. Every nonidentity element in the three-character representation of B has trace −1. Since tr(δ₂)=−1, u acts by +1. In particular δ₂ and b act identically, so v lies on the single + eigenline of b, one of the three B-character axes. Therefore
```
e_T v = (v + Wv + W²v)/3 ≠ 0.
```
The three summands lie on distinct axes. This is an identity in the actual local representation, not a table of guessed character values. Since K₂/Q₂ is unramified, K₂×/Q₂× is represented by its units; a unitary pairing makes the local normalized toric integral positive as a nonzero multiple of ‖e_T v‖². In fact mutually orthogonal axes give ‖e_T v‖²/‖v‖²=1/3. Thus these exact dyadic lattices carry a genuine trivial-character test vector, even though the standard maximal-order X₀ Heegner condition fails. The other local vectors are the usual split prime-3 and good-prime maximal-order vectors.

The explicit Gross–Zagier comparison is the **variation** case at 2, not a blind application of the standard Eichler admissibility clause at conductor zero: [Cai–Shu–Tian, Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1733) permits this nonzero toric integral in place of the invariant reference vector. Equivalently use the general Gross–Zagier formula with the same local integrals. The ring-class sum differs from the normalized torus measure by a nonzero volume, and the based cusp differs from a canonical degree-one class only by a rational point on A, torsion since A(Q) has rank zero. Here the base central value is nonzero (the exact ratio for A is in HHT §3.2). The Rankin derivative is therefore proportional to L′(1,E_d), since the quadratic twist of A by −d is E_d. A proved nontorsion **full** trace would imply analytic and rational rank one and finite Sha; with the same verified Sel₂ size 8 this would realize the required (β,1) class. No statement about the independence of the two twists is needed once each full trace is detected.

## 3. Actual conjugation and a nontorsion partial trace

In the above dyadic basis the field involution is J=[[1,−1],[0,−1]]. The exact matrix identity WJ=Sδ₂ with S=[[0,−1],[1,0]] is the centered W₁₆ coset identity. Thus conjugation differs from W₁₆, on that part of P, by a genuine torus multiplier ω⁻¹ whose class has order dividing 3. At 3 the involution swaps the two split axes and differs from the W₃ representative by one norm-3 torus factor or its inverse (depending on the chosen axis/sign convention). More explicitly, in the lattice chain Z₃² ⊂ (1/3)Z₃ e₁+Z₃ e₂, take W₃=[[0,1],[−3,0]]; then [[0,1],[1,0]]=diag(1,−1/3)W₃. Away from 2,3 the maximal-order lattices are stable. Thus complex conjugation on all labels takes the form
```
cc(P_a)=W₄₈ P_{b a⁻¹}
```
for one fixed Artin offset b. The projection of b to the maximal class group C is one class of a prime above 3 or its inverse; the dyadic order-3 multiplier maps trivially to C. Reversing the global Artin convention only inverts the fixed offset. This statement preserves the oriented level, not just the base elliptic curve.

There is a useful exact torsion obstruction over H₄. Its extension over Q is unramified at3, so √3 and √−3 do not lie in H₄. Also √2 does not lie in H₄: the local quadratic χ₈ composed with Norm from K₂ is nontrivial on the very ring-class unit 1+4ω, whose norm is 13≡5 mod8. Conversely i lies in H₄, since the norm of O₄-units is a square modulo4 and the corresponding χ₋₄ is unramified away from 2. The full-rational-2-torsion halving criterion says a point with x-coordinate x can be halved over a field only when all three differences x, x+1, x−3 are squares there (for a root, the nonzero differences suffice). Hence only (−1,0) can be halved among the three nonzero rational 2-torsion points. Its halves have x=1 or −3 and y=±2i or ±6i; no one of these can itself be halved over H₄, as the first pair requires √2 and the second √−3. It follows that A(H₄)[2∞] is precisely the subgroup generated by rational A[2] and these halves, isomorphic to C₄×C₂.

For **any** Q-automorphism ρ of H₄, the image of 1+ρ on that 2-primary torsion subgroup is contained in {O,(−1,0)}: it doubles rational torsion to zero and either fixes or negates each half, depending on i. Consequently T=(3,0) is not the image of any torsion point under 1+ρ (project to the 2-primary part). Put ρ=σ_{b⁻¹}cc with the displayed label convention. For any odd-order subgroup U≤Pic(O₄), the partial trace Y_U=Σ_{a∈U} f(P_a) satisfies
```
Y_U + ρ Y_U = |U|T = T.
```
This uses inversion permuting U and the exact Fricke constant. Hence Y_U is **nontorsion**. For the largest odd subgroup this proves nonvanishing in at least one 2-power-order ring-class character component. It does not specify the trivial component.

## 4. The remaining exact obstruction to the selected odd endpoint

The full class degree is 6 h(K), since 2 is inert and the order index is 4; its entire unweighted Fricke correction is therefore zero. Even projecting first to a maximal-order genus subgroup cannot repair parity from the marked gate alone: the gate forces r₄(C)=1, hence |2C| is even, and doubling the ring class group surjects onto 2C. A nonzero partial trace in an odd suborbit can have zero further norm. Thus the above torsion obstruction cannot on its own force the **trivial** character, despite the exact prime-3 offset.

A sufficient new arithmetic theorem would show the full trivial-character trace of this actual conductor-four marked construction is nontorsion for d≡11 mod24 with the one-class certificate of the earlier note, or even for one simultaneous fixed (d₀,d₁) from the same chosen q. An integral genus-character induction would have to control all nontrivial-character contributions to that full trace; no such congruence is proved here. [He–Hu–Tian, Theorem 1.1 and §3.3](https://www.sciengine.com/doi/pdf/C674839BE2234E14B7ACCE693F245CF6) is not that theorem: their n≡23 mod24, every prime ±1 mod24, r₄=0 and classical conductor-one level all fail the required selected 11-type setting. Nor is the even central-value theorem [Feng–Liu–Pan–Tian, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11132) an assertion about this derivative.
