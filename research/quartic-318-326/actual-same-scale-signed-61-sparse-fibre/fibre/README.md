# Sparse actual evaluation at 61: one marked zero and the missing 17-fibre model

The target is the same lower semistable curve and prime as the
reviewed detector:
\[
 E^\flat:y^2=x(x+76768)(x-407),\qquad
 N=205042530,\qquad k=\mathbb F_{61^2}.
\]
Write \(\pi^\flat:X_0(N)\to E^\flat\) for the based modular
map, \(g=(16,12)\) for the fixed order-17 generator, and
\(v(X)g=[224]\bar\pi^\flat(X)\) for a geometric marked
supersingular \(X\). The previous signed statistic packet
proves that a signed sum of these readouts is required.
Here the evidence is one explicit genuine zero, and a
one-fibre evaluation criterion for any other supplied
point. No nonzero production readout is certified.

## 1. An actual, completely specified marked zero

The local Atkin–Lehner signs at
\(2,3,5,7,11,37,2399\) are \(-,+,+,-,+,-,+\).
If \(W_m\) fixes an actual supersingular label at 61,
the quotient endomorphism satisfies \(u^2=-m\) and
must preserve all remaining marked lines. This was proved
in the signed-statistic packet by the dual kernel and the
fact that the supersingular curves have automorphism
group \(\{\pm1\}\). The local quaternion algebra at 61
is division, so \((-m/61)=-1\) is also necessary
(inserting a split algebra is impossible; \(61\nmid m\)).
The exact check of the 127 products of level primes finds
just one negative-eigen \(m\) passing these tests at all
odd residual marked primes and at 61:
\[
                  m=251895=3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot2399 .
\]
This elimination alone would not give a point. Here is
an explicit endomorphism and cyclic marking giving one.

Take the supersingular curve \(B:y^2=x^3-35x-98\) over
\(\mathbb F_{61}\). Its \(j\) is 41, its rational count
is 62, and its 61-Frobenius \(F\) satisfies \(F^2=[-61]\)
by the elliptic characteristic equation. Let
\(k=\mathbb F_{61}[e]\), \(e^2=2\), and take the literal
degree-2 quotient at \(x_0=27+12e,\ y=0\).
In coordinates \(X=x-x_0,Y=y\), the curve is
\(Y^2=X^3+aX^2+bX\), with \(a=20+36e,\ b=27+53e\).
The exact quotient formulas are
\[
 X_1=X+a+b/X,\qquad Y_1=Y(1-b/X^2),\qquad
 Y_1^2=X_1^3-2aX_1^2+(a^2-4b)X_1 .
\]
The displayed identity follows by substitution; the map is
the normalized two-isogeny (kernel \(O,(0,0)\) and
\(dX_1/(2Y_1)=dX/(2Y)\)). Translate the target to short
form, then divide \(X,Y\) by \(s^2,s^3\) respectively,
where \(s=31+12e\), to return to \(B\). The checker verifies
the actual coefficient equalities \((A',B')=(s^4 A,s^6 B)\).
Call the resulting endomorphism \(\gamma\).

Its differential is \(s\), and the dual has differential
\(2/s\). The integral trace is 1: \(s+2/s=1\) and the
possible trace integers have absolute value at most
\(2\sqrt2\). Consequently \(I=2\gamma-1\) has
\(I^2=[-7]\). Frobenius sends \(s\) to \(1-s\), so
\(\gamma^{(61)}\) and \(1-\gamma\) have identical
differential and degree 2. Their difference has degree
at most 8 by the parallelogram identity for the degree
norm. If it were nonzero and had zero differential its
degree would be divisible by 61. Hence
\(\gamma^{(61)}=1-\gamma\), and \(FI=-IF\) exactly,
not merely on the finite sample.

Now use the actual endomorphism
\[
                  u=164I+14F+11IF .
\]
Its three summands anticommute pairwise and
\(u^2=-7\cdot164^2-61\cdot14^2-427\cdot11^2=-m\),
so its degree is \(m\). This is prime to 61. At each
\(\ell\mid m\), the restriction of \(u\) to \(B[\ell]\)
is nonzero nilpotent (otherwise \([\ell]\) factors out
and \(\ell^2\mid\deg u\)). Choose the unique line
\(\ker u\) at those four primes. At 11 and 37 choose
\(\ker(u-[4])\) and \(\ker(u-[1])\), respectively:
the roots \(4,7\) at 11 and \(1,36\) at 37 are distinct
and each occurs by the trace-zero characteristic
polynomial, so the choices really have order 11 and 37.
At 2 choose the line generated by \((7,0)\):
\(u\) acts as \(F\) on \(B[2]\) since \(I=2\gamma-1\)
and the coefficients have parity even, even, odd.
This specifies all seven cyclic level marks by geometric
endomorphisms, without enumerating the geometric
2399-torsion or reducing a Hilbert class polynomial.
All define a \(k\)-point, since square Frobenius is scalar.

The quotient of \(B\) by the \(m\)-part, identified back
with \(B\) by \(u\), sends its dual kernel back to the
old \(m\)-part since \(u(B[m])=\ker(u)|_{B[m]}\).
It preserves the three remaining marks. Thus the
specified point is genuinely \(W_m\)-fixed. Since
\(w_m=-1\), its actual readout is
\(v(X)=-v(X)\) and therefore \(v(X)=0\).
Here the rational cuspidal constant has zero order-17
projection as proved in the preceding packet. This
does not assert that \(\pi^\flat(X)=O\); it gives its
precise order-17 coordinate only.

An independent characteristic-zero zero-control on
the same lower level is the conductor-one CM family
of fundamental discriminant \(-251895\), inert at 61.
For \(\omega=(1+\sqrt{-251895})/2\), choose the roots
\(0,4,0\) for the level splits \(2,11,37\) respectively;
the two roots at each are checked in the replay.
Use the unique ramified ideals at \(3,5,7,2399\).
Their product equals the principal ideal
\((\sqrt{-251895})\), hence \(W_m\) fixes every class
label before reduction and gives the same exact zero
test. No identification with the above explicit
finite label is asserted. Neither control is a selected
\(D\) or a vanishing result for the selected family.

## 2. An exact evaluation by one cyclic fibre, if its equations are supplied

The curve \(E^\flat\) has \(\#E^\flat(\mathbb F_{61})=68\),
\(a_{61}=-6\), and \(\#E^\flat(k)=3808=224\cdot17\).
On \(E^\flat[17]\) the prime-field Frobenius roots are
\(1,10\) and over \(k\) they are \(1,15\). The two distinct
eigenlines are canonical. Write \(G\) for the nonrational
line and \(\beta:E^\flat\to E^\star=E^\flat/G\).
Its dual \(\varphi:E^\star\to E^\flat\) has constant
cyclic kernel \(C_{17}\): Cartier duality gives eigenvalue
\(61/10=1\bmod17\) for prime-field Frobenius, hence
also eigenvalue one over \(k\). The curve quotient
and dual exist at degree prime to the characteristic.

For \(P\in E^\flat(k)\), let \(\delta(P)\in C_{17}\)
be the translation of arithmetic \(k\)-Frobenius on its
\(\varphi\)-division fibre. This is independent of the
geometric point chosen in that fibre, since the kernel
is constant. It is the connecting homomorphism: lifting
two points and adding their lifts adds their Frobenius
translations. It kills precisely \(\varphi E^\star(k)\).
The isogenous curves have equal cardinalities, and the
rational kernel has 17 points, so this subgroup has
index 17 and the entire prime-to-17 part is in it.
Consequently \(\delta(g)\) is a generator, and exactly
\[
        v(P)=224\,\delta(P)/\delta(g)\quad\bmod 17
        \quad\text{when }[224]P=v(P)g .
\]
Only a fixed unit matters for a zero/nonzero signed sum;
the target generator makes the normalization exact.

Thus the finite étale pullback of \(\varphi\) by
\(\bar\pi^\flat\), with deck generator and the rational
based cusp fibre marked, implements sparse evaluation:
pull back to the given \(k\)-rational label, give one
geometric point of that degree-17 fibre, apply \(k\)-
Frobenius, compare the two under the deck action.
Either the fibre splits completely or the points form
one degree-17 orbit. This is bounded field arithmetic
**once the fibre equation and action are supplied**.
A fixed model or reliable way to specialize this
particular cover has not been constructed.

## 3. Why extension of ordinary congruence level or a modular unit does not supply it

The actual based \(\pi^\flat\) must be normalized at 17.
Factor its Jacobian homomorphism through the connected
optimal elliptic quotient and then a rational isogeny
into the same curve \(E^\flat\). The residual
representation is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_{17}\):
at the good prime 13, the count is 16, hence trace
\(-2\) and characteristic discriminant \(4-4\cdot
13\equiv3\bmod17\), a nonsquare. If the isogeny degree
is divisible by 17, its dual kernel intersects
\(E^\flat[17]\) nontrivially, hence contains all of
it. Factoring \([17]\) on the dual and dualizing shows
that the map itself factors through \([17]\) on the
target. Such a normalization would make every readout
zero. One may choose the primitive rational isogeny
with degree prime to 17 (remove all such factors);
this retains the same elliptic curve, not a new
local detector. The following obstructions concern
precisely this primitive-at-17 normalization, or any
fixed based map known to have it.

For such a map the pullback on the full 17-torsion of
the elliptic Picard group is injective: dual of the
connected optimal quotient is an inclusion, and dual
of the final isogeny is invertible on 17-torsion.
There is also injectivity after reduction at 61,
by finite étale specialization of 17-torsion on
the good Jacobian and elliptic models. Over an algebraic
closure the pulled-back cyclic cover is connected:
its character line in \(\operatorname{Pic}^0(X_0(N))[17]\)
is exactly \(\pi^{\flat *}G\), the pullback of the
kernel of the dual isogeny to \(\varphi\). A nonzero
Kummer line represents a nontrivial geometric torsor
and hence a connected cyclic cover of prime degree.
For the complex argument one may lift the 17-isogeny
from the unramified good 61 model to characteristic
zero via the unique corresponding finite étale
subgroup of the elliptic 17-torsion. A geometric
generic fibre and the complex fibre have the same
nontrivial Picard line; no arithmetic Frobenius is
identified with a complex loop.

Over \(\mathbb C\) this connected geometric cover,
lifted to the upper half-plane, cannot come from
any congruence cyclic quotient of \(\Gamma_0(N)\).
Indeed, at any finite modulus first enlarge it to
a multiple of \(N\). The image of \(\Gamma_0(N)\)
is a product of its local \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) groups
with lower-left entry zero modulo \(\ell\) when
\(\ell\mid N\), by CRT and surjectivity of the ordinary
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\) reduction. At each
\(\ell\mid N\) the reduction is a Borel subgroup
with diagonal of order \(\ell-1\); the residual
unipotent and deeper kernel are \(\ell\)-groups.
None of these orders is divisible by 17. For
\(\ell\nmid N\) all primes are at least five, and
the full \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z/\ell^a)\) has no
abelian quotient: its elementary unipotents generate,
and each is a commutator with
\(\operatorname{diag}(2,2^{-1})\), since \(2^2-1=3\)
is invertible. This includes \(\ell=17\) and 61.
Consequently no finite congruence quotient can
surject onto \(C_{17}\), even before imposing the
parabolic conditions of the compact curve. This is
a geometric noncongruence test, not a classification
of all possible small special-fibre formulas.

There is also a direct positive-characteristic barrier
to a Kummer **modular unit** equation for this torsor.
The pullback Picard character line over \(k\) has
Frobenius eigenvalue \(15\ne1\) (or its inverse after
choosing a dual character). All cusps at squarefree
level are rational, and the same is true after
reduction; their divisors are Frobenius-fixed. After
adjoining a primitive 17th root of unity, if a
Kummer equation \(t^{17}=U\) with \(U\) a modular
unit modeled this étale cover, unramifiedness would
force \(\operatorname{div}(U)=17B\) for a
cusp-supported divisor \(B\). Its 17-torsion
class would be nonzero and Frobenius-fixed, in
contradiction to the actual nontrivial eigenvalue.
This argument also excludes taking larger coefficient
extensions; the *geometric* Picard class of a
cuspidal divisor is still fixed. On the same line the
actual \(T_{13}\) eigenvalue \(-2\) differs from
\(1+13\bmod17\), so an Eisenstein coefficient with
these eigenvalues would not be the desired line.

A noncuspidal divisor or an explicit eigenquotient
model could still implement the fibre test, without
a full supersingular graph. There is no claimed
asymptotic bound for constructing it. At present
the missing input is the actual noncuspidal
eigencover or its fibre model at selected CM points,
not just a field extension or a choice of generator.
