# A factorless companion-only handoff on the literal Ready poles

There are two distinct ways to bind the remaining arithmetic to an original pole. One keeps the published paired twist selector and binds the actual factor classes. The other uses the literal rational pole directly: on every Ready pole, one specified normalized first point and its ordered mark are rational, and the remaining input is a rational point on one explicit everywhere locally soluble quartic. Neither approach here supplies a new global companion witness or completes the original system.

## 1. Factored binding if keeping the selected pair

Let \(j\) be any original detector slot. Write \(D_{\rm clr}\) for the original common slope clearing, \(Q\) for its subsequent wheel enlargement, \(q\) for the genuine denominator prime (not the final selector prime), and \(p_{j,i}\) for the five genuine contact numerator primes of that literal row. Preserve the actual \(t=u/(D_{\rm clr}^2 Q^2 q)\), common index \(h\), labels, and original scale \(c_j\) (with \(c_0=1\)).

The native lemma **ThreeDetectorUniformFamily318.witness_squareclass** gives the actual fold identity
\[
 b_j=F(c_j^2t)\,S_j^2,
\]
where \(S_j\) is its literal rational squareFactor function, not an independently chosen unit. The exact linear product in the proved source is
\[
  F(c_j^2t)=\left(\frac{c_j^2}{D_{\rm clr}^2Q^2q}\right)^5
          \prod_{i=0}^4p_{j,i}.
\]
Consequently the original pole has the exact unnormalized square multiplier
\[
 d_j=q\prod_i p_{j,i},\qquad
 r_j=\frac{c_j^5 S_j}{(D_{\rm clr}Q)^5 q^3},\qquad
 b_j=d_j r_j^2 .
\]
Here \(r_j\ne0\) from the actual positive \(b_j\). With certified actual distinct primes, \(d_j\) is squarefree and prime to 17, hence the first mark has the certified positive class \(17d_j\). This needs no factorization of the reduced numerator/denominator of the huge fold. The exact complement can be bound by **one further rational identity**
\[
                       1=17d_j(r_j/17)^2+\theta_j z_j^2,
                       \qquad z_j\ne0 ,
\]
with an exact squarefree prime-factor certificate for \(\theta_j\). It pins the actual complement \(1-b_j/17\), not just its finite local residues. Replacing this identity by prescribed congruences at finitely many places would not certify the global class. A symbolic proof of the identity in an arithmetic circuit would suffice; no claim is made that arbitrary numerical equality of huge circuits can be certified without a proof.

This factored route is suited to the exact size-eight paired selector, where \(n_2\) must have class \(n_1\theta_j\), and a nontorsion point on each *same selected* curve yields the two prescribed marks. It retains the missing source problem: a literal admitted prime/fold input or a theorem operating uniformly on such actual sources. It also needs either a compact witness of the complement class or a way to avoid its factorization in the paired selector. A selected first-arm point may not be preserved by subsequently changing its helper primes. The universal selector switch is an existing narrower **conditional** exception: its one fixed but uncomputed \(u(d_j)\) suffices for a whole first-arm progression with companion Selmer admission. It does not compute \(u(d_j)\), the companion point, or a source key.

## 2. A fixed rational first mark without factoring

For the direct route assume the literal rational pole \(b\) satisfies these explicit numerical Ready conditions:
\[
 0<b<17,\qquad b\text{ has square reduced denominator},\qquad
 v_2(b)\ge3,\ v_3(b)\ge1,\ v_{17}(b)\ge2 .
\]
The proved linear-\(q\) four-Ready handoff supplies these for the three actual detectors and the scalar on its one eventually chosen index. The archived scalar controls separately verify them on their actual compact inputs. Nothing in the argument that follows chooses a new pole \(b\) or changes that index.

Put \(B=17b\), \(\delta=1-b/17=1-B/289\). Use the **literal**, not squarefree-normalized, version of the first-mark parametrization. Take the one rational constant \(z=1/100\) and put
\[
 \nu=Bz^2,\quad A=1+3\nu,\quad
 T=B\frac{(1-\nu)(1-9\nu)}{A^2},\qquad
 y_1=\frac{8B^2z(1-3\nu)}{A^2}.
\]
Since \(0<B<289\), \(0<\nu<1/9\); thus \(0<T<B\) and \(y_1\ne0\). The two identities
\[
 B-T=(4Bz/A)^2,\qquad
 B+3T=B\{2(1-3\nu)/A\}^2
\]
prove \(y_1^2=B(B-T)(B+3T)\). They certify the full ordered mark \(([B],1)\) on the rational twist \(E_T\), without computing \([B]\). This is a fixed specialization of the existing weak-approximation first-mark chart, dispensing with its auxiliary chosen seed and squarefree normalization. It does not keep a prescribed Selmer-eight twist \(n_1\), claim a rank or strengthen its generic companion obstruction.

Put \(\lambda=\delta T/B>0\). A rational point on the single fixed quartic
\[
                         C_b:\quad w^2=B(v^2-\lambda)(v^2+3\lambda)
\]
gives the exact original **NormalizedPolePoints318.PointPair(b)** with the first \(T,y_1\) above and
\[
                         s=Bv,\qquad y_2=B^4 v w .
\]
Indeed the point \((Bv^2,Bv w)\) lies on \(E_{\delta T}\); scale \(x,y\) by \(B^2,B^3\) to land on \(E_{B^2\delta T}\) with \(x=Bs^2\), as required. An affine rational point has \(v\ne0\), since the right side at zero is strictly negative. The native interface permits \(w=0\); no ordinate or rank requirement has been added. Conversely a PointPair with this \(T,y_1\) gives this affine quartic point by dividing by these same nonzero factors. The smooth completion has genus one: over an algebraic closure the four branch roots of the separable quartic are distinct. We ask for an **affine** rational point.

## 3. Every local place of this fixed quartic

At the real place \(\delta>0\). At 2, 3 and 17, \(\delta\) is a local square by the displayed positive depths. At each other prime of odd \(B\)-valuation, the square denominator makes the valuation positive; thus \(\delta=1-B/289\) is again a local square unit. At all these places, the exact first mark on \(E_T\) transfers to \(E_{\delta T}\). No exact decomposition of \(\delta\) is needed.

Consider a remaining \(p\ge5\). If \(B\) is a local square, the mark is the trivial Kummer class, which is always represented. Otherwise \(B\) has even valuation and nonsquare stripped unit. Then \(\nu=Bz^2\) has even valuation. If it is a unit, neither \(1-\nu\) nor \(1-9\nu\) can vanish in the residue field, since \(1,1/9\) are squares. At positive depth both factors are units; at negative depth both have the same even valuation as \(\nu\). It follows that \(v_p(T)\) is even, regardless of the square denominator \(A^2\). Also \(B/289\) has even valuation and the same nonsquare class. By the same three cases, \(v_p(1-B/289)\) is even. Thus \(v_p(\delta T)\) is even.

After a local square scaling, \(E_{\delta T}\) now has distinct unit-separated roots \(0,n,-3n\) with \(n\) a unit. It is a good curve at \(p\ge5\). Its ordered Kummer image consists of the two unit squareclasses on each coordinate: valuations of the root differences are even on all curve points (use the cubic when approaching a root, and the leading term if \(x\) has negative valuation), and specialized torsion differences are units. On the other hand this image has size 4, by good reduction and invertibility of doubling on the formal reduction kernel. It therefore contains \(([B],1)\). This verifies the full companion mark at every place, not just the existence of a local boundary point.

At every local place a Kummer coset contains a nonempty open neighborhood, by the local nonzero differential of doubling on the smooth curve. Choose a point off the finitely many roots and origin on \(E_{\delta T}\). The ordered classes give \(x\in[B]\) because the cubic's three squareclasses multiply to 1. Choose \(v^2=x/B\ne0\) and set \(w=y/(Bv)\). This is an actual local affine quartic point, as required. When \(\delta\) is globally square there is an elementary shortcut \(v=\sqrt\delta,\ w=\delta y_1/B\); no such exact square condition is claimed for any literal original pole in this handoff.

## 4. A compact equality certificate and the already available source

No squareclass factorization is needed even to *present* the direct conditional certificate. Write the actual \(B=N/D_1\) with any positive integer numerator and denominator, not necessarily reduced. Define
\[
 C=10000D_1,\qquad J=(C-N)(C-9N),\qquad A_1=C+3N,
\qquad L=(289D_1-N)J,\quad M=289D_1 A_1^2.
\]
Then \(\lambda=L/M\) with both entries positive. An integer triple \((X,W,Z)\), \(XZ\ne0\), satisfying the one weighted homogeneous identity
\[
           D_1 M^2 W^2=N(MX^2-LZ^2)(MX^2+3LZ^2)
\]
gives \(v=X/Z,\ w=W/Z^2\) and hence the literal PointPair. Conversely an affine rational point admits such a triple by clearing the denominators of its two coordinates. This is a useful proof-producing interface: the \(N,D_1\) terms can remain an actual source circuit, and a symbolic polynomial or arithmetic proof of the displayed identity can supply the exact point. A list of local residues alone is not a proof of this global equality. The replay checks the elementary symbolic coefficient identities and one small **nonoriginal** square-complement control with an unreduced pair \(N,D_1\).

The archived input-0 same-scalar/short-anchor packet already binds a literal scalar \(b\) by its compact elliptic chart indices, and proves an actual physical anchor for its fixed labels. That scalar can instantiate \(C_b\) now, without computing \(\theta\) or using the later fixed 103 and 241. This is an exact reusable conditional **scalar** PointPair certificate if one affine point on this literal \(C_b\) is supplied. The archived \(h,q=3162001\) do **not** give all-place Ready at the three detector poles. For the simultaneous four-Ready theorem, the index is newly chosen by the original prime source. At that common index the present formula simply supplies all four first marks and all four local companion coverings. Equipping each corresponding \(C_b\) with an affine rational point would reach the unchanged exact source consumer. None of those four global second points is proved here.

The practical contrast is exact: preserving the original selected pair needs a bound or certificate for the actual complement support and then a global witness on the fixed pair; the direct route drops those factor/selector requirements, but still needs a global witness on each explicit genus-one specialization. Neither a checked control point on a different numerical pole nor existence of the abstract linear key gives that arithmetic.
