# The actual selected coarse point field already splits quaternion multiplication

This strengthens the non-CM same-\(A\) paper route. With the exact
arithmetic twists \((-14,-6,42,-21)\) of the original \(t=32s\) curve,
one need not adjoin a further quadratic extension of the chosen
coarse field to kill its scalar gerbe. The two choices of genuinely

## 1. Literal local cases at the four division primes

For the selected finite \(t\in\mathbb Q\) outside the branch set, put
\[
(d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3)=
(-14t,\ -6(t-32),\ 42(t-76832),\ -21(t+343)).
\]
The exact arithmetic field comparison identifies the coarse residue
field \(F_c\) with the rational field generated by four choices
\(\sqrt{d_i}\). The signs do not affect the field, which is multiquadratic
and of degree at most 16.

* At \(p=2\), if \(v_2(t)<0\), all \(t-b_i\) have its valuation,
  but the multiplier valuations \((1,1,1,0)\) disagree in parity.
  If \(t\) is integral and odd then \(d_0\) has valuation one.
  For \(t\) even, \(d_3\) is a unit, and \(d_3\equiv1\bmod8\) would
  force \(t\equiv4\bmod8\). Then \(v_2(d_0)=3\), impossible.
* At \(p=3\), the negative case likewise fails against multiplier
  valuations \((0,1,1,1)\). An integral \(t\) would need
  \(t\equiv2\bmod3\) to avoid \(v_3(d_1)=1\).
  Then \(d_0\equiv2\bmod3\) is a nonsquare unit.
* At \(p=7\), in the negative case the multipliers have valuations
  \((1,0,1,1)\). In the integral case \(t\equiv0\bmod7\) is needed
  to avoid \(v_7(d_0)=1\), but then \(d_1\equiv3\bmod7\)
  is a nonsquare unit.
* At \(p=5\), the \(c_i\) are units. For negative \(t\), the ratio
  \(d_2/d_1\equiv3\bmod5\) (after cancellation of the equal \(t\)
  valuations) is nonsquare. For integral \(t\not\equiv2\bmod5\),
  \(t-b_2\) and \(t-b_1\) are units with equal residue, so the
  same ratio is nonsquare. When \(t\equiv2\bmod5\),
  \(d_0\equiv2\bmod5\) is nonsquare.

Thus at each \(p\mid210\), at least one literal \(d_i\) is nonsquare in
\(\mathbb Q_p\) and every field \(F_{c,v}\) above \(p\) has even local
degree. The real argument is also immediate: \(d_0\ge0\) requires
\(t\le0\), whereas \(d_2\ge0\) requires \(t\ge76832\).
These local arguments use only the finite nonbranch selected point;
no branch or formal-line approximation is needed.

Write \(\mathscr B/\mathbb Q\) for the indefinite division quaternion
algebra of discriminant 210. At each division prime its local Brauer
invariant is \(1/2\), multiplied on restriction by the even local
degree; all other invariants were already zero, including at infinity.
ABHN therefore gives
\(\mathscr B\otimes_{\mathbb Q}F_c\simeq M_2(F_c)\).

## 2. Intrinsic identification of the same non-CM object's obstruction

Let \(x\) be this exact selected coarse point and
\(A=(A,\iota,\lambda)\) its geometric non-CM polarised QM object.
Use the actual O-linear polarized isomorphisms
\(u_\sigma:{}^\sigma A\to A\) over a finite auxiliary field,
extended continuously as in the earlier scalar-gerbe construction.
The geometric automorphisms preserving \(\iota,\lambda\) are
\(\{\pm1\}\): the non-CM endomorphism algebra is the quaternion
algebra, whose centralizer of its own order is \(\mathbb Q\).
Hence the descent defect of the \(u_\sigma\)'s is the scalar
2-cocycle \(\alpha\) with values \(\{\pm1\}\).

On the two-dimensional tangent space at the identity, their
differentials give matrices \(g_\sigma\in
\operatorname{GL}_2(\overline{F_c})\) after choosing a basis.
Their projective images are a PGL\(_2\) descent cocycle, and the
matrix coboundary is exactly the image of \(\alpha\) in
\(\overline{F_c}^{\times}\), since the derivative of
\([-1]\) is \(-I\) in characteristic zero.

The associated twisted matrix algebra is the descent of
\(\operatorname{End}(\operatorname{Lie}A)\) under
\(m\mapsto g_\sigma\,{}^\sigma m\,g_\sigma^{-1}\).
Every element of the specified quaternion order is fixed through
its differentiated action, because the \(u_\sigma\) are O-linear.
It extends to an \(F_c\)-algebra map from
\(\mathscr B\otimes F_c\) into the descended algebra. The map is
unital, hence injective by central simplicity, and is an isomorphism
because both algebras have dimension four. The Brauer image of
\(\alpha\) is consequently \([\mathscr B\otimes F_c]\) (the inverse
convention makes no difference for a quaternion class).
Hilbert 90 and the Kummer sequence make
\(H^2(F_c,\mu_2)\to\operatorname{Br}(F_c)\) injective.
Section 1 now kills \(\alpha\) itself, not just its matrix
projectivization. Correcting \(u_\sigma\) by a \(\mu_2\) 1-cochain
gives genuine descent of the **same** polarized O-object to \(F_c\).

The former unconditional degree-32 comparison field for the
non-CM object may therefore be taken to equal \(F_c\), of degree
at most 16. Keeping the same global f, nebentypus and determinant
argument, the old relative degree-at-most-two step over the
cyclotomic field yields \([E:\mathbb Q]\le32\varphi(n)\) rather than
\(64\varphi(n)\). This is a strictly stronger field cap without
altering any isogeny constants or any witness table. It does not
claim that the particular descended \(A/F_c\) has a selected fine
level structure, that it is good at all even-contact places, or
that its arithmetic Kodaira–Spencer map has been produced natively.
The previous true \(\mu_2\) argument only needed projective
comparison on inertia, which is unaffected by this choice.
The older full normal degree-576 level-3 field is still kept for
the separate CM endomorphism/period argument.
