# Complete split support is not enough: an exact saturated square contact

This is a literal unscaled slot190 example after all fixed numerator
normalization primes have been saturated. It proves that controlling
all inert valuations of the rational F is not enough to find a
PointPair; an exact split orientation may already be impossible
at one actual prime before any multi-prime CRT.

## One exact joint norm condition after saturation

In the Pythagorean construction r=m²−n²,d=2mn,s=m²+n², suppose
the reduced denominator leg d is divisible by 2,3,5,17,407,2399.
Use H3=G3/2399 and H4=G4/407 (ordinary integers under these
saturations). For p=3,5,11,37, both H3,H4 are p-units:
the two possible numerator normalization factors at 11,37 were
canceled exactly by v_p(G4)=1. Any other odd inert p cannot
divide both G3 and G4. Indeed p|d makes both normalized forms
units, and otherwise simultaneous vanishing would force

    p | 2399·32 + 407 = 77175 = 3²·5²·7³

by the determinant and primitivity. The remaining possible common
prime 7 is split in Q(sqrt(2)).

Consequently H3H4 is a norm from Q(sqrt(2)) exactly when
both H3 and H4 separately are norms. In detail, a rational
nonzero number is a norm from Q(sqrt(2)) if and only if its
valuation at every inert prime is even. Inert valuations
of a norm double. Conversely Z[sqrt(2)] is norm-Euclidean:
rounding two coordinates of a field element to the nearest
integers gives norm error of absolute size <=1/2<1. Hence each
split prime is generated by an element of norm plus or minus
that prime, sqrt(2) accounts for 2, and the unit 1+sqrt(2)
has norm −1, so there is no further sign condition. This gives
the converse by exact factorization. The disjoint inert
supports proved above make the product test equivalent to the
two tests, not weaker via accidental parity cancellation.

This is a special-value criterion; the earlier irreducible
generic quartics remain nonnorms in Q(x). Neither a generic
formula nor a complete orientation follows from this norm test.

## A completely certified split-supported special value

Take (m,n)=(40783,12210). Then

    (r,d,s)=(1514168989,995920860,1812337189),
    r²+d²=s², gcd(r,d)=1, r²>2d².

The exact d includes 2²·3·5·17·407·2399. The five literal
numerators G0,...,G4, including the factors that normalize F,
are completely certified by

    G0=r²,    G2=s²,
    G1/2     = 253537 · 10998934699633,
    G3/2399  = 433 · 479 · 11052154848503,
    G4/407   = 1573893649 · 1506259129.

All seven displayed primes are distinct, split in Q(sqrt(2)),
and prime to d. The checker proves primality recursively
by exact full p−1 factorizations with Fermat/gcd witnesses
(and trial division for small leaves). No probable-prime
or partial factoring assumption is used. Thus the entire
rational F(t) has just these seven simple odd valuations:
the two squared numerator forms and d^10 contribute no odd
valuation, and all the literal fixed normalization factors
cancel as shown. In particular this example attains the exact
joint norm condition just described, not only small-prime
tests or a partial support list.

## Why no h works at this exact t

The prime 479 is an actual simple contact in G3 and a unit
in every other factor and in d and Delta. Its mod-p² row
and Delta numerator 265 mod p are checked precisely. At the
two square roots 187 and 292 of 2 modulo 479, direct finite
addition gives the actual half-point orders 240 and 120,
respectively, with midpoints (184,0),(289,0), neither T0.
Thus the necessary early-eight primary/opposite index
conditions would be either

    120 | h and 120 | (h−1),
    60  | h and 240 | (h−1).

Both are impossible. The exact early-eight necessary
orientation therefore excludes a PointPair at this one t for
every guarded h and its original Lucas labels. This is
independent of the previously retuned h, and independent of
conditions at the other six split primes.

It is a complete split support for **one literal rational
contact only**, not a compatible support for the three-scale
family or any positive witness. A genuinely usable contact
must satisfy exact split-order congruences as well as these
norm conditions. No general nonexistence or Lean assertion.
