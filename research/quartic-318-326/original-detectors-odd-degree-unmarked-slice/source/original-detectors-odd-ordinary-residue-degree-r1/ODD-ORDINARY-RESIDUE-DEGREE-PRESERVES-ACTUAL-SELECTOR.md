# An exact odd-residue-degree refinement of the selected ordinary first arm

For the symmetric original first arm, the final auxiliary prime can be reselected within the exact marked Selmer-eight conditions so that the ordinary CM residue degree at \(2\) is odd. This sharpens the [unmarked genus-slice receiver](../original-detectors-ordinary-slice-unmarked-resultant-r1/UNMARKED-ORDINARY-GENUS-SLICE-RESULTANT.md) to one power-sum trace. It neither computes the genus-slice polynomial nor proves a nonzero bit or rank.

## 1. Two higher class conditions with independent governing inputs

Fix the actual original \(b,\beta=17d\), its old shared helpers \(I\) all \(1\bmod24\), and the originally permissible **quadratic** signs for one shared final \(q_0\equiv7\bmod24\), including the companion's exact fixed-prime signs. No previously chosen final prime or finer residue row is held fixed. The first arm is \(n=\beta I q_0\), with every prime outside \(q_0\) positive \(1\bmod4\) and all positive factors \(1\bmod8\); write \(a=\beta,\ b_*=-I q_0\), so \(D=-n=a b_*\). The finite selector gives the row-sum-zero symmetric Legendre matrix \(L\), \(L\beta=0\), and invertible deleted sharp matrix \(C=(L+\lambda (e_{17}+e_{q_0})^{\mathsf T})^{q_0}\). Its only retained correction is \(e_{17}e_{17}^{\mathsf T}\). Thus if \(t=\omega(n)\), then \(\operatorname{rank} L=t-2\): the two independent vectors \(1,\beta\) give the upper bound and the one-rank correction gives the lower bound. The signed prime discriminants are all positive \(p\) except \(-q_0\). Their signs do not change \(L\) in the Rédei matrix, because \((-1/p)=1\) at each other row. Hence the class group of \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt D)\) has 4-rank one, and the nontrivial decomposition of the second kind is \(D=a b_*\). In particular \(a\) names the unique nontrivial liftable quadratic genus character, and the product of the ramified ideals over \(a\) generates \(C_K[2]\cap2C_K\). The latter product is not principal: its norm \(a<n/4\) is squarefree nonsquare, whereas any nonrational algebraic integer has norm at least \(n/4\).

Use additive Rédei symbols in \(\mathbf F_2\). The 8-rank datum for this one-dimensional pairing is \([a,b_*,a]\); choose it to be \(1\), giving 8-rank zero. The second condition is \([a,b_*,2]=0\). These are exactly the symbols of [Stevenhagen, *Rédei reciprocity, governing fields and negative Pell*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.06250), definitions 4.4 and 7.8 and Theorems 4.1, 7.9. Its admissibility is literal: \((a,b_*)_v=1\) from that second-kind decomposition (also from the marked kernel directly); \((a,a)_v=(a,-1)_v=1\) since all \(p\mid a\) are \(1\bmod8\); \((a,2)_v=(b_*,2)_v=1\) since all odd prime factors in either have \(2\) a square and both \(a,b_*\equiv1\bmod8\). At the real place only \(b_*\) is negative. The relevant triple discriminant gcd's are 1.

By reciprocity, \([a,b_*,a]=[a,a,b_*]\). Stevenhagen section 7, equations (35)--(39), identifies the latter with the cyclic quartic cyclotomic character \(\psi_a=\prod_{p\mid a}\psi_p\) of conductor \(a\), with each \(\psi_p\) of order four and \(\psi_a^2=(a/\cdot)\). The full \(b_*\) is in its quadratic kernel, so the value is \(\pm1\) multiplicatively. The exact old residues of \(q_0\) at a prime \(p\mid a\) can be chosen with either quartic sign while retaining their original quadratic sign: inside either Legendre class mod \(p\), the \(\psi_p\) values differ by minus. Choose this at one such \(p\), retain all other original quadratic constraints including those at the companion's primes, and make the total \(\psi_a(b_*)=-1\). This step retains the original quadratic signs, not an arbitrary previously fixed residue class mod \(a\).

Next \([a,b_*,2]=[a,2,b_*]\) is governed by a fixed minimally ramified \(D_4\)-extension \(F_{a,2}\), with maximal abelian subextension \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt a,\sqrt2)\). The only moving local contribution to its Artin symbol is the one at \(q_0\): use the actual ideal of norm \(|b_*|\) and its local factors, and keep the infinity contribution fixed. There is no need to assert that each factor is independently an admissible Rédei triple. The selector has \((a/q_0)=+1\), and \(q_0\equiv7\bmod8\) gives \((2/q_0)=+1\). So on the fixed dihedral field the final prime can take either central Frobenius element. Both are available independently of the now completed cyclotomic CRT conditions: the intersection of this \(D_4\)-field with an abelian extension lies inside its stated maximal abelian subfield. The Frobenius condition at that subfield is the same for both central choices. Chebotarev then supplies infinitely many shared \(q_0\), away from any given finite list, with \([a,2,b_*]=0\). This construction does not require factoring a growing class group or imposing an additional root sign.

Every signed prime discriminant dividing \(D\) is \(1\bmod8\), including \(-q_0\), so the ideal over \(2\) lies in \(2C_K\) by genus theory. Since the 4-rank is one and the 8-rank is zero, the 2-primary part of \(2C_K\) has order two. The unique quartic character modulo quadratic twists is nontrivial on that part. Its value on the prime over 2, already in the quadratic kernel, is exactly the Artin datum \([a,b_*,2]\). The imposed zero therefore says this ideal class has **odd order**. This is the residue degree \(f\) of a prime over the chosen split \(2\)-arm in the ordinary Hilbert class field. Exact original Selmer sizes and marks are unaffected on both arms; the odd-degree conclusion has been proved on the first arm.

If, additionally, every prime of the companion \(\theta\) is \(1\bmod24\), the same selected calculation applies to both class groups over their symmetric Legendre matrices. The two governing bits on the companion differ from those on the first by the **fixed** increments \([a,a,\theta]\) and \([a,2,\theta]\) (actual local Artin factors). The protected \((\theta/p)=+1\), \((a/t)=+1\) and the extra residue assumption make these symbols admissible. Thus both desired governing equalities can be enforced at once by this recipe if both increments are zero, and if either is nonzero this particular pair of governing equalities cannot be enforced on both with one \(q_0\). This is not a claim that each companion prime splits at 2 in general or an obstruction to odd residue degrees by all other means.

## 2. One power sum instead of a marked edge resultant

Take one actual joint genus slice for the real residue-neutral characters. It is closed under horizontal 3-edges. Let \(H(X)\) be its squarefree ordinary reciprocal-\(j\) polynomial and \(A=\mathbf F_2[X]/H(X)\). Each root has the same degree \(f\), the order of the class of the prime above 2, because reduction is injective on the canonical maximal-order CM \(j\)'s and Frobenius acts by that same class translation. No knowledge of the labels inside each irreducible factor is needed below.

For a reciprocal source \(x=1/j\), let \(y,z\) be the two horizontal reciprocal neighbors. The exact quartic neighbor equation is \(Y^4+xY+x^4=0\); the chosen pair is inside the slice. When \(f\) is odd, it can also be identified as the only pair over \(\mathbf F_{2^f}\): the ordinary source 3-torsion has two Frobenius eigenlines from its split CM, whose two eigenvalues in \(\mathbf F_3^\times\) are distinct because their product is \(2^f=-1\bmod3\). The remaining two lines are interchanged by Frobenius and their target \(j\)'s are distinct, by the quartic parametrization. The difference \(y+z\) of the two true roots satisfies \((y+z)^4+x(y+z)=0\), so \(y+z=x^{1/3}\), the **unique** cube root in \(\mathbf F_{2^f}\) when \(f\) is odd.

On the actual closed cycles the binary phase is
\[
 \sum_{\rm edges}(x+y)^5
   =\sum_{\rm edges}(x^2y+xy^2)
   =\sum_{\rm vertices}x^2(y+z).
\]
The first equality uses \(xy=(x+y)^4\). Each edge is included once around its fixed horizontal orientation; the last sum uses the two true neighbors of each vertex. Let \(e\) be the inverse of \(3\) modulo \(2^f-1\). Under the selector just constructed the normalized phase is therefore
\[
                   \operatorname{Tr}_{A/\mathbf F_2}(X^{2+e}).
\]
It is a single finite computation from the supplied unmarked slice polynomial \(H\) and \(f\). It needs neither a modular-neighbor gcd, a choice of an individual 3-orientation, nor more dyadic digits. For \(f=1\), interpret the exponent \(e\) as any inverse representative modulo \(1\), all giving the same because \(X\) is a unit with value 1. This receiver does not produce \(H\) at acceptable cost for the actual original first arm, nor does it assume its power sum nonzero.
