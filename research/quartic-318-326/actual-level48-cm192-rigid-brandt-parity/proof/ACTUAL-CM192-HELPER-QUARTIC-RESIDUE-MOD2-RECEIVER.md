# The helper quartic residue is a finite unsigned multiplicity parity

Fix the same original first arm and one of its old helpers $I$ with $(17/I)=-1$, $I\equiv1\bmod24$. In the single-helper reciprocity formula let $\mathfrak i$ be either prime of $\mathbf Q(\sqrt3)$ above $I$, and put $S=\prod_{\mathfrak a}h_T(\phi(q_{\mathfrak a}))$ on the literal oriented level-48 class orbit. The required mark is $S^{(I-1)/4}\bmod\mathfrak i$. One can evaluate this from **mod-two** multiplicities of finitely many marked supersingular labels, without a genus sign, a fourth root, or multiplicities modulo four. There is no numerical production run on the original detector.

## 1. Two free actions, on the indices and on the marked special fibre

Work at a prolongation of the prescribed $\mathfrak i$ to the Hilbert class field of $K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt D)$ and to the fixed level inputs. The local extension $K/\mathbf Q$ at $I\mid D$ is ramified quadratic, and the Hilbert class field is unramified over $K$. The arithmetic Frobenius on its residue field over $\mathbf F_I$ is the ideal class of the ramified prime over $I$. It has order two: its square is principal and its actual $\chi_{17}$ value $(17/I)$ is $-1$. Thus the complete literal orbit specializes into $\mathbf F_{I^2}$, and this Frobenius acts on the class indices by a **free** translation, though not necessarily freely on their geometric labels. The fixed $\sqrt3$ itself lies in $\mathbf F_I$.

Choose the usual actual Fricke involution $w=w_{48}$ and arithmetic Frobenius $F$ on the level-48 chart. Inertia at $I$ over $\mathbf Q$ acts on the modular points as $w$ followed by a permutation of the literal class indices: its restriction to $K$ is complex conjugation, and the exact level conjugation rule expresses complex conjugation as a class-index permutation followed by $w$. Inertia is trivial on geometric reduction, so the multiplicity $r(q)$ of a reduced chart label $q$ satisfies $r(wq)=r(q)$. The Frobenius class acts on indices by the preceding translation, so $r(Fq)=r(q)$. These equalities concern literal indexed multiplicity, not a count of distinct labels. At a label fixed by $F$ the free translation pairs all its indices, giving $r(q)$ even.

Neither CM reduction is a cusp, since its $j$-value is integral and forgetful $j$ is a pole at the cusp. All labels obtained at $I$ are supersingular because the original CM field is ramified there. The literal long-chart Fricke action on $X$ is $X\mapsto(X+1)/(X-1)$. Its fixed values are exactly $X=1\pm\sqrt2$, neither zero nor infinite. At those values the action $Y\mapsto4Y/(X-1)^4$ fixes both possible ordinates, giving precisely four fixed flags, all with $t=X-1/X=+2$. No point above another $X$ can be fixed. These four flags are the actual CM −192 specializations, which are all ordinary at $I$ since $I\equiv1\bmod3$. Their long coordinates and reductions are regular and distinct (both roots $X$, both signs of $4\sqrt3 X^2$, $I>3$). Thus $w$ is free on the full supersingular label set. It commutes with $F$.

The rational function $H(q)=h_T(\phi(q))$ is nonzero and finite on these supersingular labels: its two cuspidal-pole pairs cannot occur and the two zeros over this $T$ are among the ordinary −192 flags. The exact $h_T(T_3-P)=h_T(P)$ gives $H(wq)=H(q)$, and all coefficients in the given residue norm lie in $\mathbf F_I$, so $H(Fq)=H(q)^I$.

## 2. Only the mod-two representation count is needed

Use one representative $q$ of each orbit generated on the supersingular label set by $w,F$. There are only these possibilities:
- If $Fq=q$, the two labels $q,wq$ each have the same even multiplicity. Their combined factor in $S^{(I-1)/4}$ is $1$.
- If $Fq=wq\ne q$, the common value $H(q)$ belongs to $\mathbf F_I^\times$. The combined factor is the quadratic character of $H(q)$, raised to $r(q)\bmod2$.
- Otherwise the orbit has four distinct labels. Their combined product in $S$ is $N_{\mathbf F_{I^2}/\mathbf F_I}(H(q))^{2r(q)}$, so the factor in $S^{(I-1)/4}$ is the quadratic character of that norm raised to $r(q)\bmod2$.

These formulas independent of the representative follow simply by squaring the $w$-pair factor before applying the quartic exponent. They explain why the signed genus field is not needed to form the final bit. The two-dimensional finite-field norm is itself computable by $x\mapsto x^{I+1}$.

For an exact finite input at a single label, use a concrete elliptic curve with its actual cyclic subgroups of orders $16$ and $3$. In its reduced endomorphism order enumerate the trace-zero elements $u$ of norm $n$ satisfying that $(1+u)/2$ is integral and acts by the two prescribed roots of the original level ideals. At $I\mid D$ the normalized CM tangent condition has only the one residue root (the image of $\sqrt D$ is zero). [Normalized Deuring lifting](https://people.math.wisc.edu/~tonghaiyang/deuring.pdf), with tame lifting of the cyclic flag, identifies those **marked embedding classes** with precisely the literal class indices contributing to $r(q)$. A flag is considered over the algebraic residue field. Its stabilizer is just $\{\pm1\}$ (higher elliptic automorphisms at $j=0,1728$ cannot preserve a cyclic 16-line), so there is no divide-by-units ambiguity after fixing the marked label. Only the parity of this count is required. Counting in the unflagged endomorphism order without the two level-root tests would in general give a different result.

This is a receiver for the actual first-arm mark using a single characteristic and one parity weight per nontrivial orbit. It is not a claim that extracting these parity coefficients takes polynomial time in the bit size of $n$, or that any of the labels actually has odd multiplicity. An explicit admissible numerical source and a proven aggregate coefficient (or a rational point by another producer) remain missing.
