# Paired original dyadic Heegner bits are a finite Frobenius condition

This note gives a characteristic-zero cuspidal modular-form receiver for the first binary ordinary CM phase on both selected twists of **one fixed original detector pole**. It is valid after selecting the shared helper primes and a permissible residue row for the final prime, but before choosing that prime. All exact original local marks and the pole are unchanged. The result replaces the question about two growing CM packets by one finite joint Frobenius condition. The modular level/weight bounds below are only existence bounds. No admissible joint Frobenius class is shown to hit both bits; no original twist, point, or rank is produced here.

## 1. The source and the weighted half used in the trace

Fix an original linear-\(q\) Ready detector as in the [marked Selmer selection](../../../rc-native-helper-20261120/ct-original-balanced-detector-selmer-eights-r1/BALANCED-ACTUAL-DETECTOR-SELMER-EIGHTS.md), taking the final \(q_0\equiv7\bmod24\). In particular its stable first mark is \(\beta=17d\), with \(d\) a product of six distinct primes \(1\bmod408\). Let \(\theta\) be the positive squarefree class of \(1-b/17\). In the unbalanced convention, the two selected twists are \(n_1=d A q_0\), \(n_2=d A\theta q_0\), with \(A=17\prod I_j\) fixed and prime to \(6d\); both are \(23\bmod24\) and have the exact original \(2\)-Selmer eights and the mark \((\beta,1)\). The same result below works for the balanced split by replacing \(A,A\theta\) by its fixed coprime-to-\(6d\) multipliers, provided both \(n_i\equiv23\bmod24\). Fix the exact nonempty admissible prime residue row. Enlarge its excluded finite set, but not its assigned local residues, when evaluating Hecke operators.

For such an \(n\) set \(K=\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-n})\) and use the literal cusp-normalized level-48 map \(\phi\) to \(E_0=E_{-1}\). Denote the full raw class trace by \(P\). The [selected fourth-divisibility result](../../../rc-native-helper-20261120/ct-level48-actual-ramified-kummer-fourth-r2/) and the [first binary ordinary phase](../original-detectors-chi17-dyadic-as-edge-phase-r1/FIRST-DYADIC-HEEGNER-BIT-IS-AN-ORDINARY-EDGE-MOMENT.md) give \(\log_2 P\in16\mathbf Z_2\) at a split place, with \(\log_2 P/16\) modulo \(2\) the desired sufficient nonvanishing bit. The two choices of level-16 orientation differ by a torsion translation, immaterial to the logarithm. The factorization \(-n=d(-n/d)\) gives an unramified nontrivial real genus character \(\chi_d\). Unlike \(\chi_{17}\), it has both level signs \(+1\), because \((d/2)=(d/3)=1\). Let \(R_d\) denote the same \(\chi_d\)-weighted trace. Its two genus eigenspaces identify it with a rational point on the shorter twist \(E_{n/d}\): it is class-anti over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-n},\sqrt d)\), conjugation-anti by \(\phi w_{48}=T_3-\phi\), \(\chi_d([\mathfrak n])=1\), and the sum of the weights is zero. These two actions give the twist character for \(-n/d\) on \(E_0\).

In fact \(\log_2R_d\in32\mathbf Z_2\). Here is a height check independent of the original \(\chi_{17}\) calibration. If \(R_d\) is torsion the result is immediate. Otherwise apply [Cai–Shu–Tian, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1733) to this weighted trace and to the unweighted raw trace \(P'\) of the **same map** over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-n/d})\). Both fields have level split, conductor-one orders and only \(\{\pm1\}\) as units. The real factorization has sign \(+\) on \(E_{-d}\), since \(d\) is positive and split locally at 2 and 3. Heights give
\[
 \frac{\widehat h(R_d)}{\widehat h(P')}
 =\sqrt d\,\frac{L(E_{-d},1)}{L(E_{-1},1)}
 =4^{\omega(d)}\,\frac{\mathcal L(-d)}{\mathcal L(-1)}.
\]
The ratio is on the common rational twist \(E_{n/d}\) under the height-preserving twist isomorphisms, with absolute height; any field extension conventions cancel. For the last equality, twist by \(d>0\): periods scale by \(1/\sqrt d\), the old Tamagawa factors at 2 and 3 are unchanged, each new \(p\mid d\) has split \(I_0^*\) and \(c_p=4\), and the two curves have rational torsion of order four. For torsion on \(E_{-d}\), inertia at two different ramified good odd primes kills any odd torsion and all additional 2-primary torsion. For \(E_{-1}:y^2=x(x+1)(x-3)\), counts at 5 and 7 are both 8, excluding odd torsion, and no rational 2-torsion point has two square root differences, excluding order four. The model is minimal at 2 and 3.

The normalizing denominator \(\mathcal L(-1)\) is odd. The actual modular map has \(z_E(\phi)=-Q+O(Q^2)\) at \(\infty\), obtained from \(X=Q^{-1}+O(1),\,y_{48}=Q^{-4}+O(Q^{-3})\) in the [level-48 chart](https://www.leonardocolo.com/documents/thesis/PhD_Thesis.pdf), so its pullback differential has Manin multiplier \(\pm1\). Its endpoints on the real winding path \(i\infty\) to \(0\) are \(O,T_3=(3,0)\), with \(T_3\) the nonzero 2-torsion point of the real identity component. Thus \(L(E_{-1},1)/\Omega^+\) is an odd half-integer. Since the full real period is \(2\Omega^+\) and \(c_2=c_3=2\), \(\mathcal L(-1)=2L(E_{-1},1)/\Omega^+\) is odd. By [Feng–Liu–Pan–Tian, Proposition 2.6(i)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11132) \(\mathcal L(-d)\) is an even integral square, because \(d>3\) is \(1\bmod12\) and the central sign is \(+\). Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin gives rank one on the shorter twist if \(R_d\) is nontorsion; then \(P'\) is also nontorsion and the ratio of their rational free coordinates has 2-adic valuation at least \(\omega(d)+1=7\). The target identification at 2 is over \(\mathbf Q_2\), and the elliptic logarithm of any point on \(E_0(\mathbf Q_2)\) lies in \(2\mathbf Z_2\) by the previously checked component and formal log. This proves the required depth. It neither evaluates the weighted point nor uses the rank of the two target twists.

Consequently, also on the \(\chi_d=+\) half, the sum of logarithms \(L=\log_2\phi\) has its first residue
\[
 \frac{\log_2((P+R_d)/2)}8=\frac{\log_2P+\log_2R_d}{16}
 \equiv\frac{\log_2P}{16}\pmod2.
\]
Here the notation \((P+R_d)/2\) refers to the actual half-orbit sum, not an arbitrary division operation; its logarithm removes any torsion ambiguity. This half is stable under arithmetic Frobenius at 2 since \(\chi_d([\mathfrak p_2])=+1\).

## 2. Exact Faber repair and all odd trace components

Use \(\Phi=f_*(J_*)\) from the [full Atkin cusp pullback](../original-detectors-invariant-cusp-pullback-r1/ATKIN-INVARIANT-CUSP-PULLBACK-OF-ORDINARY-EDGE-PHASE.md). Its Fourier parameter \(Q\) at the width-one cusp satisfies \(J_*(Q)=Q^{-1}+O(1)\) with integral coefficients; the quotient by the four Atkin operations has just one cusp, and \(J_*\) has a simple pole there. Let \(\mathcal P_j\in\mathbf Z[J_*]\) be the monic Faber polynomial determined by \(\mathcal P_j(Q)=Q^{-j}+O(Q)\). Integrality and uniqueness follow by triangular subtraction using the unit leading coefficient; \(\mathcal P_0=1\). Put
\[
 \widetilde\Phi=\sum_{j=1}^3 b_j(\mathcal P_{2j}-\mathcal P_j),\qquad
 (b_1,b_2,b_3)=(205856642639,3391107826,26851175).
\]
The \(b_j\) are the exact principal coefficients of \(B_*(T(Q))\) in the previous cohomology proof. The attached independent Fourier replay computes them and all \(\mathcal P_j\) directly from the integral level-six eta product. The same triangularity and the verified \(\Phi\) identity show **as polynomials** that \(\widetilde\Phi\equiv\Phi\bmod2^{25}\). Thus this change preserves the binary packet output after division by \(2^{24}\). All cusps have zero constant term, and for every odd positive \(k\) and quadratic \(\chi\) with \(\chi(2)=1\),
\[
  \sum_{j>0}\chi(j)[Q^{-kj}]\widetilde\Phi=0
\]
as an exact equality, not just a congruence.

Use the twisted [Alfes–Ehlen lift](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.3961) at \(N=6\), \(\Delta=1,d\) and label \(r=1\). Its common discriminant module is \(D=\mathbf Z/12\), \(Q(h)=-h^2/24\). The trace definition (5.1) and theorem 5.5 give the positive Fourier coefficients, with trivial effective stabilizers for the nonexceptional CM orders used below. In particular the interpretation is the literal weighted trace, with the two definite lattice signs counted separately (section 6.3). Equivalently the lifts can be scalar-normalized to that convention. All source constants vanish, so these vector forms are weakly holomorphic by corollary 5.6.

Every **odd** component has no negative Fourier coefficient. To see the cusp seam directly, the negative indices in proposition 5.7 are \(m=-\Delta k^2/24\) with \(k>0\) integral. At the infinite cusp the triangular lattice representatives may be written
\[
  \lambda=\begin{pmatrix}\pm\Delta k/12&-a/6\\0&\mp\Delta k/12\end{pmatrix},
       \qquad a\bmod \Delta k .
\]
They are used in the \(h\) or \(-h\) term according to orientation; in every such term \(k\) is odd if \(h\) is odd. Here the genus character is \((\Delta/a)\), since the associated binary form is \([a,\pm\Delta k,0]\). The geodesic pairing with the \(Q^{-j}\) coefficient multiplies by \(e(\pm aj/(\Delta k))\). Its Gauss sum over \(a\) vanishes unless \(k\mid j\); for \(j=k j'\) it is \(k\sqrt\Delta\,(\Delta/j')\), including the principal case \(\Delta=1\). This term therefore vanishes by the exact Faber identity. The original \(\Gamma_0(6)\) lattice, its dual, and the genus character are invariant under the Atkin operators (Alfes–Ehlen (3.1)–(3.2)); conjugating the cusp lattice to \(\infty\) preserves the primitive translation, the Fourier uniformizer and the geodesic pairing. Under each operator the discriminant label is multiplied by a unit modulo 12 (sign change on its own primary arm), so odd parity is preserved. The function \(\widetilde\Phi\) is invariant as well. This proves vanishing separately for every source cusp and both oriented terms, without a root-of-unity cancellation between different \(\lambda\)-orbits. There is no constant Fourier term in an odd component. In every component that can have a pole the pole exponent has magnitude at most \(36\Delta/24\); this follows from the same conjugation and \(k\le6\), the largest available source principal exponent.

## 3. An integral-weight classical cusp form, with all cusps checked

Write \(f_\Delta(\tau)=I_{\Delta,1,h=1}(24\tau,\widetilde\Phi)\). Its Fourier exponents are integral, all \(s\equiv23\bmod24\), and positive by the preceding step. Define Fourier coefficient operations
\[
  U_d\!\left(\sum_s a_s Q^s\right)=\sum_s a_{ds}Q^s,\qquad
  \mathcal C_d\!\left(\sum_s a_s Q^s\right)=\sum_{(s,d)=1} a_sQ^s .
\]
Consider
\[
 \mathcal A=\frac{\mathcal C_d(U_d f_1+f_d)}{2^{26}} .
\]
At \(\infty\) this is integral at either chosen prime over 2 in \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt d)\). Indeed for \(s>0,\,s\equiv23\bmod24,\,(s,d)=1\) both inputs refer to the discriminant \(-ds\). One fixed positive-definite sign for \(h=1\) gives all ring classes on one level arm. The negative definite sign gives the other orientation \(-h\), with exactly the same weighted trace by Fricke invariance and \(\chi_\Delta(-1)=+1\). There are no extra roots of unity (the maximal discriminants \(-3,-4\) are excluded by \(-ds\equiv1\bmod24\)). When the discriminant is imprimitive, separate the forms by content \(g\); each order's conductor is prime to \(6d\), since each prime of \(d\) occurs exactly once. Its fundamental discriminant still has real genus factor \(d\), so \(\chi_d\) is nonzero on those classes and identifies the same \(+\) half that pairs the two input sums. These are ordinary split at 2, including the ring-class unramified completion because the conductor is prime to 2. The chosen 2-orientation is canonical there; switching to it by \(w_2\) is harmless through full Atkin invariance and \(\chi_d(\mathfrak p_2)=1\). Thus for each content the numerator of \(\mathcal A\) is \(4\sum_+\widetilde\Phi\), a \(2^{26}\)-multiple by the source identity, before ordinary reduction. The same reasoning works for either orientation of the level-3 mark. This proves integrality of **all** retained coefficients, not just of the two target prime terms. The unweighted trace coefficients are rational by conjugation and Fricke, while the weighted values belong to \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt d)\) by the class character and the same conjugation formulas. At a squarefree selected \(n=ds\), the resulting coefficient is \(\log_2P/16\bmod2\), by sections 1 and 2.

For congruence level, one safe bound is \(M=576d^4\). On the group \(\Gamma(576)\) each \(f_\Delta\) uses the usual theta multiplier in weight \(3/2\). Indeed conjugation by \(\operatorname{diag}(24,1)\) puts the matrix in \(\Gamma(24)\). [Strömberg, lemmas 5.12 and 5.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.0202) give the scalar \((c/24\,/\,v)\) for lower row \((c,v)\), which equals \((c/v)\) since \(v\equiv1\bmod24\). The theta \(\epsilon_v\) is one in this group. For \(U_d\) use the average of \(\tau\mapsto(\tau+j)/d\); for \(\mathcal C_d\) use inclusion–exclusion over \(e\mid d\) of all \(j/e\)-translations. Each combined affine change is \(\tau\mapsto(\tau+B/e)/m\) with \(e\mid d\), \(m=1\) or \(d\), \(B\in\mathbf Z\). Conjugation by \(\begin{pmatrix}e&B\\0&me\end{pmatrix}\) sends a matrix of \(\Gamma(M)\) to \(\Gamma(576)\) with lower row \((mc,v-Bc/e)\). The fractional linear denominator after the transform is exactly \(c\tau+v\). For \(c>0\), the Kronecker character with numerator \(c\) has conductor dividing \(8\operatorname{rad}(c)\) with odd primes to first power, and \(Bc/e\) is a multiple of that conductor because \(e\) is odd and \(d^4\mid c\); the extra symbol for \(m\) is trivial since \(v-Bc/e\equiv1\bmod4d\). Thus the multipliers agree; the case \(c<0\) follows by using the inverse matrix, and \(c=0\) is translation. This proves the same half-integral multiplier for \(\mathcal A\) on \(\Gamma(M)\). Multiplication by \(\vartheta(\tau)=1+2\sum_{j\ge1}Q^{j^2}\) gives ordinary weight two there because the multiplier to the fourth power is trivial.

At every cusp with \(v_2(c)\ge6\), after any of the above affine maps and the \(24\tau\) scaling, factor the resulting integer matrix of positive determinant into an \(SL_2(\mathbf Z)\) matrix \(\gamma'\) followed by an upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal. The first column of \(\gamma'\) is the primitive part of that of the product. The numerator of the top entry before taking the primitive part has exact valuation 3 (original \(c\) even and original top entry odd, all affines odd); hence \(v_2(c_{\gamma'})=v_2(c)-3\ge3\), including \(c=0\). The 2-primary part of the Weil representation on \(D_2=\mathbf Z/4\), of level 8, therefore acts monomially by an odd multiplier on its labels, by Strömberg lemma 5.13. The tensor decomposition follows directly from the \(S,T\) formulas and the primary orthogonal decomposition of the module. In particular only odd components can contribute to the transformed \(h=1\) series at these cusps. They have no principal or constant term by section 2. The upper triangular variable change, and the holomorphic theta factor, preserve vanishing. This is an exact characteristic-zero assertion; no 2-adic integrality principle at bad cusps is needed.

At the other cusps, clear the actual poles by multiplying by \(E^{d^3}\), where
\[
 E(\tau)=\frac{\Delta_{\rm mod}(\tau)^{64}}{\Delta_{\rm mod}(64\tau)}
       \equiv1\pmod2
\]
and \(\Delta_{\rm mod}=Q\prod_{j\ge1}(1-Q^j)^{24}\). This is holomorphic of weight 756 on \(\Gamma_0(64)\), no zeros in the upper half-plane. By the same triangular cusp factorization its order in \(Q\) after slashing at a reduced cusp of denominator \(c\) is \(64-\gcd(c,64)^2/64\); it is at least 48 on the remaining cusps. For an input affine with determinant \(me^2\), the triangular Fourier exponent amplification is at most \(24me^2\) after the scaling by 24. The pole bound from section 2 is therefore at most \(36d^3\), because \(\Delta m e^2\le d^3\) for each input. The stated power yields strictly positive order at every remaining cusp. We have constructed
\[
 \mathcal G=E^{d^3}\vartheta\,\mathcal A
   \in S_{\,2+756d^3}(\Gamma_1(576d^4)).
\]
The final passage from \(\Gamma(M)\) to \(\Gamma_1(M)\) follows from invariance under integral translation (the Fourier exponents are integral) and generation of the latter group by the former and the translation. All coefficients at \(\infty\) are 2-integral and \(\mathcal G\equiv\mathcal A\bmod2\) there.

## 4. The paired finite condition and what is not furnished

Fix the selected constants \(A_1=A,A_2=A\theta\), or the balanced multipliers from section 1. For any admissible prime \(q_0\nmid 2M A_1A_2\), each required bit becomes
\[
 [Q^{A_jq_0}]\mathcal G=[Q^{A_j}](T_{q_0}\mathcal G)\pmod2,\qquad j=1,2.
\]
There is no extra level factor \(\theta\); the two coefficients are distinct linear functionals on a single fixed Hecke algebra. Use its action on the 2-integral \(\infty\)-expansion lattice of the classical cusp space. This lattice is finite over the 2-adic integers of a sufficiently large coefficient field by finite dimensionality and an injective finite coefficient truncation, and the good Hecke operators preserve it. The characteristic-zero algebra generated by the good \(T_q\) embeds in the product of coefficient fields of its classical eigenpackets (old copies have the same good eigenvalues). In the product of their local rings, the finite integral Hecke algebra is a closed lattice in its span. For each packet, the continuous 2-adic Galois representation has trace equal to \(a_q\) at good Frobenius, by Deligne. By density of Frobenius conjugacy classes, the tuple of traces of every Galois element lies in that closed lattice. Its reduction modulo the maximal-ideal multiple of the Hecke lattice is controlled by a finite quotient: taking a sufficiently high common modulus in the product rings suffices by the elementary-divisor description of the lattice inclusion. Thus both bits, **with the full integral Hecke action and not only the semisimple mod-2 eigenvalues**, are functions on one finite Galois group. Intersect with the prescribed cyclotomic residue row. If there is any compatible Frobenius class on which both functionals return one, Chebotarev supplies infinitely many permissible shared \(q_0\). Both actual Heegner traces are then nontorsion and each original rational mark follows by the verified exact Selmer-eight receiver, without a rank converse. A zero bit is inconclusive about that trace or rank. This construction does not prove that the joint compatible set is nonempty, compute it, or supply a practically small Hecke basis.
