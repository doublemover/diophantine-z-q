# One binary ordinary CM edge moment suffices for a selected detector arm

This is an exact low-precision refinement of the
[tame dyadic logarithm receiver](../original-detectors-chi17-tame-dyadic-jet-r1/ONE-TAME-ORDINARY-JET-FOR-THE-ACTUAL-DYADIC-LOG.md).
For each actual original selected arm it gives one sufficient binary
certificate requiring no higher dyadic lifts. Its value is not
computed for either selected arm. The only numerical evaluation
below is on the genuine \(D=-119\) reference, not on an original pole.

## 1. The exact local logarithm modulo 16

Let \(u=t_3\) be the actual tame level-3 parameter on the selected
ordinary, unit dyadic level-48 branch. Put \(T=u-1\), \(z=T^{-1}\);
\(z\) here is a tame rational function, not the formal EC parameter.
Let \(F_2(u)\) be the **canonical connected** 2-quotient on the marked
tame level. Its exact ordinary formula uses the first and second
maps of \(X_0(6)\to X_0(3)\), from
[Colò, Appendix A](https://www.leonardocolo.com/documents/thesis/PhD_Thesis.pdf):
\[
   u=\frac{q^2(q+9)}{(q+8)^2},\qquad
   F_2(u)=\frac{q(q+9)^2}{q+8},
   \qquad q\equiv u-1\bmod 2 .
\]
The first map has derivative a unit on this branch, so \(q\)
is uniquely obtainable at every finite 2-adic precision.
On the actual ordinary unramified lifts, \(F_2\) is the arithmetic
Frobenius on the tame coordinate, including the transported
3-subgroup.

Write \(L(u)\) for the local \(E_0\) logarithm of the literal
level-48 modular image in the same orientation, as in the prior
receiver. Let \(B_0(u)=z+4z^2+8(z^5+z^3)\). The exact identity in
\((\mathbf Z/16)[T,T^{-1}]\) is
\[
 L(u)=B_0(F_2(u))-B_0(u)+8(z^9+z^3)\pmod {16}.
\]
The replay uses only the finite expansions already proved:
\(x/X^2\equiv1+4/(X^4-1)\), the exact first tame tower
for \(u(X)\), its ordinary inverse, the duplication parameter
\(W=z_E(2\phi)/2\), and the integral formal logarithm
\(L=W+(4b_2/3)W^3\pmod {16}\).
All inverse elements have reduction a monomial in \(T\),
so geometric 2-adic inversion remains in the indicated Laurent
ring. The checked identity includes all cancellations.
In particular the log up to modulus 8 is entirely a
Frobenius coboundary. On any actual Frobenius-stable half
the coboundary sums to zero before dividing by 8.
Thus the first possible residue is the one binary phase
\(\sum (z^9+z^3)\) in the characteristic-two packet.

## 2. No dyadic lift or ordered flag value is needed for this bit

In \(\mathbf F_2(z)\) the difference
\[
 (z^9+z^3)+(z^2+z)^5=(z^5+z^3)^2+(z^5+z^3)
\]
is another Frobenius coboundary, and disappears under the same trace.
The elementary moment variable \(z^2+z=u/(u-1)^2\) is unchanged
when the selected 3-edge is reversed (\(u\mapsto1/u\)).
The mod-2 source and target invariants of the exact tame edge are
\[
 j_s=u+u^{-3},\qquad j_t=u^3+u^{-1},\qquad
                    z^2+z=j_s^{-1}+j_t^{-1}.
\]
Therefore, for the fixed ideal \(\mathfrak p_3\) above 3, the required
phase on a chosen genus half is
\[
 {\cal A}_+(n)=
 \sum_{\chi_{17}([\mathfrak a])=+1}
       \left(j_{\mathfrak a}^{-1}
                  +j_{\mathfrak a\mathfrak p_3}^{-1}\right)^5
 \quad\in\mathbf F_2 ,
\]
where all invariants are the ordinary mod-2 CM invariants
at a chosen place. Use the inverse ideal if required by
the Artin convention. The edge observable itself is symmetric in
its endpoints; it does **not** erase the requirement to select
the correct matching of horizontal neighbors from a genus half.
Simply counting both 3-arms or only giving an unmarked Hilbert
class polynomial does not give that matching. Frobenius orbits can
alternatively be aggregated by their finite field trace, with one
representative and one correct matched neighbor per orbit.

In terms of the tame genus-half polynomial, the same bit needs
only its jet modulo \(T^{10}\) over \(\mathbf F_2\):
\[
 \left([T^8]+[T^2]\right)
       \frac{F_+'(1+T)}{F_+(1+T)} .
\]
Indeed \(F_+(1)\) is a unit and expanding at the nonroot 1
gives the sums of the respective inverse ninth and third powers.
Characteristic two removes the sign.

## 3. Why a nonzero bit would certify an actual selected arm

Use either actual selected \(n=17s\equiv23\bmod24\), with exact
marked Selmer eight as in the
[both-weighted construction](../original-detectors-chi17-both-weighted-r1/BOTH-ACTUAL-CHI17-WEIGHTED-TRACES.md).
The raw unweighted trace descends to \(P_D\in4E_n(\mathbf Q)\)
by the separate
[rational fourth theorem](../../../rc-native-helper-20261120/ct-level48-actual-ramified-kummer-fourth-r2/ACTUAL-LEVEL48-RAMIFIED-KUMMER-FOURTH.md)
(checksum list SHA-256
\(90603501f1955f5913ba6817defc55da788656ec75a3378dfb2e6c42c9ed4dab\)).
For a rational quarter \(Q\) of \(P_D\), its local Kummer
class at 2 is in the rational torsion image. Indeed the global
exact 2-Selmer group is generated by rational torsion and
\((\beta,1)\), and \(\beta\) is a product of primes \(1\bmod8\)
including 17, hence a local square. Thus \(Q\) is a local
double modulo torsion.

The fixed twist identifies \(E_n\) with \(E_0\) over \(\mathbf Q_2\)
since \(D=-n\equiv1\bmod8\), with differential a unit multiple.
The logarithm image of \(E_0(\mathbf Q_2)\) is \(2\mathbf Z_2\).
In fact the model is minimal, the formal group logarithm is
bijective from \(2\mathbf Z_2\) to itself, the special smooth group
is additive with two elements and is covered by \((0,0)\) and
the identity, and the component group has order 2
([tiling paper, Lemma A.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11132)).
The singular-reducing rational torsion \(T_3\) covers the other
component. Hence rational torsion maps onto the quotient by the
formal group, and removing it does not change the logarithm.
Therefore \(\log Q\in4\mathbf Z_2\) and \(\log P\in16\mathbf Z_2\).

The independently calibrated weighted point has
\(v_2(\log R)=\omega(s)+2\) and is zero mod 32 on this support.
For the two half sums \(A_\pm\), \(2A_\pm=P\pm R\).
Using the fixed \(E_0\) logarithm (not altering its unit normalization),
\[
 {\cal A}_+(n)={\cal A}_-(n)
            =\frac{\log P}{16}\pmod 2.
\]
Either half suffices. A \(1\) proves \(P_D\) nontorsion;
a \(0\) is inconclusive. In particular the whole reference
formula modulo 16 does not by itself give the selected bit,
since the selected whole raw log is zero modulo 16.
No dyadic claim for a rational quarter assumes that the mark
is already rational. If the phase is nonzero on both exact
selected arms, the existing Selmer-eights and literal same-pole
back-map give the required rational marked witnesses.

## 4. The usual Igusa character alone cannot supply this phase

For comparison, the first nontrivial cyclic-two quotient of
the standard ordinary Igusa tower in characteristic 2 is the
projective level-eight character: whether a generator of the
étale 8-torsion is rational up to sign. This character over the
\(j\)-line is the Artin--Schreier class of \(1/j\).
One direct calculation uses the ordinary model
\(y^2+xy=x^3+a\), \(j=1/a\).
Its rational \(x\)-coordinate for an order-four point satisfies
\(x_4^4=a\). The formula \(x(2P)=x(P)^2+a/x(P)^2\)
gives \(V^2+V=x_4^2\) for \(V=x(P)^2/x_4\) at order eight.
Squaring the relation gives the stated separable class of \(a\);
the purely inseparable \(x\)-changes do not change the
projective étale cyclic-two quotient.

On the ordinary tame level-3 chart this is the class of
\(z^4+z^3+z^2+z\), whose reduced odd pole terms are
\(z^3+z\). It is not the class of \(z^9+z^3\): their
difference has an odd pole of order 9, impossible for
\(g^2+g\) at that point. This rules out substituting the
ordinary projective Igusa character, not all possible
CM reciprocity or non-Igusa correspondences.

The replay also computes the exact two Frobenius halves
of the genuine \(D=-119\) reference in \(\mathbf F_{32}\),
as justified in the tame reference. Their ordinary
3-edge phases are \(1,0\) in the displayed order,
agreeing with the separate mod-\(2^{14}\) log replay.
The level-eight Igusa bit is \(1\) on both. This reference
difference does not contradict the preceding production
equality: the reference does not meet the many-prime
rational-fourth and weighted-depth hypotheses.
