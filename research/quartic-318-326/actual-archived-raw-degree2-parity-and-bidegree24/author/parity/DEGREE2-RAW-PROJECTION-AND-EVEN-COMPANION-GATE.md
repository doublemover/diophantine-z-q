# A degree-two raw projection forces an even companion degree

This is a rational-function obstruction for the original fixed two-conic surface, not a restriction on individual rational points. It uses the reviewed genus-one models from the exact original fixed scalar and the nonisogeny of their Jacobians. It is independent of coefficient searches for the original detector.

Let $\beta,\delta>0$ be rational nonsquares, $f(t)=(t^2-\beta)(t^2+3\beta)$, and set
$$C_1:u^2=f(t),\qquad C_\delta:v^2=\delta f(t').$$
Their two involutions $\iota_1,\iota_\delta$ reverse the signs of the corresponding ordinates. The Jacobian of $C_1$ is the known $E'_\beta$ of $j=2048/3$, and that of $C_\delta$ is its $\delta$-twist, as in the independently verified original model. In particular $\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(J(C_1),J(C_\delta))=0$: the nonintegral $j$ is not CM, so the geometric endomorphism space is one-dimensional over $\mathbb Q$, and the nontrivial twist character acts by minus on the space of homomorphisms. The literal old input-0 donor `61ddafd` supplies this same surface at its actual positive nonsquare $\delta$; the exact degree-$(2,2)$ obstruction `fc6ce55e089c5ceb37f173704b69f4537f5234227eb4ef7a3a29af3a592021f8` is a prior result, not re-enumerated here.

Suppose $t,t',W\in\mathbb Q(z)$ satisfy $W^2=\delta f(t)f(t')$, with $\deg t=2$ and $t'$ nonconstant. We show that $\deg t'$ is even and that the degrees and coefficient conditions in §3 characterize the only possible structural case.

## 1. Descent of a companion involution even when its projection is not Galois

The common double cover $H$ has field $\mathbb Q(z)(u)$, $u^2=f(t(z))$, and maps $F:H\to C_1$, $G:H\to C_\delta$ via the exact functions $u,v=W/u$. The cover is geometrically integral: over an algebraic closure, a square $f(t(z))$ would give a nonconstant map from a projective line to a genus-one curve. The maps to the two proper curves extend everywhere on the smooth models. The degree-two $F$ has its deck involution $s$ over $\mathbb Q$. The involution $\iota$ of the common double cover reverses both ordinates; it normalizes the group $\{1,s\}$, because it preserves the subfield $F^*\mathbb Q(C_1)$. Thus it commutes with $s$. The restriction $S$ of $s$ to its invariant $z$-line is the nontrivial deck of $t$, with fixed field $\mathbb Q(t)$; if it were trivial then $s=\iota$ or $1$, incompatible with $s$ nontrivial and fixing $u$.

On Jacobians, $1+s_*=F^*F_*$. This is the norm/pullback equality for the degree-two quotient, including ramified fibres with multiplicities. Because $G_*F^*$ is a rational homomorphism between the two nonisogenous elliptic Jacobians, it is zero. Hence $G_*s_*=-G_*$. We do **not** assume $G$ is a Galois map. Over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ choose origins for its curve of genus one and for the Albanese description of $H$. The displayed relation shows $G(sP)+G(P)$ is constant in the chosen group coordinate: subtract its value at one point and use $G_*([P-P_0])$. Consequently there is an affine involution $j$ of $C_\delta$ with
$$G\circ s=j\circ G,$$
acting by $-1$ on the Jacobian. It descends to $\mathbb Q$ because both sides before solving for $j$ are rational maps over $\mathbb Q$ and $G$ is surjective, so each Galois conjugate of $j$ agrees after composition with $G$. No rational origin on this genus-one torsor is needed. The same surjectivity, with $s\iota=\iota s$ and $G\iota=\iota_\delta G$, shows $j\iota_\delta=\iota_\delta j$. Therefore $j$ descends further to a rational Möbius involution of the $t'$-line preserving its four branch points.

A rational Möbius map sends the real pair $\{\pm\sqrt\beta\}$ to itself and the nonreal pair $\{\pm\sqrt{-3\beta}\}$ to itself. Exchanging both pairs gives $t'\mapsto-t'$ by agreement at more than two points. There cannot be an involution exchanging only one pair: a nonidentity involution fixing $\pm a$ individually is $t'\mapsto a^2/t'$ (equivalently diagonalize at the two fixed points). For it to exchange $\pm b$, one needs $a^2=-b^2$, false for the pair of squares $\beta,-3\beta$. Thus on the $t'$-line the induced map is identity or negation. In the identity case $j=\iota_\delta$, since a nontrivial involution over $t'$ is the unique deck and $j$ acts by minus. In the negation case, its two possible lifts are $(t',v)\mapsto(-t',\pm v)$; the sign is $+$ on $v$, because $dt'/v$ must be negated by $j$.

## 2. The identity branch is impossible; the negation branch has even degree

In the identity case $t'=r(t)$ with $r\in\mathbb Q(t)$ and $W$ is $S$-anti-invariant, as $u$ is fixed and $v$ reversed. Thus the rational line has quadratic field $\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(t)(W)$, whose branch set is the symmetric difference of the four-root set $A$ of $f(t)$ and the set $A_r$ of odd-multiplicity geometric preimages of the four roots under $r$. Poles of $r$ contribute exponent divisible by 4 and no branches. The four target roots are two Galois-conjugate pairs. Within each pair the number of odd preimages of one member equals that of the other, and the number for each member is congruent to $\deg r$ modulo 2 by the total fibre degree. It follows that $|A_r|\equiv0\pmod4$. The intersection $A\cap A_r$ has even cardinality as a rationally invariant subset of the two quadratic pairs of $A$. Consequently the symmetric difference has cardinality divisible by 4. A geometrically integral degree-two map of a genus-zero curve to a projective line has exactly two geometric branch points in characteristic zero by Riemann–Hurwitz. This is a contradiction.

In the negation case $t'$ is $S$-anti-invariant and $W$ is $S$-invariant. Put $M(t)=(t')^2\in\mathbb Q(t)$. The exact field and equation are
$$
\mathbb Q(z)=\mathbb Q(t)(t'),\qquad (t')^2=M(t),\qquad
W(t)^2=\delta f(t)(M(t)-\beta)(M(t)+3\beta).
$$
The equality of degrees is $2\deg_z(t')=\deg_z((t')^2)=2\deg_t M$. The odd geometric places of $(M-\beta)(M+3\beta)$ are therefore exactly $A$. Poles of $M$ have even exponent in this product. In each of the **rational** fibres $M=\beta,-3\beta$, the odd points form their own Galois-stable subset of $A$, hence have even cardinality. In either fibre their cardinality is congruent to $\deg M$ modulo 2, again by the total fibre degree. This forces $\deg M=\deg_z t'$ even. The argument also applies with the two raw projections interchanged, replacing the same Jacobian Hom vanishing by its symmetric counterpart.

## 3. Exact shaped feasibility test for degree (2,4)

No positive instance is claimed. The following is both necessary and sufficient for the **raw identity** at degree $(2,4)$:
1. Take a degree-four rational $M=P/Q$ in $t$, with $P,Q$ coprime homogeneous binary quartics over $\mathbb Q$.
2. The odd vanishing points of $P-\beta Q$ and $P+3\beta Q$ must partition the two quadratic pairs $(t^2-\beta)$ and $(t^2+3\beta)$, with one pair per fibre or both in the same fibre. In binary forms, the separate-fibre forms are constants times their indicated quadratic times a squared linear form; when both pairs lie in one they are a constant times their product and a constant times a squared quadratic, respectively. Their two nonzero constants $a,b$ satisfy $\delta ab\in\mathbb Q^{\times2}$. This is exactly the second square identity above: all other factors occur to even order, and after division by their polynomial squares the constant is $\delta ab$.
3. The quadratic curve of $Y^2=P/Q$ must be geometrically genus zero and have a rational point. In particular $P,Q$ have just two odd vanishing places in their disjoint supports: each homogeneous quartic has an even number, so one is a constant times a squared quadratic and the other a constant times a separable binary quadratic times a squared linear form. Strip these squares and test the resulting rational conic. Requiring a rational point on the conic is essential; geometric genus zero alone is not sufficient.

If all the conditions hold, choose a rational parameter $z$ on that conic, then $\deg_z t=2$ and $t'=Y$ has degree four by the same degree equality. The square identity from step 2 supplies $W\in\mathbb Q(t)$, proving sufficiency. The test supplies neither a solution nor a general exclusion for higher even degrees. It acts on the raw parameter identity, not just one of the stronger ordered-mark equations; the usual nonzero affine domain would be imposed when using a particular rational specialization for a PointPair.
