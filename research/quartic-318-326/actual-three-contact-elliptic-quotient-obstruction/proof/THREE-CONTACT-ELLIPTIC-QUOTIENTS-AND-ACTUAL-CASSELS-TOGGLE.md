# Five actual elliptic quotients, and a governed obstruction on the positive-root pair

Keep the exact three-contact cover \(C_3\) of 48ae5e44 and the
pointed genus-17 construction 4d43636f/ae867ee0, with all original
fixed shallow labels and roots
\[
 s=-32/(407c_1^2),\quad t=1/(2399c_2^2),\quad u=1/2399.
\]
Write \(a=p_aq_a,\ b=p_bq_b,\ c=p_cq_c,\ p_c=6469193\).
The first two supports and the pointed 4-cover are fixed before
selecting \(q_c\). We verify the five elliptic quotient tests
and show that the last prime can make **either** value of the
one supported Cassels test on the positive-root quotient \(E_{bc}\).
In particular one can retain all local cells and choose a globally
pointless \(C_3\) and genus-17 pullback. Another choice avoids this
specific obstruction and is **not** proved rational. No numerical
new prime, Lean result or claim about the original rigid clone's
absolute pairing value.

## 1. Literal elliptic quotient maps and supported classes

The function field of \(C_3\) over \(z=w^2\) is generated by
\[
 \sqrt z,\quad \sqrt{(z-s)/a},\quad
 \sqrt{(z-t)/b},\quad \sqrt{(z-u)/c}.
\]
The four radicals are independent by their valuation at their
respective simple finite root. Products of three, and the
product of all four, give the five genus-one quadratic
subcovers. Each quotient map has degree eight. In the
ordered Kummer coordinates attached to the roots, the maps give:

| Curve | Roots of its monic cubic | Marked label from \(C_3\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(E_{ab}\) | \(0,ab\,s,ab\,t\) | \((ab,b,a)\) |
| \(E_{ac}\) | \(0,ac\,s,ac\,u\) | \((ac,c,a)\) |
| \(E_{bc}\) | \(0,bc\,t,bc\,u\) | \((bc,c,b)\) |
| \(E_{abc}\) | \(D s,D t,D u,\ D=abc\) | \((bc,ac,ab)\) |
| inverted | \(-Dtu,-Dsu,-Dst\) | \((bc\,tu,ac\,su,ab\,st)\) |

For a cubic subset use \(x=d z\), and \(y=d^2\) times
its three ordinates, where d is the product of the coefficients
of those three equations (coefficient 1 for the zero root).
Every root difference then gives exactly the indicated square
multiple. For the fourfold product first put
\(Y=D wUVQ\), giving \(Y^2=D z(z-s)(z-t)(z-u)\).
With \(K=-Dstu\), put \(x=K/z,\ y=KY/z^2\).
Direct substitution gives the inverted monic cubic, and
\(x-K/s=-(K/s)(z-s)/z\) gives the marked first label,
since \(z=w^2\); the two other labels follow identically.
All maps extend to smooth projective curves. The five
nontrivial genus-one character quotients exhaust the five
Jacobian dimensions by rational idempotents for the sign
action, but the present argument only uses the literal map
to \(E_{bc}\).

The exact pilot replay independently enumerates the Kummer
triples supported only on the product coefficients of each
of the five quotients. Their dimensions are \(3,3,2,3,2\),
in the order displayed. Indeed at each participating support
prime the twisted three root differences have depth one,
so the full local image is the four actual torsion rows
distinguished by valuation. Their units come from the pilot
root signs and the exact opposite-factor phases; the checker
allows either own stripped unit sign rather than mistaking it
for a stored pole sign. All coefficients are squares at B and
positive over the reals; at other good primes these triples
are unramified. This is only the supported subspace, not full
Selmer rank. For the inverted model the marked label also
contains fixed root classes, as shown.

On \(E_{bc}\) the exact two supported directions are
\[
 A=(bc,c,b),\qquad F_{\rm raw}=(1,bc,bc).
\]
These are independent modulo rational torsion: for the two
nonzero roots the torsion first coordinate has class \(bc\cdot2399\),
not 1 or bc, and the zero-root torsion has negative second
coordinate. In particular their Cassels pairing is a meaningful
single extra test on this supported space.

## 2. Exact fixed normalized cup and its quartic primitive

Set \(T=2399\), \(\Delta=u-t\). Both t,u have squareclass T.
For purposes of varying the last prime, normalize by the unique
rational torsion row that kills the valuation at \(q_c\).
The two fixed representatives are
\[
 A^*=A+\kappa_2(T_u)=(u,b\Delta,bu\Delta),\qquad
 F^*=F_{\rm raw}+\kappa_2(T_0)=(1,-T,-T).
\]
Here T_u and T_0 denote actual points, not rational parameters.
Indeed the torsion rows are \((bc\,u,bc\,\Delta,u\Delta)\)
and \((tu,-bc\,t,-bc\,u)\), respectively. Normalized rows are
locally trivial at \(q_c\) by the four torsion parity rows.
They are Selmer on every admitted new-prime choice. Adding
rational torsion preserves the pairing.

The cocycle \(F^*\) is \(\chi_{-T}\) times the root-zero
torsion direction, because its first Kummer coordinate is
trivial and the other two are the same. Thus the actual Weil
cup for \(A^*,F^*\) is
\(\chi_T(g)\chi_{-T}(h)\). Take the real positive fourth root
\(\rho=T^{1/4}\), and in the dihedral field
\(L=\mathbf Q(i,\rho)\) write
\[
 g(i)=i^{(-1)^{\varepsilon(g)}},\qquad
 g(\rho)=i^{\psi(g)}\rho,\qquad
 \psi=\chi_T+2\Gamma .
\]
Use representatives 0 and 1 for each bit. The twisted
addition \(\psi(gh)=\psi(g)+(-1)^{\varepsilon(g)}\psi(h)\)
in \(\mathbf Z/4\) gives
\(d\Gamma(g,h)=\chi_{-T}(g)\chi_T(h)\).
Adding the 1-cochain \(\chi_T\chi_{-T}\) interchanges the two
factors in this cup. Therefore
\(\Gamma'=\Gamma+\chi_T\chi_{-T}\) is an exact primitive of
the required oriented cup. At primes split in \(\mathbf Q(i,\sqrt T)\)
it is the usual quartic high bit of T; changing conventions
to the inverse has no effect on this sign.

Apply [Smith's proof of Theorem 3.2, eq. (3.3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.07860)
to the twists \(q_c,q_c'\) of the fixed positive-root curve
with multiplier \(b p_c\). All fixed bad primes are on B or
\(b p_c\), with 2 and infinity included. Both roots and the
normalized classes are the same there under the local square
twist: \(q_c/q_c'\) is square by the original reciprocal
phases, including the fixed primes of b and the self phase
at \(p_c\). The augmented ray below keeps these phases.
At the two variable primes the normalized classes are trivial.
The primitive is unramified outside \(2T\), a subset of B.
Consequently the difference of the two raw pairing signs
is the product of the two signs \((T/q_c)_4\) and \((T/q_c')_4\).
No claim about the absolute reference sign is needed.

## 3. Both quartic phases preserve the exact order and local conditions

The scale integer B is divisible by \(T\). The fixed
17-row anchor modulus is nonzero modulo T, with residue 1194.
The **first two** actual clone selectors fix their Gaussian
generators modulo T, since they retain the old \(8B\) ray.
Their two group sizes, in either orientation, are norms
of \(\pi\pm1\); the pilot residues modulo T are
2144,1645 for the first, 1854,1621 for the second.
All are nonzero. Neither q_a nor q_b equals T.
Hence the actual post-two-class index modulus remains
prime to T, including after the pointed Sel₄ and all-cell
refinements (which changed no order or Gaussian phase).
At the T-place of \(G=\mathbf Q(i)\) the order-layer division
fibers and torsion layers for selecting q_c are unramified by
good reduction. They depend on levels prime to T.

In the q_c Gaussian ray, keep all factors and residues
unchanged except the factor at T. Let
\(\pi_0=1387+2132i\) be the actual third pilot generator.
At T and any retained exponent of T prescribe either
\[
 \pi_0\quad\text{or}\quad \pi_0 g,\qquad
 g=(5+i)/(5-i)=(24+10i)/26 .
\]
Here g is a unit modulo every \(T^e\) and has exact norm one.
Thus the rational norm remains \(p_c\bmod T^e\), not just the
quadratic symbol. In \(\mathbf F_{T^2}\), the two residues are
\((1387,2132)\) and \((1383,287)\); both have norm 1489.
Their tame quartic symbols are +1 and −1, respectively,
as verified by exponent \((T^2-1)/4\). Neither residue is
in \(\mu_4\) (their norms are not 1).

For precision the extension \(G(\rho)/G\) is cyclic quartic
and totally tamely ramified at the inert place \(T\): \(\rho^4=T\)
is Eisenstein over the unramified local quadratic Gaussian
field. Local reciprocity on units there acts by the fourth
power residue character of the residue field, or its inverse,
which has the same two displayed signs; see [tame norm-residue
formula](https://math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch09.html).
The extension is unramified away from \(2T\), and its conductor
at T has exponent 1. If needed, enlarge the fixed ray's
2-power precision to contain the finite dyadic conductor and
keep the same pilot residue there. Changing just the T-local
residue then changes Frobenius on \(G(\rho)\) by its order-two
element: all other local Artin components are unchanged.
This is a genuine Gaussian ray adjustment, not an assumption
of independence for two arbitrary Frobenius restrictions.

The re-anchored selector's old finite Kummer layers are
unramified at T as shown above; its separate ray at T
is abelian, unramified elsewhere. Use the same trivial
unramified intersection over the class-number-one Gaussian
field to add the ray at T after putting the rest in the
old clone layers. Thus both new residues permit the
same old point-order Frobenius class, prescribed depths
and local quotients. Their rational norms have the same
residue modulo the whole original B part, so the exact
quadratic reciprocity check of 48ae5e44 at both old factors
is intact. Keep the same split-component exclusions at the
other retained primes. At T the individual π±1 norms are
also nonzero, because T is inert and the Gaussian residue
is not ±1. All analytic sieve tails and real index density
are unchanged with the new fixed positive-density constant.
The added quartic field only ramifies at already fixed primes.

Both choices therefore supply actual third-prime families
with the same first two completed factors, same pointed C₄,
one common real-accepted h, and the same exact local squared
cells. In the first branch the actual quotient quartic sign
is that of the pilot (Euler's check gives +1); the other
branch reverses it. Section 2 now gives **opposite actual
Cassels pairing values** within these families. Choose the
branch with nonzero pairing to get an everywhere locally
soluble \(C_3\) without a rational point, and therefore a
pointless genus-17 pullback. Indeed a rational point would
give the exact marked rational Kummer class on \(E_{bc}\),
which lies in the ordinary pairing radical. This nonzero
obstruction remains valid even at a hypothetical exceptional
point of the covering: the marked torsor identity is global,
or note that w or any ordinate zero, and infinity, already
impose a nonsquare ratio in the three original equations.
The branch with zero pairing is not proved to pass the
other Selmer tests, and no independent local points on
quotient curves give a common global parameter.
