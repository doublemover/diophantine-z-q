# Certified degree-two auxiliary search using seven formal coefficients

Use only the fixed actual reduced marked ξ-lift, not a new Selmer
or theta index. Its two quadrics are Q₁,Q₂ from the exact CFS
reduction source pinned in the verifier. An actual rational point
here is an original marked nontorsion point via that packet's
explicit determinant-quartic back-map. Here the two methods differ:
the preceding search used an auxiliary *plane*, while the present
search builds a new *quadric* on each p-adic tube. We do not
enumerate integer vectors in a coordinate-height box.

“Seven” means coefficients of orders 0 through 6, sufficient modulo
N⁷, not an asserted derivative of order seven.

## The independent monomials and the exact formal lifts

The ten homogeneous quadratic monomials are in the old order
(i,j) with i≤j, lexicographic. Remove the first two positions whose
two columns in the full 2×10 old coefficient matrix have nonzero
determinant; here they are positions 0 and 1 (x₀²,x₀x₁).
The other eight monomials form a basis of the degree-two coordinate
ring of the complete intersection: the two old equations solve
uniquely for the removed coordinates, and its degree-two ideal is
exactly the span of those equations. Thus any nonzero combination
of retained monomials is a genuinely new quadric, not a member
of the old pencil.

Take a smooth mod-p point, the last nonzero projective coordinate
as pivot one, and the same two dependent coordinates with
invertible gradient minor as in the exact
[dual-plane search](../actual-one-I-dual-plane-search-r1/ACTUAL-SECOND-JET-DUAL-PLANES.md).
For N=p^k fix the residue r mod N of the one free parameter.
Lift `v0` (parameter r, pivot one) modulo N⁷. Then compute
vectors v1,…,v6, with pivot zero and free parameter one only at
v1, solving at order j modulo N^(7−j). The unknown dependent
coordinates in that order have coefficient the **same**
gradient minor at v0, which is still invertible. The other
terms at order j are the already computed symmetric bilinear
products of orders i and j−i for 0<i<j. When i=j−i the term is
the quadratic form at v_i; otherwise use the polar form with
no division. Each step verifies the two residual equalities.
Substituting into the two quadrics gives, for every s in Z_p,

```
Q(v0 + N s v1 + ... + N^6 s^6 v6) = 0 mod N^7.
```

By Hensel uniqueness this is the actual lift for free parameter
r+N s, not just one formal path or first-order approximation.
The normalized local coordinates of any integral primitive
projective vector in that tube are such a lift. The integral
vector itself comes by multiplying by its actual pivot coordinate,
which is a p-unit.

Evaluate the eight retained monomials at this polynomial.
Their coefficient vectors `u_j` in s, for j=0,…,6, are divisible
coordinatewise by N^j. All larger terms vanish mod N⁷.
Thus the eight monomial values of any such primitive vector remain
in the lattice Λ generated by the seven u_j and N⁷ Z⁸; here
multiplying back by pivot acts as multiplication by its **square**.
In the finite quotient (Z/N⁷)^8 the order of u_j is at most
N^(7−j); hence the lattice has index at least `N^56/N^28=N^28`.

## The dual relation and its exact cost

The code obtains an integer row basis by HNF and a scaled dual
basis by inversion. It checks all seven original generators
against the final nonzero integer normal λ and proves the
stronger exact bounded statement directly. Write w=λ/N⁷.
For every integer x in the chosen tube the dual pairing with
the retained monomials of x is an integer. If x has sup height
at most B, that pairing has absolute value at most
`B² · ||λ||₁/N⁷`. Therefore the literal auxiliary quadric
vanishes whenever this bound is strictly less than one.
The exact certified height per tube is
`floor sqrt(floor((N⁷−1)/||λ||₁))`. Scaling λ to a primitive
integer normal does not change its rational auxiliary quadric.

The dimension-eight LLL bound at δ=3/4 gives
`||w1||₂ ≤ 2^(7/4) det(Λ*)^(1/8) ≤ 2^(7/4) N^(−7/2)`;
the first inequality follows by multiplying the Gram–Schmidt
bounds from consecutive squared-length ratios at least 1/2.
Using `||w1||₁≤sqrt(8)||w1||₂`, all bins are covered if
`N^28 > 2^26 B^16`. This is a sufficient bound, not a
heuristic prediction of short vectors. For the fixed good prime,
Hensel gives order N bins, and the first power N with that inequality
is O(B^(4/7)). Each computation is of fixed dimension and degree,
with coefficient lengths polynomial in log B; rational factoring
and rational solution of a fixed-dimensional zero-dimensional
system of bounded degree are polynomial in that bit length.
Thus this is a deterministic `O(B^(4/7) polylog B)` bit upper bound
on trying the complete box for this fixed curve, improving
the dimension-four plane exponent 2/3. It is a finite use
of the [p-adic point-search method described by Fisher, §6.2](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~taf1000/papers/sixandtwelve.pdf),
not an existence theorem.

## Complete intersection at all heights

The new quadric is not in the degree-two ideal, so on this smooth
geometrically integral degree-four curve it cuts a zero-dimensional
scheme of degree eight, with multiplicity. Reduction of primitive
integer point coordinates gives a complete projective incidence
test at each small auxiliary good prime. A single no-incidence
test rules out *all* its rational points. Nothing is inferred when
every such local test passes.

For survivors, the program checks all four charts of the
actual projective complete intersection. In last-nonzero chart k,
set that coordinate to one and all later coordinates to zero.
Over Q compute a lexicographic Gröbner basis, take all rational
roots of all elimination polynomials in the last variable via
their gcd, and substitute in the original solution ideal, repeating
in the remaining variables. If the ideal becomes the unit ideal,
there is no solution in the chart. The computation neither
assumes independent roots nor drops rational solutions at
infinity. Each recovered tuple must evaluate to zero in all
three actual quadrics. Separate controls recover exactly eight
distinct affine rational solutions and four distinct rational
solutions on an infinity chart in example degree-eight schemes.
