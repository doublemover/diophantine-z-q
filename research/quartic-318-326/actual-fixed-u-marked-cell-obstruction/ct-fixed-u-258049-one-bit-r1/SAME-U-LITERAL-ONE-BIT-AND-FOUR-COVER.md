## 1. The exact discriminator, including divisible elements

Classical alternating Cassels–Tate pairings on elliptic curves have

hence the usual finite-cotype decomposition of a primary group with
finite involutions, or the project's exact reduced primary finiteness

Write S₂=Sel₂(E), T=δ₂(E(Q)[2]). Since no nonidentity rational 2-torsion
is rationally halved, dim T=2. The old ordinary pairing on S₂ is zero;
surjectivity S₂→A[2] implies CT vanishes on A[2]×A[2]. As α,γ both

with 2x_a=ā (the image of a in A[2]). Define

    B(a,g)=CT(x_a,ḡ) ∈ (1/2)ℤ/ℤ.

Changing the lift changes x_a by an element of A[2], so B is
independent of the choice. It is bilinear and alternating since
B(a,a)=CT(x_a,2x_a)=0. The rational Kummer image is in the radical
in both slots: choose the literal global δ₄ point lift in the first.
In particular it descends to the same 2D quotient S₂/T.

Claim: rad(B) is exactly image(Sel₈(E)→Sel₂(E)), with coefficient map
[4]:E[8]→E[2]. Because D is divisible, A[2]→M[2] is onto (lift r and
remove half its double in D), and inverse image of 2M in A is 2A
(divide any residual discrepancy in D). Perfectness gives
Ann(M[2])=2M: inclusion follows by bilinearity; orders agree because
|M[2]|·|2M|=|M| and dual characters restrict surjectively to M[2].
Therefore B(a,−)=0 iff x_a∈2A. Writing x_a=2z yields z∈A[8]
and 4z=ā. Lift z to Sel₈ by the Selmer exact sequence and correct the
difference from a by a rational Kummer element, using δ₈(P)→δ₂(P).
Conversely a lift from Sel₈ gives x_a=2z, up to the harmless A[2]
choice, so B is zero on its row. This proves the exact image even when
there are nonzero divisible classes and without any parity theorem.
