## 2. Every non-good exceptional ideal contribution vanishes

- At any odd prime ramifying in $M_i$ and unramified in $F$, the relative extension $E_i/F$ is ramified (an unramified base change cannot remove ramification of this quadratic). Every prime there is fixed under complex conjugation, and $R_i\overline R_i=1$ makes its valuation zero. This includes $17,103,241$ and all other odd prime divisors of $n_i$ except possibly 5, which now does not divide $n_i$.
- At 5 the moving $K_i$ is split by §1 whereas $K_*$ is ramified. The potentially good reductions of the two CM fields at 5 are respectively ordinary and supersingular, whether or not the auxiliary order is locally maximal. Thus no horizontal support meets an actual point. At 7 the **auxiliary field** $K_*$ is split, since $-20\equiv1\bmod7$. Its fixed CM points are all ordinary, including any with 7 in the order conductor. A target ordinary point would have a different quadratic endomorphism field; a target supersingular point has the other reduction type. Neither can coincide with the auxiliary. This argument also excludes horizontal collisions at any other good prime at which either CM field is split, even if that prime divides a fixed order conductor. It does not require changing the prescribed genuine mod-7 row.
- At 2 and 3 both moving $K_i$ are split and $241$ is prime to these primes. Every actual cyclic $p^a$ component of the chosen level ($a=4$ at $p=2$, $a=1$ at $p=3$) is the **same pure branch**, connected for all indices or étale for all indices. Indeed $O_{241}\otimes\mathbb Z_p=\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p$ on the ordinary $p$-divisible group; the named local CM embedding and chosen ideal select the same one of its two idempotents over the entire class orbit. Transport along representatives prime to $p$ preserves this branch. Fixing a different named local embedding may exchange the branches, but not for individual indices.

For precision at 2 and 3, add auxiliary full tame level 5 with one chosen Weil value; it makes the prime-to-$p$ modular curve smooth and geometrically connected, hence with irreducible ordinary special fibre. Work formally over a sufficiently large complete DVR with this fixed residue embedding. On its ordinary locus the unique canonical order-$p^a$ subgroup is the connected part of the $p^a$-torsion (Katz, [§3.7](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/padicpropMFMS.pdf)). This is the formal parameterization of the pure connected branch by the prime-to-$p$ moduli. For the pure étale branch apply $w_{p^a}$ first: the dual isogeny to an étale subgroup has connected subgroup on the quotient, giving the same formal parameterization with $U\circ w_{p^a}$ on the connected branch. Atkin–Lehner and the tame neighbours do not change the auxiliary CM field.

Pull back the rational function to this one regular connected-branch formal surface. The vertical multiplicity is a single integer for its irreducible special fibre. At the actual sections no horizontal component of that divisor meets their reductions. If an auxiliary is supersingular the reductions differ; if it is ordinary and a meeting occurred, specialization injects both distinct CM fields into the one ordinary rational endomorphism field. This argument only excludes horizontal components **through the actual sections**; the whole ordinary locus need not avoid the horizontal divisor. The no-horizontal assertion and regularity imply locally $U=\pi^k\cdot$unit at each actual section with the same vertical $k$. Thus, after preserving the same normalization of $v$, every $v(U(P'_a))$ is equal, and $\sum_a\chi_{241}(a)=0$ makes the relative product valuation zero.

At $p>3$ the good-fibre vertical part cancels in the same weighted way. This proves the promised complete exceptional exclusion: only the unramified-prime formula of the preceding good-prime receiver can contribute, because all fixed auxiliary conductor or ramification primes lie in $\{2,3,5,7,241\}$. It does not identify any of the remaining prime contributions.

## 3. Recover the cubic class from four quadratic ideals

On either arm write $R=R_i$, $n=n_i$, $E=E_i$, and $M_s=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-sn})$ for $s=1,5,6,30$ (squarefree classes). These are precisely the four imaginary quadratic subfields of $E$; $M$ remains disjoint from the fixed real biquadratic field. For the four characters $\chi_s$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$ with $\chi_s(\text{complex conjugation})=-1$, define

$$R_s=N_{E/M_s}(R),\qquad I_s=N_{E/M_s}((R)).$$

The four norm subgroup sums in the group algebra have coefficient 4 at identity, 0 at complex conjugation, and 2 at each of the other six elements. The latter pair with their complex conjugates and cancel multiplicatively on $R$, so

$$R^4=\prod_{s=1,5,6,30} R_s.$$

Each $R_s$ has absolute norm one and generates $I_s$. In a fixed integral binary basis of the imaginary quadratic field, multiply $I_s$ by any positive integer $a$ making it integral. Its nonzero elements are $aR_s z$ for $z\in O_{M_s}\setminus\{0\}$. Thus the shortest nonzero Euclidean vector in this rank-two ideal lattice has norm $a^2$ and equals $aR_s$ up to roots of unity. An exact two-dimensional Gauss reduction supplies that vector and independently checks its squared norm. None of the four $M_s$ equals $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ or $\mathbb Q(i)$: 103 ramifies in every $M_s$ since it occurs once in $n$ and not in $s$. The only ambiguity is therefore $\pm1$, a cube. The principal ideal condition is known here from the product identity; this is not a claim that arbitrary ideals are principal or that their norm certifies the desired element.

At the chosen prime above 29, $F$ is completely split: $(5/29)=(6/29)=+1$. Each $M_s$ then has completion $\mathbb Q_{29^2}$ with the embedding specified by the *named* $\sqrt{-n}$ and the actual chosen $\sqrt s$ residue. Use those four names when reducing the four $R_s$; arbitrary independent conjugation can change the answer. By $4\equiv1\bmod3$, the product of these four local cubic classes is exactly that of $R$. Thus the good-prime valuation enumeration can produce four oriented ideal vectors directly using residue-degree weighted ideal pushforward, without building or reducing any lattice in a degree-eight field. A single unsigned rational norm, a single nonzero exponent, or the four ideal classes without oriented exponents is insufficient. The four actual ideal vectors remain to be computed. A resulting noncube on either full signed receiver gives a sufficient finite order-nine witness for that arm, with no claim that a cube excludes order three or rational rank.
