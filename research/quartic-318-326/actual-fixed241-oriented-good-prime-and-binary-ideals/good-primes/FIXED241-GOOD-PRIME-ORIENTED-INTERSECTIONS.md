# Oriented good-prime intersection input for the fixed-241 primitive receiver

This applies to the actual fixed-103 receiver **with moving fundamental base**, not to an unramified approximation to its fixed-base moving conductor. Write $f=241$, $D=-n/f$ fundamental, $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$ and $M=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-n})$. Use an eligible selected arm with $f$ inert in $K$, $2,3$ split in $K$, and $29$ inert in $K$. The primitive character on the conductor-$f$ ring class group is $\chi_f$. The exact same-target identification and the tame signed vertical operator were established separately. Here we give conditional exact **good-prime** ideal arithmetic for a fixed-algebraic-coefficient modular function with CM-supported finite divisor; no moving discriminant, ideal, prime contribution, algebraic value or nonzero coefficient has been evaluated.

Fix a number field $F$ unramified at $f$, and a nonzero $U\in F(X_0(48))$ whose horizontal divisor over $\overline F$ is supported on cusps and finitely many genuinely marked, fixed CM points $Q$. External to this lemma one needs the reduction identity with the desired cubic function and, for ideal retrieval alone, the norm-one descent in a totally real coefficient field. The construction below works also for non-real $F$, and does not remove a possible unit ambiguity there. Let $R=\prod_a U(P'_a)^{\chi_f(a)}$ over the literal conductor-$f$ chosen level orientation, with the calibration coefficients held fixed. Assume all auxiliary CM fields are distinct from $K$. In a strict local calculation the index set is still the complete indexed orbit, even if different labels coincide in reduction. If $R$ descends to $E=FM$ with $R^{\iota}=R^{-1}$ for $\iota$ the nontrivial automorphism over $F$, the final valuations below are taken in $E$ at named primes, not in a rational norm.

## 1. Fixed dual transport removes the moving ring conductor

At residue characteristic $p\nmid 6f$, on the open noncuspidal moduli, both arrows of the $f$-isogeny correspondence on $X_0(48)$ are étale. A geometric flag has only the automorphisms $\{\pm1\}$: a nontrivial automorphism of order three cannot preserve a cyclic 16-subgroup (its action there would be trivial), and one of order four would have a unit square $-1$ modulo 16. The stack statement is the usual unique extension of a prime-to-$p$ finite étale subgroup through nilpotent thickenings; passing to the coarse curve does not create branches, since the common $\{\pm1\}$ acts trivially on these deformation parameters and on all cyclic groups. Thus an actual conductor-$f$ point $P'$ which meets $Q$ at a geometric residue flag has a unique lift of its **dual** $f$-kernel on $Q$. Write $Q^*$ for the corresponding quotient. It is one of the finitely many fixed $f+1$ quotients of $Q$ in characteristic zero. The marked intersection length between $P'$ and $Q$ equals the length between the maximal-order ancestor $P$ and this $Q^*$, computed with the marked line on $P$ transported from the dual line on $Q^*$.

In detail, reduction equality over $\mathcal O/\pi^j$ in one projection identifies the prescribed dual kernels using the unique lift, hence equality over that same thickening in the correspondence and under the second projection. The same argument reversing the arrows gives the converse. This retains each literal occurrence of $Q$ in the divisor and each indexed branch, even when two distinct $Q^*$ or two different lines have the same coarse reduction. As $f$ is inert in $K$, every $f$-line of the maximal source is vertical; no spurious horizontal line need be deleted. Auxiliaries can acquire conductor $f$, but that is fixed. Only maximal-order, fundamental-$D$ lifts occur on the moving side.

## 2. The signed ideal has a common absolute orientation

Let a named extension of a prime of $E$ specify embeddings of $\sqrt D$ and $\sqrt f$ in the strict local field, with their product equal to the specified $\sqrt{-n}$. When $p\ne f$, choose a primitive $f$th root $\zeta$ whose quadratic Gauss sum is the specified $\sqrt f$; this can equally be imposed in the residue field since the roots and the Gauss sum are $p$-units, and $p$ is odd. Use $f$-torsion frames with Weil pairing $\zeta$. Changing among such frames has determinant 1; changing the primitive root within the same Gauss-sum half has square multiplier.

The analytic principal maximal-order lattice is $\langle1,\tau\rangle$ with $\tau=(1+\sqrt D)/2$ and fixed upper-half-plane CM type. For the line generated by $1/f$ the target is $(1/f)O_f$, the principal conductor-$f$ class. In the positive analytic symplectic frame $(1/f,\tau/f)$, $\sqrt D\cdot 1=2\tau-1$; therefore the determinant on this line is $2$, a square modulo $241$, matching the principal weight $+1$. Reversing the global Weil convention only multiplies by $-1$, also a square. In each fibre the finite quadratic module is free of rank one. Translating a line by a unit $w$ of that module multiplies the determinant by $N(w)$, exactly its quadratic class-character change. Transporting from the principal base by an ideal isogeny of degree $d$ coprime to $pf$ multiplies the Weil determinant exponent by $d$; it also changes the genus character by $(d/f)$. Such prime-to-$pf$ representatives always exist. Therefore on all good primes and all genuine indices the **named** character is exactly

$$\chi_f(a)=\left(\frac{\det(z,\iota_D(\sqrt D)z)}{f}\right),$$

with the named differential and the transported line $\langle z\rangle$. This is stronger than an unknown common sign at each independent prime. Replacing both $\sqrt D,\sqrt f$ by their negatives leaves $M$ fixed: the two split level orientations are reversed by $w_{48}$, and the Gauss-sum convention changes the quadratic sign of the determinant. If $U\circ w_{48}=U^{-1}$, the two changes cancel on the total valuation. The replacement does not identify opposite primes of $E/F$, which instead have opposite valuations under the inverse-conjugation hypothesis.

## 3. Explicit good-prime thickness and the finite support envelope

Let $D_*$ be the fixed order discriminant of one dual neighbour $Q^*$. Work at $p\nmid 6fDD_*$, under a named geometric reduction of $Q^*$ and its exact 48-flag and distinguished line. If one CM field splits at $p$ and the other is nonsplit, the reduction types differ. If both split, equality would force two distinct quadratic fields inside the single commutative ordinary endomorphism algebra, which is impossible. The sole case for the count below is when both fields are inert. Denote the fixed maximal residue endomorphism order by $\mathcal R=\operatorname{End}(\widetilde{Q^*})$, its rational quaternion algebra by $B_{p,\infty}$, and put $U_*=\iota_*(\sqrt{D_*})$. This embedding is part of the fixed calibration input and is locally maximal at $p$. Put $e_*\in\{0,1\}$ for the parity of $D_*$. For the moving base choose each element $V\in\mathcal R$ with

$$\operatorname{Trd}(V)=0,\qquad V^2=D,\qquad (1+V)/2\in\mathcal R.$$

Keep exactly those inducing the named Lie action of $\sqrt D$ and the prescribed split cyclic eigensubgroups modulo 16 and 3. The element describes a maximal-order embedding, so optimality is automatic; normalized Deuring lifting (Elkies–Ono–Yang §3, following Gross–Zagier) yields exactly one indexed characteristic-zero CM curve for it, and the tamed flag ascends uniquely. The two central automorphisms of the flag act trivially by conjugation, so one must not divide this count by two. The eigenvalue check modulo 16 uses the full selected root, not its reduction modulo 2. The distinguished line of $Q^*$ itself is read by the determinant of §2 on $V$.

Write $x=\operatorname{Trd}(VU_*)/2$. Since the two discriminant elements are $2\omega-1$ and $2\omega_*-e_*$, we have $x=2\operatorname{Trd}(\omega\omega_*)-e_*\in\mathbb Z$ and $x\equiv e_*\pmod 2$. Since the two CM fields differ, $V$ and $U_*$ are not rational multiples. Positive definiteness at $\infty$ gives $|x|<\sqrt{D D_*}$. At $p$, fix the decomposition $\mathcal R_p=\mathcal O_{p^2}\oplus\mathcal O_{p^2}\Pi$, with $\Pi b=\bar b\Pi$, $\Pi^2=p$, and the first factor supplied by the auxiliary CM embedding. Write $V=(x/D_*)U_*+b\Pi$. Then

$$D D_*-x^2 = D_*p N(b),\qquad v_p(D D_*-x^2)=1+2v_p(b).$$

In particular every such collision has a prime divisor of the positive integer on the left, with **odd** depth. Its exact unramified marked thickness is

$$\frac{v_p(D D_*-x^2)+1}{2}.$$

Indeed, the deformation endomorphism order modulo $p^j$ of the fixed auxiliary canonical lifting is $\mathcal O_{p^2}+p^{j-1}\mathcal R_p$, by [Lauter–Viray, (6.6)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.6942). The selected moving root of $D$ on Lie, a unit, is uniquely determined at every $j$ by its reduction (the roots of $X^2-D$ are simple); membership of $V$ in that congruence order therefore comes with exactly the correct Lie action. The lifted embedding criterion from their Proposition 4.1 proof and normalized Deuring lifting identify the isomorphism of the two models over the thickening. Prime-to-$p$ flags extend uniquely, so this is exactly their intersection depth, not an unsigned $j$-multiplicity. The maximum such $j$ is $1+v_p(b)$, proving the assertion. The two fields being unramified inert at $p$ means the strictly unramified base already suffices; no additional ramification factor enters this depth.

Consequently at each such named good prime the valuation of $R$ is obtained by summing the exact multiplicity of each $Q$ in $\mathrm{div}(U)$ over its fixed dual neighbours and over these embeddings, times the determinant sign, times the above depth. Normalize intermediate valuations by $v(p)=1$ and multiply by $e(P/p)$ for integral ideal exponents in $E$ if necessary. The vertical contribution from the model of $U$ is constant on the smooth geometrically integral good fibre, hence cancels by $\sum_a\chi_f(a)=0$. Cusps do not meet actual CM points because their $j$ is a pole and CM $j$ is integral. One has not confused a chosen Lie embedding with its Frobenius conjugate: changing the named prime generally changes which embeddings pass.

## 4. What is bounded and what remains uncomputed

All rational primes outside the finite exceptional set $\{2,3,f\}\cup\{p\mid D D_*\text{ for some }D_*\}$ are among the divisors of

$$D D_*-x^2,\quad |x|<\sqrt{D D_*},\quad x\equiv D_*\pmod2,$$

for a fixed finite family of discriminants $D_*$, with the stricter odd-depth and inert filters above. To list candidate prime divisors one can sieve the $O(\sqrt{|D|})$ positive values for each fixed $D_*$ through primes at most $\sqrt{D D_*}$ by solving $x^2\equiv D D_*\bmod p$, divide out found powers, and keep the leftover prime if greater than 1. When $p\mid D D_*$ the unique residue root 0 is simply treated separately. This is an exact support-only sieve: with Las Vegas modular square-root precomputation it uses expected $O(\sqrt{|D|}\,\operatorname{polylog}|D|)$ bit operations up to fixed constants, by at most two progression roots per prime and expected polylogarithmic root work; it **does not** bound the cost of building all marked endomorphism orders and counting the passing embeddings. Factoring the support polynomial separately per $x$ is not required.

There is nevertheless a fixed-quadratic, rather than a moving ternary, description of each candidate row. Fix $Q^*,p,x$ and write $B_{p,\infty}=K_*\oplus K_*J$ with $K_*=\mathbb Q(U_*)$, $J^2\in\mathbb Q_{<0}$ and $Jb=\bar bJ$. Any candidate is $(x/D_*)U_*+bJ$ with

$$N_{K_*/\mathbb Q}(b)=(D-x^2/D_*)/J^2.$$

The projection of $\mathcal R$ to $K_*J$ supplies a known fractional lattice in the **fixed** imaginary field $K_*$. One can enlarge this to an integral denominator-cleared maximal-order module and solve the resulting binary norm exactly, then filter $\mathcal R$, Lie and ordered torsion conditions. This is a replacement for enumerating the moving class group, not an evaluated ideal or a runtime lower bound on faster aggregation. In particular the residue endomorphism order and its actions on the actual 16-, 3- and 241-torsion in the named reductions are still needed.

If the relative inverse-conjugation descent holds and $p$ is odd ramified in $M$ but unramified in $F$, then all oriented ideal exponents above $p$ vanish: $E/F$ ramifies there, so $\iota$ fixes each such prime and an exponent is its own negative. This includes the conductor prime $241$ in the stipulated coefficient field. At $p=2,3$, or at a prime ramified in a fixed auxiliary field or an auxiliary order conductor, the present unramified thickness formula does not apply. Those separate local contributions must be proved zero or computed before a full ideal can be asserted. In particular this note does not posit a true real-coefficient calibration that has not been verified, and no nonzero primitive readout or original point follows from the support reduction alone.
